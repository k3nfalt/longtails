# A last-iterate bound from the displacement of one cyclic pass

For the cyclic gradient method with step $1/L$, the average objective gap after $K\ge 2$ complete passes satisfies

$$
 F(x_K)-F(x_\star)
 \le \frac{2B\sqrt B\,L\|x_0-x_\star\|^2}{m\sqrt{K(K-1)}}
 \le \frac{2m^2LR^2}{K-1},\qquad B=1+m(m-1).
$$

The previous certificate gave $m^3L\|x_0-x_\star\|^2/K$. Both refer to the current iterate at the end of the specified pass. [New theorem](CyclicGradientPassDisplacement.lean) · [Previous theorem](CyclicGradientInterpolation.lean)

## Setup and exact call budget

There are $m\ge1$ differentiable convex functions on a real Hilbert space, each with $L$-Lipschitz gradient. A common minimizer $x_\star$ lies within $R$ of the initial point. The objective $F$ is their arithmetic mean. The method receives the space, $m,L,x_0$. At call $t$, starting at zero, it queries the exact gradient of component $t\bmod m$ at its current point and subtracts that gradient divided by $L$. The setup fixes the order, step, and output. The radius $R\ge0$ and accuracy $\varepsilon>0$ enter only the budget, and $L>0$. [Setup](../Setups/InterpolatedFiniteSum.lean) · [Algorithm](../Algorithms/CyclicGradientPassDisplacement.lean)

Write $u=LR^2/\varepsilon$. The proved budget for average gap at most $\varepsilon$ is

$$
 N=\begin{cases}
 0 & LR^2\le2\varepsilon,\\
 \min\{m\lceil m^3u\rceil,\ m(1+\lceil 2B\sqrt B\,u/m\rceil)\} & LR^2>2\varepsilon.
 \end{cases}
$$

The theorem identifies the output of `execute` at exactly $N$ calls, for every seed. A complete pass costs $m$ calls. At zero calls the method returns $x_0$.

### Comparison including rounding

The only earlier theorem on this setup has budget $N_0=m\lceil m^3u\rceil$. Lean proves $N\le N_0$ pointwise in `budget_le_existing`. The exact regimes are as follows.

| Parameters | Comparison | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| $u=0$ | Equal | Both budgets vanish. |
| $0<u\le2$ | Strictly smaller | The old budget charges at least one pass. |
| $u>2$, $m=1$ | Equal | The second candidate has $1+\lceil2u\rceil$ passes. |
| $u>2$, $m\ge2$ | Strictly smaller | The difference in unrounded pass counts absorbs the added pass and both ceilings. |

For the last row put $a=2B\sqrt B/m$. At $m=2$, $m^3-a=8-3\sqrt3>2$. At $m\ge3$, $B\le m^2$ gives $m^3-a\ge m^2(m-2)\ge9$. Thus $m^3u>au+2$, implying $\lceil m^3u\rceil\ge\lceil au\rceil+2$. For fixed $m\ge2$ the ratio $N/N_0$ tends to $2B^{3/2}/m^4$ as $u$ grows. This coefficient is asymptotic to $2/m$ as $m$ grows. The leading call count falls from order $m^4u$ to order $m^3u$.

## Why displacement controls the returned point

Let $y_t$ denote the point after $t$ calls and $x_K=y_{Km}$. Set $r_K=\|x_K-x_\star\|$, $d_K=\|x_K-x_{K+1}\|$, and let $E_K$ be $2/L$ times the sum of the component gaps at their respective pre-update points during the pass starting at $x_K$.

1. The one-pass Fejér inequality gives $0\le E_K\le r_K^2-r_{K+1}^2$. Hence $r_{K+1}\le r_K\le r_0$. Factor the difference of squares and use $r_K-r_{K+1}\le d_K$ to get $E_K\le2r_0d_K$.
2. The existing potential bounds $K E_K+K(K-1)d_K^2+r_{K+1}^2+(B-1)r_K^2$ by $r_1^2+(B-1)r_0^2$. Keeping the displacement term gives $\sqrt{K(K-1)}d_K\le\sqrt B\,r_0$.
3. The existing transfer inequality bounds the sum of the gaps at the single point $x_K$ by $LB E_K$. Divide by $m$ and substitute the two estimates above. Using $\sqrt{K(K-1)}\ge K-1$ and $\sqrt B\le m$ gives the simpler bound. The old argument used $K E_K\le B r_0^2$ directly and lost an extra factor of order $\sqrt B$.
4. Outside the zero branch, $K=1+\lceil au\rceil\ge2$ makes the sharper estimate at most $\varepsilon$. The alternative pass count uses the existing accuracy lemma for the very same recursion. For the zero branch, each gradient vanishes at $x_\star$, so the smooth upper model there gives $f_i(x_0)-f_i(x_\star)\le LR^2/2$.

All three pass inequalities use the same $K$. Values in the continuation to $x_{K+1}$ appear only in the proof. The execution stops at $x_K$ without certification queries. ## Prior work and scope

In the comparisons below $T=m$ is the number of tasks, $K$ counts cycles and $k=TK$ counts individual updates at a cycle boundary. For a closed convex set $C_i$, taking $f_i=(L/2)\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,C_i)^2$ makes one gradient step its metric projection. Thus the average gap measures the **mean squared distance** times $L/2$. This specialization is on paper. In realizable linear regression the exact task fit projects onto an affine solution space. Choosing task matrices to be orthogonal-complement projections identifies their squared residuals with squared distances.

- **Cyclic linear regression.** Evron, Moroshko, Ward, Srebro and Soudry (2022), Eq. (10) and Theorem 11, give normalized worst-case forgetting between $T^2/(24e k)$ and $\min\{T^2/\sqrt{k},T^2(d-r_{\max})/(2k)\}$ for $T\ge3$, $k=TK\ge T^2$. Here $d$ is dimension and $r_{\max}$ the maximum task rank. The lower rate is $\Omega(T/K)$ per cycle. Theorem 10 treats two tasks and gives order $1/k$. Table 1 of Evron et al. (2025) attributes the cyclic $T^2/k$ lower bound to the 2022 paper. The existing LongTails note reports that table entry in individual iterations. [2022 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.09588) · [2025 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.04579)
- **The previously known quadratic power.** Swartworth, Needell, Ward, Kong and Jeong (2023), Theorem 1, bound normalized forgetting after $K$ repetitions of $T$ tasks by $O(T^2/K)$, independently of dimension. Lemma 2 bounds the norm of $(I-A)A^K$ for a product of $T$ orthogonal projections by order $T/K$. Their proof connects norm decrease over a cycle to forgetting and controls the numerical range of the product. Their per-cycle upper bound already gives the component power here for subspace projections. The present proof establishes that power for general smooth convex components on Hilbert space with the setup's fixed update and exact last iterate. [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/d72ae75abaa70a3b19c5d4f436c680d1-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **Convex sets: the same order was already derivable.** For cyclic projections onto closed convex sets $C_i$ with nonempty intersection $C$, Reich and Zalas, Lemma 3.1, Eq. (3.2), give at every cycle endpoint $\frac1m\sum_i\operatorname{dist}(x_K,C_i)^2\le\frac m2\|x_K-x_{K-1}\|\operatorname{dist}(x_{K-1},C)$. The last distance is at most $R$. The previous LongTails note already records the displacement bound $K(K-1)\|x_K-x_{K+1}\|^2\le B\|x_0-x_\star\|^2$ from the existing potential. Applying it at index $K-1$ and multiplying by $L/2$ gives, for $K\ge3$, $F(x_K)-F(x_\star)\le Lm\sqrt B\,R^2/(4\sqrt{(K-1)(K-2)})=O(m^2LR^2/K)$ in the projection specialization, with the same mean squared distance, cyclic order and endpoint. So for projections onto arbitrary intersecting closed convex sets the order of the present bound follows from that lemma and the repository's earlier displacement estimate, and Swartworth et al. state it explicitly for linear tasks. The present theorem contributes the displacement-to-objective bound for general smooth convex components, where a gradient step is no projection, and the exact call budget. No earlier theorem found in the search gives this full-step, repeated-order last-iterate guarantee for arbitrary smooth interpolating components. [Reich–Zalas, Lemma 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13843) · [Previous note](CyclicGradientInterpolation.md)
- **Cyclic and random convex projections.** Evron et al. (2023), Proposition 4.13, bound the maximum squared distance for intersecting closed convex sets by $R^2/(k+1)$ for two sets and $2T^2R^2/\sqrt{k}$ for $T\ge3$. Theorem 4.12 obtains a geometric rate for separable classification with a geometry-dependent exponent, proportional to $-k/(T^2\|w^*\|^2R_{\rm data}^2)$ for $T\ge3$. In Evron et al. (2025), Reduction 3 and Theorem 14 bound the expected half mean squared distance by $7R^2/k^{1/4}$ for random projections, with replacement or up to one pass without replacement. These universal convex-set results concern different residuals or sampling orders. [2023 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.03534) · [2025 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.04579)
- **Classical projection estimates.** For linear subspaces, Bauschke, Borwein and Lewis, Theorem 3.7.4, characterize when the norm $q$ of the cycle operator on the orthogonal complement of the intersection is strictly below one: the sum of the orthogonal complements must be closed. This gives a bound $q^K R$ on the distance to the intersection, with a geometry-dependent $q$. Bauschke and Borwein, Theorem 5.7, obtain linear convergence for boundedly linearly regular convex families under their projection and control assumptions. Neither gives a geometry-independent factor from $T$ alone. [Bauschke–Borwein–Lewis](https://people.orie.cornell.edu/aslewis/publications/97-method.ps) · [Bauschke–Borwein](https://cmps-people.ok.ubc.ca/bauschke/Research/05.pdf)
- **Residuals and increments.** Reich and Zalas, Theorem 3.2, Eqs. (3.12–13), obtain $o(K^{-1/2})$ cycle increments and $o(K^{-1/4})$ root mean squared distance for a fixed intersecting convex family. Their Theorem 1.4 gives $o(1/K)$ increments for subspaces. Ariza-Ruiz, López-Acedo and Nicolae, Theorem 3.4 and Remark 3.5, give a sufficient order $T^3(R/\delta)^4$ individual steps for increments at most $\delta$ for cyclic firmly nonexpansive maps in Hilbert space. Kohlenbach's Corollary 2.15 gives a cycle-displacement estimate even for inconsistent sets. Substitution of Theorem 2.11 and Lemma 2.14 into Theorem 2.5 gives a sufficient $O(\delta^{-8})$ cycles for displacement below $\delta$, with the set count and geometric data fixed. Baillon, Combettes and Cominetti, Theorem 2.3 and Eq. (2.4), rule out a universal variational characterization of cycles for three or more sets in dimension at least two. These results concern different quantities from an explicit finite-pass average objective guarantee. [Reich–Zalas](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13843) · [Ariza-Ruiz et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.6779) · [Kohlenbach](https://www2.mathematik.tu-darmstadt.de/~kohlenbach/inconsistentfeasibility.pdf) · [Baillon et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.1378)
- **Small-step incremental and shuffled gradients.** For smooth convex components under interpolation, the optimum variance vanishes. Mishchenko, Khaled and Richtárik (2020), Theorem 3, bound the expected gap of the average of epoch endpoints under reshuffling or a single shuffle by $R^2/(2\gamma mK)$ in this case. A step of order $1/(mL)$ gives $O(LR^2/K)$. Cai, Lin and Diakonikolas (2023 preprint), Theorems 1–2 for linear-predictor empirical risk and 4–5 for general smooth finite sums, give the analogous averaged-epoch term $R^2/(2m\eta K)$ for singleton batches, with random or fixed permutations and data-dependent step restrictions. For example their fixed-order Theorem 2 permits $\eta\le1/(m\sqrt{2\widehat L_0\widetilde L_0})$, giving $O(\sqrt{\widehat L_0\widetilde L_0}R^2/K)$ at that step. These averaged guarantees permit better dependence on $m$ for their own choices of step and output. [Mishchenko et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05988) · [Cai–Lin–Diakonikolas](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.12498)
- **Last iterates with other steps or orders.** In Cai and Diakonikolas, Theorem 2.3, Eq. (2.3), setting $\sigma_*=0$, $\alpha=4$, $\beta=4\max(1,\log K)$ and $\eta=1/(mL\sqrt\beta)$ gives $O(LR^2\sqrt{1+\log K}/K)$ for cyclic smooth convex losses. Liu and Zhou, Theorem 4.4, also allow arbitrary permutations and get the same order under interpolation, equal smoothness and zero regularizer, using their small horizon-dependent step. At the full step $1/L$, Attia, Schliserman, Sherman and Koren (2025), Theorems 1 and 3, give expected last-iterate gap $O(LR^2/\sqrt{k})$ for smooth convex interpolation with independent random draws or sampling without replacement up to one pass (their Eq. (4)). The latter has no repeated fixed order. For independent random draws, Schliserman and Koren (2026), Theorem 1, sharpen this at the full step under interpolation to an expected last-iterate gap of at most $1056\,LR^2\log^3(ek)/k$ at horizon $k$ in their indexing; the independence of the draws matters there. [Cai–Diakonikolas](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.06873) · [Liu–Zhou](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.07723) · [Attia et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11274) · [Schliserman–Koren](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25469)

The search covered the cited primary papers on shuffled gradients, continual regression and classification, cyclic projections, and asymptotic regularity, plus the existing repository results. The verified improvement is over the repository's full-step cyclic certificate. For unrestricted optimization, batching the $m$ gradients at a common point gives the exact gradient of the $L$-smooth convex average. The repository's accelerated convex theorem then costs $m\max\{1,\lceil\sqrt{2u}\rceil\}$ component queries, substantially fewer at high accuracy. Its updates fall outside this setup's algorithm family. [Accelerated full-gradient theorem](AcceleratedConvex.lean)

## Lower bounds, numerics and limitations

The earlier note reports small cyclic projection computations suggesting linear growth in $m$: it tested $K E_K$ for $m\le8$, $K\le4$ (up to $K=12$ at $m=3$), and $K\max_i\operatorname{dist}^2(x_K,C_i)$ for $m\le6$, $K\le6$ (up to $K=16$ at $m=4$). It separately reports pass-inequality constants computed through $m=10$. Those finite grids measure online loss or maximum distance and do not certify an exponent for all $K,m$. [Previous note, Limitations](CyclicGradientInterpolation.md#limitations)

Two informal hard-instance arguments concern precisely the fixed method. Neither lower bound has been formalized here.

- For $m\ge3$, $R>0$ and $0<\varepsilon<LR^2/8$, one instance in dimension three fails simultaneously at all integer budgets $0\le N<m^2LR^2/(32\pi^2\varepsilon)$. Its component losses are scaled squared distances to lines through a common point. Their unit directions lie on a cone at azimuths $2\pi j/m$. With $b=4\varepsilon/(LR^2)$, successive projections shrink the length by $q=1-b(1-\cos(2\pi/m))$. The mean squared residual gives $F(y_N)\ge2\varepsilon q^{2N}$, and $2N(1-q)<1/2$ in the stated range.
- For $m\ge8$ and each $K\ge m^2$, a family of cyclic subspace projections in an infinite-dimensional real Hilbert space gives gap at least $c m^2LR^2/K$, with $c=1/(10{,}240{,}000\pi^2)$. The instance may depend on $m,K$. The construction uses a product of projections approximating a rotation on a function space, followed by projections that damp one direction. The rotating initial point retains a substantial component in that direction at the selected horizon. If correct, the example at $K=m^2$ rules out every uniform pass bound $C m^p LR^2/K$ with $p<2$. Starting at a suffix of a hard run and cyclically relabeling tasks gives the corresponding call obstruction even mid-pass: for $0<\varepsilon<cLR^2/2$, every $N<c m^3 LR^2/(2\varepsilon)$ can fail. This would make the power of $m$ in the call budget tight on infinite-dimensional spaces in that regime.

The constant $2$ in the upper bound comes from $r_K+r_{K+1}\le2r_0$ and has no sharpness certificate. The three-dimensional example alone leaves a factor of order $m$ in calls. There is no formal lower bound for this setup in the repository. A formal lower-bound theorem needs a setup with appropriate dimension and accuracy restrictions because valid parameters here include cases where zero calls suffice. The lower-bound constructions, the projection specialization, and any claim of optimal constants remain outside the Lean result.
