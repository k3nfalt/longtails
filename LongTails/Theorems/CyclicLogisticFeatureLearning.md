# A finite-stepsize separation between linear and convolutional logistic GD

Training both taps of a cyclic convolution and its linear readout can accelerate the first time full-batch logistic gradient descent classifies every observation correctly. On the same six-observation datasets in dimensions $d=4,8,12,\ldots$, joint training takes $O(\sqrt d\,\log^2 d)$ full-gradient steps. Freezing the filter at the identity gives ordinary linear logistic regression. From the same zero predictor, **every positive constant stepsize**, even one chosen with knowledge of the entire dataset, requires more than $\lfloor d/2^{20}\rfloor$ steps. Both models represent every linear predictor on the inputs. The distinction comes from their training dynamics. The upper bound extends to an entire class of finite sample clouds. ## Data, oracle and training rule

Let $b_i=y_ix_i\in\mathbb R^d$ be the signed examples, $1\le i\le n$, and let $P$ rotate the coordinates by one position. A filter $h\in\mathbb R^2$ and readout $v\in\mathbb R^d$ give the margin
$$
m_i=h_1\langle b_i,v\rangle+h_2\langle Pb_i,v\rangle.
$$
GD minimizes the empirical mean of $\log(1+e^{-m_i})$. The initial filter is $(1,0)$ and the readout is zero. One oracle query returns a single signed margin and its parameter gradient. At a finite margin this information is equivalent to the component logistic value and gradient by inversion of the loss and division by its nonzero derivative. One full pass computes the mean gradient at a single point, including all correctly classified observations. The algorithm takes a simultaneous constant step on both layers after each unsuccessful pass. The first pass with all margins strictly positive freezes its point. Every subsequent query required by the fixed-budget execution is counted. The output is this certified iterate. [Setup and allowed GD family](../Setups/CyclicLogisticFeatureLearning.lean) · [Algorithm and output](../Algorithms/CyclicLogisticFeatureLearning.lean) · [Execution proof](../Proofs/CyclicLogisticExecution.lean)

Write $z_i=(b_i+Pb_i)/\sqrt2$ and $r_i=(b_i-Pb_i)/\sqrt2$. The theorem allows arbitrary positive sample count, positive even dimension, and $0<\mu,\kappa,\rho\le1$. It assumes

- $\|z_i\|\le1$ and $\|r_i\|\le\mu$. These bounds measure scale and spatial contrast.
- Some unit vector $u$ has $\langle r_i,u\rangle\ge\mu\kappa$ for every $i$. Thus differencing exposes a common weak class signal.
- For every $v$ in the span of the $z_i$, some $i$ has $\langle z_i,v\rangle\le-\rho\|v\|$. Equivalently, their convex hull contains the radius-$\rho$ ball in its own span. Spatial averaging exposes a nuisance component that cannot separate the classes. This is a worst-observation margin condition. The mean-negative-part degree of nonseparability in the logistic-regression literature aggregates observations differently. [Freund, Grigas and Mazumder, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08727)
- $\langle z_i,r_j\rangle=0$ for all pairs. This exact structural assumption holds for signals symmetric about a common reflection center. Indeed, the inner product equals half the difference of the two opposite lag-one cross-correlations, and reflection interchanges them. It permits interacting, nonorthogonal examples within either feature space. The theorem gives no perturbation tolerance for misalignment.

The shift, filter and margin come from Tyurin's model. The joint quantitative nuisance and signal geometry is a new restriction for this comparison. It models reflection-aligned signals with weak label information in spatial differences and conflicting information after averaging. It imposes no prescribed vectors, sample order, fixed rank, or pairwise nonnegative Gram entries within a channel. For example, in a common-center cosine subspace one may use paired positive and negative mixtures of low-frequency modes and add a small signed alternating component shared by the examples. Opposite mixtures supply the relative inradius, their slowly varying modes have small adjacent differences, and the alternating component supplies a separator. The radius can shrink as the number of modes grows. Even dimension is intrinsic here. A convex combination canceling the summed features must have a nonzero differenced component by strict separability, so $I+P$ has a nonzero kernel. The setup concerns one full empirical dataset per instance. It does not give an online or population guarantee.

## Rate and mechanism

Put $L=1+\log(2+n/(\mu\kappa))$. The algorithm uses $\eta=\rho\kappa/(2^{16}nL)$. Its deterministic budget in component calls is
$$
n\left(\left\lceil \frac{2^{23}n^2 L^2}{\rho\mu\kappa^2}\right\rceil+1\right).
$$
The extra pass certifies separation. Only the count and numerical geometry bounds are public. Neither the training rule nor its output selection uses a separator, the nuisance span, or a decomposition of the samples. The theorem claims strict training classification. It gives no prescribed logistic-loss tolerance, normalized margin or generalization error. [Parameter proof](../Proofs/CyclicLogisticTuning.lean)

Rotating the filter into its sum and difference coordinates gives scalars $a,t$. The readout splits along the two feature spans into $v_z,v_r$. Orthogonality gives the exact finite margin $a\langle z_i,v_z\rangle+t\langle r_i,v_r\rangle$ and the corresponding simultaneous GD update. Let $F$ be the mean logistic loss, $E=a^2+\|v_z\|^2+t^2+\|v_r\|^2$, and $G$ the update direction. A local finite-step estimate gives $F^+\le F-\eta\|G\|^2/2$, while each block's imbalance, scalar squared minus readout norm squared, changes by at most $\eta^2\|G\|^2$. Telescoping keeps the weak factor positive and $t^2-\|v_r\|^2\ge1/4$. To justify descent along the whole prefix, the proof uses the strongly negative nuisance margin and $F\le1$ to bound the strong block energy by $1+(2n+\mu E)/\rho$. This prevents the growing weak block from imposing a stepsize proportional to $\mu$. [Coordinate identities](../Proofs/CyclicLogisticCoordinates.lean) · [Finite logistic descent](../Proofs/BilinearLogisticDescent.lean) · [Coupled trajectory](../Proofs/CyclicLogisticTrajectory.lean)

Until strict separation, some sigmoid weight is at least $1/2$. The weak block's imbalance turns this into multiplicative growth of $1+\langle v_r,u\rangle$ by at least $1+\eta\mu\kappa/(8n)$ per step. Meanwhile $E+2\eta F\le2+4\eta k$ after $k$ steps. The two inequalities become incompatible at the stated horizon. A frozen identity filter lacks this feedback from the readout to the filter. [Signal growth and nuisance control](../Proofs/CyclicLogisticSignal.lean)

## Comparison on identical observations

For $d$ divisible by four, let $z,c,v$ be the orthonormal unit constant, half-period square-wave and alternating vectors. Set $\delta=d^{-1/2}$ and use the six signed rows
$$
 b_i=(s_i+\delta v)/2,\qquad
 (s_i)=(z,z,-z,c/128,c/128,-c/128).
$$
There are four distinct rows. The multiplicities implement two ordinary $2:1$ empirical class imbalances. Both nuisance axes are independent. Under the original one-position shift, the square wave has difference-feature norm $2/\sqrt d$, its sum feature has norm at least one, and its two features are orthogonal. These give the upper setup with exactly $n=6,\mu=2\delta,\rho=1/512,\kappa=1/4$. The optimal signed Euclidean margin of the raw rows is $\delta/2$, attained in direction $v$ and bounded above using either opposite nuisance pair. [Cyclic wave identities](../Proofs/CyclicLogisticWaves.lean) · [Inradius proof](../Proofs/CyclicLogisticInradius.lean) · [Admissibility and delay](../Proofs/CyclicLogisticComparison.lean)

After absorbing the common input scaling into the step, write $h$ for the linear stepsize divided by six. The three scalar scores are the two nuisance contributions and the shared weak contribution $Y$, with $0\le Y_k\le6kh\delta^2$. For $h\le2000$, the small nuisance block keeps its score at least $Y$ while $2^{20}k\delta^2\le1$. One of its signed margins is then nonpositive. For $h\ge2000$, the large nuisance block visits three phases. The first phase lies in $[h/3,2h/3]$, the second is at most $-h/4$, and the third is at least $h$. The finite sigmoid corrections at the two ends push the return map inward. This prevents error accumulation over $O(\delta^{-2})$ steps and covers intermediate stepsizes as well as arbitrarily large ones. The lower proof follows the **actual raw logistic iterates** at every iteration through the cutoff. [Three-phase and small-step bounds](../Proofs/FiniteLogisticLinearComparison.lean) · [Exact linear GD recurrence](../Proofs/FiniteLogisticHardFamily.lean)

At fixed $n,\rho,\kappa$ the upper rate is $O(\mu^{-1}\log^2(1/\mu))$; the comparison family requires $\Omega(\mu^{-2})$ raw steps. Each method uses the same six component evaluations per full gradient, and the nonlinear budget includes its certification pass. The certified improvement applies asymptotically as contrast decreases. The constants give a conservative crossover. For general growing $n$ or deteriorating $\rho,\kappa$, the displayed factors must be retained. The lower bound is specific to zero-initialized raw GD with a constant stepsize and the first strictly correct iterate. Adaptive steps, an explicitly chosen difference filter, or direct separation after reading the data can change the comparison. There is no lower bound here on unrestricted algorithms.

## Relation to prior results

Tyurin's Theorem 2.1 relates suitable logistic training limits to generalized perceptron dynamics. Theorems 5.1–5.2 exhibit a dimension-dependent acceleration on a prescribed pair via a priority, perturbed quadratic perceptron and a classical perceptron comparison. Transporting that comparison to finite GD would require controlling both finite trajectories and every candidate linear stepsize. The upper theorem here handles variable multi-observation clouds with the original adjacent two taps, the identity filter, zero readout and genuine finite full-batch sigmoid steps. The six-observation lower bound supplies the same-data comparison. The new geometry and training rule change the hypotheses of Tyurin's theorem; the two results need not apply to the same pair. [Tyurin, ICML 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/tyurin26a.html) · [Theorems 2.1 and 5.1–5.2, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11587v2)

Musat and Boumal's Lemma 5.5 and Proposition 5.8 imply that a sufficiently large data-dependent constant step finds a perfect linear classifier within $O(n/\gamma^2)$ steps at raw margin $\gamma$ under their normalization. This is the relevant large-step baseline. For fixed $n$, the dependence on margin agrees with the linear delay on the family above. Wen, Wen and Teng study the onset of permanent monotone loss decrease, a different stopping time. Zhang, Wu and Bartlett establish very fast risk convergence with adaptive steps and averaged outputs. Their guarantees do not imply the same-data lower or the displayed joint-training bound. [Musat and Boumal, October 2026 preprint, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26748v1) · [Wen, Wen and Teng, October 2026 preprint, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01459) · [Zhang, Wu and Bartlett, ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhang25cf.html)

The repository also has a many-sample gated convolutional perceptron and a finite-scale stochastic logistic result with randomized sampling, different initialization and a restrictive small-contrast condition. This theorem allows the full stated contrast range under exact cross-channel orthogonality, uses deterministic full gradients, and proves an all-constant-step linear obstruction on an admissible family. No claim of a faster best-possible separator search, robustness to broken orthogonality, or optimal sample-count dependence is made.
