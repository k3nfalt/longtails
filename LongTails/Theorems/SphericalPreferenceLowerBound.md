# A comparison lower bound on the median-Lipschitz class

For every $d\ge2$, $R,B>0$ and $0<\varepsilon\le BR/16$, every adaptive randomized algorithm using at most $\lfloor d(BR/\varepsilon)^2/524288\rfloor$ binary comparisons fails the expected objective accuracy criterion on some admissible instance. This includes zero queries and the stated endpoint. For example, $d=2$, $B=R=1$ and any $0<\varepsilon\le 1/16$ belong to the regime. The instance may depend on the algorithm and budget. Its objective and distribution are fixed before drawing the algorithm's seed. The companion upper theorem covers exactly the same ball, sample law assumptions, public information, accuracy criterion and cost model. Their constants differ substantially. Together they determine the minimax parameter dependence $\Theta(dB^2R^2/\varepsilon^2)$. ## Hard instances

Set $a=8\varepsilon/R$. A sign vector $v\in\{-1,1\}^d$ indexes the linear objective $f_v(x)=-a\langle v/\sqrt d,x\rangle$, minimized at $Rv/\sqrt d$. The same spherical noise law works for every vertex. With probability $3/4$ draw a centered isotropic Gaussian with coordinate standard deviation $B/(8\sqrt d)$, and otherwise draw one with standard deviation $2B$. A second-moment bound gives the first component probability at least $15/16$ of landing within radius $B/2$. Since $a\le B/2$, more than half the realized utilities are $B$-Lipschitz. The problem class also includes nonlinear objectives and infinite-moment tastes. Linear objectives suffice to witness worst-case difficulty. [Gaussian estimates](../Proofs/SphericalPreferenceGaussian.lean) · [Hypercube construction](../Proofs/SphericalPreferenceHypercube.lean)

For distinct queried points, let $u$ be their normalized difference. The chance of a positive answer depends on $a\langle v/\sqrt d,u\rangle$. The wider Gaussian component keeps both answers at probability at least $1/16$, and the mixture response probability is $4\sqrt d/B$-Lipschitz in this scalar. Flipping coordinate $i$ changes the scalar by $2a|u_i|/\sqrt d$. Thus a query can allocate information among coordinates, with total squared directional weight exactly one. Identical-point queries return the fixed tie response and contribute zero information. The square-root distance between transcript probabilities accumulates along the actual adaptive decision tree. Summing over adjacent sign vectors bounds the Hamming accuracy of every decoder. Each incorrectly decoded coordinate at the output costs at least $4\varepsilon/d$ in the objective-gap lower bound. [Adaptive transcript estimate](../Proofs/SphericalPreferenceTranscript.lean) · [Objective risk](../Proofs/SphericalPreferenceRisk.lean)

Conditioning on a fixed algorithm seed reduces execution to a finite binary tree. The argument first averages over all sign vectors for each seed, then integrates over the algorithm seed. For any algorithm meeting the setup's integrability condition on all these instances, at least one vertex has expected loss greater than $\varepsilon$. Failure of that integrability condition already violates the formal success criterion. ## Relation to earlier work

Kovalev, Stonyakin and Gasnikov's preprint v1, Theorem 2(b) and Appendix B.2, already yields the lower-bound order from a Gaussian random-coefficient probit subclass on a ball. In their notation take objective Lipschitz bound $M$ proportional to $B$ and coordinate standard deviation $\sigma$ proportional to $B/\sqrt d$, with sufficiently small constants for the median envelope. Their convex risk lower bound scales as $\min\{MR,d\sigma R/\sqrt n\}$. The two-component construction here gives a direct formal proof for the repository's unrestricted comparison algorithm and every covered budget. The substantive advance of the pair is the upper bound for unknown spherical tastes under the median envelope. Comparing the lower bound to the upper theorem requires neither a known Gaussian link nor a further output-selection cost.
