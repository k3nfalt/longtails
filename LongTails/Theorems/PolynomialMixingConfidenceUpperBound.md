# High-confidence convex optimization from one polynomially mixing stream

A method observing one subgradient per time step can attain objective accuracy $ε$ with confidence $1-δ$ despite heavy tails and polynomial temporal dependence. For fixed moment order $p$ and mixing exponent $β$, the paired lower bound shows that both the accuracy-dependent time scale and the confidence-dependent time scale are necessary. [Upper theorem](PolynomialMixingConfidenceUpperBound.lean) · [Lower theorem and hard instances](PolynomialMixingConfidenceLowerBound.md)

## Oracle and scope

The objective is convex on a known Euclidean ball of radius $R$, in arbitrary finite dimension $d≥1$. Each call chooses a point and returns one vector $g(x,Z_t)$ from the next state of a single exogenous stationary stream. The joint law of any finite past and the entire future at lag $t$ differs in total variation from their product by at most $(1+t/τ)^{-β}$. The latent samples in Lean are real Borel variables. Standard Borel observations such as feature–response pairs can be represented by a measurable encoding. At every fixed point, the oracle is integrable, its stationary mean is a supporting subgradient, and $\mathbb E\|g(x,Z_0)\|^p≤G^p$. The query and sample enter jointly measurably. These marginal conditions permit bias conditional on previous queries. The setup directly records attainment of the minimum, which also follows from compactness and the implied $G$-Lipschitz property. [Setup](../Setups/PolynomialMixingSubgradient.lean) · [Objective regularity](../Proofs/PolynomialMixingValidation.lean)

For example, consider absolute-deviation regression with nonorthogonal covariates, $f(x)=\mathbb E|a^\top x-b|$. Choose a feature distribution with mass on several nonparallel, nonorthogonal directions and responses with finite first moment. The reply $\operatorname{sign}(a^\top x-b)a$, with sign zero at ties, has raw moment at most $\mathbb E\|a\|^p$. This gives interacting, nonlinear, nonsmooth objectives; the assumptions also permit unbounded features. A stationary observation can, for example, persist at each step with probability $\rho=\exp(-β/τ)$ and otherwise refresh independently. Its absolute-regularity coefficient is at most $\rho^t≤(1+t/τ)^{-β}$. The upper theorem also covers non-Markov streams satisfying the displayed envelope. Its dimension dependence enters through the actual norm-moment bound $G$.

## Algorithm and call count

Let $a=p/(p-1)$, $S=(GR/ε)^a$, and $L=\log(8/δ)$. The algorithm knows the setup parameters. It runs $K=\lceil 8L\rceil$ candidate trials. Each training block starts at zero, uses projected scalar AdaGrad, and returns the average of its $M=\lceil(32768GR/ε)^a\rceil$ pre-update iterates. For each candidate $x$, another $K$ blocks estimate $f(x)-f(0)$. In a scoring block, the $i$th query is $(i/M)x$, for $0≤i<M$, and its scalar score is the average of the inner products of the returned vectors with $x$. A majority-based center combines the block scores. The returned point is the candidate with smallest combined score. All blocks use disjoint portions of the same stream. The method only requests subgradients. [Algorithm and output](../Algorithms/PolynomialMixingSelection.lean)

The method makes $u=\lceil τ(256M)^{1/β}\rceil$ padding calls after each useful call and $v=\lceil τ(4K(K+1)/δ)^{1/β}\rceil$ after each block. The plan settles on its output after $K(K+1)[M(1+u)+v]$ calls. If the public budget is larger, the template continues querying the origin and retains that same output through the final call. The public theorem proves the following single upper budget suffices for the actual execution, including padding. It holds for $1<p≤2$, $β>0$, $τ≥1$, $0<ε≤GR/8$, and $0<δ≤1/32$. [Execution identity](../Proofs/PolynomialMixingExecution.lean) · [Budget proof](../Proofs/PolynomialMixingBudget.lean)

$$
\left\lceil τ(32L)^2
\left[\left(2^{24}GR/ε\right)^{a(1+1/β)}
+\left((32L)^2/δ\right)^{1/β}\right]\right\rceil .
$$

## What the proof adds

A direct simulation of an independent-sample procedure with $N$ observations spaces *all* of them far enough apart for total coupling error at most $δ$. Even with $N$ of order $S\log(1/δ)$, that construction costs order $τ N^{1+1/β}δ^{-1/β}$. Here two spacings suffice. The inner spacing only has to give a constant-success training or validation block. The longer outer spacing has to separate only $K(K+1)$ blocks at high confidence. Inside the independent-sample comparison, self-normalized projected regret controls training under a raw $p$-moment, and the two endpoint supporting inequalities on each subinterval make the chord quadrature bias telescope to at most $2GR/M$. This gives a gradient-only score valid for a nonsmooth objective. The block law is compared in total variation before evaluating the adaptive procedure, avoiding a conditional-unbiasedness assumption along the real run. [Independent-block error](../Proofs/PolynomialMixingBlocks.lean) · [Training and score guarantees](../Proofs/PolynomialMixingAccuracy.lean) · [Confidence amplification](../Proofs/PolynomialMixingAmplification.lean)

For fixed $p,β$, the upper order is $τ[S^{1+1/β}L^2+δ^{-1/β}L^{2+2/β}]$. The lower order is $τ[S^{1+1/β}\log(1/δ)+δ^{-1/β}]$ under exactly the same setup and success criterion, for arbitrary randomized adaptive algorithms. At fixed confidence the dependence on accuracy and on $τ$ matches. At fixed accuracy the exponent of $1/δ$ matches. Across the full parameter range the remaining ratio is at most a constant depending on $p,β$ times $L^{2+2/β}$. Exact logarithmic optimality and uniform near-equality as $p$ approaches one or $β$ approaches zero remain unresolved.

## Closest prior results

- Duchi, Agarwal, Johansson and Jordan study convex optimization from ergodic observations. Their Theorem 3 uses Assumption D on the tails of conditional mixing times and the sample Lipschitz assumption of Theorem 2. The stationary-average polynomial envelope and fixed-point raw moments here permit rare, persistent stretches with large replies. [Ergodic Mirror Descent, v3, Theorems 2–3 and §6.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.4681)
- Lerasle and Oliveira already obtain an additive block-coupling penalty for robust scalar estimation and study estimator selection. Proposition 16 uses stronger moment and mixing summability conditions in the absolutely regular case. Lovas and Tukora give recent median-of-means deviation bounds for fixed observables under polynomial strong mixing. These precedents explain why blocking and robust validation alone would be insufficient as a novelty claim. The characterization here also supplies the adaptive optimization procedure using one gradient per step and adversarial instances forcing both time scales. [Lerasle and Oliveira, v1, Proposition 16 and Remark 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.3914) · [Lovas and Tukora, v1, Theorem 2.4 and Corollary 2.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15974)
- Sarkar, Chakrabartty and Aggarwal obtain a heavy-tailed high-probability bound for smooth PL objectives driven by a uniformly geometrically mixing Markov chain. Their matching heavy-tail test controls constant-probability error. Its exponents apply to that different objective and mixing class. We found no previous upper and lower characterization of the joint accuracy–confidence dependence for the subgradient-only model specified above. This gap was inferred from the theorem scopes in the literature search. [High-Probability PL-SGD with Markovian Noise, v1, Corollary 3 and Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.26316)
