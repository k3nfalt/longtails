# Exact accuracy dependence for gradient-only optimization under initial-sublevel gradient dominance

Let $f$ have a globally $L$-Lipschitz gradient on a finite-dimensional real Euclidean space. Write $h(x)=f(x)-\inf f$. The local assumption is
$h(x)\leq\tau\|\nabla f(x)\|^\alpha$ whenever $f(x)\leq f(x_0)$, for $1\leq\alpha<2$. A call returns one unbiased stochastic gradient at the requested point, with variance at most $\sigma^2$. Samples are IID and the gradient oracle is jointly measurable. The infimum is finite and need not be attained. The public setup includes $\Delta\geq h(x_0)$, although the algorithm and its budget do not use it. This is the existing [sublevel problem specification](../Setups/SublevelPLBoundedVariance.lean).

Put $A=(\tau/\varepsilon)^{2/\alpha}$ and $T=L\varepsilon A$. The [upper theorem](SublevelPLWeightedGradient.lean) guarantees an integrable last-iterate gap with expectation at most $\varepsilon$ under the execution law. Its budget counts individual gradients:

$$
\left\lceil 110000\,\frac{T}{2-\alpha}
\left(1+\frac{\sigma^2 A}{2-\alpha}\right)\right\rceil .
$$

For each fixed $\alpha<2$ and sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, this matches the known worst-case lower bound up to a constant. The exact dependence is $\Theta_\alpha(T+T\sigma^2 A)$, including the transition from little noise to noise-dominated optimization. The lower bound requires $0<\varepsilon\leq c_\alpha\min\{\Delta,L^{\alpha/(2-\alpha)}\tau^{2/(2-\alpha)}\}$ and allows dimension to grow with the budget. Its construction uses a fixed objective and a fresh Bernoulli sample at each call, hence fits the IID gradient model. The upper bound is dimension-free and includes $\sigma=0$. We use the published lower bound for comparison. The new Lean declaration is the upper bound. [Masiha, Kiyavash and Thiran, arXiv v3, Theorem 4.1 and Appendix D.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28278v3)

## The algorithm and the boundary of the sublevel set

If $2T<1$, the smoothness inequality $\|\nabla f(x_0)\|^2\leq2Lh(x_0)$ and gradient dominance already give $h(x_0)\leq\varepsilon$. Otherwise the [algorithm](../Algorithms/SublevelWeightedGradient.lean) takes $K=1+\lceil16T/(2-\alpha)\rceil$ steps of length $1/L$ using ordinary batch means. At step $j=1,\ldots,K$, it uses $1+\lceil v\sqrt{K/j}\rceil$ new samples at its current iterate, where $v=1+128\sigma^2(K/(L\varepsilon)+A)$. It returns the point after the last update. The sum of all batch sizes is at most $2K(1+v)$. Once the computation finishes, the fixed-budget execution pads any remaining calls at $x_0$ and retains the output. The displayed budget includes this padding.

The initial point lies on the boundary of the set where gradient dominance holds. A sample mean can send the first update outside, even under bounded variance. To analyze this, write $e_j$ for the squared gradient estimation error at update $j$. Smoothness gives
$2L(h_j-h_{j-1})\leq e_j-\|\nabla f(x_{j-1})\|^2$ everywhere. If the first exit occurs at update $t$ before the gap ever falls below $h_0/2$, summation gives
$\sum_{j\leq t}e_j>t(h_0/(2\tau))^{2/\alpha}$. Thus the single quantity $\sum_{j\leq K}e_j/j$ pays for an early exit, whatever its time. An exit after halving costs at least $h_0/2$ in the unweighted accumulated error $W=\sum_j e_j/(2L)$. After either exit the final gap is at most $h_0+W$. For a trajectory that stays inside, subtract the accumulated error from the running gap. While this corrected gap is positive it satisfies the noiseless power-descent inequality, which bounds the final gap by the deterministic target plus $W$. These alternatives give a pathwise bound linear in the two error sums. [Weighted descent proof](../Proofs/SublevelWeightedDescent.lean)

The conditional mean-square error of the $j$th batch is at most $\sigma^2/b_j$. Uniform batches in the early-exit estimate incur a harmonic sum. The decreasing batch schedule gives both $\sum 1/(jb_j)\leq2/v$ and $\sum b_j\leq2K(1+v)$ by summing $1/\sqrt j$. Integrating the pathwise bound now controls escaped trajectories directly under the original finite-variance hypothesis. The original sample sequence is abstract IID and the argument applies at the adaptive points reached by the actual queries. ## Comparison and scope

The closest earlier repository theorem uses the same oracle, objective class, and expected last-iterate criterion. It established a self-contained upper bound with a logarithmic overhead on the deterministic term and two logarithms on the stochastic term, including dependence on $K\Delta/\varepsilon$ in the tuning. It robustifies every batch and bounds the probability of any bad update. For fixed $\alpha$ and $\Delta>0$, both the overheads and the dependence on $\Delta$ disappear here. Changing its batch count to a constant in its existing analysis leaves a term proportional to $\Delta K$ times a constant failure probability. In the opposite direction, the earlier concentration argument also gives a tunable-confidence statement. The present theorem establishes expected accuracy. [Earlier upper bound and proof](SublevelPLRobustBatchedGd.md)

The polynomial dependence in the rate was known under gradient dominance along the stochastic trajectory. Applying that result directly would require the very containment property that noisy updates can violate. For $\alpha<2$, imposing gradient dominance everywhere together with global smoothness forces a lower-bounded objective on the whole space to be constant. The initial-sublevel version is important even for smooth convex functions whose initial sublevel set has bounded diameter. Masiha et al. distinguish the gradient-only question from a monotonicity check with access to function values. [Fatkhullin et al., NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01748) · [Masiha et al., Theorem 2.1, Definition 2.3 and Remark 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28278v3)

A nonconvex family in arbitrary dimension is $f(x)=\phi(q(x))$, where $q(x)=x^\top Hx/2$, $H$ is positive definite with nonzero off-diagonal entries, and $\phi(t)=t+4(1-e^{-t})$. The coordinates interact through $q$. Since $1\leq\phi'\leq5$ and $\|Hx\|^2\leq2\|H\|q(x)$, the Hessian norm is at most $(5+8/e)\|H\|$. Along any ray the second derivative has the sign of $\phi'(q)+2q\phi''(q)$, which at $q=3/2$ equals $1-8e^{-3/2}<0$. Yet $h=f\leq5q\leq5\|\nabla f\|^2/(2\lambda_{\min}(H))$. On the initial sublevel set this gives $h\leq h_0^{1-\alpha/2}(5/(2\lambda_{\min}(H)))^{\alpha/2}\|\nabla f\|^\alpha$. The algorithm uses $L,\tau,\alpha,\sigma,\varepsilon$ and $x_0$. Uniform optimality as $\alpha\uparrow2$, the endpoint $\alpha=2$, and fixed-dimensional lower bounds remain outside this comparison.
