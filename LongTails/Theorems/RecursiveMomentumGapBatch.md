# Recursive momentum with a gap-dependent initial batch

For a smooth nonconvex objective under affine variance and mean-squared smoothness, the initial objective gap bounds the variance of the first stochastic gradient. Using this bound to size the initial batch removes the additive powers of the relative-noise parameter from the pair-call budget.

## Guarantee and comparison

Write `S=L+L̄`, `D=SΔ/ε²`, `z=Λ/ε`, and `r=1+3Γ+6z`. One call returns two unbiased gradient estimates at the queried points using the same fresh IID sample. The squared noise at each point has expectation at most `Γ²‖∇f(x)‖²+Λ²`, and the expected squared difference of the two estimates is at most `L̄²‖x-y‖²`. The gradient of the objective is `L`-Lipschitz, and `f(x₀)-f(x)≤Δ` for every point. The space can be a separable real Hilbert space. [Formal setup](../Setups/AffineVarianceMeanSquaredSmooth.lean)

Use the existing recursive momentum update with `a=1/(1+8Γ²+32z²)` and `γ=1/(4Sr)`. Take `b₀=⌈8z²+64ΓD⌉+1` initial samples and set `T=⌈20rD⌉`. The budget is

```math
N=b_0+T=\lceil 8z^2+64\Gamma D\rceil+1+\lceil20rD\rceil.
```

For an independent index uniform on `0,…,T`, the output `X` is measurable and `E‖∇f(X)‖²≤(57/60)ε²`. Its gradient norm is integrable and has expectation at most `ε`. The algorithm uses `b₀` diagonal calls at `x₀`. At each subsequent update it uses one fresh pair at the new and previous points. On seed `j`, the state freezes after `b₀+j` calls. Execution still consumes the remaining `T-j` calls, for a total of `N` on every seed, including `j=0`. The output requires no independent gradient test. [Algorithm](../Algorithms/RecursiveMomentumGapBatch.lean) · [Theorem](RecursiveMomentumGapBatch.lean)

The only previous theorem for this setup gives `N_old=⌈16+131Γ²+5Γ⁴+516z²+64D+192ΓD+384zD⌉+2`. Each ceiling in the new budget adds less than one. The resulting upper bound on `N` and lower bound on `N_old` have the following coefficients. [Previous theorem](RecursiveMomentumAffineVariance.lean)

| Calls | Constant | Γ² | Γ⁴ | z² | D | ΓD | zD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| New, upper bound | 3 | 0 | 0 | 8 | 20 | 124 | 120 |
| Previous, lower bound | 18 | 131 | 5 | 516 | 64 | 192 | 384 |

Thus the **exported integer budget is strictly smaller at every valid parameter tuple**. The set of ties and the set of increases are both empty. [Lean comparison, `budget_lt_existing`](../Proofs/RecursiveMomentumGapBatchTuning.lean)

The previous proof uses an initial batch of `⌈Γ²r+z²⌉+1` and a horizon of `⌈64rD+16/a⌉`. Its execution identity only requires the allowance to cover their sum, so equality suffices. At `Λ=0` and large `Γ`, the batch has order `Γ³` and the horizon has order `(1+Γ)D+Γ²`. Their sum has order `Γ³+(1+Γ)D`. The quartic term comes from simplifying the exported budget. [Previous proof, batch and horizon](../Proofs/RecursiveMomentumConvergence.lean#L18-L21) · [Execution identity](../Proofs/RecursiveMomentumConvergence.lean#L364-L373)

At `Λ=0` the new count has order `1+(1+Γ)D`. As `Γ` grows, this gives an order gain over the previous exported budget when `D=o(Γ³)`. An order gain over the old unpadded count requires `D=o(Γ²)`. For example, at `L=L̄>0` and `D=Γ^(3/2)`, the new count has order `Γ^(5/2)` and the old unpadded count has order `Γ³`. The usual single-gradient strong-growth SGD guarantee takes order `(1+Γ²)LΔ/ε²` iterations. Since `LΔ/ε²=D/2`, this gives order `Γ^(7/2)` pair calls, charging one diagonal pair per SGD step. At `D=Γ^(5/2)`, the new and old unpadded counts both have order `Γ^(7/2)`. [Vaswani et al., Theorem 3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/vaswani19a/vaswani19a.pdf)

At `D=z=0` the new budget is one call for every `Γ`. For fixed `Λ>0` and `Δ>0`, the leading accuracy dependence is cubic in `1/ε`. With pure relative noise (`Γ>0`, `Λ=0`) and fixed positive gap, it is quadratic in `1/ε`.

## Proof

1. Let `δ=f(x)-f_low`. Until the gradient norm reaches a value at most `H`, with `H>0`, each deterministic gradient step of size `1/L` decreases the objective by at least `‖∇f‖²/(2L)`. The decrease pays for `H/2` times the step length. Induction with a step allowance greater than `2Lδ/H²` yields a point `y` with `‖∇f(y)‖≤H` and `‖x-y‖H≤2δ`. These points occur only in the proof.
2. Split the noise at `x` into the noise at `y`, the stochastic gradient difference, and the true gradient difference. Twice applying the squared triangle inequality bounds its second moment by `2(Γ²H²+Λ²)+4(L̄²+L²)‖x-y‖²`. At `H²=4Sδ/Γ` this is at most `2Λ²+12ΓSδ`. For `Γ=0` use the given variance bound directly. For `δ=0`, smooth descent forces the gradient to vanish. This global inequality fits the repository's objective-gap variance lemma for batch means. Dividing by `b₀` at the initial point bounds the mean squared estimator error `U₀` by `ε²/4`. [Variance proof](../Proofs/RecursiveMomentumGapBatchVariance.lean)
3. Put `Q_j=E‖∇f(x_j)‖²`, `P_j=E‖d_j‖²`, and `U_j=E‖d_j-∇f(x_j)‖²`. With the chosen parameters the existing one-step recursion gives `U_{j+1}≤(1-a)²U_j+a C_j`, where `C_j=Q_{j+1}/4+ε²/16+P_j/4`. Sum the excess `(U_j-U₀)_+` by telescoping. Over `n=T+1` points the initial error contributes at most `nU₀`. Each forcing term `a C_j` has accumulated weight at most `1/a`, so each `C_j` has accumulated weight at most one. The gradient indices in the forcing range from 1 through `T`, inclusive. [Finite-horizon sum](../Proofs/RecursiveMomentumGapBatchSum.lean)
4. Smooth descent, summed through the last output index, gives `ΣQ≤2Δ/γ-ΣP/2+ΣU`. Use the error sum, `2Δ/γ≤(2/5)nε²`, and nonnegativity of `P_j`. The result is `(3/4)ΣQ≤(2/5+1/4+1/16)nε²`. Integrating over the independent uniform index divides the sum by `n`. Nonnegative variance bounds the square of the expected norm by its second moment. [Output and execution proof](../Proofs/RecursiveMomentumGapBatch.lean)

The previous note describes removal of the accuracy-independent `Γ⁴` term as open and suggests a time-varying momentum warm-up. The initial batch remains here. The gap bound changes its size, and the finite-horizon sum removes the earlier requirement `T≥16/a`. [Previous contribution note, Limitations](RecursiveMomentumAffineVariance.md#limitations)

## Prior work and limitations

The comparisons below use gradient evaluations when a paper charges them individually. A same-sample difference then costs two evaluations, or one pair call in this setup. Squared-norm accuracy `ε²` implies norm accuracy `ε` in expectation.

- **STORM.** Cutkosky and Orabona use a recursive same-sample difference and a single initial sample. Equations (2)–(3) give the update. Section 3 assumes uniformly bounded variance and samplewise smoothness. The adaptive version also uses a uniform gradient bound. Algorithms 1–2 and Theorems 1–2 give a stochastic expected-norm rate of order `T^(−1/3)` up to logarithms. The variance parameter bounds the initial error directly. The theorems have no relative-noise parameter. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10018)
- **SPIDER.** Fang et al., Assumption 1 and Theorem 1, equations (3.4)–(3.6), use mean-squared smoothness and a uniform variance bound `σ²` in the online case. The refresh batch, including the first, has size `2σ²/ε²`. The leading cost is `16LΔσ/ε³` for expected norm `5ε`, so their tolerance is one fifth of the target norm. Here their smoothness constant controls stochastic differences, and `σ` corresponds to `Λ` at `Γ=0`. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.01695)
- **SPIDER with affine variance.** Chen, Zhou, Liang and Lu assume exactly the affine moment inequality in (17) and allow expected generalized smoothness. Theorem 4, equation (22), gives expected-norm accuracy in `O(ε⁻³)` samples for fixed parameters. For epoch length `q`, the theorem gives a sufficient tuning condition of `Ω(max{Λ²/ε², Γ²q²})` samples per refresh. This tuning includes the first refresh, whose prescribed size grows at least quadratically in `Γ` for fixed `q` even at small additive noise. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.02854)
- **Single-gradient SGD.** Bottou, Curtis and Nocedal, Assumption 4.3 and Theorem 4.8, (4.8)–(4.9) and (4.28b), give an average squared-gradient bound. Specializing to unbiased gradients gives the two terms `2Δ/(ηT)+ηLΛ²` with `η≤1/[L(1+Γ²)]`. Their update needs one sample from the outset. At `Λ=0`, Vaswani, Bach and Schmidt's Theorem 3 gives `2ρLΔ/T` for the best expected squared norm, where their uncentred growth factor is `ρ=1+Γ²`. Multiplicative noise changes the step size and thus the iteration count. [Bottou et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.04838) · [Vaswani et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/vaswani19a/vaswani19a.pdf)
- **Adaptive SGD.** Faw et al. use conditional affine variance with `σ₀=Λ, σ₁=Γ`. Theorem 4, equation (11), gives the general high-probability bound on the smallest squared gradient, of order `1/√T` up to logarithms for fixed confidence. Equation (12) additionally assumes `σ₁≤1/8`. Theorem 35 gives the detailed bounds. Their `b₀` initializes a scalar accumulator. Relative noise enters the rate constants, including a coefficient of the initial gradient norm. Attia and Koren, Theorem 1(i), use almost-sure affine noise bounds and discuss sub-Gaussian extensions in Appendix C. Their single-gradient update (1) yields high-probability average squared norm of order `Λ/√T+1/T` up to logs and problem-dependent coefficients involving `Γ`. Neither method needs a relative-noise-dependent initial sample batch. [Faw et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v178/faw22a/faw22a.pdf) · [Attia and Koren](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/attia23a/attia23a.pdf)
- **Normalized recursive momentum.** Upadhyay et al., Theorem 2(i), analyze variance growing as `G²+B²‖x-x₀‖²` under mean-squared smoothness. Their `O(ε⁻⁴)` result uses a one-time batch of order `√T` to control the initial error. At the starting point only `G²` contributes to that variance bound. Their distance coefficient `B` has a different meaning from `Γ`. Appendix C.7 gives the bounded-variance `O(ε⁻³)` specialization. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15314)
- **Other recursive estimators.** In the online bounded-variance case, PAGE uses a first batch of size `b=min{⌈2σ²/ε²⌉,n}` and probabilistic refreshes. For the infinite stream relevant here, `b=⌈2σ²/ε²⌉`. Theorem 3 and Corollary 4 give order `b+√b LΔ/ε²` gradient evaluations for that batch size `b`. Hybrid SARAH–SGD uses a horizon-dependent initial batch of order `σ^(8/3)(m+1)^(1/3)` under average smoothness and uniform variance. Theorem 3.1 and (11) give order `ε⁻³` total gradients at fixed positive variance. Both analyses control the batch using absolute noise. [Li et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/li21a/li21a.pdf) · [Tran-Dinh et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.05920)
- **Adaptive recursive momentum.** META-STORM, Algorithm 2, begins with a single sample. Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.4 assume mean-squared smoothness and a uniform almost-sure bound on differences between gradients from two samples at one point. At fixed confidence the gradient norm decays as `T^(−1/3)` up to logarithms for fixed positive noise. Jiang et al., Algorithm 1 and Theorem 1 with (3), obtain expected norm `O(T^(−1/3))` under average smoothness and uniform variance, starting with `T^(1/3)` samples. Neither initialization uses a relative-noise coefficient. [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.14853) · [Jiang et al.](https://jiangw1998.github.io/pdf/NeurIPS-2024-Jiang-A.pdf)
- **Second-moment formulations of SGD.** Unbiasedness gives the uncentred bound `E‖g‖²≤(1+Γ²)‖∇f‖²+Λ²`. In the ABC model this sets `A=0, B=1+Γ², C=Λ²`. Khaled and Richtárik, Corollary 1, (19), and Li and Richtárik, Lemma 2 and Corollary 2, (B.1), then give order `(LΔ/ε²) max{1+Γ²,Λ²/ε²}` iterations. Relative noise enters the single-sample step-size limit. [Khaled and Richtárik](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.03329) · [Li and Richtárik](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.07013)
- **Repository expected-smoothness theorem.** The normalized STORM result assumes a variance bound `2A(f−f_low)+C`. Smooth descent gives this with `A=LΓ², C=Λ²`, taking a common smoothness bound `M=max{L,L̄}` for gradient and stochastic differences. Its initial batch is `⌈192(4AΔ+2Aε²/M+C)/ε²⌉+1`. For fixed positive gap and relative noise its leading term is proportional to `MΔ√(4AΔ+C)/ε³`, including at zero additive noise. [Normalized STORM theorem and tuning](NormalizedStormExpectedSmoothness.md)
- **Convex strong-growth results** address a different output criterion. Schmidt and Le Roux, §§2–4, assume a pointwise component-gradient bound relative to the full gradient and give inverse-time convex objective error and geometric strongly convex error with constant-step SGD. Valls et al., Theorem 1 and Corollary 1, relate accelerated convex objective rates to a noise condition on gradient estimates, with strong growth as an unconstrained special case. Mishkin, Pilanci and Schmidt, Corollary 8, (15)–(16), obtain a geometric strongly convex rate and `O(ρL/k²)` convex objective error up to initialization factors under their scheme (5). In these strong-growth specializations the noise affects tuning and rate constants. They do not prescribe an affine-variance starting batch for the present nonconvex criterion. [Schmidt and Le Roux](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.6370) · [Valls et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.11833) · [Mishkin et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.02378)

**Lower bounds.** The repository has no formal lower bound for this setup. Under bounded variance and mean-squared smoothness, Arjevani et al. prove a lower bound of `Ω(L̄σΔ/ε³+σ²/ε²)` for zero-respecting methods in Theorem 2 and for unrestricted randomized algorithms in Theorem 3, equation (24). Both results assume positive `Δ` and `σ²`, sufficient dimension, and `ε≤c√(L̄Δ)` for a universal constant `c>0`. Their model (8) uses one smoothness allowance `L̄` for the objective and stochastic differences. A direct common specialization is `L=L̄>0`, `Γ=0`, and `σ=Λ>0`, with two points per query. The bound concerns total calls and assigns no cost specifically to the initial batch. An independently imposed, arbitrarily small objective-smoothness cap `L` is outside their model. Even in their accuracy regime, if `2LΔ≤ε²` then smooth descent gives `‖∇f(x₀)‖²≤2LΔ≤ε²`, so returning `x₀` solves this setup without a query. [Arjevani et al., §§2–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.02365)

The previous contribution note gives a hand sketch for zero-respecting algorithms. In equations (17) and (20) of the paper, the gradient error in each coordinate is the gradient component times a mask in `[0,1]` times `Z/p-1`, with `Z` Bernoulli of mean `p`. Squaring and averaging bounds relative variance by `(1-p)/p`. Lemma 4, equation (22), gives an MSS constant scaling as `1/√p`. The note's scaling yields `Ω(min{(1+Γ²)L, (1+Γ)L̄}Δ/ε²)` at `Λ=0`, subject to the construction's chain-length and accuracy conditions. In the MSS branch this requires `ε² ≲ L̄Δ/Γ` for large `Γ`. Without MSS the corresponding sketch gives `Ω((1+Γ²)LΔ/ε²)`. Extending this repository sketch to unrestricted randomized algorithms requires a separate proof that the lifted hard instance satisfies the relative-noise bound. [Arjevani et al., §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.02365) · [Repository sketch](RecursiveMomentumAffineVariance.md#lower-bounds-hand-checked-sketches-not-formalized)

The new dependence has the shape of the MSS branch when `S` is comparable to `L̄` and that branch attains the minimum. The mixed additive-noise term likewise has the sketched scale in that regime. A uniformly matching lower bound for these public `L,L̄`, every accuracy, and the full algorithm class remains unproved. For instance, at `Δ=0` the initial point is already stationary. All parameters are public, the constants are not sharp, and the guarantee concerns a random iterate in expectation. The literature check covered the cited primary results, the previous note and searches for nonconvex recursive momentum, variance reduction and strong growth. It establishes the comparison with the earlier repository theorem. Priority and optimality remain unresolved.
