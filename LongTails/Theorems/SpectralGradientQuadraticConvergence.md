# Spectral feedback for nonlinear scalar gradient descent

The theorem concerns deterministic gradient descent whose update direction is always the current negative gradient. It obtains quadratic contraction across windows of 2d current-gradient calls, under standard strong convexity, gradient smoothness, and Hessian regularity at the minimizer. The contribution is a nonlinear rate uniform over singular gradient histories and all feasible fitted models. Quadratic spectral finite termination, spectral step selection, and symmetric fitting are already known.

The Lean setup is [MinimizerRegularHessian](../Setups/MinimizerRegularHessian.lean), the controller is [SpectralGradient](../Algorithms/SpectralGradient.lean), and the public result is [SpectralGradientQuadraticConvergence](SpectralGradientQuadraticConvergence.lean).

Let d≥1 and let f on a d-dimensional real Hilbert space be twice Frechet differentiable, µ-strongly convex, and have L-Lipschitz gradient, with 0<µ≤L. Write g=∇f and H=Dg. Supply x0 and R>0 with ||x0−xstar||≤R, and M≥0 satisfying ||H(x)−H(xstar)||≤M||x−xstar|| for every x. This is the anchored Hessian condition of Jin–Mokhtari, Assumption 3.3, equation (11), https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-022-01887-4 . The specification uses the standard strong-convex supporting inequality, not merely a lower bound on gradient differences. It does not assume Hessian continuity away from the minimizer, a spectral gap, gradient-history conditioning, or access to xstar or H. Global Hessian Lipschitzness suffices. A concrete nonquadratic family is ridge logistic regression f(x)=µ||x||²/2+(1/m)Σ log(1+exp(−bᵢ⟨aᵢ,x⟩)), with bᵢ∈{−1,1} and dense, nonorthogonal feature vectors. Elementary derivative bounds give valid constants L=µ+Σ||aᵢ||²/(4m) and M=Σ||aᵢ||³/(4m). Taking x0=0 permits R=√(2log2/µ), since f(0)=log2 and f(x)≥µ||x||²/2. The supplied radius and regularity parameters are known to the controller.

Write κ=L/µ, C=4(d+1)²(4κ²)^d, B=LR, Hbar=1+CMLR/µ². The controller takes a fixed warmup of ceil((2L/µ)log(2Hbar)) short steps, then alternates d short steps of length a=1/(2L) with d spectral steps. If q0,...,qd are the gradients observed during probing, it finds any symmetric matrix Bfit in [µI,LI] satisfying ||q(j+1)−(I−a Bfit)qj||≤M||q0||²/(2µL). The true Hessian at xstar proves feasibility but is not supplied to the algorithm. The next d steps have lengths equal to the reciprocal eigenvalues of Bfit. Each uses a freshly queried current gradient. The query qd is reused for the first spectral step, and the final endpoint is not queried solely for certification. The actual endpoint is retained for the next probing phase; there is no momentum update, rollback, auxiliary query point, or independent solver restart. The controller recomputes a fit from each new d-transition history.

The critical bound is $\|g(\operatorname{window}(x))\|\le (CM/\mu^2)\|g(x)\|^2$. After warmup, successive window ends obey $\|g(x_j)\|\le (LR/\bar H)2^{-2^j}$. Strong convexity then bounds objective error by ||g||²/(2µ). The explicit public call formula is

$$
\left\lceil\frac{2L}{\mu}\log\!\left(2+8(d+1)^2
\left(\frac{4L^2}{\mu^2}\right)^d\frac{MLR}{\mu^2}\right)\right\rceil
+2d\left\lceil\frac{\log(1+\log(1+L^2R^2/(\mu\varepsilon)))}{\log 2}\right\rceil.
$$

All calls are current-gradient calls. Its order is

$$
O\!\left(\kappa\left[d\log(4\kappa)+\log(d+1)+\log\!\left(1+\frac{MLR}{\mu^2}\right)\right]
+d\log\!\left(1+\log\!\left(1+\frac{L^2R^2}{\mu\varepsilon}\right)\right)\right).
$$

For every ε>0, the returned output is exactly the last iterate x_out after this budget and satisfies f(x_out)−f(xstar)≤ε. Only window endpoints have the quadratic bound; individual spectral steps need not decrease the objective.

The mechanism transfers polynomial action to an actual nonlinear scalar-step execution, without estimating the Hessian in operator norm. Bounded annihilating-polynomial coefficients and Taylor perturbation are established tools; the new claim is the uniform endpoint guarantee obtained from a data-consistent symmetric fit and its positive reciprocal-eigenvalue steps. Both the true and fitted short-step operators are contractions consistent with the same d gradient transitions up to δ. Their j-th powers acting on q0 differ by at most 2jδ. A degree-d polynomial annihilating the fitted operator therefore has small action on the true trajectory, without inverting the history matrix. Commutation transfers this estimate to the probe endpoint. Anchored Hessian regularity controls nonlinear spectral-step errors, with strong convexity bounding each actual point's distance by its current gradient norm. The proof is uniform over the choice of feasible fit, repeated eigenvalues, and rank loss. For example, take the quadratic f(x)=(x₁²+4x₂²)/2, x0=(t,0), µ=1, L=4, and M=0, so H(xstar)=diag(1,4) and q0=t e₁. The probe gradients are qⱼ=(7/8)ʲt e₁. The fit Bfit=I has zero residual for every t, although ||H(xstar)−Bfit||=3 even as t→0. Its spectral sweep nevertheless annihilates the observed orbit. Thus operator-norm consistency cannot be inferred from feasibility, even in the zero-remainder limit; the polynomial-action argument avoids precisely that obstruction. M=0 recovers known quadratic finite termination; that case is not a novelty claim.

The closest primary results differ as follows:

- Fletcher2009/2012, §§2,4: full-memory quadratic finite termination and nonlinear spectral sweeps. https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2009/12/2487.pdf
- Yang–Yuan2026, Proposition 5, Theorems 19 and 21: rank-compressed quadratic finite termination and uniform R-linear convergence. https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12114 . Their compatible-state continuity concerns one fixed quadratic; it does not quantify perturbations of nonlinear histories. No claim of first rank-free spectral method is appropriate.
- Ferrandi–Hochstenbach2024, Propositions 5–6 and §§3.3–3.4: symmetrization, symmetric secant fitting, rank handling, and nonlinear line-search globalization. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11075-024-01895-9 . Fitting and rank handling themselves are therefore not new; the contribution is a uniform nonlinear action bound for every feasible fit.
- Burdakov–Dai–Huang2019, Theorems 3.2–3.3: nonlinear stabilized BB, gradient-only and no line search; Theorem 3.3 gives global R-linear convergence with a sufficiently small stabilization cap. https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/7298.pdf . Their anchored regularity is local. Our known global M provides explicit uniform initialization; a local constant and radiusρ together with global Hessian bounds imply a global anchored bound max(Mlocal,2L/ρ), retaining that quantitative cost.
- Gu–Du2020/2021's inexact superlinear discussion is quadratic; nonlinear extension is future work. https://academic.oup.com/imajna/article/41/1/247/5819219
- Narushima–Wakamatsu–Yabe Extended BB2010, Theorem 4, §4 and equation (4.7): nonlinear Q-superlinear scalar convergence conditional on convergence to the solution, eventually unit line-search multipliers, and convergence of normalized step directions. https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2008/05/1987.pdf . This trajectory-conditional result must be acknowledged; it does not give our uniform, explicit oracle bound under objective assumptions alone.
- [Mouhssine–Ratnani–Sadok2025, §3.3, Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02644#S3.SS3) uses bounded minimal-polynomial coefficients to bound the generalized reduced-rank-extrapolation residual quadratically without the normalized-history conditioning assumption. This residual is a projection of history differences, not the true nonlinear residual at the returned point. Their Theorem 3 gives actual quadratic convergence with an additional conditioning hypothesis; their output combines history vectors. A small generalized residual does not by itself ensure positive real roots for the computed extrapolation polynomial or control the nonlinear trajectory obtained by executing its factors. The present proof instead transfers observed polynomial action through every feasible symmetric fit, obtains bounded positive scalar steps, and controls the gradient at the actual nonlinear endpoint. Polynomial cancellation and its perturbation alone are not claimed as new.
- Gao et al.2025, Theorems 6.4 and 6.9, §7.1: nonlinear superlinear online scaling, but matrix directions and a lookahead action; the inverse Hessian must lie in the allowed scaling set. https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23081 . It does not supply a scalar-current-gradient theorem on general Hessians.
- Altschuler–Parrilo strong-convex silver guarantee O(κ^q log(1+LR²/ε)), q=log_(1+sqrt2)2≈.78644. https://arxiv.org/html/2309.07879 . This upper bound applies to our stricter class too. Our guaranteed order improves only at sufficiently high accuracy. With ell=log(1+L²R²/(µε)), Theta=MLR/µ², sufficient joint regimes are ell≥2logκ, κ^(1−q)[dlog(4κ)+log(d+1)+log(1+Theta)]=o(ell), and dlog(1+ell)=o(κ^q ell). Fixed d,κ,Theta and ε→0 satisfy these. This compares proved upper orders, not actual silver trajectories or a lower bound on the Hessian-regular class.

Limitations: the initialization has worse conditioning dependence than silver; constants give a small local basin before the warmup. The method stores O(d²) real data and solves dense feasibility/eigenvalue problems. The theorem counts oracle calls, not arithmetic, bit complexity, finite precision, or wall-clock time. Classical nonlinear conjugate-gradient n-step quadratic results use other directions and generally line-search/restart mechanisms. It does not improve unrestricted first-order methods using matrix or auxiliary directions, nor resolve the original general smooth-convex adaptive silver barrier. Optimal dimension and conditioning dependence remain open. The mathematical result is restricted to this scalar update family; no claim is made that it is optimal among unrestricted first-order methods.

[Liu–Ye2026, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26873) gives a matching polynomial conditioning exponent for predetermined schedules on the broader smooth strongly convex class. That lower bound is not asserted for this fixed-dimensional anchored-Hessian class. Neither it nor the smooth-convex silver lower bound establishes a barrier on the assumptions of the present theorem.
