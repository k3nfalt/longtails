# A quadratic oracle lower bound for generalized Lipschitz Minty operators

An exact oracle gives $F(x)$ for arbitrary $x\in\mathbb R^d$. A continuous $F$ has an unknown zero $z$ with $\|x_0-z\|\le R$ and $\langle F(x),x-z\rangle\ge0$ everywhere. The variation on a segment is bounded by its length times $L_0+L_1\max_{[x,y]}\|F\|$. These are the classical Minty condition and the symmetric generalized Lipschitz condition with exponent one. The public data are $d,x_0,R,L_0>0,L_1\ge0,\varepsilon>0$. The setup allows every randomized algorithm, queries anywhere and an arbitrary returned point. The upper note gives an exponentially growing, nonmonotone family of coupled rotational fields in this class. [Operator and oracle specification](../Setups/GeneralizedLipschitzMinty.lean) · [Examples and upper analysis](GeneralizedMintyExtragradient.md) · [Choudhury and Loizou, Assumption 1.1 and Section 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22421v1)

Write $T=R(L_0/\varepsilon+L_1)$ and $m=\lfloor(T/1024)^2\rfloor$. Suppose

$$
T\ge1024,\qquad 32\log(1+L_1\varepsilon/L_0)\le L_1R,\qquad d\ge8192(1+m)^3 .
$$

For **each** budget from zero through $m$ and every algorithm, some deterministic oracle on a fixed instance makes its returned residual nonintegrable or gives $\mathbb E\|F(X)\|>\varepsilon$. The hard instance can depend on the algorithm and budget and is chosen independently of its realized seed. The companion deterministic upper bound takes $2(1+\lceil32T^2\rceil)$ calls in every dimension. Both use exactly the same operator access, radius, returned-point residual and template execution. Together they give $\Theta(T^2)$ in the displayed regime. ## Why the relative term is necessary

The globally Lipschitz Minty lower bound already yields the $(L_0R/\varepsilon)^2$ term. Substituting $L=L_0+L_1\varepsilon$ into that bound would require checking the generalized condition even near a zero, where the allowed local slope falls to $L_0$. Multiplying the entire construction by a large constant loses this property. The new construction amplifies a hidden-coordinate chain only where its residual can pay for the additional slope. It also keeps the exact multiplier constant throughout the simulated query transcript. Otherwise a single real-valued oracle answer could reveal information about the hidden coordinates through its magnitude. [Classical Minty chain and prior bound](EuclideanWeakMintyLowerBound.md) · [Amplified field and regularity proof](../Proofs/GeneralizedMintyChain.lean)

A direct residual-only remapping illustrates the loss avoided here. If the original chain certifies a hidden-output residual of order $L_0r$, a radial exponential remapping with scale $L_0/L_1$ reaches $\varepsilon$ at spacing of order $L_1^{-1}\log(1+L_1\varepsilon/L_0)$. The radius constraint then gives only order $(L_1R/\log(1+L_1\varepsilon/L_0))^2$ hidden links from that calculation. For $\varepsilon=(L_0/L_1)e^{aL_1R}$ with fixed $0<a<1/32$ and large $L_1R$, that count stays bounded while the theorem forces order $(L_1R)^2$ calls. The construction earns the amplification distance from a group of hidden coordinates simultaneously.

Use $2m+1$ distinct hidden coordinate axes, spacing $r=R/(2\sqrt{m+1})$, base slope $\kappa=L_0/12$ and ball radius $B=2R$. In coordinates centered at $x_0$, let $P_B$ be projection onto this ball. Form $D(x)$ by subtracting the sum of the last $m+1$ hidden coordinates of $P_Bx$ from $(m+1)r/2$, dividing by $\sqrt{m+1}$ and taking the positive part. This function is 1-Lipschitz. For the original Minty chain $H$, define

$$
M=1+L_1\varepsilon/L_0,\qquad a(x)=\min\{\log M,L_1D(x)/2\},\qquad F(x)=e^{a(x)}H(x).
$$

Positivity preserves the root and its Minty inequality. Where $D>0$, some tail coordinate is at most $r/2$, so the base chain has residual at least $\kappa r/4$, including outside the ball. This yields $6\kappa e^{a(x)}\le L_0+(L_1/2)\|F(x)\|$. Ordering two points by their exponents bounds the variation of the scalar multiplier by the larger exponential times the exponent difference. Together with the Lipschitz bound on $a$, this proves the required regularity on every segment. [Scaling estimates](../Proofs/GeneralizedMintyScales.lean) · [Regularity proof](../Proofs/GeneralizedMintyChain.lean)

For any fixed algorithm seed, on at least three quarters of the finite hidden coordinate words, each unexposed tail coordinate of each projected probe, including the output probe, has magnitude at most $r/4$ through the budget. Thus $D\ge R/8$ and $e^a=M$ at every such probe. The transcript agrees exactly with a simulation exposing at most one coordinate per query. At its output, $M\kappa r\ge8\varepsilon$ gives residual at least $2\varepsilon$. Finite summation followed by integration selects a fixed embedding on which the randomized algorithm fails. ## Scope and closest bounds

Choudhury and Loizou's extragradient analysis already supplies the upper order by using the one-point Minty inequality in the proof of Theorem 3.6. The new lower bound shows that an unrestricted method cannot accelerate away the relative regularity cost. For monotone operators, Rudomanov et al. instead obtain $1+L_0R/\varepsilon+(L_1R)^{6/5}$ up to logarithms when $L_0R/\varepsilon\ge1$. For instance, fixing $L_0=R=\varepsilon=1$ and letting $L_1$ grow puts both comparisons in range, and the Minty complexity grows as $L_1^2$. A stationarity bound parameterized by an initial function-value gap would require a scalar potential and an additional relation between gap and radius. The lower theorem covers general unconstrained operators and does not assert the same separation for gradients of functions or gradients of minimax games. [Choudhury and Loizou, NeurIPS 2025, Appendix D.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22421v1) · [Rudomanov et al., arXiv:2611.20296v1, Sections 3 and 4.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20296v1)

At $L_1=0$ the logarithmic condition is automatic. For example $R=L_0=1$, $\varepsilon=1/1024$ gives $m=1$ and permits $d=65536$. For $L_1>0$ the proof covers accuracies through $(L_0/L_1)(e^{L_1R/32}-1)$ subject to the size and dimension conditions. The logarithmic window supplies enough distance for the multiplier to reach its plateau. At accuracies at or above $(L_0/L_1)(e^{L_1R}-1)$, segment growth from a zero makes $x_0$ itself accurate. The intervening coarse-accuracy region and the optimal dimension dependence remain open in this comparison. [Segment growth, Lemmas 2.4–2.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20296v1)
