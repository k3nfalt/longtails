This result answers, up to logarithmic factors and inside an explicit parameter regime, the open question raised in Section 5 of *Descending Everest, Part I* (Rudomanov, Fomin, Gasnikov, Stonyakin, [arXiv 2610.21461v2, 5 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21461v2)): for convex (L₀,L₁)-smooth minimization with a known distance bound R **and a known initial-gap bound Δ** (their class 𝒫_Δ), "a matching lower bound in terms of the dependence on Δ is unknown." The answer is given in the exact-gradient oracle model with randomized algorithms and an expected-gap success criterion; Everest's own lower bounds are for deterministic methods that query both f and ∇f, so the model differs (see the limitations). The new lower bound Ω(√(L₀R²/ε) + min{(L₁R·log(Δ/ε))^{2/3}, L₁R}/log(2+L₁R)) interpolates between Everest's two known endpoints, the radius-only term L₁R (Theorem 4.10) and the small-gap term (L₁R)^{2/3} (Theorem B.3), and it matches their upper bound, Theorem B.7(b), O([1 + √(L₀R²/ε) + (L₁R)^{2/3} log^{2/3}(2Δ/ε)]·log(2 + L₀R²/ε + L₁R)), up to logarithmic factors in L₁R and L₀R²/ε wherever the interpolation branch is active.

## Research question and why the pair closes it

The missing side of this popular problem was the lower bound: the upper bound for 𝒫_Δ is known (Everest's Theorem B.7(b), and the formalized [GeneralizedAcceleratedConvex](GeneralizedAcceleratedConvex.lean)), while no lower bound with any Δ-dependence existed (Everest I §5(b); Everest II, [arXiv 2611.04171v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04171), §8.1 still lists "What is the optimal dependence of the complexity on Δ?" as open). This contribution proves the missing lower bound, including the classical accuracy term √(L₀R²/ε), in one `NonConvergence` theorem, and pairs it with the [companion upper theorem](KnownGapGeneralizedSmoothUpperBound.lean), which transfers both repository upper bounds into the same setup. Inside the valid regime the two budgets agree up to logarithmic factors, as quantified below; (L₀,L₁)-smoothness (Zhang, He, Sra, Jadbabaie 2020) is one of the most studied relaxations of Lipschitz smoothness, and 𝒫_Δ is the natural known-gap version of it used by most convex (L₀,L₁) analyses.

## Setup

The [setup](../Setups/GeneralizedSmoothConvexKnownGapPolynomialDimension.lean) is the Euclidean specialization of [GeneralizedSmoothAndConvex](../Setups/GeneralizedSmoothAndConvex.lean): E = ℝ^d, the objective f is convex with a differentiable gradient satisfying ‖D∇f(x)‖ ≤ L₀ + L₁‖∇f(x)‖, the oracle returns exact gradients, and some minimizer x* satisfies ‖x₀ − x*‖ ≤ R and f(x₀) − f(x*) ≤ Δ. All parameters (d, L₀, L₁, x₀, R, Δ, ε) are public: the algorithm knows both R and Δ, exactly as in Everest's class 𝒫_Δ. Success is the expected-gap criterion used by [GeneralizedSmoothConvexPolynomialDimension](../Setups/GeneralizedSmoothConvexPolynomialDimension.lean): an integrable objective gap of the execution output with expectation at most ε. No algorithm-family restriction is imposed; randomized algorithms with arbitrary seeds are allowed, and the hard instance may depend on the algorithm and budget but not on its realized seed.

Write q = L₁R, A = L₀R²/ε, Λ = log(Δ/ε), ℓ = log(2 + q). The valid regime is

**L₀, L₁, R, ε > 0, 16ε ≤ L₀R², q ≥ 2²⁰, Δ ≥ ε(2 + q)², Δ ≥ ε√A, d ≥ ⌈2⁴⁶(1 + q + √A)⁶⌉.**

The two numerical conditions q ≥ 2²⁰ and d ≥ poly(q, √A) are of the same kind as in the earlier radius-only polynomial-dimension lower bound (PR #168). The condition Δ ≥ ε(2 + q)² restricts the known-gap bound to be at least a polynomial multiple of ε, so Λ ≥ 2ℓ. The condition Δ ≥ ε√A is an artifact of the instance used for the accuracy term: that instance (below) has initial gap about ε√A/64. It only excludes the corner (2+q)² ≤ Δ/ε < √A, where √A > q² is far larger than the Δ-term (which is at most q/ℓ), so the corner is a purely classical regime in which the complexity is Θ̃(√A); there Everest's Theorem B.3, whose equal-slope chain has initial gap 4ε, already gives the √A lower bound in the deterministic model. Together the two gap conditions exclude Everest's band 4ε ≤ Δ < ε·max{(2+q)², √A}; see the limitations. The known-gap assumption f(x₀) − f* ≤ Δ is the standard one in the convex (L₀,L₁) literature: it is part of the repository setup [GeneralizedSmoothAndConvex](../Setups/GeneralizedSmoothAndConvex.lean), of Everest's class 𝒫_Δ (defined in their Section 2 and studied in their Appendix B), and of the convex (L₀,L₁) analyses of Gorbunov et al. ([arXiv 2409.14989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14989)) and Li, Qian, Tian, Rakhlin, Jadbabaie ([arXiv 2306.01264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01264)), whose rates depend on Δ. The regime is nonempty ([valid_nonempty](../Proofs/KnownGapParameters.lean)): the explicit [witness](../Proofs/KnownGapParameters.lean) (L₀ = L₁ = ε = 1, R = 2³⁰, Δ = e⁴⁰⁹⁶, so √A = 2³⁰) satisfies every condition, lies in the interpolation branch Λ ≤ √q of the minimum, and has Δ-term at least 4096.

## Main theorem

The [main theorem](KnownGapGeneralizedSmoothLowerBound.lean) is a `NonConvergence` statement: every randomized adaptive gradient algorithm fails on some admissible instance at every integer budget

**0 ≤ b ≤ ⌊ max{ √(L₀R²/ε)/8192, min{ (q·log(Δ/ε))^{2/3}, q } / (2048 · log(2+q)) } ⌋.**

The proof splits on which term of the maximum the budget falls under: budgets up to the floor of the Δ-term use the decreasing-slope chain, and the remaining budgets (which are at least 1 and at most √A/8192) use the accuracy instance. Both branches of the inner minimum are realized. For log(Δ/ε) ≤ √q the Δ-term reads (qΛ)^{2/3}/(2048ℓ); for larger Δ it saturates at q/(2048ℓ), the radius-only answer, which is forced because Theorem 3.7 of Everest solves 𝒫_R itself in Õ(√A + q) calls.

## Hard instances and mechanism

**Δ-term.** The instance is Everest's chain with geometrically decreasing slopes ([§4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21461v2)) — the conjugate over the polytope {z ≥ 0, Σ zᵢ/Gᵢ ≤ 1} of the regularizer 2Φ(Z) + Z Σ r(zᵢ/Z) that makes the curvature grow with the gradient norm — but with a **free per-link slope ratio** e^h instead of the fixed ratio e^{1/√m} used by Everest (Theorem 4.8) and by the repository's PR #168 chain. The new [weights](../Proofs/KnownGapChainWeights.lean) are Gᵢ = 2εL₁e^{(m−1−i)h}; the shifts are Everest's σᵢ with these weights. The three facts that drive the Δ-dependence are proved there and in [KnownGapParameters](../Proofs/KnownGapParameters.lean):

- the tail sums satisfy Σ_{k≥i} G_k/Gᵢ ≤ 1 + 1/h (`tailSum_bound`), so every shift is at most (1/L₁)[2 log(1+L₁G₁/L₀) + 2 + 4 log(1+√m)(1 + 1/h)];
- the initial gap of the conjugate is at most G₁·max_i σᵢ, because H ≥ 0 and ⟨σ,·⟩ is linear on the polytope (`objective_upper`), so with total log-ratio (m−1)h ≤ Λ' the initial gap is at most ε e^{Λ'}(q+1)/8;
- the robust chain property (`field_tail_zero`) holds with margin τ = min(h,1)/(8L₁), because the slope gap Gⱼ − G_l ≥ hG_l supplies the slack that Everest's KKT argument (Lemma 4.5) needs; this margin is what makes the construction survive compact mollification and randomized hiding.

Choosing Λ' = min{Λ/4, √q/8} and h = Λ'/m, the radius constraint ‖σ‖ ≤ R/4 becomes √m·[8ℓ + 2 + 2Λ' + 4ℓm/Λ'] ≤ q/16 with ℓ = log(2+q), which holds for m ≤ ⌊min{(qΛ)^{2/3}, q}/(2048ℓ)⌋ + 1 (`bracket_bound`); the gap constraint f(x₀) − f* ≤ Δ follows from Λ' ≤ Λ/4 and Δ/ε ≥ (2+q)² (`gap_bound`, `objective_initial`). The exponent 2/3 arises exactly as in Everest's Theorem B.3: the term 4ℓm/Λ' in the per-coordinate shift gives ‖σ‖ ≍ m^{3/2}ℓ/(L₁Λ'), hence m ≍ (qΛ')^{2/3}; the term 2Λ' gives ‖σ‖ ≍ √m Λ'/L₁ and caps Λ' at √q.

What is not routine relative to Everest's Theorem 4.8 and PR #168 is (i) the robust margin τ = min(h,1)/(8L₁): Everest's equal-slope and fixed-ratio chains certify only exact zeros of the tail gradient, which does not survive mollification, whereas the slope gap hG_l gives a slack that is uniform in the hidden coordinates and makes the randomized finite-family argument go through; (ii) the gap control for the actual C^∞, localized instance (not just the conjugate at x₀), which requires the linear-on-polytope bound `objective_upper` to be pushed through averaging and localization; and (iii) the saturation Λ' ≤ √q/8, which is forced by the term 2Λ' in the shift bound and coincides with the crossover of Everest's two upper bounds (Theorem 3.7 for 𝒫_R and Theorem B.7(b) for 𝒫_Δ), so the two branches of the budget are both unavoidable.

Everything downstream of the weights is inherited from PR #168 and re-instantiated for the new weights and margin: compact mollification making the instance C^∞ with the required curvature bound ([KnownGapSmoothedChain](../Proofs/KnownGapSmoothedChain.lean)), exact prefix identities ([KnownGapChainPrefix](../Proofs/KnownGapChainPrefix.lean)), the polynomial-hinge localization that makes the far field equal to a·x independently of the hidden coordinates ([KnownGapLocalizedChain](../Proofs/KnownGapLocalizedChain.lean)), the truncated partial oracle and the near/far gap lemmas ([KnownGapHardOracle](../Proofs/KnownGapHardOracle.lean)), and the finite-family expectation argument [FinitePrefixExpectation](../Proofs/FinitePrefixExpectation.lean) assembled in [KnownGapChainLowerBound](../Proofs/KnownGapChainLowerBound.lean). The hiding threshold is θ = min(h,1)/(4L₁) ≥ 1/(4L₁m), which is why the dimension requirement grows to 2⁴⁶(1+q)⁶ (`dimension_sufficient`).

**Accuracy term.** The [accuracy instance](../Proofs/KnownGapAccuracyInstance.lean) compactly mollifies the repository's randomized Nemirovski–Yudin dead-zone chain ([SmoothConvexFiniteWall](../Proofs/SmoothConvexFiniteWall.lean), used by [SmoothConvexExpectedLowerBound](SmoothConvexExpectedLowerBound.lean)), whose gradient is L₀-Lipschitz but not differentiable. For a budget N the chain has K = N + 1 links, radius R_c = 256K√(ε/L₀) ≤ R/16, step a = R_c/√K, and dead zone δ = a/(8K); the mollification radius is δ/64 and the hiding threshold is δ/8. Averaging over the bump keeps the L₀-Lipschitz gradient (dimension-free), makes it C^∞, so ‖D∇f‖ ≤ L₀ ≤ L₀ + L₁‖∇f‖, and preserves convexity. The dead zone δ/4 of the unmollified chain absorbs the threshold δ/8 plus the radius δ/64, so the prefix identity (`partialReply_eq`) and the near gap survive; beyond 4R_c the averaged field is π-free, which gives the far branch. Both gaps equal L₀a²/(8192K) = 8ε, while every minimizer value is at most L₀(δ/64)²/2 ≤ ε/8, so expected success at accuracy ε forces expected loss at most 2ε, while the finite-family argument of [FinitePrefixExpectation](../Proofs/FinitePrefixExpectation.lean) produces a failing embedding as soon as 2·2ε < 8ε. A minimizer exists in the ball of radius R by compactness, because the objective exceeds 8ε outside 4R_c and is at most ε/8 at the hidden point. The initial gap is at most L₀a²/1024 = 64Kε ≤ ε√A/64 ≤ Δ, and the dimension needed is 131072K⁵ + K, covered by d ≥ 2⁴⁶(1 + q + √A)⁶.

## Where the pair is tight

Let T_Δ = min{(qΛ)^{2/3}, q}/(2048ℓ), so the proved lower bound is of order √A + T_Δ throughout the valid regime.

- **Against the formalized repository upper bound** (the minimum of the two transferred budgets): in the interpolation branch Λ ≤ √q the ratio upper/lower is O(Λ^{1/3}·ℓ), from comparing ⌈q^{2/3}⌉·log(4 + 4Δ/ε) with (qΛ)^{2/3}/ℓ; in the saturated branch Λ > √q it is O(ℓ²), from the radius-only budget 32768(1 + q)ℓ. The √A terms match up to constants. Since Λ^{1/3} = log^{1/3}(Δ/ε), the repository pair is tight up to logarithmic factors in the problem parameters; measured in q alone, Λ^{1/3} can reach q^{1/6} at the top of the interpolation branch, where Everest's log^{2/3} counting (not formalized here) is needed for a polylog(q) gap.
- **Against Everest's upper bounds:** in the interpolation branch Theorem B.7(b) is within O(ℓ·log(2 + A + q)) of the lower bound. In the saturated branch Theorem B.7(b) alone grows like Λ^{2/3} and is not tight, but the minimum of Theorems B.7(b) and 3.7 (the radius-only bound Õ(√A + q)) is within polylog(q) of q/ℓ.
- Tightness is claimed only inside the valid regime.

## Limitations

- The band 4ε ≤ Δ < ε·max{(2+q)², √A} is excluded. In the part with √A > Δ/ε the accuracy term dominates and Everest's Theorem B.3 gives √A deterministically; in the part with Δ/ε < (2+q)² Everest's Theorem B.3 gives (q/log(2+L₁²ε/L₀))^{2/3} in the deterministic model, within polylog of the upper bound. The condition Δ ≥ ε√A comes from the accuracy instance, whose initial gap is about ε√A/64; Everest's equal-slope chain of Theorem B.3 avoids it in the deterministic model, but it is not robust enough for the randomized hiding argument used here without changing its shift spacing.
- Model differences from Everest: gradient-only oracle (weaker oracle, so a weaker lower-bound model) but randomized algorithms with an expected-gap criterion (stronger algorithm class), polynomial dimension d ≥ 2⁴⁶(1 + q + √A)⁶ instead of d = 2n, and q ≥ 2²⁰.
- Constants are not optimized, and the logarithmic factors above are not claimed to be tight; Everest's own log^{3/2} question (their first open item) is untouched.
