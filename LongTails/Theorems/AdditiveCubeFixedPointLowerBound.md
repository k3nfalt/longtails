# Lower complexity of stochastic fixed-point residuals with additive Gaussian noise

A call at a point $x$ of the cube $[-R,R]^d$ returns $T(x)+Z$, where $T$ is a nonexpansive self-map in the sup norm. Each call uses fresh noise, including repeated calls at the same point. The hard oracle uses independent centered Gaussian coordinates of a common variance and satisfies $\mathbb E\|Z\|_\infty^2\leq \sigma^2$. For each fixed noise vector the observation map is nonexpansive from the cube into the ambient space, and its values can lie outside the cube. The target is an output in the cube with expected sup-norm residual at most $\varepsilon$. The expectation includes both algorithm and oracle randomness. The model permits arbitrary measurable adaptive queries, internal state, and output rules. [Gaussian setup](../Setups/GaussianAdditiveCubeFixedPoint.lean) · [General additive setup](../Setups/AdditiveCubeFixedPoint.lean)

Write $t=R/\varepsilon$, $u=\sigma/\varepsilon$, $D=2+\log d$, and $H=1+\log(2+t+u)$. For $t\ge128$, $d\ge\lceil t\rceil$, and each integer budget up to

$$
\left\lfloor\frac{(1+u^2/D)\max\{1,t/H\}}{2^{26}}\right\rfloor,
$$

every algorithm has a fixed instance on which it fails the target. This includes zero calls and the displayed endpoint. Hence at least one more call than the displayed integer is necessary for a uniform guarantee. The map may depend on the algorithm and its call budget. Its signs are chosen before the algorithm's seed or its noise is realized. The proof uses coordinate variance $\sigma^2/[4(2+\log d)]$, which can be computed from the public parameters. ## Comparison under fresh observations

Diakonikolas, Guzmán, and Martínez-Rubio study stochastic nonexpansive fixed-point problems in general norms. Their Theorem 13 establishes a multiplicative lower bound with a Bernoulli oracle that either hides or amplifies the residual. Its conditional noise distribution changes with the query. Theorem 11 also gives the stronger, linear-in-$t$ noiseless lower bound. Their coupled sign-chain geometry, including the map in equation (8), supplies the deterministic hard family here. The new lower bound concerns the more restrictive additive location model, in which no query-dependent masking is available. [Diakonikolas, Guzmán, and Martínez-Rubio, arXiv:2609.09524v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09524v1)

Lee's Theorem 3 gives an expected-residual upper bound of order $t(1+u^2\log(2d))$ for the broader bounded-variance oracle at $t\ge1$. A cube instance extends to the whole space by composing $T$ with coordinate clipping on its input. Clipping the returned point preserves the residual bound by nonexpansiveness of clipping. The initialization bound follows from the existence of a fixed point in the compact cube and the choice of its center as starting point. In the isotropic Gaussian model the mean of $n$ noise vectors has the same distribution as $Z/\sqrt n$, so Lee's batch accuracy proof on that subclass gives order $t(1+u^2)$. [Lee, Sections 3–4 and Theorem 3, arXiv:2610.26484v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26484v1)

For example, at $R=\sigma=1$ and $d=\lceil1/\varepsilon\rceil$, the Gaussian lower bound grows as $\Omega(\varepsilon^{-3}/\log^2(1/\varepsilon))$. The Gaussian upper bound is $O(\varepsilon^{-3})$. Thus fresh additive Gaussian observations already incur the cubic accuracy dependence up to two logarithms on this family. The logarithmic gap and the restriction to sufficiently high dimension remain. The explicit $\log d$ matters if dimension grows faster than polynomially in $1/\varepsilon$. This argument applies to general nonlinear nonexpansive maps. A lower bound for a narrower class such as Bellman operators or convex gradients would need hard instances in that class.

## How the lower bound is proved

The map moves each coordinate a capped distance toward a signed target whose size is the minimum of the positive signed values attained by earlier coordinates. A wrong sign makes all later targets zero. Conversely, residual at most $6\varepsilon$ forces every active sign to be correct when the chain has fewer than $R/(6\varepsilon)$ active coordinates. The construction couples coordinates through prefix minima and allows arbitrary query points, including dense queries. [Chain geometry](../Proofs/AdditiveFixedPointChain.lean) · [Cube map and residual implication](../Proofs/AdditiveFixedPointFamily.lean)

Partition calls into phases. Under the experiment containing just the previously chosen prefix, some fixed word of $k$ new signs has total query-hit probability at most $n/2^k$ in a phase of $n$ calls. Inserting those $k$ signs changes the conditional observation mean on at most $k$ coordinates, each by at most $16\varepsilon$. For Gaussian coordinates with common variance $v>0$, the squared density at mean $b$ equals the product of the densities at means $a$ and $2b-a$, times $\exp(\|b-a\|_2^2/v)$. Applying Cauchy–Schwarz one observation at a time transfers the rare-event estimate to the extended prefix, including randomized starting states and queries chosen from past replies. This change-of-measure step accounts for information leaked by all coordinates of every noisy reply. [Adaptive Gaussian comparison](../Proofs/AdditiveFixedPointGaussian.lean) · [Noise and sup-norm moments](../Proofs/AdditiveFixedPointNoise.lean)

With $k$ proportional to $1+\log(2+b)$ for total budget $b$, the transfer loss is small enough to sum across phases. Future signs leave the simulated queries unchanged up to a hit. Choosing the phase words successively and finally choosing a least-likely output sign gives one deterministic hard map under the actual seed law. For a budget smaller than one phase, a direct two-mean comparison suffices. At zero variance the construction uses single-call phases, where the query is chosen before the response. The final probability of the correct sign is at most $3/4$, whereas expected residual at most $\varepsilon$ would give correct sign with probability at least $5/6$. The public conclusion is failure of expected accuracy. ## Why single-point access matters

If an oracle permits two evaluations with the *same* additive noise vector, their difference is exactly $T(x)-T(0)$. Estimate $T(0)$ once, obtaining $c$ with expected sup-norm error at most $\varepsilon/4$. Every subsequent paired call at $x,0$ implements the deterministic map $S(x)=\operatorname{clip}(T(x)-T(0)+c)$ exactly, conditional on $c$. It is a nonexpansive cube self-map and has a fixed point in the cube. Deterministic Halpern iteration from the center returns a point with $S$-residual at most $\varepsilon/2$ in $O(1+t)$ iterations. Pointwise, the $T$-residual exceeds the $S$-residual by at most $\|c-T(0)\|_\infty$. Counting both evaluations in each pair gives $O(1+t+u^2\log(2d))$ evaluations for general finite-variance additive noise, or $O(1+t+u^2)$ for isotropic Gaussian noise. The separation uses the ability to choose a second point for the same realization. The Lean lower theorem models fresh single-point access.
