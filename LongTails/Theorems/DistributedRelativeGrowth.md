# When can fast workers overcome an arbitrarily slow worker?

Consider three workers that take $1,1,H$ seconds per gradient. Suppose their convex losses differ, their mean has quadratic growth, and their local mean gradients satisfy the maximum strong-growth condition with squared factor two. Must the leading statistical time to reach expected squared distance $\varepsilon$ grow like $H/\varepsilon$? Tyurin’s September 2026 preprint raised this question after proving such a lower bound with an arbitrarily small additive heterogeneity term. For the pure relative condition, the answer is **no**. On these machines the statistical term is $O(1/\varepsilon)$. More generally, a worker-weighting rule yields a sharp leading statistical time under an *average* relative growth bound. [Lean theorem and deadline](DistributedRelativeGrowth.lean) · [Tyurin, Theorem 2.3 and Remark 2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17483v1#S2)

## Problem, oracle and output

Worker $i$ alone can query a stochastic gradient of its convex differentiable $L_i$-smooth loss $f_i$ on $\mathbb R^d$. Write $F=n^{-1}\sum_i f_i$ and $S=\arg\min F$. A solution lies within distance $R$ of the initial point. The two growth conditions, at every $x$, are

$$
F(x)-F^*\ge \tfrac{\mu}{2}\operatorname{dist}(x,S)^2,\qquad
\frac1n\sum_i\|\nabla f_i(x)\|^2\le \rho\|\nabla F(x)\|^2,\qquad \rho\ge1.
$$

The first is quadratic growth. For smooth convex objectives it is equivalent, up to universal factors in $\mu$, to the global Polyak–Łojasiewicz condition used by Tyurin. The second applies to the **mean** gradients. It includes Tyurin’s maximum condition when $\rho=2$. FedProx uses a related mean-square dissimilarity bound outside a stationarity tolerance. Here the inequality holds down to the solution set, which permits a guarantee at arbitrarily small accuracy. Every global minimizer minimizes each local loss since their gradients all vanish there. Individual workers may have additional minimizers and lack quadratic growth. [Lean setup](../Setups/DistributedRelativeGrowth.lean) · [Tyurin, Assumptions 1.1–1.5 and 3.1–3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17483v1) · [Li et al., Section 4 and Assumption 1](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2020/file/1f5fe83998a09396ebe6477d9475ba0c-Paper.pdf) · [Karimi, Nutini and Schmidt, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04636)

Each fresh gradient is unbiased with centered second moment at most $\sigma^2$, including at solutions. The sample maps are jointly measurable and the streams are IID within workers and independent across workers under an abstract joint law. The public inputs include the bounds, $x_0,d,n,\varepsilon$ and the timings. Each raw gradient costs $h_i>0$ on worker $i$. The coordinator has no gradient oracle. Independent directed star links each carry one message at a time, with latency $\ell$ and time $b$ per bit, including a 64-bit clock field. Distinct links and distinct workers can operate concurrently. Local arithmetic is free and service times are known and fixed. The comparison uses these fixed times. In a compute-only model with known upper service times, a worker can hold an early reply until its scheduled completion. [Physical star](../Setups/StarNetwork.lean) · [Distributed execution and law](../TemplateDistributed.lean)

The algorithm batches in parallel for a common time allowance and combines worker means with unequal coefficients. Each worker scales its raw replies, clips and quantizes its batch mean, and sends a finite packet. The coordinator rounds and broadcasts the aggregate. A constant-step phase and a diminishing-step phase return the iterate after their last update. The proof charges the additional batch performed by the underlying protocol, both message directions and the clock fields. It proves finite execution at every deadline up to the budget and $\mathbb E\,\operatorname{dist}(X,S)^2\le\varepsilon$ for the coordinator’s actual output. The algorithm uses no minimizer, objective values or realized local curvatures. The problem specification permits any algorithm with the stated information. ## Time bound

Let $u$ minimize $\sum_i v_i^2/h_i$ subject to $\sum_i v_i=n$ and $\sum_i v_i^2\le n\rho$. Its entries are positive. The following scalar calculation determines the minimizer from the public inputs. For $0\le t\le1$ put $r_i(t)=h_i/(1-t+t h_i)$ and $u_i(t)=n r_i(t)/\sum_j r_j(t)$. Choose any $t$ in this interval satisfying $\sum_i u_i(t)^2=n\rho$ if one exists, and use $t=0$ otherwise. Continuity ensures the latter choice is feasible. Every such choice gives the same minimizer by strict convexity of the weighted energy. At $t=0$ the allocation is proportional to service time. At $t=1$ every component is one. Whether the unconstrained optimum meets the RMS bound depends on the entire speed vector. A single scalar threshold handles arbitrary worker counts and speed profiles. [Profile construction](../Algorithms/GrowthProfile.lean) · [Threshold and supporting inequality](../Proofs/RelativeGrowthProfile.lean)

With $a_i=\mu u_i$, write

$$
 I=\sum_i a_i^2/h_i,\quad M=\max_i a_i\max(a_i,L_i),\quad
 \kappa=\frac{2M}{I}\sum_i h_i^{-1},\quad \Gamma=\frac{\sigma\max_i a_i}{M}.
$$

Up to universal constants, the finite deadline is

$$
 \frac{\sigma^2}{I\varepsilon}
 +\kappa\left[1+\log(1+R^2/\varepsilon)\right]
 \left[\max_i h_i+\ell+
 b\left(64+d\log\left(4+d+\kappa+(R^2+\Gamma^2)/\varepsilon\right)\right)\right].
$$

The first term has no accuracy logarithm. The second pays for starting every worker in every batch and communicating the update. At zero noise only the second term remains. The code reuses the existing batch protocol by setting its internal coefficients to reproduce weights $a_i/h_i$, energy $I$, and clipping scale $M$. The calibration proof checks these identities exactly, with the true local smoothness bounds retained in the convergence proof. The worker weights optimize the leading statistical term. The finite-accuracy transient, dimension and communication costs have no matching lower bound here. ## Why changing the weights preserves enough signal

At a nonstationary point let $g=\nabla F(x)$ and $v_i=\langle\nabla f_i(x),g\rangle/\|g\|^2$. These scalars can be negative. They sum to $n$, and Cauchy–Schwarz and relative growth give $\sum_i v_i^2\le n\rho$. The optimality inequality for the speed-weighted quadratic gives

$$
 \sum_i \frac{u_i}{h_i}v_i\ \ge\ \sum_i\frac{u_i^2}{h_i}=:J.
$$

For an interior threshold, $(1-t)u_i/h_i+t u_i$ is independent of $i$. Taking its inner product with $v-u$, whose entries sum to zero, proves the inequality by the active squared-norm constraint. The endpoints have separate proofs. For $H=\sum_i(u_i/h_i)f_i$, this yields $\langle\nabla H,\nabla F\rangle\ge J\|\nabla F\|^2$ everywhere. It gives exact objective-gap comparison $H(x)-H(z)\ge J(F(x)-F(z))$ for $z\in S$. The Lean proof telescopes along sufficiently small analytic gradient steps on $F$ and uses ordinary convex GD convergence. These steps occur only in the proof. The comparison also follows from the established convex slope-comparison principle. [Alignment and gap comparison](../Proofs/RelativeGrowthGeometry.lean) · [Pérez-Aros et al., Theorem 3.1](https://repositorio.uchile.cl/bitstream/handle/2250/177278/Determination-of-convex-functions.pdf?sequence=1)

The surrogate has quadratic growth at least $\mu J$. For parallel batches with $T/h_i$ samples, the unit-time noise factor in its gradient is $\sigma^2\sum_i (u_i/h_i)^2h_i=\sigma^2 J$, before integer rounding. The ratio to squared growth is exactly $\sigma^2/I$. Thus the same profile controls the signal retained by a biased mixture on *all* admissible nonlinear objectives and the independent noise it accumulates per second. The finite-bit proof controls clipping bias and rounding errors through both phases. The new contribution is the hardware-dependent statistical coefficient and the distributed complexity comparison it settles. Slope comparison, parallel batching and the two-phase quantized SGD protocol have prior analyses. ## Comparisons

**Three workers and the open question.** Set $\rho=2,h=(1,1,H),L_i=3\mu$ and take $H\ge1$. Any feasible $v$ has $v_3\le1+\sqrt2$, by applying Cauchy–Schwarz to $v_1-1,v_2-1$. Therefore $J\ge(2-\sqrt2)^2/2$, uniformly in $H$. The transient has $\kappa=O(1)$. With $\varepsilon=H^{-2}$, positive $\sigma$ and the other parameters fixed, the whole deadline is $O(H^2+H\log H)$. The arithmetic-mean statistical benchmark in Tyurin’s Theorems 2.3 and F.2 is of order $H^3$ on this profile even with free communication. Theorem 2.3 uses an additive discrepancy that persists at the optimum. Relative growth rules that out, including on the maximum-growth subclass in Remark 2.4. The earlier weak-interpolation guarantee alone permits nearly all curvature on the slow worker for $L_i=3\mu$. Tyurin’s harmonic-time Theorem 3.8 requires every worker to have the global solution set and a local PL inequality. [Tyurin, Sections 2–3 and Appendix F](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17483v1) · [Earlier weak-interpolation bound](DistributedWeightedInterpolation.md)

**Optimizing the fastest prefix leaves a logarithmic gap.** Take $n=2m$, $\rho=2$, service times $1/m$ on the first $m$ workers and $1,2,\ldots,m$ on the rest. On the feasible profile with zero signal on the fast half and two on the slow half, every fast-only prefix has zero signal. A uniform prefix containing the fast half and the first $j$ slow workers has unnormalized signal $2j$. Its parallel noise cost is $1+j(j+1)/2$. Thus every uniform-prefix signal-squared to noise ratio is at most eight even on the same instance. This profile is realized by local losses scaled by zero or two, within the smoothness caps below. A method retaining individual replies can choose different weights.

For the optimized profile, write $H_m=\sum_{j=1}^m1/j$. The feasible signal zero on the fast half and two on the rest gives $J\le4H_m$. If the optimal profile has mass $s$ on the fast half, then $s\le\sqrt J$ by Cauchy–Schwarz, and $\sum_j(u_{m+j}-2)^2\le4s$ by the squared-mass constraint. Expanding the energy of the slow half and applying Cauchy–Schwarz against $1/j$ gives $J\ge4H_m-8\sqrt{s\sum_j1/j^2}$. Hence $J=\Theta(\log m)$. Inverse-variance weighting $1/h_i$ alone can do worse than a uniform prefix. The same zero/two signal gives squared signal $4H_m^2$ against noise factor $m^2+H_m$. These comparisons concern the RMS class. The zero/two signal has maximum squared value four.

For $L_i=4\mu$, fixed $\mu,R,\sigma,\ell,b,d>0$ and $\varepsilon=m^{-6}$, the gain survives the whole deadline. From $\sum u_i^2\le4m$ one gets $M=O(m)$, $\kappa=O(m^3)$, and $\Gamma=O(1)$ since $\max u_i\ge1$. The transient is $O(m^4\log m)$. The total time is $O(m^6/\log m)$, versus at least order $m^6$ for the uniform-prefix statistical certificate. Classical parallel batching that retains the uniform objective gives the arithmetic coefficient $\sum_i h_i/n^2$. HeteRSGD optimizes unbiased importance sampling under heterogeneous sample costs. FedNova studies objective inconsistency from unequal client updates. Inkheart’s homogeneous weights estimate a common gradient, and Rescaled ASGD corrects unequal arrival rates to target the original average. Those analyses do not give this biased mixture’s sharp statistical time under RMS growth. [HeteRSGD, Sections 2–3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/chen23i.html) · [FedNova, Assumption 3 and Theorem 2](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/564127c03caab942e503ee6f810f54fd-Paper.pdf) · [Inkheart, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07795v2) · [Rescaled ASGD, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.13434v1)

## Nonlinear instances and statistical sharpness

For a sensor model with shared parameter $z$, let each client use the convex ridge loss $0.04\log\cosh(v_i^\top(x-z))$ with dense, nonorthogonal unit directions $v_i$. Three quarters of the workers also observe a full-rank quadratic calibration loss $\|x-z\|^2/2$. The mean has quadratic growth $\mu=3/4$ and gradient norm at least $3\|x-z\|/4$ by its radial inner product. Every local gradient has norm at most $1.04\|x-z\|$, so even the maximum squared growth factor is less than two. Ridge-only workers have a hyperplane of minimizers in dimension $d>1$ and no positive global PL constant because the ridge grows linearly in its tails. The Hessian changes with $x$ in interacting directions. Independent centered sensor perturbations of bounded variance give the specified oracle. More uneven signal strengths are possible under RMS growth. Scaling a common nonlinear loss by $1+\sqrt{n-1}$ at one worker and $1-1/\sqrt{n-1}$ at the others has mean scale one and mean squared scale two.

A two-point benchmark applies for $d\ge1,\sigma>0,R\ge2\sqrt\varepsilon$ and $L_i\ge a_i=\mu u_i$. Take $f_i(x)=a_i(x_1-\theta)^2/2$ for $\theta=\pm2\sqrt\varepsilon$, with $x_0=0$. The mean curvature along the first coordinate is $\mu$, the remaining directions are flat, and the RMS condition holds for every query. Choose the sample space $\Omega=\mathbb R$ with standard Gaussian draws and add independent Gaussian noise of variance $\sigma^2$ in that coordinate. Even with free communication, known $a_i$ and adaptive queries, a run of duration $T$ is simulable from at most $\lfloor T/h_i\rfloor$ observations per worker with mean $-a_i\theta$ and variance $\sigma^2$. The KL divergence between the Gaussian tapes is at most $8T\varepsilon I/\sigma^2$. For $T\le\sigma^2/(64I\varepsilon)$, Pinsker’s inequality gives equal-prior testing error at least $3/8$. Squared-distance error at most $\varepsilon$ at both centers would yield a sign test with error at most $1/4$. This benchmark establishes the leading coefficient up to universal factors in the stated regime. The benchmark is derived here in prose. The Lean theorem proves the elapsed-time upper bound. Smaller individual smoothness caps can exclude the hard profile. Improving the transient or bandwidth cost remains open.
