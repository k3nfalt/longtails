# Every single frozen convolution can take a linear number of passes

Consider the same two chains of adjacent pairwise comparisons as in the three-pass upper theorem. Choose any two real taps $a,b$ and freeze them. Start the readout at zero and run full-batch logistic GD, with any positive scalar step at each update. Each gradient requires $2n$ indexed replies. For each $n\ge8$, $d\ge32(n+1)$ and every budget $K\le n(n-3)$, there is a single explicit dataset on which this procedure's current readout fails to classify every row strictly. The dataset works for every initial tap pair, every step schedule and every seed. A partial pass leaves the previous readout in place. ## Data and output

The signed rows in group $g$ are $f_g(u_{g,i}-u_{g,i+1})$ for $0\le i<n$. The two scales are $1$ and $1/2$. The items are orthonormal, and cyclically shifting one group's items keeps them orthogonal to the other group. Within each group only the diagonal and the contacts between endpoints and interior items contribute to the lag Gram. The contact $\tau_{g,i}$ decreases along one path and increases along the other. The upper note gives all constants. Raw GD is the choice $(1,0)$. The frozen setup allows either tap to vanish and allows arbitrary positive rates based on information present at the update, including the final gradient reply. It requires the output to be the actual current iterate. [Shared geometry and GD update](../Setups/SpatialRankingLogistic.lean)

The construction uses disjoint blocks of 32 coordinates. In each comparison path an interior item contains a positive run of length $L$ followed by a negative run of the same length, normalized to unit norm. The two groups have $L=6$ and $L=2$. One pixel of the left endpoint is immediately before each run and one pixel of the right endpoint immediately follows it. Assigning each contact pixel amplitude $\tau_{g,i}\sqrt{2L}$ gives exactly the prescribed lag, with opposite signs at the ends. For each endpoint the total contact energy $P$ is at most $12/64$. Three private pixels with weights $(1/2,\pm1/2,1/2)$ contribute norm squared $3/4$ and shift inner product $\pm1/2$. One isolated pixel of amplitude $\sqrt{1/4-P}$ brings the endpoint to unit norm. The guard positions prevent unintended wraparound contacts. This yields all the Gram equalities without granting the optimizer the item dictionary. ## Why no scalar rate can evade the delay

For a frozen $T=aI+bS$, the readout update is a weighted sum of $T(u_i-u_{i+1})$. Write $q_i=f_g\sigma(-m_i)$ in one group and $z_i=\langle T u_i,v\rangle$. Summation by parts gives the following change at an interior vertex, with $h$ the positive mean-loss step divided by $2n$:

$$
z_i^+-z_i
=h\,ab(q_0+q_{n-1})\tau_{g,i}
+h(a^2+b^2+2ab\,d_{I,g})(q_i-q_{i-1}).
$$

Initially all scores vanish. After $t$ full passes, for $t<i<n-t$ the scores have the form $ab\,\ell_{g,t}\tau_{g,i}$ with $\ell_{g,t}\ge0$. Indeed, adjacent differences of an affine sequence agree. The corresponding sigmoid weights are equal, so the second term vanishes one vertex farther into the chain on the next pass. The first term adds a nonnegative quantity to $\ell_{g,t}$. The proof permits different endpoint weights in both groups and a different positive scalar step at every pass. [Propagation proof](../Proofs/FrozenRankingPropagation.lean)

If $2t+2<n$, two adjacent interior vertices still obey this formula on both paths. Their signed edge margins have opposite weak signs, since $ab$ is shared and the two contact slopes have opposite signs. At least one margin is nonpositive, including when $ab=0$. At a budget $K\le n(n-3)$ one has $2\lfloor K/(2n)\rfloor+2<n$. The execution proof accounts for all calls and incomplete scans. [Execution proof](../Proofs/FrozenRankingExecution.lean)

## Comparison and limitations

On this same construction, ordinary GD updating both taps and the readout separates the paths in $6n$ calls from identity taps and zero readout. For $n>9$, the two guarantees separate in query count; the ratio of the displayed lower and upper budgets is $(n-3)/6$. This compares the adaptability of a *single* spatial filter. An unrestricted optimizer can query every row at $((1,0),0)$. The readout component of each reply equals $x_i/2$. After those $2n$ queries, it may solve the linear feasibility problem $\langle w,x_i\rangle\ge1$ without further oracle calls. Feasibility follows by rescaling the strict separator guaranteed by the upper theorem on this finite dataset. The joint upper budget is three times this available unrestricted construction. The quadratic lower budget applies exclusively to the stated single-filter GD family. Numerical tests with two separately trained fixed-filter heads and combined predictions already evade the single-filter obstruction.

Tyurin's Theorems 5.1–5.2 compare a small quadratic-perceptron example with a raw perceptron. PR 253 lifts a two-example result to finite logistic GD at small random initialization. PR 254 gives a different six-observation construction against every positive *constant* step of raw logistic GD. PR 256 permits many defects but derives its delay for the perceptron. Here the obstruction holds for true sigmoid updates with every single constant spatial filter and adaptive readout rates on one family with unboundedly many distinct rows. The exact lag sparsity and arithmetic contacts make the finite propagation proof possible. A fixed tuned spatial filter can work well on CIFAR, as described in the companion upper note. Extending this lower bound to arbitrary perturbations or random image distributions is open.
