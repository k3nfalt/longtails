# Expected stationarity with one burn-in per batch

For an $L$-smooth objective with initial gap at most $\Delta$, one stationary Markov stream suffices to produce a measurable $X$ with integrable gradient norm and $\mathbb E\|\nabla f(X)\|\le\varepsilon$. Write $T=L\Delta/\varepsilon^2$ and $V=\sigma^2/\varepsilon^2$. The deterministic number of calls is

$$
N=\lceil 8T\rceil\big(\lceil\tau\log 16\rceil+\lceil1024\tau V\rceil\big)
\le 9261\,\tau TV.
$$

The oracle returns $g(x,Z_t)$ at call $t$. At each fixed $x$ its stationary mean is $\nabla f(x)$ and its stationary mean squared error is at most $\sigma^2$. The transition kernel satisfies $\sup_{z,A}|P^t(z,A)-\pi(A)|\le 2e^{-t/\tau}$ for measurable $A$. The theorem covers every valid input, namely $L,\varepsilon>0$, $\tau\ge1$, $T\ge2^{26}$, $\sigma\ge4096\varepsilon$, and $d\ge2^{40}(1+\tau TV)^4$. ## Comparison on the full parameter range

The earlier upper budget is $(\lceil32768TV\rceil+1)\lceil\tau\log(8192V)\rceil$. Dividing it by $N$ gives at least $(32768/9261)\log(8192V)>1$ since $V\ge2^{24}$. The new integer budget is strictly smaller at every valid input. The ceiling estimate uses $\lceil8T\rceil\le9T$, $\lceil\tau\log16\rceil\le4\tau$, and $\lceil1024\tau V\rceil\le1025\tau V$. [Earlier upper bound](MarkovNonconvexStationarity.lean)

The lower theorem proves failure through $\lfloor\tau TV/2^{56}\rfloor$ calls on the identical setup. The smallest sufficient integer budget is therefore at least $\lfloor\tau TV/2^{56}\rfloor+1>\tau TV/2^{56}$. The remaining multiplicative gap is bounded by $9261\cdot2^{56}$, including when the exported lower budget rounds to zero. The orders agree in all four quantities $\tau,L\Delta,\sigma,\varepsilon$. [Same-setup lower bound](MarkovNonconvexStationarityLowerBound.lean)

The related high-dimensional average-mixing setup has a lower budget of $\lfloor\tau TV\log(\sigma/\varepsilon)/2^{65}\rfloor$ on its own parameter range. Worst-start mixing here makes the burn-in effective from **every** state at the beginning of a block, including states where the history selects an atypical query. Stationary-average mixing permits exceptional slow starts and does not give this pointwise control of the alignment loss. [Average-mixing lower theorem](AverageMixingMarkovSmoothNonconvexLowerBound.lean) · [Its parameter range](../Setups/HighDimensionalAverageMixingMarkovSmoothNonconvex.lean)

## Algorithm and proof

Take $K=\lceil8T\rceil$, $b=\lceil\tau\log16\rceil$, $n=\lceil1024\tau V\rceil$, and $\eta=\varepsilon/(2L)$. Hold $x_j$ fixed for $b+n$ calls. Discard the first $b$ replies, average the next $n$ into $Y_j$, and set $x_{j+1}=x_j-\eta D(Y_j)$, where $D(v)=v/\|v\|$ for nonzero $v$ and $D(0)=0$. The implementation normalizes their sum, which gives the same direction for positive $n$. An independent uniform index selects $x_I$ from the $K$ pre-update points. The execution identity counts every slot in all $K$ blocks, including slots after the selected iterate has been stored. [Algorithm](../Algorithms/MarkovNonconvexBurnInBatches.lean) · [Execution](../Proofs/MarkovNonconvexBurnInBatchesExecution.lean)

At a deterministic query let $h=g(x,\cdot)-\nabla f(x)$ and $m_2=\pi\|h\|^2$. Hahn decomposition splits $P^t(z,\cdot)$ and $\pi$ into a common measure and two residual measures, each of mass at most $\delta_t=2e^{-t/\tau}$. For $\pi$-almost every $z$, by invariance, the integrals under the transition law exist, and Cauchy–Schwarz against the sum of the residual measures bounds the squared discrepancy of the two vector integrals by $2\delta_t(P^t\|h\|^2(z)+m_2)$. Invariance gives $\pi\|P^th\|^2\le4\delta_t m_2$. The stationary two-time law and Cauchy–Schwarz now bound the lagged inner product from above by $3e^{-t/(2\tau)}m_2$. Summing the off-diagonal terms uses $\sum_{t=1}^k e^{-t/(2\tau)}\le2\tau$. The diagonal contributes $m_2$ per sample, giving $\mathbb E\|Y-\nabla f(x)\|^2\le13\tau\sigma^2/n$. The expected error norm is at most $\varepsilon/8$. [Mixing estimate](../Proofs/MarkovNonconvexBurnInBatchesMixing.lean) · [Covariance](../Proofs/MarkovNonconvexBurnInBatchesCovariance.lean) · [Stationary batch](../Proofs/MarkovNonconvexBurnInBatchesVariance.lean)

For $u=\nabla f(x)$ the score $\langle u,D(Y)\rangle$ is bounded in absolute value by $\|u\|$ and is at least $\|u\|-2\|Y-u\|$. Integrating the remaining kept transitions gives a measurable function of the query and first kept state with the same absolute bound. At the block's first discarded state the query is already determined by the prefix. Exactly $b$ transitions later, replacing the first kept state's law by $\pi$ costs at most $2\|u\|\delta_b\le\|u\|/4$. This yields expected alignment at least $(3/4)\mathbb E\|u\|-\varepsilon/4$ even at adaptive queries. All assumed noise moment bounds are stationary and at fixed queries. [Block recursion](../Proofs/MarkovNonconvexBurnInBatchesContinuation.lean) · [Adaptive alignment](../Proofs/MarkovNonconvexBurnInBatchesAlignment.lean)

Smooth descent and telescoping give
$$
\frac{3}{4K}\sum_{j<K}\mathbb E\|\nabla f(x_j)\|
\le \frac{\Delta}{K\eta}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}+\frac{L\eta}{2}
\le \frac{3\varepsilon}{4}.
$$
Uniform selection identifies the average on the left before its $3/4$ factor with the output's expected gradient norm. Bounded step length and gradient Lipschitz continuity bound each iterate's gradient norm by $\|\nabla f(x_0)\|+jL\eta$. [Descent](../Proofs/MarkovNonconvexBurnInBatchesTrajectory.lean) · [Random output](../Proofs/MarkovNonconvexBurnInBatchesRate.lean)

## Prior work and limitations

The comparison below covers the primary nonconvex Markov-gradient papers identified in the task as well as related constrained optimization work and the lower-bound papers cited in the existing setup notes. In comparing stationarity criteria, an expected squared norm at most $\varepsilon^2$ implies expected norm at most $\varepsilon$ by Cauchy–Schwarz.

- Doan, Nguyen, Pham and Romberg (2020) impose geometric mixing of the conditional **mean oracle**, with mixing time $C\log(1/\alpha)$ at tolerance $\alpha$, and a statewise linear growth bound, equations (7)–(8). Their nonconvex SGD Theorem 3 uses randomized selection (50). The squared-gradient bound (51) contains $\log^2(H/\alpha_0)/\sqrt H$ for horizon $H$, hence retains accuracy-dependent logarithms. Their accelerated paper's Theorem 1, equations (12)–(14), gives a $\log H/\sqrt H$ term under bounded Lipschitz sample gradients. [Finite-time SGD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.10973) · [Accelerated Markov gradient](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=HwnLNKH2FO-)
- Dorfman and Levy (ICML 2022) use finite-state worst-start total variation, equation (2). Their adaptive method needs no mixing-time input. Under bounded sample gradients and a bounded smooth objective, Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 4.4 give expected norm $\widetilde O((\tau_{\rm mix}/H)^{1/4})$ for fixed problem constants. Here $H$ counts iterations. Lemma 3.1 charges $O(\log H)$ expected samples per iteration, so the sample guarantee retains logarithms in accuracy. [Paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/dorfman22a/dorfman22a.pdf)
- Even (ICML 2023) defines worst-start mixing at tolerance $\pi_{\min}/2$ in Definition 2.2. Theorem 5.3(1) for MC-SGD (2) assumes smooth component objectives, statewise bounds $\sigma_v$ on gradient dissimilarity, an attained minimum (Assumption 5.2), and a gap parameter $\Delta'$ enlarged by $\sigma_{\max}^2/L$. For $H\ge2\tau_{\rm mix}\log\tau_{\rm mix}$ its expected squared-norm rate is $\widetilde O(L\Delta'\tau_{\rm mix}/H+(\sqrt{L\Delta'\bar\sigma^2\tau_{\rm mix}}+\bar\sigma^2)/\sqrt H)$, where $\bar\sigma^2=\mathbb E_\pi\sigma_v^2$. Appendix C.1 uses a delay of order $\tau_{\rm mix}\log H$ in equation (9), yielding a bound with logarithms at accuracy $\varepsilon$. [Paper, §5.1 and Appendix C.1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/even23a/even23a.pdf)
- Beznosikov, Samsonov, Sheshukova, Gasnikov, Naumov and Moulines (NeurIPS 2023) use uniform geometric contraction between pairs of starting states in (3). Assumption A4 gives a statewise squared-error bound $\sigma^2+\delta^2\|\nabla f(x)\|^2$. Theorem 3 and Corollary 2 give $\widetilde O(\tau[(1+\delta^2)T+TV])$ calls for expected squared norm of order $\varepsilon^2$. Their randomized batching uses a logarithmic batch parameter. Their stationary batch calculation in Lemma 1 is closely related to the variance step here. The existing LongTails note already describes the burn-in and normalization argument informally. [Paper, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.15938) · [Earlier discussion](MarkovNonconvexStationarity.md)
- Huynh et al. (NeurIPS 2025), Theorem 4.5 under Assumptions 3.2 and 4.3, already give a minibatch estimate without an accuracy logarithm: with a single client and step $1/L$, an expected squared gradient of order $L\Delta/H+C_\infty\sigma^2/(\nu_{\rm ps}n)$ after $H$ rounds of $n$ samples. It holds under two additional conditions: a bounded density ratio $C_\infty$ of the transition kernel with respect to $\pi$, and a sample error bounded uniformly over states. The ratio $C_\infty$ can be infinite in the present setup, for example for a chain on a nonatomic space that keeps its state with positive probability and otherwise refreshes from $\pi$. [Paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/ba0dd55207ef557f6774bc85332f94a8-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Alacaoglu and Lyu (ICML 2023) study constrained optimization with dependent data. Assumption 2.2 controls the conditional total variation at a growing lag, and Assumption 2.3 imposes a conditional expected gradient bound for a Markov model or a uniform gradient bound. For exponential mixing, lag of order $\log H$ and steps proportional to $t^{-1/2}$, Theorem 3.1(i), equation (12), gives a squared Moreau-envelope gradient bound of order $\widetilde O(H^{-1/2})$. This yields $\widetilde O(\varepsilon^{-4})$ samples for expected envelope gradient norm at most $\varepsilon$, with logarithms from the lag and the step sums. [Paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/alacaoglu23a/alacaoglu23a.pdf)
- Arjevani et al., Theorem 3, equation (23), prove fresh-sample randomized hardness $\Omega(TV)$ without an accuracy logarithm. Sun and Wei's two Theorems 3.1 use hitting-time parameter $\tau_h$ and zero-respecting algorithms. Their smooth lower bounds have a common term $\Omega(\tau_h\sigma^2\min\{\sigma^2,L\Delta\}/\varepsilon^4)$ without an accuracy logarithm. The ICML result for countable stationary chains also has a $\tau_h\sigma^2/\varepsilon^2$ term. The NeurIPS finite-chain result has $\tau_h L\Delta/\varepsilon^2$. These hitting-time bounds concern different classes from the same-setup mixing-time lower bound above. [Arjevani et al., (23)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.02365) · [Sun and Wei, ICML 2025, Theorem 3.1 and (21)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/sun25u.html) · [Sun and Wei, NeurIPS 2025, Theorem 3.1](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/1cdbce34681a3aaa0154b300706743d8-Paper-Conference.pdf)

The method requires known bounds $\tau,\sigma,L,\Delta$ and the target $\varepsilon$. The constants are not sharp. The guarantee concerns one independently selected pre-update iterate and expectation over both sources of randomness. The formal contribution is the explicit logarithm-free sufficient budget under the existing oracle and execution semantics.
