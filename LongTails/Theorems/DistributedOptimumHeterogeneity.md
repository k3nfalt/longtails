# How much does each worker contribute when local optima disagree?

Worker speeds can differ by orders of magnitude. Giving faster workers larger gradient weights changes the objective, and a small local smoothness bound does not make a worker's gradient at the global optimum small. A nearly affine worker can be valuable for canceling an offset at the optimum even when it contributes little curvature. This result prices both effects for stochastic optimization of the **uniform average**.

## Model and result

Let $F=n^{-1}\sum_i f_i$ on $\mathbb R^d$, for arbitrary finite $d$. Each $f_i$ is differentiable, convex, and has gradient Lipschitz constant at most $L_i$. Write $S=\arg\min F$. For some $z\in S$ within $R$ of $x_0$ suppose
$$
 F(x)-F(z)\ge \tfrac\mu2\operatorname{dist}(x,S)^2,\qquad
 n^{-1}\sum_i\|\nabla f_i(z)\|^2\le\zeta^2.
$$
Only the average needs quadratic growth. Local minimizers may differ or fail to exist. The heterogeneity bound concerns a single solution, allowing disagreement that grows without bound away from it. Woodworth, Patel and Srebro use the same quantity in their analysis of local and minibatch SGD. For example, data shards in regularized generalized linear regression can have different feature covariances and different mean scores at a pooled solution. A public upper bound on that score dispersion is additional information relative to unrestricted heterogeneous-data models. [Woodworth et al., NeurIPS 2020, Eq. (4)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/45713f6ff2041d3fdfae927b82488db8-Paper.pdf) · [Patel et al., COLT 2024, Assumption 1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/patel24a/patel24a.pdf)

Worker $i$ returns one unbiased gradient of $f_i(x)$ in $h_i>0$ time, with centered second moment at most $\sigma^2$. Each jointly measurable oracle uses fresh IID draws from its own law. The workers' streams are independent under the abstract joint law. Dedicated directed star links operate concurrently, with latency $\ell$ and time $b$ per bit. A worker can have one pending computation, and a directed link can have one pending transmission. Local arithmetic on public parameters is free. The algorithm knows the bounds, times, initial point, and requested RMS accuracy $\varepsilon>0$. It sees gradients through completed queries. The solution, worker offsets, and realized curvature are private. [Lean setup](../Setups/DistributedOptimumHeterogeneity.lean) · [Event and network model](../TemplateDistributed.lean)

Let $P=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/n$ center a worker vector. With $\mathcal A=\{0\le a_i\le L_i,\ \sum_i a_i\ge n\mu\}$ choose
$$
 w=\arg\min_{v\ge0}\ V(v),\qquad V(v)=\sum_i h_i v_i^2,\qquad
 \min_{a\in\mathcal A} v^\top a\ge1,\quad 64\sqrt n\,\zeta\|Pv\|_2\le\varepsilon .
$$
This finite convex problem is feasible since $v_i=1/(n\mu)$ works. Positive $h_i$ give coercivity and a unique minimizer. The inner constraint can be checked by filling the smallest-weight coordinates to their caps until total mass $n\mu$ is reached. The resulting support equals $\max_j[n\mu v_j-\sum_i L_i(v_j-v_i)_+]$ for $v\ge0$. The budget uses this finite maximum, so the outer minimization is its only implicit tuning choice. This applies also to $L_i=0$. [Design](../Algorithms/OptimumHeterogeneityDesign.lean) · [Attainment and feasibility](../Proofs/OptimumHeterogeneityDesign.lean) · [Exact finite support formula](../Proofs/OptimumHeterogeneitySupport.lean)

Define $C(v)=2(\sum_i h_i^{-1})\max_i h_i w_i v_i$, $\kappa=C(L)\ge1$ and $G=C(\mathbf1)(\sigma+\sqrt n\,\zeta)/\kappa$. The coordinator returns the iterate after a **predetermined number of updates**. It satisfies $\mathbb E\operatorname{dist}(x_{\rm out},S)^2\le\varepsilon^2$ by the deadline
$$
 O\!\left(
 \frac{\sigma^2 V(w)}{\varepsilon^2}+
 \kappa\left[1+\log(1+R^2/\varepsilon^2)\right]
 \left[\max_i h_i+\ell+b\left(64+d\log\!\left(2+d+\kappa+
 \frac{(R+G)^2}{\varepsilon^2}\right)\right)\right]\right).
$$
The Lean budget gives absolute constants and uses integer bit length. The first term prices stochastic observations. The second includes the slowest worker's initial batch, every upward and downward transmission, and the final scheduled batch. The guarantee includes measurability, integrability, and finite events for every deadline up to the stated time. ## Why the design transfers to nonlinear optimization

At a nearest solution $y$ define $D_i=f_i(x)-f_i(y)-\langle\nabla f_i(y),x-y\rangle$. At positive distance the vector $2D_i/\|x-y\|^2$ belongs to $\mathcal A$ by convexity, smoothness, stationarity of the average, and quadratic growth. The vector may change with $x$ and its direction. Between two minimizers the nonnegative local Bregman remainders sum to zero. Cocoercivity then gives $\nabla f_i(y)=\nabla f_i(z)$ **for every worker**. Since their sum is zero, the residual weighted gradient at any solution has norm at most $\sqrt n\,\zeta\|Pw\|\le\varepsilon/64$. This explains the centered constraint and couples the curvature and offset certificates at the same solution. In scalar quadratic instances the offset bound can be attained by balanced linear terms proportional to $Pw$. [Growth and bias lemmas](../Proofs/OptimumHeterogeneityGrowth.lean) · [Corrected gradient step](../Proofs/OptimumHeterogeneityDrift.lean)

The algorithm takes $H$ constant steps followed by $H$ decreasing steps, with $H$ of order $\kappa[1+\log(1+R^2/\varepsilon^2)]$. Each round takes $\lceil t/h_i\rceil$ samples at worker $i$, with $t=\max\{\max_i h_i,4096\sigma^2 V(w)/(H\varepsilon^2)\}$. A worker rescales by its design weight and batch fraction, averages, clips to a public radius depending on the iterate, and sends finite grid coordinates. The coordinator averages the messages and broadcasts a quantized update. Independent centered batch errors contribute order $\eta_k^2\sigma^2 V(w)/t$ per step. The deterministic bias contributes order $\eta_k\varepsilon^2$ to squared distance and is absorbed by contraction down to the target. The clipping and rounding proof controls the second moment about the original solution throughout both phases. A whole extra batch is charged to deliver the selected iterate, with $64$ clock bits charged per packet. ## Statistical benchmark at a fixed time

The following calculation explains why optimizing these two constraints matters. It also bounds what a protocol that learns worker offsets or uses past gradients can gain. The formal export is the distributed upper bound.

Fix $T,\sigma>0$, put $s=\sigma^2/T$, $c=n\zeta^2$, $M=s\operatorname{diag}(h)+cP$ and $Q=\min_{a\in\mathcal A}a^\top M^{-1}a$. For a minimizer $a^*$ let $u=M^{-1}a^*$. Its entries are nonnegative since
$$
 m=\frac{\sum_i a_i^*/(s h_i+c)}{\sum_i s h_i/(s h_i+c)},\qquad
 u_i=\frac{a_i^*+cm}{s h_i+c},\qquad m=\frac1n\sum_i u_i .
$$
The directional derivative toward any $a\in\mathcal A$ gives $u^\top a\ge Q>0$. Thus $v=u/Q$ has normalized support and $sV(v)+c\|Pv\|^2=1/Q$. Conversely, every normalized $v$ has $v^\top a^*\ge1$, and matrix Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its energy below by $1/Q$. At $\varepsilon=C_0/\sqrt Q$, $C_0^2\ge49152$, this witness is feasible and the actual optimizer's statistical time is at most $T/2$. If its startup and communication term at **this accuracy and this optimizer** also fits $T/2$, the theorem gives squared risk at most $C_0^2/Q$ by $T$.

For an information lower benchmark take $d\ge1$ and the allowed Borel sample space $\Omega=\mathbb R$. In direction $e$ of unit length let $t=\langle e,x-x_0\rangle$, $r=\min(R,1/(4\sqrt Q))$, $\theta=\pm r$, and
$$
 f_i^\theta(x)=a_i^*(t-\theta)^2/2+\theta c(Pu)_i(t-\theta).
$$
The solutions have $t=\theta$. The curvature sum gives global growth and $r^2c^2\|Pu\|^2\le c$ gives the required optimum offsets. A reply has scalar component $a_i^* t+Z_i$, $Z_i\sim\mathcal N(-\theta s h_i u_i,\sigma^2)$, and zero orthogonal component. Its expectation is the local gradient because $Mu=a^*$. Grant a coordinator all $\lfloor T/h_i\rfloor$ potential independent intercepts from worker $i$ and the slopes for free. These finite observations and the independent algorithm seed suffice to simulate any completed query, even with adaptive points, stopping and offset estimation. Aborting a job reveals no sample. Their KL divergence is at most $2r^2sV(u)\le 2r^2 Q\le1/8$. Pinsker's inequality therefore gives equal-prior sign error at least $3/8$. On a sign error squared distance is at least $r^2$. This proves worst-case risk at least $(3/8)\min(R^2,1/(16Q))$ in this subclass. The Gaussian separation argument is classical. The shared worker profile for this experiment and the nonlinear upper bound is the point of the comparison. [Goldenshluger, Juditsky and Nemirovski, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.6765)

These are same-clock risk comparisons. A constant change in target accuracy can change the optimal design and hence its time by an unbounded factor across profiles. The benchmark supplies no lower bound on deterministic startup, bit precision, or latency. It does not apply to noiseless calls or a fixed sample space incapable of supporting the chosen experiment.

## A positive-heterogeneity regime with a logarithmic gain

Take $M_0=\lfloor N^{3/4}\rfloor$, $m=\lceil\log N\rceil$, and $\delta=N^{-10}$. There are $N$ workers each of types $(h,L)=(1,1),(1,\delta)$. For each $j=1,\ldots,M_0$ include one $(j,1)$ worker and $m$ workers of type $(j,\delta)$. Set $n\mu=M_0$, $R=\sigma=\ell=b=1$, $d\ge2$ fixed, $\varepsilon=N^{-2}$ and $\zeta=\varepsilon N^{-3/16}$. The ratio $\zeta/(\mu\varepsilon)$ grows as $N^{1/16}$.

For $t=N^{7/10}$ give both cheap groups unnormalized weight $1/t$, the $M_0$ useful workers $\min(1/t,1/j)$, and their low-curvature partners zero. Its worst curvature signal $S_w=\min_{a\in\mathcal A} w^\top a$ is $\Theta(\log N)$. Filling the useful workers realizes the upper estimate. Shifting curvature mass from them to the cheap strong group cannot decrease signal, and the total capacity of the small-cap workers is at most $(N+mM_0)\delta$. Also $V(w)=\Theta(\log N)$ by the harmonic sum over $j>t$. Centering costs at most the distance to the constant vector $1/t$, so $\sqrt n(\zeta/\varepsilon)\|Pw\|=O(N^{1/2-3/16+3/8-7/10}\sqrt{\log N})=o(1)$. Dividing by $S_w$ gives a feasible witness for large $N$ and $V_{\rm opt}=O(1/\log N)$.

* Every speed-only weighting $v(j)$ satisfies $S_v\le\sum_j v(j)$ and $V(v)\ge(m+1)\sum_j jv(j)^2$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $S_v^2/V(v)=O(1)$. A uniform subset containing $k$ useful workers has signal at most $k$ and variance price at least $k(k+1)/2$ for unit weights.
* Even retuning the interpolation cap and shrinking toward uniform weights leaves a gap. Write $v_i=\beta+\alpha\min(c,L_i/h_i)$, $\alpha,\beta,c\ge0$. For $c\le1/M_0$ the useful workers have identical weight. Otherwise the two cheap groups differ by at least $(1-o(1))a$, $a=\alpha\min(c,1)$. Their centered bias divided by $\varepsilon$ is at least a constant times $N^{13/16}a$ while signal is at most $M_0(\beta+a)$. Feasibility after normalization requires $\beta+a\gtrsim N^{1/16}a$, giving $V(v)\gtrsim\beta^2M_0^2$ and again $S_v^2/V(v)=O(1)$. These comparisons concern the specified gradient-weight certificates.
* The actual optimizer satisfies $\max h_i w_i\le\sqrt{(\max h_i)V_{\rm opt}}$. Hence $C(\mathbf1),\kappa=O(N^{11/8})$, and the width is $O(\log N)$. Startup and communication cost $O(N^{17/8}\log N+N^{11/8}\log^2 N)$. Total time is $O(N^4/\log N)$, compared with an $\Omega(N^4)$ statistical term for the certificates above. Conversely the admissible curvature that fills precisely the useful workers gives $Q\le (T/\sigma^2)\sum_{j\le M_0}1/j$ because $M\succeq s\operatorname{diag}(h)$. At a sufficiently small constant times $N^4/\log N$, the preceding two-instance bound exceeds $\varepsilon^2$. The same-target elapsed-time order is therefore sharp on this family in the Gaussian-capable model.

Genuinely nonlinear instances occur on the same profiles. With $y=x-z$, assign isotropic quadratics $k_i\|y\|^2/2$ to enough strong workers to get $\sum k_i\ge M_0$, taking $k_i\le L_i/2$. Add scaled $\log\cosh(p^\top y)+\log\cosh(q^\top y)$ with nonorthogonal unit vectors $p,q$ and coefficients at most $L_i/4$. Balanced linear terms $b_i^\top y$ with $\sum b_i=0$, $\sum\|b_i\|^2\le n\zeta^2$ preserve the pooled solution and allow different local minimizers. One can use opposite offsets on two identical strongly convex workers. A further worker with only a positive log-cosh loss has bounded gradient and unbounded objective gap, hence no positive global PL constant. The aggregate still has quadratic growth, and the gradient directions interact and change with the point.

## Relation to earlier results and limitations

Tyurin's Theorem 3.2 gives a slow-worker obstruction under weak interpolation, and Theorem 3.8 obtains a harmonic-time rate using both equal local minimizer sets and local PL inequalities. The weak-interpolation theorem in this library uses only aggregate growth but still requires a common minimizer. Setting $\zeta=0$ here recovers its normalized curvature design, with the accuracy convention changed from mean square to RMS. For positive $\zeta$ the previous proof's local gaps need not even be nonnegative. Subtracting the optimum gradients would restore interpolation but requires unknown worker-specific vectors. Uniform batching remains valid with $V=\sum_i h_i/(n\mu)^2$. The joint constraint selects when deliberate bias buys throughput and when a cheap, weakly curved worker should be retained to cancel offsets. For fixed positive $\zeta$ and a fixed worker profile the centered weights must approach zero dispersion as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The gain above concerns a joint parameter regime. [Tyurin, September 2026 preprint, Assumptions 3.1, 3.3, 3.4 and Theorems 3.2, 3.7, 3.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17483v1) · [Earlier Lean result](DistributedWeightedInterpolation.md)

Rescaled ASGD corrects for unequal arrival rates in optimizing a uniform objective. Inkheart's heterogeneous-time weights for a shared gradient solve a different variance problem. SCAFFOLD learns client drift corrections, charging local queries and communication rounds. Patel et al.'s Theorem 11 concerns intermittent communication with equal local budgets and objective error. None of those cost formulas supplies the centered-bias, smoothness-cap profile for this solution-distance target. A recent spectral-filter analysis also treats local-proximal residuals for nonlinear uniformly locally strongly convex losses. Here curvature can vanish at individual workers, and each stochastic gradient and transmitted bit is priced. [Rescaled ASGD, Sections 2–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.13434v1) · [Inkheart/M4, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07795v2) · [SCAFFOLD, ICML 2020, Theorem III](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/karimireddy20a/karimireddy20a.pdf) · [Patel et al., Theorem 11](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/patel24a/patel24a.pdf) · [Local-proximal analysis, November 2026 preprint, Theorem 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.24057v1)

Deterministic or communication-dominated deadlines can be much larger than the statistical term. Every worker participates, including a very slow one with zero design weight. No optimality claim covers that startup choice, variable runtime, shared link contention, or the full cost of optimizing the public weights on hardware.
