# Growing-depth acceleration with adversarial relative gradient error

[Main theorem](RecursiveRelativeErrorConvex.lean), [setup](../Setups/RecursiveRelativeErrorSmoothConvex.lean), [algorithm](../Algorithms/RecursiveEnvelope.lean), [recursive proof](../Proofs/RecursiveEnvelopeConvergence.lean), and [budget proof](../Proofs/RecursiveEnvelopeBudget.lean).

## Guarantee and scope

Let f be convex and differentiable on a real Hilbert space, with L-Lipschitz gradient, L>0, and a minimizer x* satisfying ‖x₀−x*‖≤R. Each physical gradient query at x returns an arbitrary vector a satisfying

‖a−∇f(x)‖≤α‖∇f(x)‖.

Errors may change with the query index and past execution; they need not be unbiased, independent, conservative, or generated by a fixed perturbation of f. No function-value oracle, proximal oracle, exact gradient, strong convexity, bounded sublevel set, or bound on iterates is supplied. The algorithm knows L, R, ε, and the certified error bound α. It does not know x* or f*. The specification reuses the existing [relative-error model](../Setups/RelativeErrorSmoothConvex.lean) unchanged and adds only the numerical regime below. It does not restrict the admissible algorithm family.

Write T=LR²/ε, with R≥0 and ε>0. Assume

α≤exp(−8(1+√log(2+T)))/100.

The deterministic recursive algorithm returns an original-objective point x with f(x)−f(x*)≤ε for every admissible sequence of oracle seeds, within the explicit public budget

B=⌈4√(2+T) exp(32(1+√log(2+T)) log(32+log(2+T)))⌉.

The Lean theorem expresses the resulting success almost surely under the framework's execution law; its supporting execution lemma proves the inequality for every seed sequence. Thus no sampling assumption is needed. The guarantee includes T=0 and zero error. There is no dimension factor outside L and R; the proof applies in arbitrary finite dimension and, more generally, complete real Hilbert spaces.

As T grows, the displayed bound is T^{1/2+o(1)}, while the permitted relative error is exp(−O(√log T)), larger than every fixed negative power of T. This is a restricted-regime guarantee, not a result for fixed positive α as ε→0. It leaves a subpolynomial overhead over exact-gradient acceleration and does not establish optimality.

## What changes relative to prior guarantees

All comparisons below use original-objective error ε, distance bound R, and count physical gradient-oracle calls. Put T=LR²/ε. Constants and logarithms matter for finite comparisons.

At the public threshold α(T), the α⁴T term in the two-level bound is T^{1−o(1)}, whereas the present bound is T^{1/2+o(1)}. The α⁻² term is only T^{o(1)}. This establishes a strict asymptotic improvement between these upper bounds in the stated regime, not a lower bound on the two-level method or all other methods. It does not imply superiority when α is much smaller, where ordinary acceleration bounds are preferable.

The constants make the crossover enormous. For example, set T=4^{1024²}−2 and α equal to the public threshold. The base-2 logarithm of the displayed real budget before rounding is approximately 1,839,043, while that of α⁴T is approximately 2,041,418. Rounding changes the first number by less than one. This illustrative comparison already ignores the logarithmic factor and unspecified constant in the two-level O-bound; it is not an empirical runtime comparison. The much tighter internal count also has enormous constants. No practical speedup is claimed.

The journal version of Kornilov et al. is [JOTA 207, article 62 (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10957-025-02809-y). Its publication status was verified, but its full final theorem text was inaccessible; the comparison above is specifically to arXiv v1, not an assertion that the journal theorem is unchanged. The latest accessible arXiv version was checked. Vasin et al. [arXiv:2503.06628v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06628v3), December 9, 2025, provides additional strongly convex relative-error analysis; it does not directly supply the stated convex growing-depth guarantee.

The apparent conflict with [Vasin–Gasnikov, Theorem 10.2 and Appendix G](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12376v1#S10) deserves explicit attention. Our reading of the intended convex barrier, using Appendix G.4’s logarithm base, would preclude the improvement above. In Appendix G.3, however, the proof embeds an absolute-gradient-error oracle into a general (δ,L,μ)-oracle and invokes Theorem G.2’s lower bound for the latter class. That one-way embedding does not supply a realizable hard instance in the smaller gradient-error class. An additional hard-instance construction appears necessary. This is our objection to the derivation, not a settled correction agreed with its authors. We therefore do not use that asserted barrier to establish optimality or to reject the present construction.

## Algorithm and proof mechanism

Choose d=⌈√(log(2+T)/log 4)⌉, m=2048d2^d, Lᵢ=L/4^{di}, and βᵢ=16^i/(100·64^d). The public error restriction implies α≤β₀. These are tuning choices, not assumptions about the objective.

Define f₀=f for analysis. For i≥1, let fᵢ be the Moreau envelope of fᵢ₋₁ with regularization coefficient μᵢ=Lᵢ. Its gradient is μᵢ(y−pᵢ(y)), where pᵢ(y) minimizes fᵢ₋₁(x)+μᵢ‖x−y‖²/2. The envelope is convex and μᵢ-smooth, and shares the original minimum value. Exact pᵢ, fᵢ, and their gradients are proof objects only.

To simulate an oracle for fᵢ at y, run m steps of the existing robust strongly convex accelerated solver on that regularized problem, starting at y. Each requested gradient of fᵢ₋₁ is itself simulated recursively; at level zero it is one physical oracle query. Add the known vector μᵢ(x−y), and return μᵢ(y−x⁺). The inner solver's momentum is the explicit `InexactGradient.momentum` with smoothness Lᵢ₋₁+μᵢ, strong-convexity coefficient μᵢ, and relative parameter 2βᵢ₋₁. There is no accuracy test involving unknown quantities.

The crucial simulation estimate is uniform over the center y and every past physical query. Writing p=pᵢ(y), h(x)=fᵢ₋₁(x)+μᵢ‖x−y‖²/2, an oracle error bounded by β‖∇fᵢ₋₁(x)‖ obeys

‖error‖≤2β‖∇h(x)‖+βμᵢ‖y−p‖.

The established mixed-error accelerated potential gives, after m lower-level oracle calls,

h(x⁺)−h(p)≤(16β)² μᵢ‖y−p‖²/2.

The proof bounds the transient by 32β²μᵢ‖y−p‖² and the residual floor by 96β²μᵢ‖y−p‖². It never divides by ‖y−p‖, so the zero-distance case is included. Strong convexity turns this into a pure-relative βᵢ=16βᵢ₋₁ error bound for the simulated envelope gradient. The regularized condition number is 1+4^d, and the selected βᵢ ensure that m suffices at every level. Importantly, recursion is on pure-relative envelope oracles, not an unjustified invocation of a pure-relative theorem on mixed-error subproblems.

The top envelope already satisfies f_d(x₀)−f*≤L_dR²/2≤ε/2. Return to the original objective by approximately evaluating the level-d prox at x₀, then the level-(d−1) prox at the returned point, and so on. Each recovery step satisfies

fᵢ₋₁(x⁺)−f*≤(1+βᵢ²)(fᵢ(y)−f*).

The product of recovery factors is at most two. This proves accuracy of the final point for f, rather than merely for a smoothed objective.

One level-i prox consumes m^i physical calls. The entire recovery consumes exactly Σᵢ₌₁ᵈm^i≤2m^d. The fixed-budget framework may pad the completed plan with queries whose replies are ignored; the displayed B bounds the entire execution, including padding. The proof of the simplified budget retains d² log 2≤log(2+T)/2+O(√log(2+T)); losing that leading coefficient would lose the near-accelerated rate. Indexed plan evaluation shifts the physical call counter through every nested block and recovery step, so no oracle randomness or adversarial history is reset for free.

## Standard reductions, novelty, and limitations

Moreau envelopes, inexact proximal acceleration, the robust inner solver, and ordinary recursive composition are established ingredients. [Catalyst, JMLR 18(212), 2018](https://jmlr.org/papers/v18/17-748.html) already develops accelerated outer proximal schemes and approximate-envelope interpretations. Its standard convergence theorems assume an inner method with linear convergence to arbitrarily small error; the convex relative stopping criterion C2 decreases with the outer iteration. Adding a quadratic regularizer to the present oracle creates an irreducible absolute component. Therefore substituting this oracle into those theorems without proving the above finite-accuracy simulation does not establish the current result. Nor does averaging repeated adversarial replies improve their error bound; regularization does not make that bound relative to the regularized gradient.

The new quantitative claim is that depth can grow with target accuracy while controlling geometric noise amplification, multiplicative query costs, and recovery to f, giving the stated subpolynomial noise threshold and near-accelerated exponent. The earlier two-level note anticipated nesting but did not prove this growing-depth tradeoff. The standard nature of the ingredients and the enormous crossover are the strongest reasons to question significance; neither Lean verification nor a new setup name resolves that assessment.

The setup includes meaningful nonlinear coupled objectives, for example f(x)=Σⱼwⱼ log cosh(⟨aⱼ,x−x*⟩), wⱼ>0, with nonzero A and nonorthogonal rows aⱼ. One may take L=‖Aᵀdiag(w)A‖; x* is a minimizer, and global strong convexity need not hold. Relative error is a standard certified-oracle model, useful when an approximate gradient computation or norm-relative quantization supplies a deterministic relative certificate. The very small error demanded here requires high fidelity; it should not be confused with cheap aggressive compression. No generic floating-point computation is asserted to satisfy this certificate automatically. Interacting coordinates permit adversarial transverse errors absent in scalar rescaling diagnostics.

Targeted searches through October 24, 2026 examined the publicly available ICML 2026/2025, ICLR 2026/2025, and NeurIPS 2026/2025 editions, roughly two years of arXiv submissions and revisions, and older cited foundations. Conference status was checked through official proceedings or OpenReview. Relevant older work includes [Hallak–Levy, ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/hallak24a.html), which studies constrained relative-error methods, and [Vasin et al., arXiv:2102.02921v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02921v3), whose absolute-error restarted analysis is relevant to inner-solver robustness. Error-feedback compression and multilevel methods with tunable unbiased approximation hierarchies give different information to the algorithm; they are not interchangeable with fixed adversarial relative error. The inaccessible final JOTA text remains a literature-access limitation, not evidence of novelty.

Fixed positive relative error, sharp constants, a matching lower bound, practical usefulness, constraints, composite terms, and added absolute noise remain outside this theorem. No claimed lower bound for a larger inexact-oracle class is transferred to this smaller relative-gradient class. The result does not narrow the objective class to quadratics, separability, fixed dimension, or a chosen update family.
