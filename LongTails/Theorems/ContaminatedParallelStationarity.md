# High-confidence distributed stationarity with contaminated gradient observations

Suppose each worker measures a different local gradient. An occasional faulty observation can have arbitrarily large magnitude or even lack a first moment. The clean gradients themselves can be large and cancel in the network average. The theorem gives a deterministic deadline by which the workers return a point with small **clean aggregate gradient**, with any prescribed failure probability. For a range of confidence levels, the deadline has the same order as at constant confidence. ## Guarantee

Let $F=n^{-1}\sum_i f_i$ on $\mathbb R^d$. Each $f_i$ is differentiable, $\nabla F$ is $L$-Lipschitz and $F(x_0)-\inf F\le\Delta$. Each observation at worker $i$ uses an independent sample with law $(1-\eta_i)\mu_i+\eta_i\nu_i$. Applied to a clean sample from $\mu_i$, the jointly measurable oracle has mean $\nabla f_i(x)$ and centered second moment at most $\sigma_i^2$ at every query $x$. The law $\nu_i$ is arbitrary. Streams are IID within each worker and independent between workers. Workers execute the protocol honestly. Huber's mixture supplies the observation model, and the clean smoothness, gap and gradient assumptions are those of heterogeneous stochastic first-order optimization with worker-specific noise scales. [Huber, §1](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703732) · [Tyurin, §§1 and 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20735v1)

The theorem takes $L,\varepsilon,h_i>0$, $0<\delta<1$, $0\le\eta_i\le1/16$ and $\varepsilon\ge 2^{18}n^{-1}\sum_i\sigma_i\sqrt{\eta_i}$. The public input includes these noise and contamination bounds, the time parameters, $x_0$ and $\Delta$. The algorithm queries local observations only. For $K=1+\lceil 2^{19}L\Delta/\varepsilon^2\rceil$, $q=4+\lceil\log(1/\delta)\rceil$, $h_{\max}=\max_i h_i$, and $V=\sum_i h_i\sigma_i^2/(n^2\varepsilon^2)$, the returned point $X$ satisfies $\Pr(\|\nabla F(X)\|>\varepsilon)\le\delta$ within elapsed time

$$
O\!\left((K+q\min\{K,q\})(h_{\max}+V)
+K\left[(1+\log n)h_{\max}+\ell+
 b\left(64+\lceil d/n\rceil\log(2+n(d+1))\right)\right]\right).
$$

Worker $i$ processes one batch at a time and takes $m h_i$ for $m$ observations. Each ordered pair of workers, including self-delivery, has an independent FIFO link. Its service time for $B$ payload bits is $\ell+b(B+64)$. Separate links and opposite directions can operate concurrently. Local arithmetic and storage carry zero elapsed-time cost. The proof pays for every observation and both exchange phases, establishes finite execution at every deadline up to the budget, and identifies the actual output and its joint law under the distributed template. Smaller port counts, shared links and Byzantine workers change this model. [Schedule certificate](../Proofs/ContaminatedParallelSchedule.lean) · [Termination](../Proofs/ContaminatedParallelExecution.lean)

An example in arbitrary dimension is $F(x)=\sum_r a_r(1-\cos(u_r^\top x))$ for $a_r>0$ and dense, nonorthogonal $u_r$. Add $\sin((v^\top x)^3)$ to one local objective and subtract it from another. The average has globally Lipschitz gradient while the local gradient disagreement is unbounded. An honest worker may add clean centered finite-variance noise or, on an independent device fault, emit an arbitrary reading. Its public fault rate may differ from that of every other device. This example also explains why clipping all observed local gradients toward zero could change the aggregate direction.

## Why the contamination scale appears

There is a simple identifiability obstruction even with exact real messages and unlimited samples. In one coordinate, put $p_i=\eta_i/(1-\eta_i)$ and $a_i=\sigma_i/\sqrt{p_i(1-p_i)}$ for $\eta_i>0$. Consider two clean oracles at $x$ that return $Lx$ with probability $1-p_i$ and $Lx\pm a_i$ otherwise. At a fault the oracle returns the opposite exceptional value. Both mixtures have identical laws at every query. The clean means differ by $2\sigma_i\sqrt{\eta_i/(1-2\eta_i)}$. Workers with $\eta_i=0$ can be identical in both instances. These are gradients of shifted quadratics with a common initial gap bound. The two aggregate stationarity regions are disjoint for $\varepsilon<n^{-1}\sum_i\sigma_i\sqrt{\eta_i/(1-2\eta_i)}$. Thus confidence greater than $1/2$ on both instances requires a positive accuracy floor of this order. The constant $2^{18}$ and the cap $1/16$ in the upper bound come from the estimates in this proof. The example concerns identifiability and gives no elapsed-time lower bound.

## Estimation, training and selection

Write $s=\varepsilon/512$. At a query of precision $r$, each worker uses $O(1+\log n+r)$ pilot samples to select a center by counting neighboring observations. With high probability all centers lie within $12\sigma_i$ of their respective clean means. The worker clips fresh observations about its own center at the smaller of $8\sigma_i/\sqrt{\eta_i}$ and $nsm_i/(2048r)$, omitting the first cap for $\eta_i=0$. The fresh counts $m_i$ are proportional, up to rounding and universal constants, to $r(1+\sum_j h_j\sigma_j^2/(n^2s^2h_i))$. The first cap controls the total displacement due to outliers. The second cap bounds each centered increment in the *sum* of the clipped sample averages. A Hilbert-space Bernstein estimate gives aggregate error at most $s$ with conditional failure probability at most $2e^{-r}$, including pilot failures. This holds at every adaptive query using only fresh independent samples. A failed pilot can still have unbounded error. [Mixture and clipping estimates](../Proofs/DistributedContaminationMoments.lean) · [Adaptive conditional bound](../Proofs/ContaminatedParallelFailures.lean)

Training traverses a complete four-ary tree in preorder with $K_0=4^{\lceil\log_4(\max\{1,\lceil 2L\Delta/s^2\rceil\})\rceil}=\Theta(K)$ leaves. Each leaf takes a normalized descent step of length at most $s/L$ and saves its query point. Internal nodes refresh the coding reference for their descendants. Workers round their local vectors relative to the same reference and use reduce-scatter and allgather to sum modular residues. Only the aggregate must fit the decoding radius. Local gradients may be arbitrarily far apart. This multiscale communication scheme originates in the repository's clean-oracle result. [FiniteBitLeon](FiniteBitLeon.md)

The confidence allocation accounts for how long an erroneous reference can affect later steps. A node covering $u=4^j$ leaves can cause $O(u^2s)$ cumulative leaf error, from $u$ affected steps and $O(us)$ error per step. Set $w=\min(1,u^2/K_0)$ and $r=8(1+j)+2\lceil qw\rceil$. The conditional exponential moment of the sum of $w$ over failed nodes gives $\Pr(\sum w\ge1)\le e^{-q}$. Below this threshold every failed node has $u^2<K_0$, so its uncapped contribution is charged in full. The deterministic tree estimate gives total leaf estimation error at most $17K_0s$, and smooth descent bounds the sum of the **true** leaf gradient norms by $35K_0s$. The training precision sums to $O(K_0+q\sqrt{K_0})$. These bounds allow failed nodes and require an error probability estimate only, even at a node whose ancestors failed. [Tree probability](../Proofs/ContaminatedTrainingProbability.lean) · [Tree error and descent](../Proofs/ContaminatedTrainingDescent.lean) · [True gradient sum](../Proofs/ContaminatedTrainingHistory.lean)

At least three quarters of the stored points then have true gradient norm at most $\varepsilon/2$. Sample $q$ leaf indices independently and uniformly using the shared seed, or take all leaves when $q\ge K_0$. Sort the distinct indices and recompute each gradient at precision $2q$ with fresh samples. The smallest norm among the **decoded validation estimates** selects the output. For two successive indices separated by $g$ saved steps, the true gradient changes by at most $gs$. The next coding width therefore uses level $\lceil\log_4(1+g)\rceil$. The gaps telescope and their levels sum to $O(K_0)$ for every seed. Training failure, missing every suitable leaf, and any validation failure have total probability at most $2e^{-q}+2qe^{-2q}\le\delta$. Validation accounts for $O(q\min(K,q))$ computation units and leaves the network term of order $K$. The method stores all leaf points. ## Closest guarantees and regimes of improvement

Tyurin's Leon SGD Theorem 1.7 gives heterogeneous computation and coordinate-AllReduce time with unbiased gradients of bounded variance. Its target is expected squared norm. At norm accuracy $\varepsilon$, the corresponding squared target is $\varepsilon^2$. FiniteBitLeon proves that target on the complete binary network, including arbitrary cancellation and the amortized range bound. Neither clean-oracle theorem covers the mixture above. An arbitrarily biased observation with an infinite first moment cannot be inserted in its stochastic descent or second-moment tracking argument. Conversely, the tunable probability guarantee here controls the probability of exceeding a prescribed tolerance. The clean second-moment result gives more direct control of the magnitude of errors on failed runs. Tyurin also treats broader network topologies. [Tyurin, Theorems 1.7 and 5.2, arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20735v1) · [FiniteBitLeon theorem](FiniteBitLeon.lean)

Hübler, Fatkhullin and He give high-probability bounds on weighted average true gradient norms of centralized normalized stochastic gradient iterates under moment assumptions. Their directional descent estimate motivates using bounded-length steps. Applying it to a distributed corrupted run needs an estimator, a communication analysis when an anchor fails, and a way of selecting a single output. Ghadimi and Lan's two-phase randomized stochastic gradient method restarts independent trajectories and validates their candidates. Its Theorem 2.4 includes the validation observations. A modern variant can replace each validation mean by a robust high-confidence estimate. Starting even with a constant-confidence version of the present estimator and protocol, this serial restart construction spends $O(Kq)$ training units, followed by candidate testing. Workers have one computation server each and every trajectory requires all local means. [Hübler et al., Theorem 4 and Appendix C.2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.13849v3) · [Ghadimi and Lan, Theorem 2.4 and equations (2.23)–(2.27)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.5549)

Another direct construction uses the same local estimate at precision $O(q+\log K)$ on **every** training node and takes a union bound. Its precision sum is $O(K(q+\log K))$. The hierarchy above spends $O(K)$ training and validation units together for $q\le\sqrt K$. For example, with fixed $n,d$, positive sample costs and $q=\Theta(K^{1/4})$, the new deadline has order $K(h_{\max}+V+\ell+b(64+\lceil d/n\rceil\log(2+n(d+1))))$. In this regime serial restarts spend a factor of order $K^{1/4}$ more training time, and the all-nodes union bound incurs that factor in its precision-dependent computation term. A union-bound protocol using the same one-vector local estimator can already have $O(K)$ network time. The gain against it matters when sampling is a material part of the deadline.

There is also a stronger comparison in a real-vector network. With constant-precision robust directions one can localize the normalized descent argument by stopping when the objective gap exceeds a threshold of order $\Delta+\varepsilon^2 q^2/L$. The resulting directional-loss bound can already support $O(K)$ training steps when $q\le\sqrt K$. A direct binary implementation that always covers the deterministic gradient range $O(K\varepsilon)$ spends an additional $O(\log K)$ bits per coordinate. This overhead can be negligible if sampling dominates, or if $\log(n(d+1))$ already dominates $\log K$. The substantive guarantee here combines the confidence regime with a finite-bit network term free of that range logarithm, even with unlimited local disagreement. No lower bound excludes alternative adaptive encoders.

### How much can robust batching already do?

Independent high-confidence local estimates followed by averaging give a sampling term proportional to $(\sum_i\sigma_i\sqrt{h_i})^2/(n^2\varepsilon^2)$ before confidence factors. To see the difference, a wall-clock batch duration $T$ gives worker $i$ roughly $T/h_i$ samples. Adding individual error radii gives $\sum_i\sigma_i\sqrt{h_i}/(n\sqrt{T})$. Pooling clean fluctuations in quadrature gives $\sqrt{\sum_i h_i\sigma_i^2}/(n\sqrt{T})$. The difference is substantial for many comparably noisy workers and disappears when one worker dominates. Even a median of ordinary pooled blocks works when the probability that *any* retained observation in one block is corrupt is small.

An alternative obtains both the pooled variance and the contamination floor with multiple aggregate estimates. Condition on good local pilot centers and put $A=\sum_i\sigma_i\sqrt{\eta_i}$. From $M_i$ available draws retain each independently with probability $b_i/M_i$, where $b_i=\min(M_i,c\sigma_i/(A\sqrt{\eta_i}))$ and $c$ is a small positive constant. Use the full allocation for a clean worker and its pilot alone for a worker of zero clean variance. If $A=0$ these conventions suffice. Correct each pilot by the sum of retained residuals divided by $b_i$. In a coupling with clean draws this correction is unbiased, its variance is $O(\sigma_i^2/b_i)$, and $\sum_i\eta_i b_i\le c$. Also $\sum_i\sigma_i^2/b_i\le\sum_i\sigma_i^2/M_i+A^2/c$ on the positive-variance workers. A median of independent pooled blocks therefore gives the same two statistical scales. It requires multiple vector aggregates at each precision increase. Local clipping produces one aggregate per node, including during validation. The pooled statistical scale alone does not distinguish these approaches. Chaudhuri, Li and Courtade study unequal corruption probabilities for observations with a common clean distribution and allow outliers to depend jointly on the clean data and corruptions. Aliakbarpour et al. also treat corrupt users and heterogeneous batches, with a bounded shift from a common target. Here the equally weighted gradient being estimated can average arbitrarily different means. [Chaudhuri et al., Definition 1 and Theorems 1 and 4, NeurIPS 2025](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/99fc8bc48b917c301a80cb74d91c0c06-Paper-Conference.pdf) · [Aliakbarpour et al., Problem 2 and Theorem 1.3, arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.20698v2)

### Limits of the confidence comparison

For $q>\sqrt K$, fresh validation can dominate the computation, reaching order $Kq$ when $q\ge K$. More efficient selection is possible if extra network exchanges are affordable. Conditional on a good trained history, uniformly drawing a stored leaf supplies a new arm. At a fixed leaf, an independent constant-confidence gradient estimate gives a Bernoulli observation by thresholding its norm at $3\varepsilon/4$. With suitable constant precision, arms below $\varepsilon/2$ have success probability at least $0.9$, and arms above $\varepsilon$ have probability at most $0.1$. This statement can use a full-range modular estimate anchored at zero since all stored gradients have norm $O(K\varepsilon)$. Gong and Sellke's fixed-budget quantile search can then use $O(q\log^2(2+q))$ such adaptive pulls, each charging a fresh aggregate. Their expected stopping-time result has a different deadline criterion. This reduction presupposes a high-probability supply of good leaves. It can improve the validation computation at extreme confidence, at the cost of additional adaptive packet exchanges. The proved deadline makes a different tradeoff by validating at most $K_0$ leaves in index order and bounding all packet widths for every seed. Optimal confidence dependence, minimax elapsed time and analogous guarantees for other topologies remain open comparisons. [Gong and Sellke, §1.1 and Theorems 1.1, 1.2 and 3.1, NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/3b3a83a5d86e1d424daefed43d998079-Paper-Conference.pdf)
