# Statistical time for distributed optimization under strong growth

A fast worker can produce more gradients per second, but following that worker's gradient can change the objective. How much statistical information can a heterogeneous cluster guarantee if the gradients obey a quantitative strong growth condition? The answer at high accuracy is a function of the entire vector of worker times. It can be logarithmically larger than the information certified by the best single uniformly weighted subset, even when that subset may discard stragglers.

## Problem and result

There are $n$ workers with private differentiable convex losses $f_i$ on $\mathbb R^d$. Write $F=n^{-1}\sum_i f_i$, $S_*=\arg\min F$ and $g=\nabla F$. The average is $L$-smooth, has a minimizer within $R$ of the public initial point, and satisfies $F(x)-\min F\ge(\mu/2)\operatorname{dist}(x,S_*)^2$. The strong growth assumption is
$$
\frac1n\sum_i\|\nabla f_i(x)\|^2\le\rho\|g(x)\|^2\qquad\text{for all }x.
$$
This is the uniform-weight gradient diversity bound of Haddadpour and Mahdavi. At a global minimizer it implies all local gradients vanish. Local minimizer sets can differ. The numerical value of $\rho$ matters. Even without a stated diversity constant, common minimizers and local smoothness $L_{\max}$ imply some such bound under aggregate quadratic growth. Indeed, convex support and quadratic growth give $F-F_*\le2\|g\|^2/\mu$, and local smoothness gives $n^{-1}\sum_i\|\nabla f_i\|^2\le2L_{\max}(F-F_*)$. [Lean setup](../Setups/DistributedGradientDiversity.lean) · [Haddadpour and Mahdavi, Definition 1 and equation (7)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.14425)

A sample at worker $i$ costs $h_i>0$ time. It is a jointly measurable unbiased stochastic gradient with centered second moment at most $\sigma^2$. Each worker has an IID stream on an arbitrary measurable sample space, with independent streams across workers. The law is abstract. A separate coordinator communicates with each worker over independent directed star links. A packet of $m$ wire bits, including its 64-bit header, costs $\ell+bm$ time. Each worker serves one pending query and each directed link carries one pending packet at a time. Local arithmetic is free. The times, $d,n,L,\mu,\rho,R,\sigma,\varepsilon,x_0$ are public. The algorithm receives gradients only through these oracles. [Computation and communication oracles](../Setups/StarNetwork.lean)

Define the information $I_0$ and its unique minimizer $a$ by
$$
I_0=\min_{v\in\mathbb R^n}\left\{\sum_i\frac{v_i^2}{h_i}\ :\ \sum_i v_i=n,\quad \sum_i v_i^2\le n\rho\right\}.
$$
All $a_i$ are positive. To evaluate them, normalize the vector $r_i(t)=h_i/(1-t+t h_i)$ to sum to $n$. Choose a root $t\in[0,1]$ at which the squared norm of the normalized vector equals $n\rho$. If there is no root, use $t=0$. Continuity and feasibility at $t=1$ ensure feasibility at $t=0$ in that case. Every root yields the same unique minimizing vector. The endpoints include homogeneous gradients ($\rho=1$, $I_0=\sum_i1/h_i$) and an inactive diversity constraint ($a_i=nh_i/\sum_j h_j$, $I_0=n^2/\sum_i h_i$). The scalar prescription handles arbitrary positive service times and ties. [Design](../Algorithms/GradientDiversityDesign.lean) · [Feasibility and variational optimality](../Proofs/GradientDiversityProfile.lean)

Set $u_i=\mu a_i/2$, $I=\sum_i u_i^2/h_i=\mu^2I_0/4$, $S=\sum_i1/h_i$, $U=\max_i u_i$, and $H=\max_i h_i$. Define
$$
\beta=\min\{n,1+\sqrt{n(\rho-1)}\},\quad
M=L\max\!\left\{U\beta,\ n\max_i(u_i/h_i)/S\right\},\quad
A=2SM/I,\qquad \Gamma=\sigma U/M .
$$
The public theorem gives the following elapsed time up to universal constants, as displayed below.
$$
\frac{\sigma^2}{\mu^2 I_0\varepsilon}
+ A\,[1+\log(1+R^2/\varepsilon)]
\left[H+\ell+b\left(64+d\log\!\left(2+d+A+\frac{R^2+\Gamma^2}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\right].
$$
At this deadline the coordinator's actual output satisfies $\mathbb E\operatorname{dist}(x_{\rm out},S_*)^2\le\varepsilon$. The proof gives finite events at every earlier deadline for every seed and integrability under the execution law. The first term is the stochastic computation cost. The second pays for synchronous rounds even at zero noise, including slow-worker startup, latency, coordinate precision, and packet headers in both directions. The proof gives no matching lower bound for that second term. ## Mechanism and execution

For $g(x)\ne0$ the signed projections $v_i=\langle\nabla f_i(x),g(x)\rangle/\|g(x)\|^2$ are feasible for the definition of $I_0$. The first-order optimality inequality gives $\sum_i(a_i/h_i)v_i\ge I_0$. Thus the surrogate $G=\sum_i(a_i/h_i)f_i$ obeys $\langle\nabla G,g\rangle\ge I_0\|g\|^2$ everywhere. To pass from gradients to function gaps, follow ordinary gradient descent on $F$ with a sufficiently small constant step. Convex support bounds each decrement in $F$ from above. Smoothness of $G$ and alignment bound the decrement in $G$ from below by $I_0/2$ times that decrement in $F$. Telescope and use convergence of the **values** of $F$. This yields $G-G_*\ge(I_0/2)(F-F_*)$. Shared minimizers follow from strong growth. The argument works for nonlinear losses even when the gradient is oblique to the displacement to a nearest solution. Convex slope comparison has earlier general formulations. The worker-time extremizer and its coupling to the variance calculation are the new ingredients here. [Alignment and gap transfer](../Proofs/StrongGrowthAlignment.lean) · [Smoothness and growth of the surrogate](../Proofs/GradientDiversityGeometry.lean) · [Daniilidis and Drusvyatskiy on slope comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16277)

The implementation scales $G$ by $\mu/2$. For a target batch time $B\ge H$, worker $i$ averages $\lceil B/h_i\rceil$ samples. The computation phase lasts $\max_i h_i\lceil B/h_i\rceil$, between $B$ and $B+H$. Independent averages give statistical variance at most $\sigma^2\sum_i (u_i/h_i)^2h_i/B=\sigma^2 I/B$ before quantization. This batching calculation is standard once the surrogate growth has been established. For the finite-bit protocol the numerical tuning must also bound the norm of a scaled gradient before clipping. The centered strong growth inequality gives $\|\nabla f_i(x)\|\le(1+\sqrt{n(\rho-1)})\|g(x)\|$. Nonnegative local Bregman remainders sum to the $nL$ average smoothness bound. Together these facts give $\|\nabla f_i(x)\|\le L\beta\|x-z\|$ for every $z\in S_*$. The synthetic parameters fed to the existing scheduler implement the desired weights and precision. Their role is numerical. The proof derives all geometry and stochastic bounds from the actual losses. [Algorithm and named output](../Algorithms/GradientDiversitySgd.lean) · [Scheduler identities](../Proofs/GradientDiversitySchedule.lean)

More precisely, let $m=\max(1,\lceil4A\rceil)$ and $J=m[1+\lceil\log(64(1+R^2/\varepsilon))\rceil]$. The target batch time is $\max(H,256\sigma^2/(I\varepsilon J))$. The first $J$ updates use step $2/(Im)$, followed by $J$ steps $4/[I(2m+j)]$ for $j=0,\dots,J-1$. Worker estimates are rescaled to account for the protocol's batch fractions, clipped and encoded with the scheduled precision. The root encodes and broadcasts the weighted aggregate update. Every worker, including the root, subtracts the decoded update from its current point. Its returned point is the iterate after update $2J$. The calculation charges all $2J+1$ batches, including the last round used by the execution protocol. ## What improves

Tyurin's Theorem 2.3 gives an arithmetic-time obstruction under additive first- and second-order similarity, with free communication and fixed computation times. Section 1.1 uses upper bounds on service times. The Lean star assigns exact times. With public upper bounds and free communication, buffering each early reply until its assigned service interval ends simulates the exact-time schedule with the same independent gradient samples. Remark 2.4 asks whether the obstruction persists under $\max_i\|\nabla f_i\|^2\le2\|g\|^2$. This pointwise condition implies the averaged assumption with $\rho=2$. For fixed $d=n>2$, take $n-1$ workers of unit service time and one with time $H$. Feasibility implies the total amplitude on fast workers is at least $n-1-\sqrt{n-1}>0$ by centering the slow coordinate. Hence $I_0=\Theta_n(1)$ while $n^2/\sum h_i=\Theta_n(H^{-1})$. With fixed positive $L,\mu,R,\sigma,b,\ell,d$ and $\varepsilon=H^{-2}$ the full deadline is $O_n(H^2+H\log H+\log^2 H)$ as $H\to\infty$. A uniform arithmetic statistical obstruction would require order $H^3$ even with instantaneous communication. This rules out that obstruction on the convex class in the question. The example alone can also be handled by dropping one straggler. Tyurin's Theorems 3.6–3.8 analyze weak interpolation and the stronger combination of identical local minimizer sets and local PL. The harmonic estimate with local PL can be better on its own domain. Our profile quantifies the effect of a supplied diversity constant when only the average has growth. [Tyurin, September 2026 preprint, Theorems 2.3, 3.6–3.8, Remark 2.4 and Appendix F](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17483v1)

A many-worker regime distinguishes the profile from a single choice of uniform subset. Take $n=2N$, $\rho=2$, with $N$ unit-time workers and $N$ workers of times $j=1,\ldots,N$. Then $I_0=\Theta(\log(1+N))$. The vector equal to zero on the first group and two on the second gives $I_0\le4\sum_{j\le N}1/j$. At the minimizer, the fast mass $s$ is at most $\sqrt{NI_0}$, and $\sum_{j\;\mathrm{slow}}(a_j-2)^2\le4s$. On the tail $j\ge N^{2/3}$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\sum |a_j-2|/j=o(1)$. Thus $\sum a_j^2/j\ge\sum (4-4|a_j-2|)/j=\Omega(\log N)$. A uniformly weighted subset of $N+k$ workers can guarantee a projected sum of only $N+k-\sqrt{N^2-k^2}$, attained by constant amplitudes on the subset and its complement. This lies between $k$ and $2k$; for at most $N$ workers zero retained mass is feasible. At fixed subset size the least variance cost uses the fastest workers and is proportional to $N+k(k+1)/2$. The resulting squared guaranteed sum divided by this cost is $O(1)$. This comparison is for a uniform aggregate with the usual strong-growth certificate. An algorithm observing individual replies can reweight them. With fixed $d,L,\mu,R,\sigma,b,\ell>0$ and $\varepsilon=N^{-8}$, $A=O(N^2)$ and the second time term is $O(N^3\log^2 N)$. The resulting total time is of order $N^8/\log N$; the uniform-subset statistical certificate costs order $N^8$ at best.

For a nonlinear instance, take two nonorthogonal unit vectors $a,b$ with $|\langle a,b\rangle|\le1/\sqrt2$. Write $s=\langle a,x\rangle$ and $t=\langle b,x\rangle$. Give four workers losses $c_i s^2/2+\psi(s)+t^2/2$ with $c=(0,2,1,1)$ and $\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0$, $\psi''(s)=(|s|-1)_+^2/[1+(|s|-1)_+^2]$. The average has quadratic growth transverse to the common kernel. Worker 1 has extra minimizers and has no positive local PL constant, since its loss grows quartically just beyond the flat interval along $t=0$. The average squared local norm is $\|g\|^2+s^2/2$. Project $g=(s+\psi'(s))a+tb$ perpendicular to $b$ to get $\|g\|^2\ge s^2/2$, giving $\rho=2$. For $t=0$ and $0<|s|<1$, worker 2 has gradient norm twice the average norm. Thus the averaged condition strictly extends the max condition in Remark 2.4. Arbitrary orthogonal copies give higher-rank instances with the same constants.

A prior repository theorem derives an elapsed-time profile from known local smoothness caps and common minimizers. This theorem applies here with the universal caps $L_i=nL$, by convexity and the aggregate smoothness bound on the summed Bregman remainders. Its uncapped optimizer $n\mu h_i/\sum_j h_j$ already satisfies every cap, so its information is $(n\mu)^2/\sum_j h_j$. Direct substitution therefore gives arithmetic statistical time. Here strong growth constrains *signed directional derivatives* even when the only public smoothness bound is for the aggregate. Treating these numbers as local Hessian caps would assume a bound on changes between two query points. The older theorem and its finite-bit execution analysis supply the scheduling and transport used above. Haddadpour and Mahdavi's Theorem 4.2 analyzes an objective for given sampling weights. Cho, Wang and Joshi's Theorem 3.1 bounds the effect of biased selection through a selection skew under local strong convexity. Neither result gives this service-time information profile from a diversity bound for the original uniform objective. ## Statistical benchmark and limitations

The leading coefficient has a direct testing benchmark on the averaged class. For $d\ge1$, use the first coordinate and local quadratics $\mu a_i(x_1-\theta)^2/2$ with $\theta=\pm3\sqrt\varepsilon$, $x_0=0$ and $R\ge3\sqrt\varepsilon$. The average has curvature $\mu\le L$ along that coordinate; orthogonal coordinates are free. The normalized gradients are precisely the feasible minimizer $a$. Give worker $i$ independent Gaussian noise of variance $\sigma^2>0$ in the first coordinate on sample space $\mathbb R$. With free communication, even an adaptive time-$T$ transcript is a randomized function of at most $\lfloor T/h_i\rfloor$ independent observations per worker with means $-\mu a_i\theta$ and common variance $\sigma^2$. One can restore the public query-dependent offset to simulate each reply. The Gaussian product KL divergence is at most $18T\varepsilon\mu^2 I_0/\sigma^2$. For $T\le\sigma^2/(144\varepsilon\mu^2 I_0)$, Pinsker gives equal-prior testing error at least $3/8$. Squared-distance risk at most $\varepsilon$ at either center would let the sign test err with probability at most $1/9$. This calculation establishes sharp dependence up to constants for the statistical term as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ at fixed nondegenerate parameters. It concerns a rich sample space and the averaged condition. The extremizer can violate the stricter max condition. The formally exported result is the elapsed-time upper bound.

The deadline still pays for the slowest worker in each round. Deterministic accuracy, small initial error, or expensive communication can make other algorithms preferable. Unknown service times, adaptive estimation of $\rho$, shared bandwidth, and correlated streams would require different guarantees.
