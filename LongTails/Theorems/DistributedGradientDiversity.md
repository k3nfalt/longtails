# Fewer communication rounds under averaged gradient diversity

The statistical time of heterogeneous workers under mean-squared strong growth is already characterized by the worker information profile. This theorem sharpens the other term in the same elapsed-time guarantee. The earlier schedule pays a factor of the worker count when bounding the size of a gradient before clipping. Using gradient diversity at the queried point reduces that cost, while a separate smoothness bound controls the actual weighted update. For equal worker times and diversity constant two, the certified round count falls from order $n$ to order $\sqrt n$ at fixed condition number, initial radius and accuracy. ## Problem and returned point

The setup is exactly the one used by the previous theorem. Worker $i$ has a differentiable convex loss $f_i$ on $\mathbb R^d$. Their uniform average $F$ is $L$-smooth, its minimizer set $X_*$ contains a point within distance $R$ of the public initial point, and $F(x)-\min F\ge\mu\operatorname{dist}(x,X_*)^2/2$. The uniform average of $\|\nabla f_i(x)\|^2$ is at most $\rho\|\nabla F(x)\|^2$ at every point. Individual minimizer sets can differ. Stochastic gradients are jointly measurable, unbiased, and have centered second moment at most $\sigma^2$. An abstract joint probability law gives IID samples within each worker and independent streams across workers. The functions and their minimizers are private. The parameters, including the diversity bound and service times, are public. This finite-sum version of strong growth bounds the local mean gradients even when samples at a minimizer have positive variance. [Shared Lean setup](../Setups/DistributedMeanStrongGrowth.lean) · [Vaswani, Bach and Schmidt, equation (2)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/vaswani19a/vaswani19a.pdf)

A coordinator communicates with the workers over a star. Each worker serves one sample at a time at cost $h_i>0$. Each directed link carries one packet at a time at cost $\ell+b(64+\text{payload bits})$ and can operate concurrently with the others. The algorithm returns the coordinator's iterate after $2J$ updates. Its proof charges all $2J+1$ batches, including delivery of that iterate. For every admissible instance the execution has finitely many events at every deadline up to the budget. Its output is measurable and satisfies $\mathbb E\operatorname{dist}(x_{\rm out},X_*)^2\le\varepsilon$ under the execution law. ## Deadline and mechanism

Let $u$ minimize $\sum_i v_i^2/h_i$ subject to $\sum_i v_i=n$ and $\sum_i v_i^2\le n\rho$, with signed $v_i$ allowed. The shared profile theorem yields a positive, unique minimizer. Normalizing $h_i/[(1-t)h_i+t]$ to sum to $n$ reduces the construction to a scalar choice on $[0,1]$. It includes tied speeds and inactive constraints. Put $a_i=\mu u_i$, $I=\sum_i a_i^2/h_i$, $U=\max_i a_i$, $W=\max_i(a_i/h_i)$, $S=\sum_i1/h_i$ and $H=\max_i h_i$. [Profile construction](../Algorithms/StrongGrowthWeights.lean) · [Variational inequality](../Proofs/StrongGrowthWeights.lean)

Define
$$
\beta=\min\{n,1+\sqrt{n(\rho-1)}\},\qquad
M=L\max\{U\beta,nW/S\},\qquad
A=2SM/I,\qquad \Gamma=\sigma U/M .
$$
Up to universal constants the deadline is
$$
\frac{\sigma^2}{I\varepsilon}
+ A[1+\log(1+R^2/\varepsilon)]
\left[H+\ell+b\left(64+d\log\left(2+d+A+\frac{R^2+\Gamma^2}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\right].
$$
The public budget uses integer bit length for the packet-width factor. The first term is the same statistical coefficient as in the previous theorem. The second pays for slow-worker computation, packet latency and payload in every round. In the actual program $m=\max(1,\lceil4A\rceil)$ and $J=m[1+\lceil\log(64(1+R^2/\varepsilon))\rceil]$. The target batch time is $B=\max(H,256\sigma^2/(I\varepsilon J))$. Each worker takes $\lceil B/h_i\rceil$ samples, so the parallel batch takes at most $B+H$. Worker replies are scaled, clipped and quantized, and the root broadcasts an encoded aggregate update. [Schedule identities](../Proofs/GradientDiversitySchedule.lean) · [Two-phase steps, batch sizes and encoding precision](../Algorithms/WeightedInterpolationSgd.lean)

Two estimates justify the new tuning. First, centering the worker gradients at $g=\nabla F(x)$ gives $\sum_i\|\nabla f_i(x)-g\|^2\le n(\rho-1)\|g\|^2$. At a global minimizer every component gradient vanishes. Aggregate smoothness and convexity also bound each nonnegative local Bregman remainder by $nL\|y-x\|^2/2$. A gradient step on this upper model bounds the local gradient at $x$ by $nL\|x-z\|$ for $z\in X_*$. Together the estimates give $\|\nabla f_i(x)\|\le L\beta\|x-z\|$, which is sufficient to calibrate clipping. Second, weighting the Bregman remainders by $a_i/h_i$ bounds the smoothness model of the surrogate $\sum_i(a_i/h_i)f_i$ by $nLW$. The common schedule must satisfy both estimates, giving the maximum in $M$. The surrogate growth itself follows from the existing signed-profile comparison. No local PL bound or new regularity of gradient differences is assumed. ## Improvement and limitations

In the same notation the previous theorem has $A_{\rm old}=6nLSU/I$ and $\Gamma_{\rm old}=\sigma/(3nL)$. Write $D=\max(\beta,nW/(SU))$. Then $1\le D\le n$, $A/A_{\rm old}=D/(3n)$ and $\Gamma=\sigma/(LD)$. For noisy workers a smaller round factor can increase the bit width through $\Gamma$. This effect is logarithmic and the full deadline displays it. At sufficiently high accuracy with fixed nonzero noise, the shared statistical term dominates. [Earlier deadline](DistributedStrongGrowthTime.lean)

For unit service times the shared profile has $u_i=1$, so $D=\beta$ and $A=2\beta L/\mu$, compared with $A_{\rm old}=6nL/\mu$. Take $\rho=2$ and fix positive $R,\varepsilon,d,L,\mu,\ell,b,\sigma$ as $n$ grows. Here $I=n\mu^2$, so the common statistical term is $O(1/n)$ and both packet widths are of order $\log n$. The full deadlines scale as $\Theta(\sqrt n\log n)$ here and $\Theta(n\log n)$ for the earlier schedule. Setting $b=0$ leaves the same $\Theta(\sqrt n)$ improvement in the round factor. At $\rho=1$ with unit service times, the new round count and packet width are independent of $n$, and the statistical term decays as $1/n$. All worker gradients coincide there, so a single worker is also an available deterministic strategy. These comparisons concern the two certified finite-bit schedules. Optimal startup and communication complexity remain open.

For nonlinear losses with unequal local solution sets, one example uses two nonorthogonal unit vectors $v,w$ with $|\langle v,w\rangle|\le1/\sqrt2$. Write $s=\langle v,x\rangle$ and $q=\langle w,x\rangle$. Use four losses $c_i s^2/2+\psi(s)+q^2/2$ with $c=(0,2,1,1)$, $\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0$, and $\psi''(s)=(|s|-1)_+^2/[1+(|s|-1)_+^2]$. Their average has quadratic growth transverse to the common kernel. The first worker has additional minimizers and quartic growth just beyond the flat interval along $q=0$. The average squared gradient norm across workers is $\|g\|^2+s^2/2$. Projecting $g=(s+\psi'(s))v+qw$ perpendicular to $w$ shows $\|g\|^2\ge s^2/2$, giving $\rho=2$. Replicating the four workers keeps these constants fixed as $n$ grows. Tyurin's Remark 2.4 concerns the stricter maximum over workers. The earlier result gives the statistical comparison with that question and the Gaussian testing benchmark. The refinement here concerns the remaining round and communication costs.
