# Optimal-exponent clipped subgradient complexity for weakly convex optimization under heavy-tailed noise

The new result is an in-expectation oracle-complexity guarantee for the projected clipped stochastic subgradient method on ρ-weakly convex, G-Lipschitz objectives over a closed convex set, when the stochastic subgradient noise has only a bounded α-th central moment, α ∈ (1,2]. Measured by the gradient of the Moreau envelope, the number of stochastic subgradient evaluations is

\[
 O\!\left(\frac{\rho\Delta\,\sigma^{\alpha/(\alpha-1)}}{\epsilon^{(3\alpha-2)/(\alpha-1)}}+\frac{\rho\Delta\,G^{2}}{\epsilon^{4}}+\frac{\rho\Delta\,\sigma^{\alpha}G^{2-\alpha}}{\epsilon^{4}}\right),
\]

whose accuracy exponent \((3\alpha-2)/(\alpha-1)\) is the one of the smooth heavy-tailed lower bound of Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2020). The strongest previous guarantee for this exact class, Zhu–Xu–Wang–Ji (ICML 2026; arXiv 2507.13283v1), has exponent \(2\alpha/(\alpha-1)\), which is strictly worse for every \(\alpha\in(1,2)\) and equal only at \(\alpha=2\); its authors state that tightness is unknown and argue that improving their rate is difficult. The proved theorem settles that question for the in-expectation criterion: nonsmoothness and nonconvexity of the weakly convex class cost nothing in the accuracy exponent relative to smooth optimization.

The public [Lean theorem](WeaklyConvexClippedSubgradient.lean) is `Convergence spec budget` for the [setup](../Setups/WeaklyConvexHeavyTailed.lean) and the [algorithm](../Algorithms/ClippedProjectedSubgradient.lean), with supporting proofs in [WeaklyConvexMoreau](../Proofs/WeaklyConvexMoreau.lean) (prox and Moreau-envelope theory), [ClippedSubgradientStep](../Proofs/ClippedSubgradientStep.lean) (one-step expected descent), [ClippedSubgradientProcess](../Proofs/ClippedSubgradientProcess.lean) (execution and trajectory), [ClippedSubgradientLyapunov](../Proofs/ClippedSubgradientLyapunov.lean), [ClippedSubgradientTuning](../Proofs/ClippedSubgradientTuning.lean), and [ClippedSubgradientConvergence](../Proofs/ClippedSubgradientConvergence.lean).

## Setup and assumptions

Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional real inner product space, \(X\subseteq E\) nonempty, closed and convex with \(x_0\in X\), and \(f:E\to\mathbb R\). The oracle answers two kinds of queries: an exact Euclidean projection \(\Pi_X\) (characterized by \(\Pi_X y\in X\) and \(\langle y-\Pi_X y,u-\Pi_X y\rangle\le 0\) for all \(u\in X\)) and a stochastic subgradient \(g(x,\xi)\) with fresh independent samples \(\xi_k\sim\nu\) for every subgradient call. The instance assumptions, all restricted to \(X\), are:

- **ρ-weak convexity.** There is a selection \(v:E\to E\) with \(f(x)+\langle v(x),y-x\rangle-\tfrac\rho2\|y-x\|^2\le f(y)\) for all \(x,y\in X\). This is the subgradient inequality satisfied by every subgradient of a ρ-weakly convex function; it is implied by the baseline's "f ρ-weakly convex and \(\mathbb E g\in\partial f(x)\)".
- **G-Lipschitz.** \(\|v(x)\|\le G\) for \(x\in X\) (only for the selection, weaker than bounding every subgradient).
- **Bounded below.** Some \(\ell\le f\) on \(X\) with \(f(x_0)-\ell\le\Delta\).
- **Unbiased, heavy-tailed oracle.** \(g\) is jointly measurable, \(\mathbb E_\xi g(x,\xi)=v(x)\) and \(\mathbb E_\xi\|g(x,\xi)-v(x)\|^\alpha\le\sigma^\alpha\) for \(x\in X\), with \(1<\alpha\le2\). No variance bound is assumed when \(\alpha<2\).

These are exactly Assumptions 1.1, 3.1 and 3.2 of Zhu–Xu–Wang–Ji (ICML 2026) and Assumptions 3.1–3.4, 3.6 of arXiv 2507.13283v1, which in turn are the Davis–Drusvyatskiy (SIOPT 2019) weakly convex model with the α-th-moment noise model of Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2020). The baseline allows an extended-valued f with \(X\subseteq\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom} f\); since every hypothesis here lives on \(X\), a total f is without loss of generality. Finite dimension is used only to prove that the proximal point below exists.

The public input is \((E,\Omega,\rho,G,\sigma,\alpha,\Delta,\epsilon,x_0)\); valid parameters satisfy \(\rho>0\), \(G>0\), \(\sigma\ge0\), \(1<\alpha\le2\), \(\Delta\ge0\), \(\epsilon>0\). The algorithm never reads \(f\), \(v\) or \(X\); the constraint set enters only through the projection oracle.

## Accuracy criterion

For \(x\in E\) let \(\hat x\) be the unique minimizer over \(X\) of \(y\mapsto f(y)+\rho\|y-x\|^2\), i.e. the proximal point of \(f+\iota_X\) with parameter \(\lambda=1/(2\rho)\). The Lean setup states success as

\[
 \mathbb E\big[(2\rho)^2\|x_{\rm out}-\hat x_{\rm out}\|^2\big]\le\epsilon^2 ,
\]

quantified over every selection of minimizers (there is only one), together with the existence of such a selection, which the proof supplies. By the identity \(\nabla f_{\lambda}(x)=(x-\hat x)/\lambda\) for the Moreau envelope \(f_\lambda\) of \(f+\iota_X\) (Davis–Drusvyatskiy 2019, Lemma 2.2), this is \(\mathbb E\|\nabla f_{1/(2\rho)}(x_{\rm out})\|^2\le\epsilon^2\): the baseline's stationarity measure, at its parameter \(\bar\rho=2\rho\). The output is a uniformly random iterate among \(x_0,\dots,x_T\), so the left-hand side equals \(\frac1{T+1}\sum_{t\le T}\mathbb E\|\nabla f_{1/(2\rho)}(x_t)\|^2\), the averaged quantity bounded by the baseline. By Jensen the squared criterion implies the baseline's non-squared ε-complexity criterion \(\mathbb E\|\nabla f_{1/(2\rho)}(x_{\rm out})\|\le\epsilon\).

## Algorithm and oracle cost

With constants

\[
 \tau=2G+2\sigma\Big(\frac{4\sigma}{\epsilon}\Big)^{1/(\alpha-1)},\qquad
 N=G^2+\sigma^\alpha(2\tau)^{2-\alpha},\qquad
 \gamma=\frac{\epsilon^2}{24\rho N},\qquad
 T=\Big\lceil\frac{288\,\rho\Delta N}{\epsilon^4}\Big\rceil,
\]

the method runs \(x_{t+1}=\Pi_X\big(x_t-\gamma\,\mathrm{clip}_\tau(g(x_t,\xi_t))\big)\) for \(t<T\), where \(\mathrm{clip}_\tau(g)=\min\{1,\tau/\|g\|\}g\), and returns \(x_J\) for an index \(J\) drawn uniformly from \(\{0,\dots,T\}\) by the algorithm's own seed. Every iteration makes exactly one stochastic subgradient call and one projection call, both counted. The public budget is the single line

\[
 K=2\left\lceil\frac{288\,\rho\Delta\left(G^2+\sigma^\alpha\big(4G+4\sigma(4\sigma/\epsilon)^{1/(\alpha-1)}\big)^{2-\alpha}\right)}{\epsilon^4}\right\rceil ,
\]

so the number of stochastic subgradient evaluations is \(K/2=T\). Expanding with \((a+b)^{2-\alpha}\le a^{2-\alpha}+b^{2-\alpha}\),

\[
 T\le 1+\frac{288\rho\Delta}{\epsilon^4}\Big(G^2+4^{2-\alpha}\sigma^\alpha G^{2-\alpha}+4^{2-\alpha}4^{(2-\alpha)/(\alpha-1)}\sigma^{\alpha/(\alpha-1)}\epsilon^{-(2-\alpha)/(\alpha-1)}\Big),
\]

which gives the three displayed terms. The cross term \(\sigma^\alpha G^{2-\alpha}\epsilon^{-4}\) is dominated by the \(G^2\epsilon^{-4}\) term when \(\sigma\le G\), and by the leading term when \(\sigma\ge G\) and \(\epsilon\le\sigma\) (then \(\sigma^\alpha G^{2-\alpha}\le\sigma^2\le\sigma^{\alpha/(\alpha-1)}\epsilon^{-(2-\alpha)/(\alpha-1)}\)); only in the regime \(\sigma>G\) and \(\epsilon>\sigma\), where the whole budget is \(O(\rho\Delta/\epsilon^2)\), can it be the largest term. When \(\sigma=0\) the threshold is \(2G\), clipping is inactive and the budget reduces to the deterministic \(O(\rho\Delta G^2/\epsilon^4)\). When \(\alpha=2\) the budget is \(O(\rho\Delta(G^2+\sigma^2)/\epsilon^4)\), the Davis–Drusvyatskiy rate.

The tuning uses \(\rho,G,\sigma,\alpha,\Delta,\epsilon\). The baseline's step size uses \(G,\sigma,\alpha\) and the horizon; its stopping horizon for a target ε also needs \(\rho\) and \(\Delta_1\), so the knowledge needed for the certified budget coincides, but the constant step here depends on \(\rho\) and \(\epsilon\) while the baseline's step does not. The degenerate budget \(\Delta=0\) gives \(T=0\): the output is \(x_0\), which is then its own proximal point.

## Comparison with the closest prior results

- **Zhu, Xu, Wang, Ji, "Stochastic Gradient Methods under Heavy-Tailed Noises in Weakly Convex Optimization", ICML 2026** ([poster 64202](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/64202); earlier version [arXiv:2507.13283v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13283), Zhu–Xu–Ji). Theorem 5.1 (high probability) and Theorem E.1 (in expectation) analyze the same projected Clip-SGD with \(\lambda_t=2G\vee\sigma T^{1/\alpha}\), \(\eta_t=1/\lambda_t\wedge1/(G\sqrt T)\) and obtain \(\frac1T\sum_t\mathbb E\|\nabla f_{1/2\rho}(x_t)\|^2=O\big((\Delta_1+G+\rho)\max\{G/\sqrt T,\sigma/T^{1-1/\alpha}\}\big)\), i.e. an ε-complexity \(O(\epsilon^{-2\alpha/(\alpha-1)})\) (arXiv v1, Theorem 5 and the paragraph after it, states this exponent explicitly and notes it exceeds the smooth lower bound). Section 5 of the ICML version writes that no lower bound is known for weakly convex first-order methods, "we do not know whether the rate is tight", and argues through Example (10) that improving it is difficult. Theorem 4.2 there gives vanilla projected SGD on a bounded set the complexity \(\tilde O(G^2\epsilon^{-4}+\sigma^{\alpha/(\alpha-1)}\epsilon^{-2\alpha/(\alpha-1)})\), again with exponent \(2\alpha/(\alpha-1)\). The present bound improves the accuracy exponent from \(2\alpha/(\alpha-1)\) to \((3\alpha-2)/(\alpha-1)\) for every \(\alpha\in(1,2)\) (for \(\alpha=1.5\): from 6 to 5) under the same assumptions, the same stationarity measure and the same oracle, with one stochastic subgradient per iteration and no mini-batching. The improvement is interior to \((1,2)\): at \(\alpha=2\) both exponents equal 4 (the Davis–Drusvyatskiy rate), and as \(\alpha\to1\) both diverge. The optimality claimed for Theorem 4.2 in the ICML paper rests on the algorithm-specific lower bound of Fatkhullin–Hübler–Lan (OPT 2025, Theorem 5.5) for vanilla SGD with polynomial step sizes, not on a lower bound for the class; together with the present result it shows that clipping attains the class-optimal exponent where vanilla SGD is only known to reach \(2\alpha/(\alpha-1)\).
- **Rebuttal of the difficulty argument (ICML Example (10)).** The example is the 2-weakly convex function \(f(x)=-x^2+1\) on \((-1,-0.5)\), \(3(x+1)^2\) otherwise, which satisfies \(f-f^*\le\operatorname{dist}^2(\partial f(x),0)\). The argument is that a faster Moreau-gradient rate would give Clip-SGD a function-gap rate faster than the convex Lipschitz lower bound \(\Omega(T^{-(\alpha-1)/\alpha})\). But that lower bound is a worst case over Lipschitz convex functions without growth; the example has a quadratic-growth-type error bound, under which faster gap rates are expected (the strongly convex class lower bound is \(\Omega(T^{-2(\alpha-1)/\alpha})\), Zhang et al. 2020, Theorem 5). The implied squared-Moreau-gradient rate here, \(T^{-2(\alpha-1)/(3\alpha-2)}\), is slower than \(T^{-2(\alpha-1)/\alpha}\), so there is no contradiction, and the argument does not bind.
- **Zhang, Karimireddy, Veit, Kim, Reddi, Kumar, Sra, "Why are adaptive methods good for attention models?", NeurIPS 2020** ([arXiv:1912.03194](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03194)). Theorem 6 and Appendix G give, for every \(\alpha\in(1,2]\), smoothness \(L\), gap \(\Delta\), moment bound \(G\ge4\sqrt{\Delta L}\) and every (possibly randomized) algorithm, an \(L\)-smooth instance with \(\mathbb E\|g\|^\alpha\le G^\alpha\) such that \(\mathbb E\|\nabla f(x_k)\|\ge\epsilon\) for all \(k\le c(\alpha)(G-4\sqrt{\Delta L})^{\alpha/(\alpha-1)}\Delta L\,\epsilon^{-(3\alpha-2)/(\alpha-1)}\). The hard instance is \(\frac{L\lambda^2}{152}f_d(x/\lambda)\) with \(\lambda=304\epsilon/L\), \(d=\lfloor\Delta L/(7296\epsilon^2)\rfloor\), and \(\|\nabla f_d\|_\infty\le23\), so its Euclidean gradient norm is at most \(2\epsilon\cdot23\sqrt d\le0.54\sqrt{\Delta L}\le G\), uniformly in ε. It therefore lies in the present class with \(\rho=L\), Lipschitz constant \(G\), centered moment \(\sigma\le2G\), \(X=E\); since \(\|\nabla f(x)\|\le\tfrac32\|\nabla f_{1/(2L)}(x)\|\) for \(L\)-smooth \(f\), the lower bound transfers to the Moreau criterion. For \(G\ge8\sqrt{\Delta L}\) it reads \(\Omega(\rho\Delta\,G^{\alpha/(\alpha-1)}\epsilon^{-(3\alpha-2)/(\alpha-1)})\), matching the leading term of the budget above up to absolute constants when \(\sigma\asymp G\). The claim is tightness of the accuracy exponent and of the leading term in that regime; no tightness is claimed for the \(G^2\epsilon^{-4}\) term or for general \((\rho,G,\sigma,\Delta)\).
- **Davis, Drusvyatskiy, "Stochastic model-based minimization of weakly convex functions", SIAM J. Optim. 2019** ([arXiv:1803.06523](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06523)), Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.6: \(O(\epsilon^{-4})\) for the projected stochastic subgradient method under a bounded second moment (\(\alpha=2\)). Recovered here at \(\alpha=2\) with the same Moreau criterion and explicit constants.
- **Hu, Sun, Chen, Wang, Wang, arXiv:2505.09279v1 (May 2025)**, distributed clipped subgradient for weakly convex problems on a compact set. Their Theorem 4.8 already contains a squared clipping-bias term, but it controls the clipped second moment by \(\tau_k^2\) instead of \(\sigma^\alpha\tau_k^{2-\alpha}\); optimized over their admissible schedules \(\alpha_k=k^{-a},\tau_k=k^{b}\), the bound yields at best \(\epsilon^{-(4\alpha-2)/(\alpha-1)}\) for the best iterate on a compact set (worse than the ICML exponent), and their Remark 4.10 example gives only \(O(1/\log T)\). The squared-bias idea thus appears there, without the clipped-variance bound that makes the optimal exponent possible.
- Smooth heavy-tailed upper bounds with the exponent \((3\alpha-2)/(\alpha-1)\) (Zhang et al. 2020, Theorem 2, in expectation; Cutkosky–Mehta 2021 and Hübler–Fatkhullin–He 2024 with normalization) require Lipschitz gradients; the Goldstein-stationarity route (Liu–Zhou, ICML 2024, \(\tilde O(\delta^{-1}\epsilon^{-(2\alpha-1)/(\alpha-1)})\)) does not reach the Moreau criterion with this exponent, because converting \((\delta,\epsilon)\)-Goldstein stationarity of a ρ-weakly convex function into Moreau stationarity needs \(\delta\approx\epsilon^2/(\rho G)\), giving \(\epsilon^{-(4\alpha-3)/(\alpha-1)}\). Mini-batching the baseline only rescales \(\sigma^\alpha\) and leaves its exponent unchanged. The literature check (arXiv through October 2026, ICML/ICLR 2025–2026, NeurIPS 2024–2025) found no other upper bound with the optimal exponent for this class; the AISTATS 2026 lower-bound paper of Fradin et al. concerns smooth classes.

## Mechanism

Write \(\hat x_t\) for the proximal point of \(x_t\), \(\varphi\) for the Moreau envelope, \(\tilde g_t=\mathrm{clip}_\tau(g(x_t,\xi_t))\) and \(b_t=\mathbb E_t\tilde g_t-v(x_t)\) for the clipping bias. Nonexpansiveness of \(\Pi_X\) and the definition of \(\varphi\) give \(\varphi(x_{t+1})\le\varphi(x_t)+2\rho\gamma\langle\tilde g_t,\hat x_t-x_t\rangle+\rho\gamma^2\|\tilde g_t\|^2\). The weak-convexity inequality together with the ρ-strong convexity of the proximal subproblem yields \(\langle v(x_t),\hat x_t-x_t\rangle\le-\rho\|\hat x_t-x_t\|^2\) (this is the Davis–Drusvyatskiy step, [`inner_weak_prox`](../Proofs/WeaklyConvexMoreau.lean)). The standard clipping lemmas (already in the repository, [HeavyTailedClipping](../Proofs/HeavyTailedClipping.lean)) give \(\|b_t\|\le\sigma^\alpha/(\tau/2)^{\alpha-1}\) when \(\tau\ge2G\) and \(\mathbb E_t\|\tilde g_t-v(x_t)\|^2\le(2\tau)^{2-\alpha}\sigma^\alpha\). The only departure from the baseline's proof is how the bias term is handled: Zhu et al. bound \(\langle b_t,\hat x_t-x_t\rangle\) by \(\|b_t\|\cdot 2G/\rho\) using the Lipschitz bound on \(\|\hat x_t-x_t\|\), which makes the bias enter linearly and forces \(\tau\approx\sigma T^{1/\alpha}\); here it is absorbed into the descent term, \(2\rho\gamma\|b_t\|\,\|\hat x_t-x_t\|\le\rho^2\gamma\|\hat x_t-x_t\|^2+\gamma\|b_t\|^2\), the bias-absorption step of Zhang et al. (2020, proof of Theorem 2), so that

\[
 \mathbb E_t\varphi(x_{t+1})\le\varphi(x_t)-\tfrac{\gamma}{4}\|\nabla\varphi(x_t)\|^2+\gamma\|b_t\|^2+2\rho\gamma^2\big(G^2+(2\tau)^{2-\alpha}\sigma^\alpha\big).
\]

Telescoping, the threshold \(\tau=2G+2\sigma(4\sigma/\epsilon)^{1/(\alpha-1)}\) makes \(4\|b_t\|^2\le\epsilon^2/4\), the step makes the variance term \(\epsilon^2/3\), and \(T\) makes the initial term \(\epsilon^2/3\). The clipping level thus scales as \(\sigma^{\alpha/(\alpha-1)}T^{1/(3\alpha-2)}\) rather than \(\sigma T^{1/\alpha}\). The technical content is this sharper treatment of a single term plus the resulting retuning; the value of the result is the optimal exponent and the resolution of a tightness question left open in an accepted paper, with a complete formal proof covering the constrained case, the random-output accounting and all oracle calls.

The formalization is the first in this repository for weakly convex optimization. [WeaklyConvexMoreau](../Proofs/WeaklyConvexMoreau.lean) proves, from the weak-subgradient inequality alone, existence and uniqueness of the proximal point over \(X\) in finite dimension, its 2-Lipschitz dependence on \(x\), the growth bound \(f(x)\ge\varphi(x)+\tfrac\rho2\|x-\hat x\|^2\), and the inner-product inequality used above; [ClippedSubgradientLyapunov](../Proofs/ClippedSubgradientLyapunov.lean) and [ClippedSubgradientConvergence](../Proofs/ClippedSubgradientConvergence.lean) run the expectation argument through the repository's IID stream machinery and the algorithm's uniform output index.

## Nontrivial instances

Robust phase retrieval with heavy-tailed sensing vectors: \(f(x)=\mathbb E\,|\langle a,x\rangle^2-b|\) over the ball \(X=\{\|x\|\le R\}\), with \(a\) having finite \(2\alpha\)-th moments but possibly infinite fourth moment. Each sample function is \(2\|a\|^2\)-weakly convex, so \(f\) is ρ-weakly convex with \(\rho=2\,\mathbb E\|a\|^2\); the mean subgradient is bounded on \(X\) by \(G=2R\,\mathbb E\|a\|^2\); the stochastic subgradient \(2\,\mathrm{sign}(\langle a,x\rangle^2-b)\langle a,x\rangle a\) has centered α-th moment at most \(\sigma^\alpha=2^\alpha(2R)^\alpha\mathbb E\|a\|^{2\alpha}\) on \(X\) but infinite variance when \(\mathbb E\|a\|^4=\infty\). Coordinates interact through \(a\); the dimension enters only through \(\rho,G,\sigma\). The same holds for robust regression \(\mathbb E|\langle a,x\rangle-b|\) composed with a smooth nonconvex model of bounded Jacobian. For smooth \(f\) the Moreau gradient is just a gradient and the result reduces to the smooth clipped-SGD theory, so scalar or smooth examples do not exhibit the nonsmooth proximal mechanism.

## Limitations

- In expectation only. The baseline's high-probability bound (polylogarithmic in the confidence) has the suboptimal exponent; an optimal-exponent high-probability version is the natural follow-up and is not provided here.
- The step size and threshold use \(\rho,G,\sigma,\alpha,\Delta,\epsilon\) (the baseline's step uses \(G,\sigma,\alpha\) and the horizon); the σ-unknown variant of the baseline is not reproduced.
- Each projection is counted as an oracle call, so the public budget is twice the number of stochastic subgradient evaluations; the baseline treats the projection as computation.
- Tightness is claimed for the accuracy exponent and, when \(\sigma\asymp G\gtrsim\sqrt{\Delta\rho}\), for the leading term; not for the deterministic \(G^2\epsilon^{-4}\) term, and no new lower bound is proved.
- Finite-dimensional \(E\) (for existence of the proximal point); the baseline works in \(\mathbb R^d\).
