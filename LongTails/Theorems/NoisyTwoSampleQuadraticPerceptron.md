# Reproduction of the two-sample quadratic perceptron bound with Gaussian noise

Tyurin’s Theorem 5.1 analyzes a particular noisy, violation-priority quadratic perceptron on the pair of inputs in Equation (5). This result reproduces that guarantee and provides the baseline for the companion finite-initialization logistic GD theorem. Both use the original two-tap convolutional classifier with trainable filter and readout. Their updates, sources of randomness and initialization hypotheses differ. [Primary theorem, ICML 2026, arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11587v2) · [Finite-radius logistic GD](FiniteLogisticTwoTapGradientDescent.md)

Write the signed scores as $w^\top A_b w/2$ for $b\in\{0,1\}$. The first input is the constant vector. The opposite-label input increases a single hidden cyclic coordinate by $\mu$. Let $d\ge2$, $0<\mu\le1/10$, and $0<\rho<1$. For fixed starting weights $w_0$, the algorithm receives a positive lower bound on the magnitude of the initial projection in the paper’s $v_{\mu,+}$ direction, $s\le |\langle w_0,v_{\mu,+}\rangle|$. The setup permits any hidden index for which this certificate holds. Equality recovers the paper’s initialization-dependent tuning. [Setup](../Setups/NoisyTwoSamplePerceptron.lean)

Each round evaluates the two signed scores and their gradients at the current point. A nonpositive first score triggers the first update. Otherwise a nonpositive second score triggers the second update. The chosen update is $w\gets w+(4\sqrt d)^{-1} A_b w+\sigma g$, with a fresh standard Gaussian vector $g\in\mathbb R^{d+2}$ independent across rounds. A strict separator is held fixed. Write

\[
X=\frac{\sqrt d}{\mu},\qquad C=\left(\frac{\|w_0\|}{s}\right)^2,\qquad
K=2^{27}X\log\frac{2^{62}C}{\rho^3}\log(2^{15}XC),\qquad T=\lceil K\log K\rceil.
\]

The algorithm uses $\sigma=s/(4096T\sqrt{\max\{d,\log(T/\rho)\}})$. After $2T$ indexed sample queries the returned iterate has both scores strictly positive with probability at least $1-\rho$. The bound counts both queries of every round and allows the final update to produce the separator. A smaller certified signal changes both the noise scale and the budget. [Algorithm](../Algorithms/NoisyTwoSamplePerceptron.lean) · [Theorem and public budget](NoisyTwoSampleQuadraticPerceptron.lean)

The proof separates the growing projection from the four remaining active coordinates. A second-sample update multiplies the growing projection by $1+\delta$ before noise, where $\delta=\mu/(4\sqrt d)$. A mixed pair of updates expands the complement by at most $1+\delta/2$ before noise. Consecutive violations of one sample instead give a norm bound using the sign of its quadratic form. Grouping these cases controls every prefix, including an unmatched last update. At any second-sample violation, the negative contribution of the complement must offset the growing projection. This limits the number of second-sample moves. [Complement estimates](../Proofs/NoisyTwoSamplePerceptronComplement.lean) · [Prefix and signal estimates](../Proofs/NoisyTwoSamplePerceptronPath.lean)

To control long runs of the first sample, the proof considers a hypothetical uninterrupted run starting at each deterministic time. Its final fast coordinate depends affinely on a fresh Gaussian projection whose coefficient grows geometrically with the run length. A Gaussian interval bound followed by a union over starts handles adaptive sample selection. A separate Gaussian tail bound covers all five active coordinates at all times. The final success probability accounts for both exceptional events. [Gaussian block estimates](../Proofs/NoisyTwoSamplePerceptronBlocks.lean) · [Stopping proof](../Proofs/NoisyTwoSamplePerceptronStopping.lean)

The reproduced bound concerns the noisy priority dynamics. The companion contribution proves a hitting-time bound for the original full-batch logistic gradient, where both sigmoid weights evolve continuously with the parameters and the finite random initialization is the only source of randomness. The paper’s Theorem 5.2 gives a lower bound for the classical perceptron under its own update rule. Neither theorem here establishes a lower bound for unrestricted separator search.
