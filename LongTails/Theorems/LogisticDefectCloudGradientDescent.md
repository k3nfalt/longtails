# How quickly does logistic GD find a translated defect?

A sensor records a constant background with a positive label. A localized increase at any of several positions carries a negative label. The position is unknown to the optimizer. How many indexed observations does gradient descent on a trainable two-tap convolution need before it classifies *every* observation correctly?

For $m$ possible nonadjacent sites in $d\ge512m$ coordinates and defect height $0<\delta\le1/10$, the answer is at most $O((1+m/\delta)\log(1/R))$ full passes from a sufficiently small random sphere of normalized radius $R$. The ordinary zero-initialized batch perceptron on exactly these observations requires more than $(d+\delta)/(\delta+\delta^2/m)$ passes. For one site the new GD bound saves a factor of order $\sqrt d$ over the finite-step GD analysis in PR 253 on the common small-radius regime. For many sites the proof controls the individually different logistic weights generated by a fully random readout. [Lean theorem](LogisticDefectCloudGradientDescent.lean) · [PR 253](https://github.com/k3nfalt/longtails/pull/253)

## Objective, data and guarantee

Write $P$ for cyclic shift, $Sx=(x+Px)/\sqrt2$ and $Tx=(x-Px)/\sqrt2$. In the orthonormal sum and difference coordinates $(h,t)$ of the two trainable taps, a trainable readout $w\in\mathbb R^d$ gives the score $h\langle Sx,w\rangle+t\langle Tx,w\rangle$. The loss averages $\log(1+\exp(-y\,\mathrm{score}(x)))$ over $n$ copies of a positive reference $b$ and $n$ negative observations $b_i$. This balanced sampling is part of the objective, including when $n>1$.

The theorem assumes orthonormal $e,u,v$ and parameters $L>0$, $0<\mu\le1/10$, $0<a\le1/16$, $d\ge3$. Put $\beta=\sqrt{1-a^2}$. The raw observations satisfy

$$
Sb=L e,\quad Tb=0,\qquad
Sb_i=L[(1+\mu a)e-\mu\beta u+z_i],\quad
Tb_i=L[-\mu v+y_i].
$$

Both residual families have mean zero. Each residual has norm at most one and is orthogonal to $e,u,v$. Their mutual inner products can have either sign. These conditions describe variation around a common mean defect while preserving its DC level and its two mean shift components. They are assumptions on actual shifted observations. Exact centering follows by subtracting a sample mean. Exact orthogonality and the common positive reference restrict the application. Residuals may have norm much larger than $\mu$. The algorithm receives the numerical parameters and indexed oracle replies. The axes, residuals and defect positions are private. [Setup and GD family](../Setups/LogisticDefectCloud.lean)

Initialize all $d+2$ weights jointly at radius $r$ in a uniform direction, using a normalized standard Gaussian. For $0<\rho<1$ assume

$$
0<R=r\sqrt L\le \left(\frac{a\mu\rho}{2^{20}(d+2)}\right)^4.
$$

At a single point query every signed score and its gradient, including correctly classified observations. Take the simultaneous GD step on the empirical mean with constant stepsize $1/[2^{48}L(a+\mu)]$. The first scan with strictly positive signed scores freezes its point. With probability at least $1-\rho$, the actual output has all signed scores strictly positive within

$$
2n\left(\left\lceil 2^{58}(1+a/\mu)\log(1/R)\right\rceil+1\right)
$$

indexed queries. The last pass certifies classification. The fixed-budget execution also charges all padded queries after an early success. Confidence enters through the radius restriction. A smaller radius can increase the runtime. The guarantee concerns training classification at a finite iterate, with no quantitative margin, loss tolerance or population-risk guarantee. ## The translated instances and the perceptron comparison

Take $b=\mathbf1$ and $b_i=\mathbf1+\delta e_i$ for the $m=n$ sites $i$ in $I$. Require $I\cap PI=\varnothing$. Put $s=\mathbf1_I/\sqrt m$, $A=Ss$, $C=Ts$ and $e=\mathbf1/\sqrt d$. Then $s$ and $Ps$ are orthonormal, as are $A,C$, with $\langle A,e\rangle=a=\sqrt{2m/d}$ and $\langle C,e\rangle=0$. Choose $u=(ae-A)/\beta$, $v=-C$, $L=\sqrt{2d}$ and $\mu=\delta/\sqrt{2dm}$. For $t_i=e_i-s/\sqrt m$, the residuals are $z_i=\delta St_i/L$ and $y_i=\delta Tt_i/L$. Each has norm $\delta\sqrt{1-1/m}/L$. Their sums vanish, and disjointness of the two supports gives the required orthogonality. The negative observations are distinct. More generally replace the individual $e_i$ by arbitrary vectors supported on $I$ of coordinate sum one and empirical mean $\mathbf1_I/m$. The same argument applies whenever the residual norm bounds hold. Mixtures can have unequal norms and lack any permutation symmetry.

On the entire specified geometry, ordinary full-batch perceptron, with any positive constant step and zero initialization, requires $k(\mu a+2\mu^2)>1+\mu a$ to separate. Indeed, in background units the raw negative mean is $M=(1+\mu a)e-\mu\beta u-\mu v$. Every iterate is a positive fixed multiple of $A e-B M$, where $A,B$ count passes in which the positive and negative group respectively update. Each negative has the same raw margin along this trajectory by residual orthogonality, and their mean update cancels residuals. Strict positive classification forces $A-B\ge1$, while strict negative classification forces $(A-B)(1+\mu a)<B(\mu a+2\mu^2)$. Since $B\le k$, the claimed delay follows. Substitution gives the lower bound in the opening paragraph. [Perceptron trajectory](../Proofs/LogisticCloudPerceptron.lean)

The general GD bound and the batch-perceptron trajectory above are machine-checked. The site specialization, sequential counting argument and two auxiliary GD comparisons below are analytic calculations.

Even a classical sequential perceptron with arbitrary ordering on the site family requires more than $(d+\delta)/(\delta+\delta^2/m)$ individual negative updates. After $A$ positive updates and $b_i$ negative updates at site $i$, write $B=\sum_i b_i$. Up to the common positive stepsize the raw predictor is $(A-B)\mathbf1-\delta\sum_i b_i e_i$. A strictly positive reference margin forces $A-B\ge1$. Averaging the required negative margins yields $(d+\delta)(A-B)<(\delta+\delta^2/m)B$. Thus indexed GD cost beats full-batch perceptron for $m\log(1/R)=o(d)$ and even this sequential comparison for $m^2\log(1/R)=o(d)$, with $\delta\le1/10$. For example, fixed $\delta,\rho$, $m=\lfloor d^{1/3}\rfloor$ and $R$ equal to its admissible upper endpoint give both improvements. Constants in the proved GD budget are large.

## Why a finite large step works

In normalized weights $X=\sqrt L(h,t,w)$ the stepsize is $\eta=1/[2^{48}(a+\mu)]$. Linearization at zero has two equally fast growing modes, one per rotated tap, with multiplier $1+\eta\mu/4$. Full-sphere initialization excites both with controlled probability. A cubic remainder estimate tracks their growth until the aligned readout components dominate the initial transverse perturbation. Exact symmetry of the sample margins at initialization would allow replacing them by their mean. A generic Gaussian seed breaks that symmetry. [Initialization](../Proofs/LogisticCloudInitialization.lean) · [Escape estimate](../Proofs/LogisticCloudEscape.lean)

For each sample, subtract the sigmoid evaluated at the common mean score. The sigmoid secant is between zero and $1/4$. Restricted to the transverse readout the update is an average of rank-one contractions while $\eta$ times the squared filter norm is small. This prevents dispersion from overwhelming either aligned component. In the remaining dynamics, orient the growing coordinates, denote the reference filter and readout by $p,q$, and follow the larger of the two aligned non-DC readout coordinates. A forward-invariant cone gives multiplicative growth of a positive aggregate at rate $1+\eta\mu/80$. When the larger coordinate reaches $64\sqrt a$, the mean defect score produces a positive drift in $q$ that forces a crossing within $\lceil4/(\eta\mu)\rceil$ further steps. The filter remains of order $\sqrt a$ throughout this interval. This *trajectory* bound permits a normalized step of order $1/(a+\mu)$ even when individual residuals are of order one. A separate overshoot estimate shows that at the first positive crossing every negative margin is already strictly correct. ## Closest comparisons and limits

Tyurin's Theorem 2.1 takes a scaling limit relating logistic GD and a quadratic perceptron. Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 separate a noisy quadratic perceptron from the classical perceptron on the single-site pair. Neither the noise injection nor violation-priority updates occur here. Appendix G reports experiments on several datasets, including faster nonlinear separation on subsets of CIFAR-10, faster linear separation on an MNIST pair and failure of both models to separate the full CIFAR-10 class pair within 400,000 iterations. Appendix G.4 varies initialization scale. These motivate asking about more than two observations. The theorem requires a balanced reference, exact residual geometry and a small sphere, none verified for those image datasets. [Tyurin, ICML 2026, §§2, 5 and Appendix G, arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11587v2)

PR 253 uses the same finite logistic GD, oracle and sphere on one site, with physical step $2^{-40}/\sqrt d$ and bound $O((\sqrt d/\delta)\log(1/r))$. Here the physical step on $m$ sites is $2^{-48}/(2\sqrt m+\delta/\sqrt m)$. The comparison uses $d\ge512m$ and radii allowed by both results. PR 253 permits smaller dimensions and larger radii. A second prior analysis already handles finite clouds with a positive margin in *every* sample's difference channel and an inradius condition in the sum channel, starting with identity filter and zero readout. Our reference has identically zero difference channel. That analysis proves a separation even from linear logistic GD on its own six-sample instance. The present comparison is with perceptron. Existing many-sample quadratic perceptron bounds use violation-triggered updates and a different initial state. Small-initialization gradient-flow alignment alone gives neither this finite stepsize nor strict correctness at the crossing. [PR 253](https://github.com/k3nfalt/longtails/pull/253) · [PR 254](https://github.com/k3nfalt/longtails/pull/254) · [Many-sample quadratic perceptron](ManySampleConvolutionalPerceptronAcceleration.md) · [Kumar and Haupt, §5](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=hfrPag75Y0) · [Kumar and Haupt, Theorem 4](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume26/25-1089/25-1089.pdf)

The improvement on the single site concerns the actual old tuning as well as its proved bound. In normalized coordinates on the five active axes, the signed sample operators have norms at most one and two, and their sum has norm $\mu$ (its two defect columns are orthonormal before the factor $\mu$). Since the sigmoid is $1/4$-Lipschitz and each score is half a quadratic form, a normalized step moves the active vector $X$ by at most $\eta(\mu/4+5\|X\|^2/16)\|X\|$. If $R^2\le\mu/4$, induction gives $\|X_k-X_0\|\le(e^{0.01}-1)\|X_0\|$ for $k\eta\mu\le1/100$. On the standard Gaussian event that the positive tap coordinate lies in $[1,2]$, the reference readout coordinate lies in $[-2,-1]$, and the other three active coordinates have absolute value at most two, the reference score remains negative throughout. Indeed the initial opposite signs have magnitudes at least $R/\|g\|$, while the displacement is less than $(e^{0.01}-1)\sqrt{20}R/\|g\|$. Independence gives this event probability $(\Phi(2)-\Phi(1))^2(2\Phi(2)-1)^3>0.01$. Thus at 99% confidence the old normalized step $2^{-40}\sqrt2$ actually needs $\Omega(\sqrt d/\delta)$ passes.

There is a faster classifier on this class using ordinary **linear logistic GD**. Scale the raw observations to unit background, put $M=e+\lambda f$ with $\|f\|=1$, $\lambda=\sqrt2\mu$, $\alpha=\langle f,e\rangle=a/\sqrt2$, and start at zero. A normalized step of $8$ (physical step $16/L^2$) gives $w_1=-2\lambda f$. Residuals cancel on the span of $e,f$. For $x=\lambda\alpha$, $y=\lambda(\alpha+\lambda)$ the second iterate is
$$
w_2=2(\tanh x+\tanh y)e-2(2-\tanh y)\lambda f.
$$
Its reference score divided by two is at least $\lambda^2-(x^3+y^3)/3>0$, using $z-z^3/3\le\tanh z\le z$ for $z\ge0$ and $\alpha+\lambda<1/5$. The common negative raw score divided by two is at most $-\lambda^2(1-3\alpha^2-3\alpha\lambda-\lambda^2)<0$. Two full linear updates therefore suffice. The contribution is a finite-time, dimension-improved guarantee for joint training of the two-tap model and its direct extension to multiple sites. Optimality of its stepsize, radius range and logarithmic factor remains open.
