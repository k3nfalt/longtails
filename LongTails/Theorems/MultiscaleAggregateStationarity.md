# Finite-bit gradient aggregation with unbounded worker disagreement

Consider minimizing the average $F=(1/n)\sum_i f_i$ of differentiable worker objectives in Euclidean dimension $d$. Only the average needs an $L$-Lipschitz gradient and a finite lower bound, with initial gap at most $Δ$. At a queried point, worker $i$ receives an unbiased gradient sample with centered second moment at most $σ_i^2$. Samples are IID within each stream and the streams are independent. Local means may be arbitrarily far apart and sample magnitudes have no public bound. For example, workers fitting regressions with different empirical covariance matrices have gradient discrepancies growing linearly along some rays. Adding a logistic loss on a dense linear predictor gives interacting, nonlinear coordinates while preserving smoothness of the average. Even opposite quartic terms on a dense linear predictor can cancel in the average, allowing components with unbounded curvature. [Lean specification](../Setups/DistributedAggregateSmoothNonconvex.lean)

A separate root and $n$ workers communicate over dedicated bidirectional star links. One sample takes $h_i>0$ time at worker $i$. A message costs $ℓ+b(64+\text{payload bits})$ on its link. Different workers and links can operate concurrently. Parameters, including service times, noise bounds, accuracy and initial point, are public. The algorithm receives no function values, gradients of other workers, or hidden bound on disagreement. The setup allows arbitrary distributed algorithms. [Network](../Setups/StarNetwork.lean) · [Event and deadline semantics](../TemplateDistributed.lean)

## The guarantee

The root returns an independently, uniformly selected pre-update iterate $X$, with $\mathbb E\|\nabla F(X)\|\le ε$. For every seed and every earlier deadline the event execution terminates. The proved elapsed time is

$$
O\!\left(\left(1+\frac{LΔ}{ε^2}\right)
\left[\max_i h_i+\frac{\sum_i h_iσ_i^2}{n^2 ε^2}
+ℓ+b\bigl(64+d\log(2+n(d+1))\bigr)\right]\right).
$$

The one-line Lean budget gives the universal constants, integer rounding and binary bit lengths. It applies also at zero variance and zero dimension. It pays for an initial round even when $2LΔ\le ε^2$, where smoothness already certifies the initial point. ## Why packets can stay short

Set $α=ε/512$. Use $K=2^J$ iterations where $J$ is the binary length of $\lceil 2LΔ/α^2\rceil$. Each worker spends a batch on the current synchronized point. With $T=\sum_i h_iσ_i^2/(n^2α^2)$, worker $i$ takes exactly $m_i=\max(1,\lceil T/h_i\rceil)$ samples per iteration. Thus the batch phase takes at most $T+\max_i h_i$ and the variance of the averaged batch noise is at most $α^2$. This uses independence across workers and fresh samples at adaptive query points. [Batch allocation](../Proofs/MultiscaleAggregateBudget.lean) · [Adaptive moment proof](../Proofs/MultiscaleAggregateMoments.lean)

Workers round their local batch means on a grid of spacing $α/(d+1)$ and send the low-order bits of each integer coordinate. Addition modulo the common period preserves cancellation, however large the individual integers. The root adds the residues and sends the aggregate packet back to each worker except after the last update. Everyone reconstructs using the same earlier **aggregate** estimate as an anchor. They choose the congruent grid vector within distance $3r_k$ of that anchor if one exists, and otherwise use the anchor. The period gives uniqueness. The normalized descent step has length at most $α/L$. Both directions use finite bit lists, and all $K\sum_i m_i$ sample calls occur in the execution. [Codec](../Algorithms/MultiscaleAggregate.lean) · [Protocol and returned output](../Algorithms/MultiscaleAggregateProtocol.lean)

For $k>0$ the anchor index is $k-2^{v_2(k)}$, and $r_k=2^{v_2(k)}α$. At zero use the origin as gradient anchor and $r_0=Kα$. Smoothness bounds the change of the true gradient between the anchor and the current query by $r_k$. An unbounded noise sample can still cause a wrong lift. Using the immediately preceding estimate indefinitely could preserve such an error even after noiseless samples resume.

Let $e_k$ and $a_k$ be the decoded and pre-transmission rounded errors relative to the true gradient. Charge zero when $e_k\le a_k$, and charge $e_k+5r_k$ otherwise. For a noninitial query with parent $j$, the new charge is at most the parent's charge plus $12(a_j^2+a_k^2)/r_k$. The proof uses exact recovery for small endpoint errors and bounded movement of the decoder even after a wrong lift. Parent radii at least double, so summing inverse radii up a branch costs at most $2/r_k$. The raw second moments yield expected decoded error at most $198α$ at every query. This estimate requires neither a simultaneous good event nor bounded noise. The normalized descent inequality then gives the displayed expected stationarity guarantee. [Tracking inequality](../Proofs/MultiscaleAggregateTracking.lean) · [Descent and accuracy](../Proofs/MultiscaleAggregateAccuracy.lean)

The coordinate width is $4+\operatorname{bits}(n(d+1))+v_2(k)$, using $J$ in place of the valuation at zero. Across $K=2^J$ uploads the scale-dependent widths sum to exactly $K-1$ per coordinate. The downlink uses the same width schedule and omits the last packet. This deterministic identity controls the deadline for **every** noise realization. [Width identity](../Proofs/MultiscaleAggregateSchedule.lean)

## Comparison and limitations

Tyurin and Richtárik give a heterogeneous full-vector benchmark. Their Theorems 8–9 and Corollary 2 obtain the smooth nonconvex iteration and heterogeneous computation rates, with a transmission time for an entire real vector. Their squared-gradient tolerance $ε^2$ implies our norm tolerance $ε$ by Jensen. Our result proves the first moment directly and does not prove the converse. It supplies finite packets at both ends under centered variance, for a dedicated star with deterministic service times. The 2026 Grace/Leon analysis allows richer routed networks and charges transmitted coordinates, each carrying a real scalar. Those communication units do not determine a bit length for an unbounded scalar. [NeurIPS 2024, Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16218v2#A3) · [Tyurin, March 2026 preprint, §§1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20735v1)

Modulo coding itself is established. Bonawitz et al. aggregate finite-ring vectors, and Moniqua gives constant-width quantized decentralized SGD under its assumptions. Moniqua's Theorem 2 uses local smoothness, a uniform disagreement bound in A3, and an almost-sure sampled-gradient bound in A5 to choose the quantization range. Its squared-norm conclusion and decentralized topology are additional differences. Here cancellation is preserved before decoding, and the dyadic error estimate handles an unknown location of arbitrarily large observations repeatedly over a stochastic optimization trajectory. [Bonawitz et al., §III](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/281.pdf) · [Lu and De Sa, ICML 2020, §4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/lu20a/lu20a.pdf)

Davies et al. analyze distributed mean estimation with error controlled by the distances between worker inputs. At a fixed aggregate gradient these distances can be arbitrarily large in the present problem. Their variance-reduction model also takes local estimates of a common mean. [Davies et al., ICLR 2021, §§3–5](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=t86MwoUCCNe)

A single modulus covering the whole trajectory uses an extra order of $\log K$ bits per coordinate per round. A stronger comparison refreshes a wide anchor every $H$ rounds and references it directly inside each block. Normalized motion requires local range of order $Hα$, giving extra average width of order $\log H+(\log K)/H$. Taking $H\asymp\log K$ leaves order $\log\log K$. Multiscale tracking removes this term with the same batch order. Boosting local accuracy with confidence-dependent replication can instead trade extra computation for narrower packets. For fixed $n,d$, positive $σ_i\asymp ε$, and comparable sample and packet service costs, the width savings affect the total bound as $K$ grows. With fixed positive variance and hardware, the compute term eventually dominates as $ε\to0$. With noiseless gradients a one-step anchor suffices. These comparisons concern achieved bounds. There is no matching bit lower bound, optimality claim for the scalar grid's dimension dependence, or squared-norm conversion.

The result concerns one run with an accurate aggregate estimate at every query. Alternative randomized direction sketches, multiple runs with fresh output validation, and better high-dimensional lattice codes are plausible competitors. Their guarantees and validation costs would need a separate analysis. Local arithmetic and storage are free in this oracle model. In particular the proof does not bound the cost of computing large intermediate integers. What the event protocol clarifies is exactly when the root has the output and which workers may already be computing. The dedicated links, fixed 64-bit overhead and free local processing matter when interpreting its time as a systems prediction.
