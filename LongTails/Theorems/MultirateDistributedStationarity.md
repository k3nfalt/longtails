# How often should a slow worker refresh its gradient?

A slow worker can hold an indispensable part of the gradient even when that part changes little as the model moves. Recomputing it at every update charges its service time repeatedly. Dropping it biases the objective toward the faster workers. For smooth nonconvex optimization with data pinned to workers, a table of local gradients refreshed at different rates gives a third option. Its recurring time depends on the joint profile of local curvature, variance and evaluation time. ## Guarantee and information model

The objective is the equal-weight mean $F=n^{-1}\sum_i f_i$ in arbitrary finite dimension $d$. Each local mean gradient is $L_i$-Lipschitz. The global objective is bounded below, with initial gap at most $\Delta$. Worker $i$ alone can query its jointly measurable gradient oracle. Each fresh reply is unbiased at its query point and has centered second moment at most $\sigma_i^2$. Sampling is IID within workers and independent across workers. The algorithm knows these bounds, the initial point, the requested accuracy and the network parameters. Local objectives may be unbounded below, gradients at one point may disagree arbitrarily, and individual sample maps may be nonsmooth. These local smoothness and stochastic oracle assumptions are also used in Assumptions 2.1–2.4 of Mahran, Maranjyan and Richtárik. [Rescaled ASGD, v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13434v1)

One worker processes one batch at a time, paying $m h_i$ for $m$ samples at the same point. Every directed link, including a self-link, is FIFO. A payload of $B$ bits takes $\ell+b(64+B)$ time. Distinct links can operate concurrently with each other and with computation. Local arithmetic and buffering are free. All workers maintain the same numerical model. They select two independent uniform iteration indices using the shared seed and return the point with the smaller stored gradient estimate norm, breaking ties in favor of the first. This requires no fresh validation query. For the root's actual output $X$, the theorem proves measurability, integrability, and $\mathbb E\|\nabla F(X)\|^2\le\varepsilon^2$ under the joint law of the seed and the worker streams. The execution has finitely many events at every earlier deadline, including the stated deadline. [Physical oracle](../Setups/BandwidthHeterogeneousSmoothNonconvex.lean) · [Output accuracy](../Proofs/MultirateAccuracy.lean) · [Execution certificate](../Proofs/MultirateCertificate.lean)

Write $\bar L=\sum_i L_i/n$, $q=\Delta/\varepsilon^2$, and $r=1+\bar Lq$. Up to universal constants the elapsed time is

$$
\max_i h_i
+ \frac{\sum_i h_i\sigma_i^2}{n^2\varepsilon^2}
+ r\left[\ell+b\left(64+d\log\!\big(2+n(d+1)r\big)\right)\right]
+ q\left[\frac{\sum_i L_i h_i}{n}
+\frac{(\sum_i \sigma_i\sqrt{L_i h_i})^2}{n^3\varepsilon^2}\right].
$$

The first two terms pay for initializing every worker. In particular, an affine component with $L_i=0$ still contributes to initialization and to the algorithm's table. Zero variances are allowed. When all curvatures vanish, lower boundedness makes the aggregate gradient zero and the protocol returns the initial point. The full constants, rounding and bit lengths are in the public budget. ## Why the worker profile appears

Choose an internal precision $\delta=\varepsilon/512$. For integer batch sizes $m_i$, the stochastic part of the table has variance at most $\sum_i\sigma_i^2/(n^2m_i)$. The entries share adaptive history. Order the samples by the logical update at which each is first used. The later sample in any distinct pair is independent of its own query point and the earlier noise term, even when the two are first used in the same update. Conditioning makes the cross inner product integrate to zero. Subsequent reuse retains this fixed-update variance identity. [Causality](../Proofs/MultirateCausality.lean) · [Orthogonality](../Proofs/MultirateMoments.lean) · [Variance](../Proofs/MultirateVariance.lean)

The allocation must control both the longest initial job and the curvature-weighted recurring cost. Let $a_i=\sigma_i\sqrt{h_i}/(n\delta)$ and $b_i=\sqrt{L_i/n}$. Give worker $i$ half of each of the two variance shares $a_i^2/\sum_j a_j^2$ and $a_i b_i/\sum_j a_j b_j$, with a zero share for a zero denominator. The resulting share $s_i$ sets $m_i=\max(1,\lceil a_i^2/(s_i h_i)\rceil)$, taking $m_i=1$ when $a_i=0$. The shares sum to at most one. Before integer rounding, the first bounds each service time by $2\sum_i a_i^2$ and the second bounds the weighted sum of service times by $2(\sum_i a_i b_i)^2$. Rounding adds one local evaluation time per worker to these bounds. [Batch allocation](../Algorithms/MultirateBatches.lean) · [Allocation inequalities](../Proofs/MultirateBatchAllocation.lean)

With packet time $c$, let $t_i=m_i h_i+c$, $S=\max_i t_i$, and $D=\sum_i L_i t_i/\sum_i L_i$. Worker $i$ refreshes every $P_i=\lceil t_i/D\rceil$ logical updates. It computes the next required batch in advance, while faster workers process earlier updates. Its query point is less than $2P_i$ normalized steps old, and $\sum_i L_iP_i\le2\sum_i L_i$. This bounds the aggregate error from stale query points by $4\delta$, even when individual gradients have large offsets. Each worker sends coordinate residues to all peers. Decoding the sum, capping the aggregate estimate, normalized descent, and the two-index output rule use the existing finite-bit Leon analysis. The new scheduling argument allows a slow worker to lag behind the common update index while its batch is in progress. Each required send has a strictly earlier reservation than its consuming receive, which yields a deadline of $S+KD$ for $K$ updates. ## Comparison and regimes

For $L_i=L$ and $\sigma_i=\sigma$, let $\bar h=\sum_i h_i/n$ and $s=\sum_i\sqrt{h_i}/n$. The recurring compute bound is $O((L\Delta/\varepsilon^2)[\bar h+\sigma^2 s^2/(n\varepsilon^2)])$. Ringleader ASGD, Theorem 2, and Malenia SGD, Theorem A.4, give the profile $h_{\max}+\sigma^2\bar h/(n\varepsilon^2)$ inside the same iteration factor, granting free communication and using the papers' squared-norm tolerance $\varepsilon^2$. Ringleader's smoothness constant in Assumption 2 can be smaller than the common local bound. The direct comparison uses comparable smoothness constants and the common oracle subclass in which replies are gradients of differentiable sample losses, as required by Ringleader's Assumption 1. The Lean setup also covers jointly measurable unbiased estimates of finite variance that need not be gradients of sample losses. Malenia only needs global smoothness. Its broader-class lower bound, Theorem A.2, permits a local chain scaled by $n$ in Appendix E.1. Fixing comparable local curvatures removes that obstruction. In Tyurin's more recent Theorem 2.3, a strongly convex quadratic with unknown local affine offsets already requires an arithmetic-mean statistical cost to estimate the optimum. On that construction, converting distance to gradient norm gives the initialization variance term above. [Ringleader ASGD, ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/08d70284b013c03ba89cd2b642bc864b-Abstract-Conference.html) · [Fixed computation model, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12387) · [Tyurin, Theorem 2.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17483v1)

A family with several speed scales makes the distinction quantitative. For $g=0,\ldots,J$, give $2^{J-g}$ workers time $h_g=4^g$. Then $n=2^{J+1}-1$, $\bar h=\Theta(n)$, $h_{\max}=\Theta(n^2)$ and $s=\Theta(J+1)$. Take $L_i=\sigma_i=\Delta=1$, $\varepsilon=n^{-2}$, $1\le d\le n$ and fixed positive latency and bit cost. The new total time is $O(n^7(1+\log n)^2)$ versus the $O(n^8)$ compute-only upper bound obtained from Theorem 2 of Ringleader with constant aggregate smoothness. The initialization variance term costs $O(n^4)$, and all communication costs $O(n^5\log n)$. A nonlinear instance can take local losses consisting of cosines of several bounded, nonorthogonal linear forms plus local affine terms whose slopes sum to zero. Scaling the coefficients bounds each Hessian norm by one. A common ridge with nonvanishing curvature keeps aggregate smoothness of constant order, while worker-specific ridges couple coordinates. For independent centered additive vector noise $Z_i$ with the stated second moment, the sample loss $f_i(x)+\langle Z_i,x\rangle$ realizes the oracle in both formulations.

Synchronous batching under a common time cap $t$ permits at most $t/h_i$ samples per worker, giving the variance certificate $\sum_i h_i\sigma_i^2/(n^2 t)$. Repeating that cap for each update recovers the arithmetic-mean noise term. A two-level anchored surrogate can already improve the single-straggler example by refreshing the slow group less frequently. For several scales one can also group workers and block on periodic refreshes. To see the limitation of that particular reduction, let $w_g=2^{J-g}/n$, refresh group $g$ every $k_g$ steps with batch size $m_g$, and allow concurrent refreshes within a step. For its usual deterministic-age and variance estimates write $A=\sum_g w_g k_g$, $V=\sum_g w_g/m_g$, and let $C$ be its mean blocking time per step over a common period. Bin costs $h_gm_g$ in dyadic intervals $[t,2t)$. For each occupied bin put $W_t=\sum_{g\in t}w_g$, $H_t=\sum_{g\in t}w_gh_g$, and $k_t=\min_{g\in t}k_g$. Geometric summation gives $C\ge\tfrac12\sum_t t/k_t$, since the sum of occupied lower endpoints at any step is at most twice its largest refresh cost. Also $A\ge\sum_tW_t k_t$ and $V\ge\tfrac12\sum_tH_t/t$. Hölder gives $CAV\ge\tfrac14(\sum_t(W_tH_t)^{1/3})^3$. A bin spanning indices $a$ through $b$ has $W_tH_t\gtrsim 2^{b-a}$, so its cube root dominates the number of groups in the bin up to a universal constant. This serial certificate therefore pays $\Omega((J+1)^3)$ where concurrent refresh across logical updates pays $O((J+1)^2)$. This comparison concerns the stated periodic blocking analysis. Minimax optimality over the distributed oracle class remains open. [Auxiliary-gradient methods, Assumption 3.3 and Theorem 4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00395)

A stale gradient table already appears in DuDe-ASGD, whose Theorem 1 controls the largest delay. Rescaled ASGD, Corollary 4.2, additionally uses harmonic service periods and bounded relative heterogeneity. Here the times can be arbitrary positive reals, and the cost is guaranteed using known timing, smoothness and noise bounds. Ringleader also handles unknown and varying computation speeds. On bandwidth-limited networks, sending the full residue vector to each peer can cost more than Leon's partitioned aggregation, which sends about $d/n$ coordinates per link per phase. The speed-profile gain is thus most relevant when computation dominates and the complete multiport model is appropriate.
