# Gaussian variational inference with logarithmic initialization cost

For a target proportional to $e^{-V}$, minimize the Gaussian reverse-KL objective
$F(m,C)=\mathbb E V(m+C\xi)-\sum_i\log C_{ii}$, with $\xi\sim N(0,I_d)$ and $C$ lower triangular with positive diagonal.
The potential is $\mu$-strongly convex and $L$-smooth. The starting pair is within Euclidean/Frobenius distance $R$ of a Cholesky minimizer.
One query returns the exact $\nabla V$ at a chosen point. All draws belong to the algorithm.
The output is measurable and Cholesky for every seed, with integrable gap and $\mathbb E[F(\hat m,\hat C)-F^\star]\le\varepsilon$.

## Two public counts

Write $\kappa=L/\mu,\ \tau=3d+8\log(48\kappa),\ u=(2+\tau)\kappa,\ a=16u+2,\ x=\mu R^2/\varepsilon,\ y=d/\varepsilon$.
The budget is $\min\{N,B\}$, where
$$
T=\left\lceil (a/2)\log\max(1,x)\right\rceil,\quad
K=\lceil 2a(1+y)\rceil,\quad N=T+K,\qquad
B=\max\{1,\lceil u(26\sqrt{x}+32y)\rceil\}.
$$
$B$ is the sole previous upper bound for this setup. When $N\le B$, run $T$ constant proximal steps at $h=2/(\mu a)$, then restart the step index at zero. Step $k=0,\ldots,K-1$ uses $2/[\mu(k+a)]$. Return the average of these **post-update** pairs with weights $k+a-1$. Each update queries $m+C\xi$ once, applies the reparameterization gradient to location and lower triangle, and applies the negative-log prox to the diagonal. Only the second phase contributes to the average. When $B<N$, use the existing method at its original count. Execution exhausts the selected count, including the update producing the final average. The Gaussian draws, averaging, and public comparison use zero gradient queries. [Existing theorem and method](ReparameterizedGaussianVI.md)

**Exact crossover.** Let $s=u(26\sqrt{x}+32y)$. Since $N>1$, the two-phase branch is strictly cheaper exactly when $s>N$, ties exactly when $N-1<s\le N$, and is more expensive exactly when $s\le N-1$. The minimum therefore equals $B$ throughout $s\le N$ and never exceeds it. This test includes $x=0$, $d=0$, and both ceilings. Before rounding, the saving is
$$
u[26\sqrt{x}-32-8\log\max(1,x)]-\log\max(1,x)-4(1+y).
$$
For $y\le1$ the crossover is near $x=2.2$. For example, $x\ge2.23$ suffices uniformly there: the displayed saving exceeds two at 2.23 even at $u=2+8\log48,\ y=1$, and increases thereafter. Large $y$ can instead favor $B$ through the extra $4y$. For fixed $u,y$, the improvement factor grows as $\Theta(\sqrt{x}/\log x)$.

## Why the restart works

Write $r=\|m-m^\star\|^2+\|C-C^\star\|_F^2$, $D$ for the expected potential Bregman divergence from the optimum using the same Gaussian draw, and $A=L(2+\tau)$. The existing one-step estimate, $D^+\le F^+-F^\star$, and $h(8A+\mu)=1$ give the conditional inequality
$$
Q=r+2hD,\qquad \mathbb E[Q^+\mid m,C]\le (1-\mu h)Q+\mu h\,d/(2\mu).
$$
Smoothness gives $D_0\le Lr_0/2$ and hence $Q_0\le2R^2$. The definition of $T$ gives $(1-\mu h)^TR^2\le\varepsilon/\mu$. Thus the expected switch energy is at most $(2\varepsilon+d/2)/\mu$, including when $T=0$.

For a fixed switch point, the existing decreasing-step potential and convexity bound the expected suffix gap by
$$
\frac{2}{W}\left(\frac{\mu a^2}{4}Q_{\rm switch}+\frac{4KAd}{\mu}\right),
\qquad W=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}(k+a-1)\ge K^2/2.
$$
Induction integrating one fresh draw at a time proves the same estimate with expected switch energy and establishes integrability. Multiplication of $K\ge2a$ and $K\varepsilon\ge2a(\varepsilon+d)$ bounds the first summand by $\varepsilon/2$. The inequality $K\varepsilon\ge32Ad/\mu$ bounds the second by $\varepsilon/2$. The old proof starts its decreasing schedule at distance $R$, so its $R^2/K^2$ error term contributes $R/\sqrt\varepsilon$ to the count. The constant phase pays logarithmically to reduce that term before averaging.

## Prior work and limitations

The comparison below uses $n$ for iterations, $\delta$ for a target **squared distance**, and $\varepsilon$ for objective gap. Strong convexity gives parameter squared distance at most $2\varepsilon/\mu$ in expectation from this guarantee. A synchronous Gaussian coupling bounds squared Wasserstein distance by parameter squared distance. A distance guarantee alone does not supply an objective upper bound at small Cholesky diagonals.

- Stich derives a constant-then-decreasing schedule with suffix weighting under a stochastic-gradient second-moment condition. Lemma 3 and Theorem 5 applied to SGD (1) give, with the paper's horizon $T$ ($T+1$ iterates), a gap plus terminal-distance bound $64LR^2 e^{-\mu T/(4L)}+36\sigma^2/(\mu T)$. This already separates exponential forgetting from the statistical cost for smooth SGD. The present proof supplies a proximal recursion for Gaussian VI with the repository's expected-smoothness constants. [Stich (2019)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.04232)
- Lacoste-Julien, Schmidt and Bach use projected stochastic subgradients of bounded second moment $B_g^2$. In §3, equations (3)–(4), linear weights telescope to gap $2B_g^2/[\mu(n+1)]$, with zero coefficient on initial distance. Here the reparameterization estimator has a state-dependent moment bound. Gower et al., Theorem 3.2, (14)–(15), switch sampled smooth gradients after $4\lceil\mathcal L/\mu\rceil$ iterations. Their last-iterate squared-distance bound has terms of order $\sigma^2/(\mu^2 n)$ and $\lceil\mathcal L/\mu\rceil^2 R^2/n^2$. [Lacoste-Julien et al. (2012)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.2002) · [Gower et al. (2019)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.09401)
- For composite finite sums, Garrigos and Gower use a sampled smooth component gradient and the exact prox. Theorem 12.9 and Corollary 12.10 of the linked handbook give, for $0<\gamma\le1/(2L_{\max})$, expected squared distance at most $(1-\gamma\mu)^nR^2+2\gamma\sigma_F^\star/\mu$. Choosing a single step for accuracy incurs a logarithmic initial-distance factor multiplying the noise-dependent iteration count. [Garrigos and Gower](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.11235)
- Domke establishes the geometry of the reparameterized energy (Theorems 1 and 9). Domke, Garrigos and Gower use point gradients and the entropy prox. Their Theorem 2 bounds the energy-estimator second moment with quadratic coefficient $2(d+3)L^2$. Theorem 7, (11)–(12), gives geometric decay of $R^2$ to a step-dependent floor at an admissible fixed step, or $O((d+3)^2\kappa^4R^2/n^2)+O(1/n)$ in squared distance at decreasing steps, with a problem-dependent noise coefficient. Kim et al. already analyze a two-stage proximal reparameterization method in Theorem 4, update (2). With one draw its last-iterate distance count is $\max\{4T_\kappa,8\sigma^2/(\mu^2\delta)+4T_\kappa R/(e\sqrt{\delta})\}$, $T_\kappa=\lceil\kappa^2(d+k_\varphi)\rceil$, $k_\varphi=3$ for normal draws. The public switch here scales with the requested accuracy and the initial radius. [Domke (2020)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.08431) · [Domke et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.03638) · [Kim et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.15349)
- Lambert et al., Algorithm 1 and Theorem 4, use sampled gradients and Hessians. With smoothness normalized to one and curvature $\alpha$, they bound $\mathbb EW_2^2$ by $e^{-\alpha hn}W_{2,0}^2+36dh/\alpha^2$ for $h\le\alpha/60$ under covariance initialization bounds. Diao et al., stochastic forward-backward Algorithm 1 and Theorem 5.8, with curvature $\alpha=\mu,\beta=L$, obtain $e^{-\alpha n h/2}W_{2,0}^2+24\beta hd/\alpha$ for $h\le\alpha^2/(48\beta^3)$ using sampled gradient and Hessian. Their small-error count for $\alpha\mathbb EW_2^2\le\varepsilon$ is $O((d\kappa/\varepsilon)\log(\alpha W_{2,0}^2/\varepsilon))$, and they bound the expected minimum gap among the first $2n$ iterates, $\mathbb E[\min_{1\le k\le2n}(F(p_k)-F^\star)]$, which is a weaker statement than a bound on the expected gap of a returned point. Luu et al. use a score control variate with sampled Hessian. Under variance reduction along the path, their Theorem 4, (8), modifies both constants in Diao's exponential-in-$n h$ initialization term and $O(hd\beta/\alpha)$ floor in the small-step limit. [Lambert et al. (2022)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.15902) · [Diao et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.05398) · [Luu et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02490)
- Kim et al. analyze the Bonnet–Price estimator, which requires Hessians. With $\Delta^2=\mu\|z_0-z^\star\|^2$ (at most $\mu R^2$ here), their Theorem 3.3 and schedule (10) give terms $d\kappa/\varepsilon,\ \sqrt d\,\kappa^{3/2}\log(\kappa\Delta^2)/\sqrt\varepsilon,\ \kappa^2\log(\Delta^2/\varepsilon)$ for $\mu\mathbb EW_2^2\le\varepsilon$. In mean-field VI, Theorem 3.2 and Corollary 3.3 of the 2025 paper use reparameterized gradients and schedule (5), with distance complexity of order $g[\sigma_*^2/\delta+\sigma_*\log(\|z_0-z^\star\|^2)/\sqrt{\delta}]$ in the paper's notation, and $g$ quadratic in $\kappa$ in the generic case. The target family and error criterion differ, and both expressions are orders in the papers' parameter regimes. Section 2.4 and Proposition 2.9 of the same paper tune a constant-then-decreasing proximal schedule to the initialization and the noise and obtain a logarithmic dependence on the initial distance for the last-iterate squared distance; the mean-field restriction enters only in the later application. That proposition is the closest precedent found for the schedule used here. [Kim et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18718) · [Kim et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.21721)
- Kim, Ma and Gardner, Theorems 5–6 and Corollary 2, analyze projected BBVI with point gradients. For fixed-step closed-form entropy or sticking-the-landing estimators the squared-distance count has a $\kappa^2(d+O(1))\log(2R^2/\delta)$ factor times a noise-dependent factor of order $1+\text{noise}/\delta$. Perfect specification removes STL's noise term. Ko et al., Theorem 3, give $\max(C_{\rm var}/\delta,C_{\rm bias})\log(2R^2/\delta)$ for proximal SGD with $M$-sample structured reparameterization gradients, with component curvatures and effective dimension entering the constants. [Kim et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.14642) · [Ko et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.10989)
- Other oracle models in the baseline bibliography address different problems. Wu and Gardner, Theorem 1, (13), give averaged gap $V/(n+1)$ after $n$ updates with no explicit initial term for conjugate stochastic natural gradients. Kumar et al., Theorem 2, (21)–(22), contract the initial objective gap geometrically using exact Gaussian expectations in square-root natural-gradient VI. Sun et al., Theorem 4.1, give a stationarity bound of order $L\Delta_0/n+\sqrt{LV^2\Delta_0/n}$ for projected stochastic natural gradients in mean-field coordinates. Guilmeau et al., Proposition 4 for Algorithm 1, contract initial Bregman distance by $(1-mh)^n$ to a variance floor under relative strong convexity. BW-FISTA, Theorem 2, (5), uses a stochastic BW gradient with multiplicative noise, giving $O(LW_{2,0}^2/n^2+\bar\sigma W_{2,0}/\sqrt n)$ with a radius-dependent $\bar\sigma$. [Wu and Gardner](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.01870) · [Kumar et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.07853) · [Sun et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19163) · [Guilmeau et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00683) · [BW-FISTA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08468)

**A restart of the existing guarantee gives the same order.** Before it solves for its budget, the proof of the existing theorem bounds the expected gap of the weighted average after a block of $k\ge1$ calls from any Cholesky start $z$ by $332\mu u^2r/k^2+16ud/k$, with $r=\|z-z^\star\|^2$. On the Cholesky domain $\mu r/2\le F(z)-F^\star$. Let $J=\lceil\log_2\max(1,x)\rceil$ and $s_j=2^{J-j}\varepsilon$, and run blocks $j=0,\ldots,J$ of public lengths $k_j=\lceil\max(52u,128ud/s_j)\rceil$, each from the preceding average. If $\mu\mathbb Er\le s_j$ at the start of block $j$, both terms are at most $s_j/8$, so the expected gap after the block is at most $s_j/4$ and $\mu\mathbb Er\le s_{j+1}$ at the next start. The total is at most $(52u+1)(J+1)+256ud/\varepsilon$ calls: a logarithm of $x$ and a separate statistical term, from the moment estimates of the existing theorem alone. This staged halving uses the multistage restart argument of Ghadimi and Lan for strongly convex stochastic composite optimization, with the repository's weighted proximal method as the inner solver. The dependence on $x$ through a logarithm is therefore available from known tools, and it establishes no new rate for the problem class. What the present theorem adds is a certificate with a single switch, an exact minimum with the existing budget, and smaller constants: $(8u+1)\log\max(1,x)$ in place of $(52u+1)\log_2\max(1,x)$ for the start-up, and $32ud/\varepsilon+4d/\varepsilon+32u+4$ in place of $256ud/\varepsilon+52u+1$ for the remainder, before rounding. The restart argument is not formalised. [Ghadimi and Lan (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1137/110848876)

**Lower bound and remaining gap.** The repository contains no lower bound for this setup. Park, Jang and Chen's preprint, Theorem 3.1, gives $N\ge\kappa\exp[-C(\log\kappa)^{3/4}]$ for randomized pointwise derivative queries of any fixed finite order at fixed expected KL accuracy, dimension $\Theta(\log^2\kappa)$, and bounded initial gap. On that family strong convexity allows bounded $\mu R^2$, and the upper bound is $\kappa$ times a polylogarithmic factor. The lower bound does not resolve the dependence on $R,d,\varepsilon$ separately. With extra Hessian-Lipschitz regularity, their Theorems 4.1–4.2 give first-order counts $O((d\sqrt\kappa+\lambda_H^2d^3/\varepsilon)\log(2\Delta_0/\varepsilon))$, where $\lambda_H=L_H/\mu^{3/2}$ for the Hessian-Lipschitz constant $L_H$ and $\Delta_0=F(q_0)-F^\star+(\mu/2)\|z_0-z^\star\|^2$, at most twice the initial gap by strong convexity. [Park, Jang and Chen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21020)

The leading statistical term remains $32(2+\tau)dL/(\mu\varepsilon)$, with an additional $4d/\varepsilon$ in the two-phase count. The guarantee requires the public radius and curvature parameters and the two-phase branch returns the suffix average. An informal, unformalised importance-sampling argument replaces the tail-split factor by $d+1$. The literature search covered the initial theorem's bibliography and primary papers on switching schedules, weighted averaging, proximal Gaussian VI and BW gradients. The contribution is the explicit first-order objective-gap budget in this setup, a refinement of the existing certificate. The comparison establishes neither optimality nor priority for the schedule, and no paper found in the search states this full-Cholesky expected-gap certificate.
