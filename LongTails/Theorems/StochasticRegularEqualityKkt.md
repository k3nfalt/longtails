The [Lean theorem](StochasticRegularEqualityKkt.lean) gives a deterministic oracle budget for expected squared KKT accuracy in fully stochastic, nonconvex equality-constrained optimization. Its contribution is a variance-sensitive guarantee under global first-order smoothness and uniform constraint regularity, without a global objective-gradient bound, a constraint-value bound, an assumed region containing the iterates, or Lipschitz Hessians. The large geometry constants and the global regularity restriction are substantial limitations. This is not a claim of minimax optimality or an improvement over every smooth constrained problem.

The [specification](../Setups/StochasticRegularEqualities.lean) concerns minimizing a differentiable, lower-bounded function \(f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R\) subject to \(c(x)=0\), where \(c:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^m\), \(1\le m\le d\). Write \(J(x)=Dc(x)^*\). Its assumptions, for all points in the whole ambient space, are:

- \(\nabla f\) is \(L_f\)-Lipschitz; \(\|J(x)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le L_c\) and \(\|J(x)-J(y)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le L_J\|x-y\|\).
- \(\|J(x)v\|\ge\nu\|v\|\) for every \(v\), with \(\nu>0\). The known starting point satisfies \(f(x_0)-f_{\rm lower}\le\Delta\) and \(\|c(x_0)\|\le C_0\); neither a feasible starting point nor knowledge of \(f_{\rm lower}\) is needed by the algorithm.
- A jointly measurable reply \(U(x,\omega)=(G(x,\omega),C(x,\omega),H(x,\omega))\) has mean \(u(x)=(\nabla f(x),c(x),J(x))\). With the squared direct-sum norm equal to the sum of the three squared Euclidean/Hilbert–Schmidt norms,
  \[
  \int U(x,\omega)\,dp(\omega)=u(x),\qquad
  \int\|U(x,\omega)-u(x)\|_\oplus^2\,dp(\omega)\le\sigma^2,
  \]
  \[
  \int\|U(x,\omega)-U(y,\omega)\|_\oplus^2\,dp(\omega)
  \le L_{\rm noise}^2\|x-y\|^2.
  \]
  The specification explicitly includes the corresponding Bochner and scalar integrability conditions. There is no fourth-moment, bounded-sample, or independence-between-components requirement.

The original sample variables are abstract IID variables \(\xi_k\) on a probability space with law \(Q\), with common marginal \(p\); the theorem does not restrict the sample space to be finite or prescribe \(Q\) as a product measure. Calls \(2k\) and \(2k+1\) share \(\xi_k\). One call returns the joint triple at one point. The algorithm precommits both points in each difference pair. Reset, restoration, and validation samples also consume two calls, with the duplicate reply discarded. If the three primitive quantities are charged separately, multiply the stated budget by three. Shared-sample access is essential to the variance-reduction guarantee; independent fresh noise at the two difference points is a different oracle model.

The [algorithm and its output](../Algorithms/StochasticEquality.lean) use only \(x_0\), the displayed parameter bounds, and oracle replies. Each trial first performs robust feasibility restoration. It then runs a clipped recursive estimator with periodic resets and shared-sample differences, and takes a thresholded least-squares tangent step plus a normal feasibility step. A uniformly selected pre-update training iterate undergoes fresh robust validation. Validation uses the raw estimated gradient and constraint values, a clipped Jacobian, and an explicitly computed bounded multiplier. All trials execute. The output is the point and multiplier from the first accepted trial, or \((x_0,0)\) if none is accepted. It is neither a last-iterate guarantee nor an unverified choice of the best true residual.

For \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), define, only to display the formula compactly,
\[
S=2+L_f+L_c+L_J+\nu+\nu^{-1}+\Delta+C_0+L_{\rm noise},
\qquad \ell=1+\log(S/\varepsilon).
\]
The public Lean budget expands both quantities into setup parameters on one line. It is
\[
K=\left\lceil 10^{47}\,
\frac{(S^{38}+S^{48}\sigma^2+S^{60}\sigma/\varepsilon)\ell
      +S^{14}\sigma^2\ell^2}{\varepsilon^2}\right\rceil.
\]
Writing \((X,\widehat\lambda)\) for the actual `output` run by `execute`, and \(\mu\) for the product of the algorithm's internal seed law and the oracle's paired-stream pushforward of \(Q\), the result establishes measurability, integrability, and
\[
\int \bigl(\|\nabla f(X)+Dc(X)^*\widehat\lambda\|^2+\|c(X)\|^2\bigr)\,d\mu
\le\varepsilon^2.
\]
Consequently each expected residual norm is at most \(\varepsilon\), by Cauchy–Schwarz. This normalizes the comparison with expected-norm guarantees without claiming that the two moment statements are equivalent. The framework runs exactly \(K\) calls; any calls after the fixed plan finishes have ignored replies and are still charged.

At fixed geometry and initialization, the budget is
\(\widetilde O(\varepsilon^{-2}+\sigma\varepsilon^{-3}+\sigma^2\varepsilon^{-2})\).
This includes zero noise: every batch is at least one, and no inverse-noise tuning is used. Tuning is explicit and requires the supplied noise and geometry bounds; no adaptivity to unknown bounds is claimed. The [cost proof](../Proofs/StochasticEqualityCost.lean) counts initial resets, partial epochs, the case where the epoch exceeds the run length, all restoration and validation batches, the factor two per paired sample, and every trial. The powers of \(S\), including \(S^{60}\), belong to the proved guarantee; they are not suppressed in any finite-parameter comparison.

The central proof avoids differentiating the least-squares multiplier. A finite-difference Fletcher-merit argument controls its Lipschitz increment and absorbs its interaction with constraint violation into the normal-direction decrease. This yields a step size independent of the target accuracy under C1,1 regularity. The [descent proof](../Proofs/StochasticEqualityDescent.lean) is coupled to [stopped estimator and energy bounds](../Proofs/StochasticEqualityEnergy.lean): the stopping event is used only in the analysis, and the exit-causing step is retained. Coercivity of the merit and the initial objective gap supply gradient and feasibility caps on the controlled event. They are derived bounds, not extra setup assumptions. The stochastic recursion closes through movement energy rather than imposing a separate per-iteration high-probability accuracy condition.

The [amplification proof](../Proofs/StochasticEqualityAmplification.lean) is also part of the expected-accuracy claim. Disjoint IID sample blocks and independent internal indices give an exponentially small probability that every trial is rejected. A separate union bound controls all validation errors, without assuming those events are independent of training. Algorithmic clipping bounds the residual on every execution by \(10^{33}S^{38}/\varepsilon^4\); each exceptional event has probability at most \(\varepsilon^6/(10^{35}S^{38})\). Accurate accepted outputs contribute at most \(\varepsilon^2/4\), and the two exceptional contributions sum to at most \(0.02\varepsilon^2\). Thus a fixed-success result is not being passed off as expected accuracy. The final `admissible_execution` lemma transports this conclusion to the exact original oracle law and proves global measurability, including streams outside its paired support.

The closest primary comparison is [Cui, Shi, Wang, and Xiao, arXiv:2606.28230v1, June 26, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28230v1), particularly [Assumptions 1.1–1.4, Corollary 4.1, and Theorem 4.2](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/SmALM-5.pdf). That preprint gives expected \(O(\varepsilon^{-3})\) oracle cost with Lipschitz Hessians and a region containing every realization on which the true gradient and constraints are bounded. Its oracle assumptions motivate the finite-variance, mean-square smoothness, and uniform-rank conditions here. Our first-order global conditions replace its second-order and trajectory conditions, but are not a superset of its local hypotheses: global uniform regularity and global oracle bounds are stronger in spatial scope. On instances with accuracy-independent Hessian constants and suitable containment, its analysis already supports the same noise interpolation after retuning; no gain is claimed there.

A standard-reduction check rules out claiming novelty merely from fixed-noise \(\varepsilon^{-3}\) complexity without Hessian smoothness. On C1,1 instances where the required bounded region and containment can independently be justified, smoothing at radius \(h\asymp\varepsilon\) gives Hessian constants of order \(h^{-1}\). Retuning the prior analysis gives a step size \(\eta\asymp\varepsilon\), \(T\asymp(\eta\varepsilon^2)^{-1}\), and, up to fixed constants and initialization, work
\[
T+\frac{T\sigma^2}{\tau\varepsilon^2}+\frac{\tau\eta}{\varepsilon^2}.
\]
Balancing \(\tau\asymp\sigma/(\eta\varepsilon)\) already recovers fixed-noise order \(\varepsilon^{-3}\). The remaining distinction is retaining \(\varepsilon^{-2}\) iteration scaling without smoothing while proving localization. For \(\sigma=\varepsilon^a\), \(0<a<1\), the new leading exponent is \(3-a\), versus \(3\) in this checked smoothing reduction. At \(\sigma=\sqrt\varepsilon\), this is \(\varepsilon^{-5/2}\) versus \(\varepsilon^{-3}\), subject to constants and logarithms. This comparison retains the reduction's containment qualification; our localization theorem is not silently attributed to prior work.

Fresh batching in the present inexact-descent analysis would give \(\widetilde O(\varepsilon^{-2}+\sigma^2\varepsilon^{-4})\). This is an internal ablation, not a verified published baseline for the full setup. Beating both that ablation and the qualified smoothing comparator gives an exponent advantage \(\min(a,1-a)\) for \(0<a<1\). It does not establish a lower bound or exclude another reduction. The large powers and prefactor mean that the crossover can occur only at extremely small accuracy; if conditioning or dimension deteriorates with accuracy, the advantage can disappear. The claim is an asymptotic theory contribution, not practical superiority of these conservative parameters.

Other relevant baselines are the [November 6, 2025 exact-penalty method of Cui et al., Lemma 3.4, Corollary 3.2, and Theorem 4.1](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Fully_Sto_DLA-8.pdf), which charges constraint values separately from gradients, and [Zhang and Xiao, arXiv:2004.04357v2, May 14, 2021, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04357v2). In the nonsmooth exact-penalty route, value-estimation precision is stricter than gradient-estimation precision; the reported fixed-noise constraint-value cost is order \(\varepsilon^{-5}\), not \(\varepsilon^{-3}\). Counting only its gradient calls would be an invalid comparison. [Vary et al., ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/vary24a.html) studies random generalized-Stiefel constraints; its corrected [arXiv version 4, including Appendix F](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01702v4) must be used when comparing landing-field assumptions. That structured oracle and its safe-region requirements do not supply an unbiased nonlinear plug-in least-squares direction here. Conversely, compact manifolds such as the Stiefel manifold do not satisfy our global regularity condition, so this theorem does not replace that result.

A concrete motivating class is stochastic equilibrium input design. Fix dense matrices \(A,B\), with \(\|A\|\le\kappa<1\), and a bounded coordinatewise C1,1 saturation \(\phi\) whose derivative has absolute value at most one and is \(H\)-Lipschitz. Set
\[
c(u,v)=u-\mathbb E\phi(Au+Bv+\zeta).
\]
Uniform regularity follows from the \(u\)-block: \(\nu\ge1-\kappa\). With \(M=[A\ B]\), one may take \(L_c\le\sqrt m+\|M\|_{\rm HS}\) and \(L_J\le H\|M\|^2\). Dense couplings give interacting nonlinear coordinates and an implicit equilibrium, not a scalar or quadratic surrogate. A regularized objective consisting of a positive quadratic, dense log-cosh terms, and bounded sinusoidal terms is lower-bounded and has Lipschitz, generally unbounded gradient. It is covered without a global gradient cap. For \(\zeta=tZ\), a finite second moment gives joint constraint/Jacobian variance at most \(t^2(1+H^2\|M\|^2)\mathbb E\|Z\|^2\), with geometry bounds independent of \(t\); discrete noise and a C1,1 saturation need not produce a C2 mean constraint. This gives a meaningful vanishing-noise regime rather than renaming a parameter. Equilibrium models have independent motivation, for example [Winston and Kolter, NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/hash/798d1c2813cbdf8bcdb388db0e32d496-Abstract.html). The example here is smooth saturated input design with fixed \(A,B\), not unrestricted network-weight training or nonsmooth ReLU models; it illustrates the assumptions and is not itself a novelty claim.

The global rank, Jacobian, and oracle restrictions are used for perturbation control, restoration, and the bound on failed executions. Their necessity for the whole problem class is not established. Hilbert–Schmidt assumptions may incur \(\sqrt m\) conversions from operator-norm assumptions, so the result is not dimension-free in those alternative parameters. The example and the comparison do not justify removing any of these restrictions from the formal claim. Nor is there a minimax claim from unconstrained stochastic lower bounds: the setup permits sequential shared-seed queries, and transferring a simultaneous-query lower bound would require a separate argument.
