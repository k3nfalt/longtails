# High-confidence complexity of convex optimization with distance-growing subgradients

Let $x_0$ be feasible and within distance $R$ of a minimizer on a closed convex set $X$ in Euclidean space. At a feasible query $x$, the stochastic oracle returns a fresh vector $g(x,Z)$ whose mean is a subgradient relative to $X$. Its full second moment obeys
$$
  \mathbb E\|g(x,Z)\|^2\le G^2+B^2\|x-x_0\|^2 .
$$
This is the initial-point Blum–Gladyshev growth condition. It permits both unbounded domains and objectives with unbounded gradients. The only other oracle is exact Euclidean projection onto $X$. Both kinds of calls cost one unit. The algorithm knows $x_0,R,B,G,\varepsilon,\delta$. The setup requires joint measurability, integrability and IID sampling on an abstract probability space. It permits arbitrary algorithms. [Setup](../Setups/DistanceGrowingSubgradient.lean) · [Algorithm and output](../Algorithms/LocalizedClippedSubgradient.lean) · [Theorem](DistanceGrowingSubgradientUpperBound.lean)

**Guarantee.** Write $v=G^2+B^2R^2$. If $GR\le\varepsilon$, the algorithm returns $x_0$ with zero calls. Otherwise it returns a feasible point $\bar x$ satisfying $\Pr(f(\bar x)-\min_X f>\varepsilon)\le\delta$ in
$$
  2\left\lceil 1024\,R^2 v\log(8/\delta)/\varepsilon^2\right\rceil
$$
calls. The result holds in every finite dimension, including $B=0$, $G=0$, and every $0<\delta<1$. The paired lower bound establishes the necessity of every parameter factor, up to universal constants, for $d\ge2$, $0<\delta\le1/8$, and $0<65536\varepsilon\le GR$. The constant in that accuracy restriction comes from the hard instance. The intervening coarse-accuracy regime remains uncharacterized. [Lower theorem and hard instance](DistanceGrowingSubgradientLowerBound.md)

**Algorithm.** In the positive-budget branch use $N$ iterations, where $2N$ is the displayed budget. At the current iterate $x_t$, clip the sampled subgradient in Euclidean norm at $16Rv/\varepsilon$, take a step of length coefficient $\eta=\varepsilon/(64v)$, project arithmetically onto the known ball around $x_0$ of radius $R$, and query the projection oracle on the resulting point. Output the average of $x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1}$. Each iteration uses one sample and one domain projection. There is no objective-value query or access to the minimizer.

**Proof.** Each projection decreases squared distance to any point belonging to its set. Both sets contain $x_0$ and the chosen minimizer. Thus every sample is taken at a feasible point within the radius-$R$ ball, where its second moment is at most $v$. The two projections need only this distance property, so no projection onto an intersection has to be computed. The clipping bias has norm at most $v/(16Rv/\varepsilon)$. A conditional exponential estimate for squared distance plus cumulative objective gaps gives the factor $\log(8/\delta)$, and convexity transfers the average gap to the returned point. The IID proof uses the even-indexed samples of the actual execution because projections occupy the intervening calls. At zero budget the subgradient inequality at $x_0$ gives gap at most $GR$. **Comparison.** Alacaoglu, Malitsky and Wright's Assumption 3.1 uses this initial-point moment model. Their Corollary 3.4 bounds the expected gap of a weighted Halpern average by $(3BR^2+2G^2/(3B))/\sqrt{k+1}$. Taking an admissible growth coefficient at least both $B$ and $G/R$ gives the same $\varepsilon$ exponent and radius scaling. Direct application of Markov's inequality to that estimate costs a factor $\delta^{-2}$ in calls. The repository's terminal clipping theorem already gives a high-probability **last-iterate** guarantee. With the same retuning it pays $\log^2(8/\delta)$. Here the output is an average, and confidence costs one logarithm. Recent analyses also study expected last-iterate accuracy on unbounded problems. [Alacaoglu et al., §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09951v3#S3) · [Terminal clipping](ClippedBgLastIterate.md) · [Last-iterate analysis, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07334v1)

Known clipped methods for a uniform second moment already explain the upper rate after localization. The contribution of the pair is the minimax characterization and the converse under interpolation and zero initial variance. At $B=0$, bounded-moment high-confidence optimization is established. The new converse matters particularly when $BR\gg G$, where using only the initial scale $G$ would miss an arbitrarily large factor. The rate concerns the full norm moment. Tighter directional moment information can support more refined guarantees. [Uniform-moment comparison, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00260v1#S2) · [Companion lower bound](DistanceGrowingSubgradientLowerBound.md)

A familiar nonlinear family consists of finite averages of convex smooth losses and a convex Lipschitz penalty, with access to a sampled loss gradient plus a subgradient of the penalty. For example, include both the logistic loss with a sampled nonorthogonal covariate and the dense quadratic regularizer in each smooth sample loss, using $H=0$. The regularizer is $\|Cx\|^2/2$. For sample gradients with Lipschitz constants $L_Z$, a penalty with subgradients of norm at most $H$, and finite second moments at the anchor, one may take $B^2=2\mathbb E L_Z^2$ and $G^2=2\mathbb E(\|\nabla f_Z(x_0)\|+H)^2$. Taking $C$ of full column rank ensures existence of a minimizer. Arbitrarily many interacting coordinates are possible. The theorem requires neither this decomposition nor smooth samples.
