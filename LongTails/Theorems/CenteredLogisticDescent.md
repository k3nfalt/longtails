# Two passes of logistic gradient descent on low-contrast classification data

Full-batch gradient descent on a learned two-tap cyclic filter and readout strictly classifies every training observation after **two updates and exactly $2n$ indexed queries**. The data share a background and have a small class-dependent signal. The step size uses only the observed root-mean-square feature norm. On an explicit family with arbitrarily many distinct samples, classical batch perceptron needs more than $\lfloor1/\mu\rfloor$ updates, where $\mu$ is the contrast. Even joint logistic GD from the same initialization needs order $1/\mu$ updates over a fixed interval of smaller step sizes. ## Data and accuracy

Let $D_i=y_i x_i$ be the signed observations in $\mathbb R^d$, with labels $y_i\in\{-1,1\}$. Write $E$ for the uniform average over the $n$ samples, $P$ for the cyclic coordinate shift and $R=(E\|x_i\|^2)^{1/2}>0$. Write $x_i/R=\ell e+h_i$, with $\|e\|=1$, $E h_i=0$ and $\ell^2+E\|h_i\|^2=1$. Thus $e$ is the direction of the common empirical background up to sign. Set $m=(E D_i)/R$ and $T=m-\langle e,m\rangle e$. The geometric conditions are

$$
\max\{\max_i\|h_i\|,\|m\|\}\le\varepsilon\le 1/32,\qquad
\min_i y_i\langle h_i,T\rangle>(9+17\|Pe-e\|)\varepsilon^3.
$$

The gap measures how well the signed class mean separates the observations after removing their background. It can be checked without running GD. Balanced labels automatically satisfy the signed-mean bound when the scatter bound holds. Mild imbalance is possible, subject to $|\ell|\,|E y_i|\le2\varepsilon$. For example, take odd $n\ge257$, $E y_i=1/n$, shift-invariant $e$, unit $v\perp e$, $\varepsilon=1/128$, and $h_i=(\varepsilon/2)(y_i-1/n)v$. Normalizing $\ell$ as above gives $\|m\|\le\varepsilon$ and a projected gap of at least $\varepsilon^2/8$. The setup allows $d,n\ge2$.

One source of this geometry is aligned observations of a common template with a small class-dependent deviation, as in registered reference-image inspection. For balanced labels, take $x_i=b_0+y_i\tau v+w_i$, with $\|v\|=1$ and arbitrary $\|w_i\|\le c\beta^2\tau$, $0\le c\le1/8$. If the projection of $v$ orthogonal to the empirical background has norm at least $\beta>0$, the projected gap before RMS normalization is at least $\tau^2(\beta^2-3c\beta^2-2c^2\beta^4)$. Indeed, the two projected factors differ from the signal by at most $2c\beta^2\tau$ and $c\beta^2\tau$. The normalized scatter and signed mean are bounded by $(1+2c\beta^2)\tau/R$. Thus sufficiently small $\tau/R$ at fixed $\beta$ suffices, including unpaired, nonorthogonal perturbations throughout the ambient space. Template inspection and centroid classification motivate the model. The cubic threshold is specific to the two-step dynamics. [Reference-image inspection dataset](https://ietresearch.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1049/joe.2019.1182) · [Tibshirani et al., centroid classification](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12011421/)

## Algorithm and proof

The score of $\theta=((a,b),v)$ on a signed row $D$ is $s(D,\theta)=a\langle D,v\rangle+b\langle PD,v\rangle$. The loss is $E\log(1+\exp(-s(D_i,\theta)))$. Each indexed query returns one signed score and its parameter derivative. Only $n,d$ are public. The algorithm scans the examples at the identity filter and zero readout. The readout derivative there is $D_i$, which gives $R$. It initializes the GD trajectory at $((R^{-1/2},0),0)$ and uses the fixed physical step $8/R$. The first scan also supplies the first gradient exactly, since scaling both layers by $c$ multiplies the score by $c^2$ and its derivative by $c$. A second scan at the first iterate supplies the second gradient. The algorithm returns the second iterate. Its signed score is positive on every example, deterministically. [Execution proof](../Proofs/CenteredLogisticExecution.lean) · [Homogeneity and GD steps](../Proofs/CenteredLogisticSteps.lean) · [Component logistic derivative](../Proofs/FiniteScaleConvolutionalSgdGeometry.lean)

In normalized units $z_i=D_i/R$, the start is the unit impulse with zero readout and the step is 8. The first readout is $4m$. For $C=E[z_i z_i^\top]$, the second readout $W$ satisfies

$$
W=8(m-Cm)+8r,\qquad \|W-8T\|\le56\varepsilon^3.
$$

The linear term follows by expanding the sigmoid around zero. The covariance splits as $C=\ell^2ee^\top+E[h_i h_i^\top]$, so the large background component cancels. The cubic sigmoid remainder, residual covariance, and energy normalization control the remaining error. Both filter taps also move on the second step. Bounding their action on the shifted background gives a uniform signed-score error of at most $(72+136\|Pe-e\|)\varepsilon^3$ around $8y_i\langle h_i,T\rangle$. [Readout estimate](../Proofs/CenteredLogisticReadout.lean) · [Joint score estimate](../Proofs/CenteredLogisticDynamics.lean)

A merely positive projected gap can be too small. In three dimensions let $e=e_1$, $v=(e_2+e_3)/\sqrt2$, $y=(+,+,-,-)$, $a=(2/5,3/5,t/5,1-t/5)$ and $h_i=y_i(t^3 e/2+t a_i v)$. Set $\ell=\sqrt{1-E\|h_i\|^2}$ and let $t\downarrow0$. The minimum projected gap is $t^3/10$. Here $m=t^3 e/2+tv/2$, the first logits are $O(t^2)$, and the sigmoid expansion gives $\langle e,W\rangle=O(t^5)$ and $\langle v,W\rangle=4t+O(t^3)$. The second filter tap is $2t^2+O(t^4)$ since $\langle Pv,v\rangle=1/2$. On the third observation the second raw linear score divided by $t^3$ tends to $4/5$. The learned second tap adds a term tending to $-4\sqrt2$ on this scale since $\langle Pe,v\rangle=1/\sqrt2$. The first joint score divided by $t^3$ tends to $-8/5$, so both joint iterates fail on that observation. This example explains the cubic order. The constants in the sufficient condition allow slack.

## Same-data comparisons

Consider the signed pairs $e+\mu u_j,\;-(1+\mu)e+\mu u_j$, with a unit constant background $e$ and $u_j\perp e$. The explicit construction takes $n=4k+4$, $d=2k+4$ and $2k+2$ linearly independent residuals satisfying $\|u_j\|\le2/3$ and $\langle u_i,u_j\rangle\ge1/32$. Every signed observation is distinct. For every $k\ge0$ and $0<\mu\le1/4096$ these data satisfy the setup. All three methods start at the zero predictor. [Instance and inclusion proofs](../Proofs/CenteredLogisticInstances.lean) · [Residual construction and independence](../Proofs/ClusteredConvolutionalPerceptronInstances.lean)

| Method on these signed observations | Full passes to strict training separation |
| --- | --- |
| Joint logistic GD, step $8/R$ | At most 2 |
| Classical batch perceptron from zero, any fixed positive multiplier | More than $\lfloor1/\mu\rfloor$ |
| Joint logistic GD, same parameter initialization, any fixed step in $[1/R,2/R]$ | More than $\lfloor1/(2048\mu)\rfloor$ |

Batch perceptron adds a positive multiple of the average of signed rows with nonpositive scores, using zero contributions for the other rows. Its count follows from the existing two-sided perceptron lower bound after identifying the observations index by index. For smaller-step joint GD, the normalized readout's background coordinate is trapped in $[-4\mu/R,-\mu/(8R)]$ following the first update, throughout the stated horizon. Meanwhile the filter and tangential readout drift by $O(t\mu/R)$, leaving a positive-class example misclassified. This comparison holds with the architecture, start, batches and accuracy criterion fixed. Each full pass costs $n$ indexed evaluations when evaluated afresh. With cached raw rows all three methods can read the dataset once and simulate subsequent passes locally. The separation then concerns their update counts. An unrestricted method can also compute a linear separator directly from stored rows. [Matched batch perceptron](../Proofs/CenteredLogisticComparison.lean) · [Smaller-step joint GD](../Proofs/CenteredLogisticDelay.lean) · [Trajectory invariant](../Proofs/CenteredLogisticSlowTrajectory.lean)

For the online perceptron, the order and the cost unit matter. Visit the positive and negative member of each pair consecutively, cycle through the pairs, and update from zero on each nonpositive margin. The same sample identity and the existing cyclic perceptron lemma imply that the *currently queried* sample has a nonpositive margin for every $t\mu\le1$. Each such step costs one indexed observation. The two-pass GD bound is strictly smaller in component calls when $2n\le\lfloor1/\mu\rfloor$, including regimes with $n$ growing and $n\mu\to0$. This comparison is specific to the stated visit order. [Index matching](../Proofs/CenteredLogisticComparison.lean) · [Cyclic perceptron proof](../Proofs/TwoSidedPerceptronLinearComparison.lean)

## Relation to prior results

- **Tyurin and PR 253.** Tyurin's ICML 2026 Theorem 2.1 relates GD at large initialization to a quadratic perceptron. Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 analyze a noisy violation-priority quadratic method and an $\Omega(d/\mu)$ delay for classical batch perceptron on the two observations in equation (5). Appendix G studies image data and varying initialization and steps. PR 253 proves finite logistic GD on the two observations from a small random sphere with an explicit confidence parameter. The present inclusion proof covers the exact signed pair $\mathbf1,-(\mathbf1+\mu e_j)$ for every $d\ge3$, coordinate $j$, and $0<\mu\le1/10$. The two-pass guarantee also covers the growing-rank clouds above. The deterministic impulse initialization and RMS step change PR 253's trajectory, so its small-sphere guarantee remains a separate result. [Tyurin, Theorems 2.1, 5.1–5.2 and Appendix G](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11587v2) · [ICML proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/tyurin26a.html) · [PR 253](https://github.com/k3nfalt/longtails/pull/253)
- **Check against Tyurin's image experiments.** We evaluated the calibrated two-step rule on all raw MNIST training images in the two class pairs from Appendix G.1.10–11. We flattened pixels in row-major order, applied the one-coordinate cyclic shift, and used the lower digit as the positive class. For 0 versus 1 and 7 versus 8 the maximum normalized scatters were 1.3803 and 1.2842, both above $1/32$, and the minimum projected gaps were $-0.1518$ and $-0.0786$. The second iterate strictly classified 12,593 of 12,665 and 11,606 of 12,116 examples, respectively. Thus these raw-image problems lie outside the proved regime and two steps leave training errors. Tyurin used a different initialization and swept step sizes. [MNIST data](https://keras.io/api/datasets/mnist/) · [Tyurin, Appendix G](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11587v2)
- **What the filter contributes.** The first two readouts agree exactly with raw linear logistic GD at normalized step 8, whose physical linear step is $8/R^2$ from zero. The readout estimate also gives positive raw linear scores under the stated gap. Calibration already accelerates linear GD here. PR 254 proves a different feature-learning advantage against *all* constant-step linear GD on a family with orthogonal sum and difference feature groups. Its positive difference-margin condition excludes the constant reference observation. The present result isolates the calibrated step's effect and controls the coupled filter motion. [PR 254](https://github.com/k3nfalt/longtails/pull/254)
- **Other GD bounds.** The two-step spectral polynomial $2\eta-\eta^2\lambda$ is classical for least squares. It predicts cancellation in the linearization. The nonlinear error on each observation and the long smaller-step trajectory require additional control. Zhang et al. give an inverse-squared-margin burn-in for risk-adaptive linear GD in Theorem 2.1 and a worst-case first-order lower bound on a larger class in Theorem 3.2. Muşat and Boumal's Theorem 1.4 obtains arbitrarily small logistic risk with a data-dependent iteration count and sufficiently large step by comparison with a hard perceptron. Bao's Theorem 23 concerns early angular alignment under linear logistic GD. These guarantees leave open the two-pass strict-separation question for the coupled filter and readout considered here. Our guarantee concerns training signs on low-contrast, projected-centroid-separable data. Prescribed logistic risk and population accuracy are outside its scope, and the training margins can vanish with contrast. [Celisse and Wahl, §2.2, Example 2](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume22/20-358/20-358.pdf) · [Zhang et al., v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04105v2) · [Muşat and Boumal, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26748v1) · [Bao, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04382v1)
