# Accelerated composite minimization with quadratic cumulative weights

For the same composite-gap problem as [CompositeGapAccelerated](CompositeGapAccelerated.md), the final primal point attains gap at most $\varepsilon$ within the following shared gradient-and-prox budget:
\[
 \left\lceil 32(2+\sqrt2)R\sqrt{H_0/\varepsilon}
       +85(1+R\sqrt{H_1})\log(2+\Delta/\varepsilon)\right\rceil .
\]
At fixed positive $H_0,R,\Delta$ and fixed $H_1$, this changes the small-accuracy bound from $O(\varepsilon^{-1/2}\log^2(1/\varepsilon))$ to $O(\varepsilon^{-1/2})$. [Lean theorem](CompositeGapQuadraticWeights.lean)

## Problem and counted execution

In a real Hilbert space, minimize $F=f+\psi$ on a convex set $Q$. The smooth part $f$ is convex and differentiable throughout the ambient space. The regularizer is convex on $Q$. A minimizer $s$ and feasible initial point $x_0$ satisfy $\|x_0-s\|\le R$ and $F(x_0)-F(s)\le\Delta$. For $x\in Q$ and any ambient $u$ satisfying $\sqrt{H_1}\|u-x\|\le1$, the linearization error of $f$ at $x$ is at most $[H_0+H_1(F(x)-F(s))]\|u-x\|^2$. The parameters $H_0,H_1\ge0$ and $R,\Delta,\varepsilon>0$ are public. Each exact gradient query and each exact minimization of $\psi(u)+\langle v,u-c\rangle+\|u-c\|^2/(2A)$ over $Q$ costs one call. [Setup](../Setups/CompositeGapSmooth.lean)

Let $S=\max(0,\lceil\log_2(\Delta/\varepsilon)\rceil)$ and $d_k=\Delta/2^k$. In phase $k<S$, set $M=H_0+H_1d_k+d_k/(2R^2)$ and take
\[
 m_k=\left\lceil\sqrt{128+256H_1 R^2+256H_0R^2/d_k}\right\rceil
\]
updates. Initialize $x=z=c$ at the preceding output (initially $x_0$), with $A=0,v=0$. For $i=1,\dots,m_k$, take $a=i/(128M)$, $B=A+a$, and $\theta=a/B$. Query the gradient $g$ at $y=(1-\theta)x+\theta z$, accumulate $v'=v+ag$, and query the prox with arguments $(B,c,v'/B)$. Its reply $z'$ gives the new state $((1-\theta)x+\theta z',z',v')$ and mass $B$. The minimizer and objective values enter only the proof. [Algorithm](../Algorithms/CompositeGapQuadraticWeights.lean)

The plan finishes after exactly $C=2\sum_{k<S}m_k$ calls, including the last prox reply. Its output is $x_0$ at call zero when $\Delta\le\varepsilon$. At any execution budget $K\ge C$, the template uses the remaining $K-C$ calls for gradients at $x_0$ and retains the completed plan's output. The accuracy and feasibility proof holds for every seed. [Execution proof](../Proofs/CompositeGapQuadraticWeights.lean)

## Why quadratic weights remain local

Write $H$ for $f$ minus its affine tangent at $s$, and $J=F-F(s)-H$. Both are nonnegative on $Q$, and $H$ is nonnegative everywhere. The cumulative model $W$ is the sum of the weighted linearizations of $H$ plus $AJ$ and $\|\cdot-c\|^2/2$. Its minimizer is $z$. The induction maintains $W(z)\ge A(F(x)-F(s))$. For every feasible $u$, strong convexity of $W$ and the tangent lower bounds then give
\[
 A(F(x)-F(u))+\tfrac12\|z-u\|^2\le\tfrac12\|u-c\|^2 .
\]
Choosing $u=c$ gives $\|z-c\|^2\le 2A(F(c)-F(x))$. Choosing $u=s$ bounds the distance to the same minimizer across restarts. [Shifted model and energy](../Proofs/CompositeGapModel.lean)

Fix $d=d_k$ and $\rho=\sqrt{d/M}$. After $n$ updates both center distances are at most $K_n=(n+1)\rho/8$, by the energy inequality, $A=n(n+1)/(256M)$, and convex mixing. Since $\theta=2/(n+2)$, $\theta K_n\le\rho/4$ and $\|y-x\|\le\rho/2$. The local model at $x$ applies throughout the ambient $\rho$-ball. The shifted gradient bound there at the anchor is $4M\rho$, so $H\le6d$ throughout the ball. Testing the tangent at $y$ a distance $\rho/2$ along its shifted gradient gives $\|\nabla H(y)\|\le G=12M\rho$. Comparing the prox model at $z$ and $c$ bounds $J(z)\le d+K_nG$. Convexity of $J$ now gives $F(y)-F(s)\le10d$. [Local estimates](../Proofs/CompositeGapQuadraticWeightsLocal.lean)

To apply the model at $y$ to the next primal point, write $D=\|z'-z\|$. Strong convexity of the old model and minimality for the new one give $D^2/2\le aGD+aJ(z)$. Multiplication by $\theta^2$, using $64\theta a M\le1$ and $\theta J(z)\le4d$, gives
\[
 (\theta D)^2/2\le (12/64)\rho\,\theta D+(4/64)\rho^2 .
\]
Hence $\|x'-y\|=\theta D\le\rho$. Only now is the model at $y$ needed. Its coefficient is at most $10M$, and $20Ma^2\le B$ lets the quadratic term be absorbed by the old model's strong convexity. This reestablishes the energy inequality. At phase end, $A_{m_k}d\ge R^2$, so comparison at $s$ gives a gap at most $d/2$. [Phase induction](../Proofs/CompositeGapQuadraticWeightsPhase.lean)

Each phase costs at most $34(1+R\sqrt{H_1})+32R\sqrt{H_0/d_k}$. The latter terms grow by $\sqrt2$ per phase, and the last active $d_k$ exceeds $\varepsilon$. The sum is bounded by the last term times $1/(1-1/\sqrt2)=2+\sqrt2$. For active schedules $S\le(5/2)\log(2+\Delta/\varepsilon)$, giving the stated constants. [Budget proof](../Proofs/CompositeGapQuadraticWeightsBudget.lean)

## Comparison over the full parameter range

The only earlier upper bound for this specification is $\lceil8192(1+\sqrt B)\log(4+B)\ell\rceil$, where $B=T+h$, $T=H_0R^2/\varepsilon$, $h=H_1R^2$, and $\ell=\log(2+\Delta/\varepsilon)$. Its proof charges all phases at a common worst-case length and uses geometric mass growth within a phase. The new **published ceiling is strictly smaller for every valid parameter choice**, including either or both curvature coefficients equal to zero. [Earlier theorem](CompositeGapAccelerated.lean)

In detail, $\ell\ge\log 2\ge2/3$. Dividing the new unrounded expression by $\ell$ gives at most $85+164\sqrt T+85\sqrt h$, since $48(2+\sqrt2)<164$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $164\sqrt T+85\sqrt h\le\sqrt{164^2+85^2}\sqrt B<195\sqrt B$ for $B>0$, with equality of the two zero terms at $B=0$. Thus the new expression is at most $195(1+\sqrt B)\ell$ even for inactive schedules. The old expression exceeds this envelope by more than one since $\log(4+B)>1$ and $\ell\ge2/3$. Taking ceilings preserves the strict improvement. The actual plans both finish at call zero for $\Delta\le\varepsilon$.

There is no repository lower bound for this specification. For the subclass $H_1=0$, $Q=\mathbb R^d$, $\psi=0$, take an $L$-smooth convex function with $L=2H_0$. Specify the prox reply as $c-av$ for every real $a$: for $a>0$ this is the required minimizer, and for $a\le0$ the setup permits this extension, so prox replies are computable from their inputs. With $\Delta\ge LR^2/2$ the supplied gap bound admits the entire smooth radius-$R$ class. Drori's Theorem 3, equations (1.3) and (4.1), gives worst-case error at least $LR^2/(2\theta_N^2)$ with $\theta_N=O(N+1)$ in dimension $d\ge N+1$ for deterministic first-order methods even with values and gradients. This accounts for the $\Omega(R\sqrt{H_0/\varepsilon})$ scale at small accuracy. It leaves open the necessity of the restart logarithm and the $H_1$ dependence, and does not give the randomized, all-parameter lower theorem for this setup. [Drori](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.01424)

## Prior work and limitations

- **Accelerated cumulative prox models.** Xiao's Algorithm 3, Theorem 6 and Corollary 7 use a globally Lipschitz smooth gradient, with a prox minimization for the regularizer. Equations (37)–(38) yield quadratic accumulated weights. In the deterministic specialization, the gap is at most $4LD^2/t^2$ with prox-function value at a solution bounded by $D^2$. A single global $L$ suffices there. Here the coefficient must be justified at each queried extrapolation point. [Xiao](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume11/xiao10a/xiao10a.pdf)
- **Scheduled restart.** Roulet and d’Aspremont, Proposition 2.2 and equations (8)–(11), restart accelerated gradient on smooth convex objectives satisfying a sharpness inequality on the initial sublevel set. For quadratic growth, fixed-length phases cost $O(\sqrt{L/\mu}\log(\Delta/\varepsilon))$ gradient iterations. Their contraction uses a shrinking distance estimate from growth. Here distance remains bounded by the same $R$ and it is the curvature bound that decreases. [Roulet and d’Aspremont](https://vroulet.github.io/files/vroulet_restart.pdf)
- **Gap-dependent smoothness.** With $F_0=f(x^0)-f^\star$ and $0<\varepsilon\le F_0$, Lobanov's Theorem 5, Algorithm 2 and equations (22), (28) give $O(\widetilde R\sqrt{H_0/\varepsilon}+\widetilde R\sqrt{H_1}\log(F_0/\varepsilon))$ accelerated iterations under smooth-only local gap curvature, for fixed tuning parameters, with the paper's convention that the logarithmic factor is at least one. The paper uses the initial sublevel-set radius $\widetilde R$, full gradients, gap values and an exact segment solve per iteration. Its local radius is $1/[(2\sqrt3+\sqrt6)\sqrt{H_1}]$, infinite at $H_1=0$. Its geometric sum already separates the accuracy term. Here $R$ bounds a single initial distance and a nonsmooth term contributes to the curvature. [Lobanov](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04884v1)
- **Projected stochastic acceleration.** Lobanov, Dvinskikh and Gasnikov use a smooth-only gap model, clipped stochastic gradients and auxiliary projections. In the zero-noise specialization of Theorem 9, equation (44) and Appendix H.3, the constant and curvature costs scale as $S+D(\sqrt{H_0/\varepsilon}+\sqrt{H_1}J_H)$ up to the paper's logarithmic factors. $D$ is the auxiliary diameter and $J_H\le S$ counts curvature-dominated phases. Theorem 10 also assumes strong convexity and can shrink the auxiliary balls. A prox over $Q$ for an arbitrary $\psi$ supplies neither a smooth gradient of $F$ nor a prox over arbitrary ball intersections. [Lobanov, Dvinskikh and Gasnikov](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33415v1)
- **Adaptive composite acceleration.** Li and Lan's AC-FGM, Corollary 1 and equations (2.13), (2.37), uses gradients, smooth-function values and a regularizer prox. The gap decays as $\widehat L_k/[k(k+1)]$ times their initialization factor, where $\widehat L_k$ includes the largest observed curvature. Applying this estimate here would require a bound on those observations and the extra value oracle. [Li and Lan](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.10082v3)
- **Composite reductions.** Bok and Altschuler's Theorem 3.4 (Theorem 2.6 of the preprint) converts suitable global smooth-convex gradient certificates into gradient-and-prox guarantees. With normalized smoothness one, the resulting gap bound is $(1+\xi)\|x_0-s\|^2/(2R_n)$, conditional on nonnegative transformed multipliers and the positive-semidefinite certificate defined in preprint equation (3.3). Local total-gap curvature provides no global cocoercivity constant for that argument. [Published theorem](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-026-02377-7) · [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.23756)
- **Gradient-dependent curvature.** Under the unconstrained smooth Hessian condition $\|\nabla^2 f\|\le L_0+L_1\|\nabla f\|$, Vankov et al., Theorem 6.2 and equations (20), (22), require, for $0<\varepsilon<L_0/(5L_1^2)$, $O((m+1)(1+R\sqrt{L_0/\varepsilon})+L_1^2R^2+1)$ first-order queries, with $m$ queries charged per segment search. Tyurin's Theorem 4.2, equation (11), gives $5R\sqrt{\ell(0)/\varepsilon}$ plus accuracy-independent initialization and logarithmic overhead using gradients under its tuning hypotheses, with $\ell(t)=L_0+L_1t$ in this specialization. These constants control the gradient norm of a smooth objective. Theorem 4.3, equation (13), refines the gradient-dependent startup cost to order $L_1\bar R$ times a logarithm plus a logarithmic initialization term. No conversion from the stated total composite gap to such a Hessian inequality is assumed. [Vankov et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10800v2) · [Tyurin](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06884v2)
- **Repository gradient-dependent composite methods.** The known-parameter theorem already gives an accuracy term of order $R\sqrt{(L_0+L_1G)/\varepsilon}$ and a startup term of order $(1+L_1R)$ times a logarithm, under gradient-dependent smoothness and $\|\nabla f(s)\|\le G$. Its backtracking counterpart uses gradients and prox queries with additional logarithmic search costs. These are bounds for different specifications. [Known parameters](CompositeGeneralizedAccelerated.lean) · [Backtracking](BacktrackingCompositeGeneralized.lean)

The comparison covers the papers cited by the earlier contribution, related gradient-dependent acceleration, scheduled restart and classical first-order lower bounds. The formal improvement concerns the shared exact-oracle composite specification. Its tuning uses the public parameter bounds, its proof needs ambient test points at feasible anchors, and the guarantee retains a logarithmic term even at $H_1=0$.
