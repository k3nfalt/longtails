# Light-tail-type high-probability last-iterate bound for plain SGD on PL objectives under finite-moment noise

The new result is a high-probability oracle-complexity guarantee for **unmodified SGD** (no clipping, normalization, batching, restarts, averaging or iterate selection) on smooth objectives satisfying the Polyak–Łojasiewicz (PL) inequality, when the stochastic gradient noise has a bounded variance \(\sigma^2\) and a bounded \(q\)-th central moment \(\nu^q\) for some \(q>2\). With \(\kappa=L/\mu\) and \(\Lambda=\log(3/\delta)\), the **last iterate** satisfies \(f(x_T)-\inf f\le\epsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\) after

\[
T=\left\lceil 44\,\kappa\sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{\epsilon}}+\frac{1024\,\sigma^2(\kappa+\Lambda)}{\mu\epsilon}
+\Big(\big(64\kappa^2(\nu/\sigma)^2\Lambda\big)^{q}(3/\delta)^2\Big)^{1/(q-2)}\right\rceil
\]

stochastic gradient evaluations, i.e.

\[
T=O\!\left(\kappa\sqrt{\Delta/\epsilon}+\frac{\sigma^2\big(\kappa+\log(1/\delta)\big)}{\mu\epsilon}
+\Big(\kappa^2(\nu/\sigma)^2\log(1/\delta)\Big)^{q/(q-2)}\delta^{-2/(q-2)}\right).
\]

The step sizes are \(\eta_t=4/(\mu(t+t_0))\) with \(t_0=\lceil 8L/\mu\rceil+2\): they use only \(L\) and \(\mu\), not \(\sigma,\nu,q,\Delta,\epsilon,\delta\) or the horizon. Every term that depends on the accuracy \(\epsilon\) involves only the variance \(\sigma^2\); the heavy tail (\(\nu,q\)) enters **only** through an \(\epsilon\)-independent burn-in, and \(\log(1/\delta)\) multiplies \(\sigma^2/(\mu\epsilon)\) but not \(\kappa\sigma^2/(\mu\epsilon)\).

The public [Lean theorem](HeavyTailedPLLastIterate.lean) is `Convergence spec budget` for the [setup](../Setups/HeavyTailedPL.lean) and the [algorithm](../Algorithms/ScheduledSgd.lean). Supporting proofs: [HeavyTailedPLStep](../Proofs/HeavyTailedPLStep.lean) (smoothness descent, weighted one-step inequality), [HeavyTailedPLNoise](../Proofs/HeavyTailedPLNoise.lean) (truncation and bias bounds), [HeavyTailedPLProcess](../Proofs/HeavyTailedPLProcess.lean) (stopped process, per-step moment-generating-function bounds, pathwise invariant), [HeavyTailedPLEvents](../Proofs/HeavyTailedPLEvents.lean) (three tail events, first-bad-time contradiction), [HeavyTailedPLTuning](../Proofs/HeavyTailedPLTuning.lean) (burn-in and accuracy algebra), and [HeavyTailedPLConvergence](../Proofs/HeavyTailedPLConvergence.lean) (assembly, execution, i.i.d. transport).

## Setup and assumptions

Let \(E\) be a real inner product space (complete, second countable; the bounds are dimension-free) and \(f:E\to\mathbb R\). The instance assumptions are:

- **L-smoothness.** \(f\) has gradient \(\nabla f\) everywhere and \(\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|\le L\|x-y\|\).
- **Global PL inequality.** \(f\) is bounded below and \(\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\ge2\mu\,(f(x)-\inf f)\) for all \(x\) (Polyak 1963; Karimi–Nutini–Schmidt, ECML-PKDD 2016). No convexity is assumed.
- **Initial gap.** \(f(x_0)-\inf f\le\Delta\).
- **Oracle.** One query returns \(g(x,\xi_k)\) where \(g\) is jointly measurable and the samples \(\xi_0,\xi_1,\dots\) are i.i.d. with law \(P_0\) under an abstract probability measure (`StochasticGeneralized.IID`). Unbiasedness \(\int g(x,\cdot)\,dP_0=\nabla f(x)\), variance \(\int\|g(x,\cdot)-\nabla f(x)\|^2dP_0\le\sigma^2\), and \(q\)-th moment \(\int\|g(x,\cdot)-\nabla f(x)\|^q dP_0\le\nu^q\) with \(q>2\), for every \(x\).

Valid parameters satisfy \(0<\mu\le L\), \(\sigma,\nu>0\), \(q>2\), \(\Delta\ge0\), \(\epsilon>0\), \(0<\delta<1\). The finite-\(q\)-th-moment noise model with \(q>2\) is the one of Lou–Zhu–Wu (JMLR 2022) and Wu (arXiv 2609.30499, Assumption 2.3); the PL/smoothness pair is the one of the focus paper below (its Assumptions 2.4 and 3.6). The i.i.d. online oracle model contains finite-sum uniform sampling, but the moment bounds here are required **uniformly in \(x\)** (the standard bounded-moment model of the clipped-SGD baselines below), whereas the focus paper bounds a conditional Orlicz norm of the noise along the trajectory; finite sums whose components have different curvature have noise growing with \(\|x\|\) and can violate a uniform bound. The two noise conditions are therefore not nested.

**Information available to the algorithm.** The setup's public input is only \((E,L,\mu,x_0)\); everything else (\(\sigma,\nu,q,\Delta,\epsilon,\delta\), the sample space) is hidden from the algorithm and used only by the budget. **Admissible algorithms** (`allowedAlgorithm`) are exactly plain SGD \(x_{t+1}=x_t-\eta_t\,g(x_t,\xi_t)\) with a deterministic positive step sequence and last-iterate output (`ScheduledSgd.method η x0`); the proof constructs \(\eta_t=4/(\mu(t+t_0))\) and checks membership.

**Accuracy criterion.** `successful` requires a measurable output and \(P\{\epsilon<f(x_{\rm out})-\inf f\}\le\delta\). One oracle call per iteration; the budget is the number of iterations. The Lean statement is proved for every \(K\) at least the budget.

**Nonlinear instances.** The class contains nonconvex functions with interacting coordinates in every dimension. Example: \(f(x)=h(Ax)\) with \(h(y)=\sum_{j=1}^m\big(y_j^2+3\sin^2 y_j\big)\) and \(A\in\mathbb R^{m\times d}\) of full row rank, \(m\le d\), not diagonal. The scalar function \(y^2+3\sin^2y\) is nonconvex (\(h''\in[-4,8]\)) and PL with constant \(1/32\) (Karimi–Nutini–Schmidt 2016, Section 2), so \(h\) is 8-smooth and \(\tfrac1{32}\)-PL; since \(\|\nabla f(x)\|^2=\|A^\top\nabla h(Ax)\|^2\ge\sigma_{\min}(A)^2\|\nabla h(Ax)\|^2\) and \(\inf f=\inf h\), \(f\) is \(8\|A\|^2\)-smooth and \(\sigma_{\min}(A)^2/32\)-PL, nonconvex, with a non-isolated minimizer set when \(m<d\). Least squares \(\tfrac12\|Ax-b\|^2\) with rank-deficient \(A\) (convex, not strongly convex) is another member. Noise: additive Student-\(t\) noise with more than \(q\) degrees of freedom, or log-normally scaled sampling noise (all moments finite, not sub-Weibull), satisfy the moment assumptions. Constants are dimension-free: only norms of the noise enter.

## Algorithm and oracle cost

The method is \(x_{t+1}=x_t-\eta_t g(x_t,\xi_t)\), \(\eta_t=\frac{4}{\mu(t+t_0)}\), \(t_0=\lceil 8L/\mu\rceil+2\), output \(x_T\). The public budget in the Lean file is the single line displayed above (`budget`), counting one oracle call per iteration. The step schedule is anytime; only the stopping time depends on \((\sigma,\nu,q,\Delta,\epsilon,\delta)\).

## Comparison with the closest prior results

- **Focus paper: Tong, Ikeda, Kawasumi, "High-Probability Guarantees for SGD under \(\beta\)-Heavy-Tailed Gradient Noise", arXiv 2609.32195v1 (26 Sep 2026; unreviewed).** Theorem 3.7 (PL, step \(4/(\mu_0(t+t_0))\), last iterate): \(\mathcal L_S(w_{T+1})-\inf=O\big(e^{2\log^{1/\beta}(T/\delta)}\sqrt{\log T\,\log(e/\delta)}/T\big)\), with constants depending on the smoothness, the Orlicz scale \(K\) and the trajectory constant \(G\). It assumes (i) conditional Orlicz \(\Psi_\beta\) tails, \(\Psi_\beta(x)=e^{\log^\beta(x+1)}-1\), so all moments are finite; (ii) the trajectory cap \(\eta_t\|\nabla\mathcal L_S(w_t)\|\le G\) (Assumption 2.5); (iii) a finite dataset. The present theorem removes (ii) and (iii), replaces (i) by the standard uniform bounded-moment model — finite variance plus a finite \(q\)-th moment for one \(q>2\), uniformly over \(x\). These noise conditions are not nested: \(\beta\)-heavy noise with a uniform Orlicz scale satisfies ours for every \(q\), but the focus paper's trajectory-wise condition in a finite-sum model need not give a uniform-in-\(x\) moment bound. The theorem also removes the multiplicative factor \(e^{2\log^{1/\beta}(T/\delta)}\sqrt{\log T}\) from the leading term: for fixed \(\delta\) and \(\epsilon\to0\) the complexity is \(O(\sigma^2(\kappa+\log(1/\delta))/(\mu\epsilon))\) instead of \(\epsilon^{-1}\exp(2\log^{1/\beta}(1/(\epsilon\delta)))\cdot\mathrm{polylog}\). For \(\beta\)-heavy noise one may apply the theorem with any fixed \(q\) (e.g. \(q=4\), with \(\nu\) the corresponding Orlicz-moment bound); the price is the burn-in term, which for small \(\delta\) at fixed \(\epsilon\) can exceed the focus paper's \(\delta\)-dependence \(e^{2\log^{1/\beta}(1/\delta)}\), so the improvement is for \(\epsilon\) small relative to the burn-in, not uniform.
- **Li and Liu, ICML 2022 (PMLR 162)**, Theorem for PL with sub-Weibull noise, and **Madden, Dall'Anese, Becker, JMLR 2024** (Theorem 8, PL, sub-Gaussian, \(O(L\sigma^2\log(e/\delta)/(\mu^2T))\)): light-tailed only; Madden et al. state their PL argument does not extend beyond sub-Gaussian noise. The present bound has the same order under polynomial tails and splits the leading term as \(\sigma^2(\kappa+\log(1/\delta))\) instead of \(\kappa\sigma^2\log(1/\delta)\).
- **Sadiev, Danilova, Gorbunov, Horváth, Gidel, Dvurechensky, Gasnikov, Richtárik, ICML 2023** ([arXiv 2302.00999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00999)), Theorem 3.1 Case 2 / Theorem E.4: *clipped* SGD under PL with an \(\alpha\)-th central moment, \(\alpha\in(1,2]\), last iterate; at \(\alpha=2\): \(O\big(\Delta e^{-\mu K/(L\log(K/\delta))}+L\sigma^2\log(K/\delta)\log^2(B_K)/(\mu^2K)\big)\), with step and clipping level depending on \(\Delta,\sigma,K,\delta\). For the problem class (smooth PL, finite variance) clipping already achieves \(\log(1/\delta)\) dependence **without** a \(q>2\) assumption and with a better initialization term; the present result is therefore **not** a problem-class improvement. It is a guarantee for plain SGD, with a step that needs only \((L,\mu)\), without the \(\log(K/\delta)\log^2 B_K\) factor in the leading term, under the stronger assumption \(q>2\).
- **Ou and Wang, arXiv 2609.03990 (unreviewed)**: staged centered-clipped mirror descent under PL, \(\tilde O(\kappa\log(\Delta_0/\epsilon)+\kappa(\sigma^2/(\mu\epsilon))^{\alpha/(2(\alpha-1))})\) with a matching lower bound at constant confidence, so \(\kappa\sigma^2/(\mu\epsilon)\) is the optimal leading term for the class; the present leading term matches it up to constants.
- **On the role of \(q>2\).** Sadiev et al. (Theorem 2.1 / D.1) prove that plain SGD with a **constant** step \(0<\gamma\le1/\mu\) and finite variance only cannot have \(\log(1/\delta)\) dependence (it needs \(\Omega(\sigma/(\mu\sqrt{\delta\epsilon}))\) iterations on a quadratic). For the \(1/t\) schedule used here, a single-shock heuristic (one noise value of order \(\sigma\sqrt{T/\delta}\) near the end) suggests a polynomial-in-\(1/\delta\) penalty under finite variance alone; this is not formalized, so the necessity of a moment beyond the second for this schedule is a heuristic, not a theorem.
- **Reductions.** (a) Markov on the in-expectation bound \(\mathbb E f(x_T)-\inf f\lesssim t_0^2\Delta/T^2+\kappa\sigma^2/(\mu T)\) gives \(T\gtrsim(\kappa\sqrt{\Delta/\epsilon}+\kappa\sigma^2/(\mu\epsilon))/\delta\) (a \(1/\delta\) factor on the leading term); (b) hypothetically, if a PL bound \(\mathbb E[r_T^{q/2}]^{2/q}\lesssim\kappa\nu^2/(\mu T)\) were available (we found none in the literature), Markov would give \(r_T\lesssim\kappa\nu^2\delta^{-2/q}/(\mu T)\); (c) treating the truncated noise as sub-Gaussian with proxy \(M^2=\nu^2(T/\delta)^{2/q}\) gives \(\kappa\nu^2(T/\delta)^{2/q}\log(1/\delta)/(\mu T)\) — the focus paper's multiplicative structure; (d) Wu's nonconvex average-gradient bound (below) gives only a best-iterate \(1/\sqrt T\)-type rate under PL. The present bound improves (a) and (c), and the hypothetical (b), by removing every \(\delta\)- or \(\nu\)-dependent factor from the \(\epsilon\)-dependent terms, **provided** \(T\) exceeds the burn-in. Regime caveat: the burn-in carries \(\kappa^{2q/(q-2)}\delta^{-2/(q-2)}\); for \(q<4\) and very small \(\delta\) at fixed \(\epsilon\) the total can exceed the Markov bound (a) (at \(q=3\) the burn-in is \(\propto\delta^{-2}\)). For every fixed \(\delta\) and \(q>2\) the result is better than (a)–(c) once \(\epsilon\) is small enough.
- **Wu, arXiv 2609.30499v2 (7 Oct 2026; unreviewed)**: plain nonconvex SGD with finite \(p>2\) moments, predictable localization and a Hilbert-space Fuk–Nagaev inequality, high-probability average-gradient bound \(\Delta/(\alpha T)+L\alpha\sigma^2+\sigma^2\log(6/\delta)/T+L\alpha\nu_p^2T^{2/p-1}\delta^{-2/p}\). It has no PL or last-iterate result; the present theorem is the PL last-iterate counterpart, proved by a different (exponential-supermartingale) argument. **Lou, Zhu, Wu, JMLR 2022**: Nagaev-type bounds for averaged SGD in the linear model.
- **Fatkhullin, Hübler, Lan, "Can SGD Handle Heavy-Tailed Noise?"** (arXiv 2508.04860v2, SIAM J. Optim.): strongly convex projected SGD with \(p\le2\) moments in expectation; high-probability statements only via Markov. Different regime (\(p\le2\), bounded domain).

## Mechanism

Write \(r_t=f(x_t)-\inf f\), \(n_t=t+t_0\), \(\varepsilon_t=g(x_t,\xi_t)-\nabla f(x_t)\). Truncate at \(M=\nu(3T/\delta)^{1/q}\): \(\tilde\varepsilon=\varepsilon\,1\{\|\varepsilon\|\le M\}\), \(c(x)=\mathbb E\tilde\varepsilon\), \(\zeta=\tilde\varepsilon-c\). Then \(\|\zeta\|\le2M\), \(\mathbb E\|\zeta\|^2\le\sigma^2\), and by Cauchy–Schwarz and Markov \(\|c\|^2\le\sigma^2\nu^q/M^q=\sigma^2\delta/(3T)\). On the event that no \(\|\varepsilon_t\|\) exceeds \(M\) (probability \(\ge1-\delta/3\) by a \(q\)-th-moment Markov bound and a union bound), smoothness and PL give with \(W_t=(n_t-1)(n_t-2)\), \(a_t=4(n_t-1)/\mu\):

\[
W_{t+1}r_{t+1}\le W_tr_t+\underbrace{\Big(-a_t\langle\zeta_t,\nabla f(x_t)\rangle-\tfrac{a_t}8\|\nabla f(x_t)\|^2\Big)}_{D_t}+\frac{8(n_t-1)}{\mu}\|c\|^2+\frac{16L}{\mu^2}\|\tilde\varepsilon_t\|^2 .
\]

The martingale part is controlled by one exponential supermartingale with a **uniform** parameter \(\lambda=\mu/(32ns^2)\), \(n=T+t_0\), \(s^2=\sigma^2+t_0M\sqrt{L\Delta}/n\): Bernstein's bound for the bounded centered variable gives \(\mathbb E e^{\lambda D_t}\le1\) as long as \(2\lambda a_tM\|\nabla f(x_t)\|\le1\), the negative term \(-\frac{a_t}8\|\nabla f\|^2\) absorbing the variance \(\lambda a_t^2\|\nabla f\|^2\sigma^2\). This range condition is enforced by **predictive localization**: the process is frozen at the first time it fails (a stopped transition), so a single exponential Markov inequality at the final time controls the frozen sum (no maximal inequality is needed). The squared-noise sum \(\sum\|\tilde\varepsilon_t\|^2\) is controlled separately by a nonnegative bounded mgf (\(\le2T\sigma^2+M^2\Lambda\)), which is why \(\kappa\sigma^2\) is not multiplied by \(\log(1/\delta)\). Finally, a pathwise argument at the first bad time shows that on the three good events the localization never triggers: there \(r_\tau\ge\|\nabla f(x_\tau)\|^2/(2L)\) would exceed the bound \(W_\tau r_\tau\le N\) as soon as \(T+t_0\ge64\kappa^2(M/\sigma)^2\Lambda\) — this is the burn-in. The floor \(s^2\ge t_0M\sqrt{L\Delta}/n\) in \(\lambda\) handles the early iterations (\(\|\nabla f(x_0)\|\le\sqrt{2L\Delta}\) is within range) at the cost of a cross term absorbed by AM–GM.

## Limitations and open questions

- **Not a problem-class improvement**: clipped methods reach \(\log(1/\delta)\) under finite variance alone (Sadiev et al. 2023; Ou–Wang). The contribution is the guarantee for unmodified SGD and the parameter-free step.
- **Burn-in.** The \(\epsilon\)-independent burn-in \((\kappa^2(\nu/\sigma)^2\log(1/\delta))^{q/(q-2)}\delta^{-2/(q-2)}\) is not shown to be optimal. A single-shock argument on \(f(x)=\mu x^2/2\) (a noise value of size \(\nu(T/\delta)^{1/q}\) at step \(\approx T\)), not formalized here, shows that plain SGD with this kind of step needs \(T\gtrsim(\kappa\nu^2/(\mu\epsilon))^{q/(2q-2)}\delta^{-1/(q-1)}\); by Young's inequality this is dominated by the second and third budget terms, but the gap between \(\delta^{-2/(q-2)}\) and \(\delta^{-1/(q-1)}\) (and in the \(\kappa\)-exponent) is open. It also degenerates as \(q\downarrow2\) and when \(\nu/\sigma\) is large.
- **Initialization.** The \(1/t\) schedule costs \(\kappa\sqrt{\Delta/\epsilon}\) iterations for the initial gap, versus \(\kappa\log(\Delta/\epsilon)\) for GD-type or clipped methods with a constant-step phase.
- **Global PL** is assumed on all of \(E\), as in the focus paper; local PL results for neural networks do not imply it.
- The noise moments are assumed **uniformly in \(x\)**; noise whose scale grows with \(\|x\|\) (e.g. finite sums with heterogeneous curvature, or Wu's distance-dependent moments) is not covered.
- Noise with only \(p\le2\) moments is not covered. For constant steps, plain SGD cannot achieve \(\log(1/\delta)\) dependence under finite variance alone (Sadiev et al., Thm 2.1); for the \(1/t\) schedule the corresponding statement is only heuristic here.
