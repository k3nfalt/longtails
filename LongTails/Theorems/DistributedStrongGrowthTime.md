# How much statistical information can heterogeneous workers supply under strong growth?

With noisy gradients and unequal worker speeds, sampling the fastest workers most often can change the objective being minimized. Insisting on an unbiased estimate of the uniform objective can be expensive. This result determines the leading statistical time for the standard **mean-squared strong-growth condition on worker gradients**. The answer depends on the entire speed profile. Both the harmonic-throughput and arithmetic-time laws arise as endpoints. [Lean theorem and public deadline](DistributedStrongGrowthTime.lean) · [Setup](../Setups/DistributedMeanStrongGrowth.lean) · [Algorithm and output](../Algorithms/DistributedGrowthSgd.lean)

## Problem and physical execution

There are $n$ convex differentiable local objectives on $\mathbb R^d$. Their average $F$ has $L$-Lipschitz gradient, a nonempty minimizer set $S$, and global quadratic growth $F(x)-F^*\ge(\mu/2)\operatorname{dist}(x,S)^2$. A minimizer lies within $R$ of the initial point. The public constant $\rho\ge1$ bounds the uniform second moment of the **local mean gradients** for every $x$,

$$
\frac1n\sum_i\|\nabla f_i(x)\|^2\le\rho\|\nabla F(x)\|^2.
$$

This is the finite-sum strong-growth condition with uniform worker sampling. A worker's actual gradient reply can have nonzero variance even at a minimizer. Each local stochastic oracle is jointly measurable, unbiased and has centered second moment at most $\sigma^2$. Samples are IID within workers and independent across workers under an abstract joint probability law. The smoothness and growth constants, hardware times, $R,\sigma,x_0$ and accuracy $\varepsilon$ are public. The algorithm has access to local functions only through worker queries. [Vaswani, Bach and Schmidt, Eq. (2)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/vaswani19a/vaswani19a.pdf) · [Formal assumptions](../Setups/DistributedMeanStrongGrowth.lean)

The network is a star with a separate coordinator. Worker $i$ completes one sample in $h_i>0$ seconds and has one computation slot. Each directed coordinator–worker link has one message slot. Links run concurrently and can overlap computation. A payload of $B$ bits costs $\ell+b(64+B)$ seconds, including the template clock header. Latency and per-bit time may be zero. Local arithmetic is free. The coordinator returns the iterate after its two scheduled phases. The theorem proves measurability, integrability, and $\mathbb E\operatorname{dist}(X,S)^2\le\varepsilon$ for the actual output under the template's joint seed law. The execution has finitely many events at **every deadline up to and including** the public bound. ## Elapsed time and the choice of weights

Define $I$ as the value of a strictly convex problem in the public parameters,

$$
I=\min\left\{\sum_i\frac{a_i^2}{h_i}:\quad
        \sum_i a_i=n,\quad \sum_i a_i^2\le n\rho\right\}.
$$

Its minimizer $u$ is positive even though signed $a_i$ are allowed. To compute it, set $v_i(t)=h_i/[(1-t)h_i+t]$ and $u_i(t)=nv_i(t)/\sum_jv_j(t)$. Choose $t\in[0,1]$ satisfying $\sum_i u_i(t)^2\le n\rho$ with equality whenever $t<1$, and set $u_i=u_i(t)$. Continuity gives such a parameter, and strict convexity gives the same profile for every valid choice. This single scalar definition covers identical speeds, inactive constraints, and $\rho=1$. It preserves the intermediate regimes without sorting workers into speed groups. The update weights are $w_i=u_i/h_i$. [Weight construction](../Algorithms/StrongGrowthWeights.lean) · [Feasibility and variational inequality](../Proofs/StrongGrowthWeights.lean)

Write $A=6nL(\max_i u_i)\sum_i h_i^{-1}/(\mu I)$. Up to universal constants, the verified deadline is

$$
\frac{\sigma^2}{\mu^2 I\varepsilon}
+
A\left[1+\log\left(1+\frac{R^2}{\varepsilon}\right)\right]
\left[\max_i h_i+\ell+b\left(64+
d\log\left(3+d+A+\frac{R^2+(\sigma/(3nL))^2}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\right].
$$

The first term counts statistical work. The second pays for at least one evaluation by **every** worker per round, and for uploading and broadcasting the finite precision vectors. The underlying two-phase protocol batches in proportion to $1/h_i$, clips and encodes the weighted batch means, and uses a constant step followed by diminishing steps. It runs $2K+1$ batches per worker, including the last batch after the returned index $2K$. The initialization cost, finite precision, and final batch are all included. Additional inputs to the reused scheduler calibrate the step and message size using a common bound $3nL$ on local smoothness derived from convexity and aggregate smoothness. ## Why a biased aggregate suffices

At a point with nonzero $g=\nabla F(x)$ set $a_i=\langle\nabla f_i(x),g\rangle/\|g\|^2$. Then $\sum_i a_i=n$. Cauchy–Schwarz and strong growth give $\sum_i a_i^2\le n\rho$. Individual projections can be negative and can change with the query. The minimizing profile has the supporting inequality $\sum_i w_i a_i\ge I$ for every such signed vector. For an interior $t$, the quantities $t w_i+(1-t)u_i$ are constant across workers and the moment constraint is tight. Equal mass and $\sum_i u_i a_i\le\sum_i u_i^2$ give the inequality. The endpoints follow by equal weights or zero dispersion. Expanding $\sum_i(a_i-u_i)^2/h_i$ then proves minimality. The very same weights satisfy $\sum_i h_iw_i^2=I$. [Support proof](../Proofs/StrongGrowthWeights.lean)

Consequently the gradient of $G=\sum_i w_i f_i$ satisfies $\langle\nabla G,\nabla F\rangle\ge I\|\nabla F\|^2$ throughout the space. For every $z\in S$, strong growth makes each local gradient vanish at $z$, so $z$ also minimizes $G$. To pass from directional alignment to $G(x)-G(z)\ge I(F(x)-F^*)$, take any $0<a<I$ and run sufficiently small exact gradient steps on $F$ in the analysis. Smoothness of $G$ and convexity of $F$ imply that each step decreases $G$ by at least $a$ times its decrease in $F$. Telescoping and convergence in value give the claim as $a$ approaches $I$. This auxiliary trajectory uses no oracle calls in the distributed algorithm. Slope-based comparisons of convex functions, including gradient-flow proofs, have precedents. The hardware-dependent support inequality on signed projections supplies the growth of this particular surrogate and simultaneously determines its sampling variance. [Discrete comparison](../Proofs/DistributedGrowthComparison.lean) · [Nonlinear geometric estimates](../Proofs/DistributedGrowthGeometry.lean) · [Daniilidis and Drusvyatskiy, Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16277)

A nonlinear example has two nonorthogonal dense unit vectors $v,w$. Take $F(x)=\mu\|x\|^2/2+c[\log\cosh(v^Tx)+\log\cosh(w^Tx)]$ for $c>0$. Give a fraction $p\le1/8$ of workers $g(x)=\mu[(v^Tx)^2/2-\log\cosh(v^Tx)]$ and the remainder $(F-pg)/(1-p)$. The ridge profile of $g$ has second derivative $\mu\tanh^2$, so both worker types are convex. The first type has a hyperplane of minimizers and quartic growth transverse to it near zero, excluding any positive local PL constant. Since $\|\nabla g(x)\|\le\mu\|x\|\le\|\nabla F(x)\|$, every worker's gradient norm is at most $(1+p)/(1-p)$ times the global norm. This even satisfies the pointwise bound with factor $\sqrt2$. The example works in arbitrary $d\ge2$ and permits arbitrarily large $L/\mu$. Shared-parameter sensor regression provides another source of these assumptions: with local noiseless-population gradients $A_i^TA_i(x-\theta)$, the smallest mean-square growth constant on the identifiable subspace is the squared operator norm of the stacked local Hessians composed with the pseudoinverse of their average, using the $1/\sqrt n$ stack scaling. Independent centered observation errors give additive variance.

## Comparisons and regimes

Tyurin's Theorem F.2 gives Malenia SGD a statistical time proportional to $\sigma^2\sum_i h_i/(n^2\mu^2\varepsilon)$, with a further initialization logarithm in its stated bound, for uniform aggregation and free communication. Theorem 2.3 establishes the arithmetic-time obstruction even for additive first- and second-order similarity. Its shifted local quadratics can have nonzero gradients at the global optimum, which strong growth rules out. Theorem 3.8 gives a harmonic-style rate when local minimizer sets coincide and all local functions satisfy PL. Here convexity and aggregate quadratic growth suffice alongside mean-squared strong growth. For smooth convex objectives the PL and quadratic-growth formulations agree up to universal factors. [Tyurin, Assumptions 1.1–1.5, Theorems 2.3, 3.8 and F.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17483v1) · [Karimi, Nutini and Schmidt, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04636)

At $\rho=1$ all local mean gradients equal the global gradient, $u_i=1$, and $I=\sum_i1/h_i$. If $\rho\ge n\sum_i h_i^2/(\sum_i h_i)^2$, the unconstrained optimum $u_i=nh_i/\sum_jh_j$ is feasible and $I=n^2/\sum_i h_i$. Between these regimes the moment constraint determines which reweightings retain enough directional information. Remark 2.4 asks about the stronger pointwise condition $\max_i\|\nabla f_i\|^2\le2\|\nabla F\|^2$. The upper bound applies there with $\rho=2$. For example, fix $n\ge3$, let all but one worker take unit time and let the last take $H\to\infty$. Any feasible profile puts at least $n-\sqrt{2n}$ total mass on the fast workers, giving $I$ bounded below independently of $H$. With fixed positive remaining parameters and $\varepsilon=H^{-2}$ the new total bound is $O(H^2)$ against the arithmetic statistical term of order $H^3$. The sharp statistical characterization below concerns the full mean-square class. [Tyurin, Remark 2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17483v1)

For a separation from the best equal-weight subset, let $n=2m$, give $m$ workers unit time, and give the others times $j=1,\ldots,m$. Set $\rho=2$. The profile zero on the fast workers and two on the others gives $I=O(\log m)$. For the reverse inequality test weights $s_i=1$ on the first group and $s_j=\min(1,\sqrt m/j)$ on the second. Write $B=\sum_j s_j=\Theta(\sqrt m\log m)$ and $W=m+B$. Every feasible $a$ satisfies $s\cdot a\ge W-\sqrt{2m\sum_i s_i^2-W^2}\ge B$ by centering and Cauchy–Schwarz. Since $\sum_i h_i s_i^2=O(m\log m)$, another application gives $I=\Omega(\log m)$. An equal-weight subset of size at most $m$ can have no signal. For size $m+k$ its guaranteed mass is at most $m+k-\sqrt{m^2-k^2}\le2k$, while its sum of service times is at least $m+k^2/2$. Hence its information certificate is $O(1)$, including after optimizing subset size. This compares guaranteed signal with the variance of independently batched worker means. It allows each retained worker a batch proportional to its speed.

For the same family, $\max_i u_i\le8$: at least $m/2$ of the $j$-workers have $v_j\ge v_m/2$ at every $t$. With fixed $d,L,\mu,R,\sigma,\ell,b>0$ and $\varepsilon=m^{-4}$, the communication and one-batch-per-worker term is $O(m^3)$ and the full deadline is $\Theta(m^4/\log m)$. Uniform aggregation's statistical term alone is $\Theta(m^4)$, even granting free communication and omitting the initialization logarithm. Equal-weight thinning also has a limitation. For accepted sample rates $0\le q_i\le1/h_i$, let $\alpha$ be the average accepted rate on the $m$ fast workers. The admissible profile placing two units on each of the least-weighted $m$ workers bounds the signal by $2\sum_{j=1}^m\min(\alpha,1/j)$. Indeed, include slow workers of rate below $\alpha$, then fill the remaining positions with the lowest-rate fast workers. The pooled variance coefficient is proportional to $\sum_i q_i\ge m\alpha$. For $m\alpha\ge1$ the resulting information ratio is at most $4(2+\log(m\alpha))^2/(m\alpha)$, and for $m\alpha\le1$ it is at most $4m\alpha$. This rules out unbounded gains for that fixed-rate, equal-per-sample pooling certificate. Adaptive algorithms retaining richer worker statistics are outside this comparison.

The repository's weak-interpolation result uses bounds on each worker's absolute smoothness to cap its local objective gap. Aggregate smoothness alone gives bounds of order $nL$, which leave its arithmetic statistical term attainable. Substituting $\sqrt\rho\,\mu$ as a local smoothness bound would exclude admissible instances. The earlier work supplies the finite-bit protocol and its two-phase analysis. The joint ellipsoidal constraint on signed worker signals, its speed-dependent supporting weights, and its attainable statistical benchmark distinguish the present result. HeteRSGD optimizes the cost of importance-corrected sampling, while Inkheart's heterogeneous-time weighting in Section 4 estimates a common mean gradient. Neither analysis establishes the surrogate growth certificate for this class. ## Statistical benchmark and limits

Here is a two-point argument for the necessity of the leading term. This argument is given in prose. The Lean public theorem is the elapsed-time upper bound. For $d\ge1$, take a unit vector $e$, start at zero and let $\theta=\pm2\sqrt\varepsilon$. Give worker $i$ the quadratic $\mu u_i(\langle e,x\rangle-\theta)^2/2$ and independent Gaussian gradient noise $N(0,\sigma^2) e$. The average has curvature $\mu$ along $e$ and satisfies the same growth condition, radius bound when $R\ge2\sqrt\varepsilon$, and mean-square gradient constraint. At time $T$ worker $i$ can have produced at most $\lfloor T/h_i\rfloor$ samples. Even after giving the algorithm all these observations and free communication, subtracting known query offsets leaves independent Gaussians with mean $-\mu u_i\theta$. Adaptive transcripts can be simulated from those observations and the algorithm seed. Their KL divergence between signs is at most $8T\mu^2\varepsilon I/\sigma^2$. Testing the sign of any output achieving expected squared distance at most $\varepsilon$ errs with probability at most $1/4$ under each sign. Pinsker's inequality therefore requires $T\ge\sigma^2/(16\mu^2\varepsilon I)$. This benchmark uses $\sigma>0$ and a sample space supporting the stated Gaussian oracle. It applies to a subclass of the modeled instances and uses the same profile bound, error criterion, and worker service costs. [Related testing construction in Tyurin, Sections 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17483v1)

Statistical optimality is a small-accuracy statement at fixed nondegenerate parameters, or a joint regime in which this term dominates. The finite-bit overhead and noiseless time can dominate outside those regimes. The algorithm knows the hardware and growth parameters, waits for all workers each round, and assumes independent directed links. Unknown or time-varying speeds, shared bandwidth, and optimal transient or deterministic times remain open for this guarantee.
