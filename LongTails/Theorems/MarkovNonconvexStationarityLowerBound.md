# The cost of finding a stationary point from one Markov trajectory

Let a sequential first-order oracle return one vector per transition of a stationary Markov chain. Conditional on the query history its answer can be biased. At every fixed point its stationary mean is the objective gradient and its stationary mean squared error is at most $\sigma^2$. How much can dependence increase the worst-case cost of finding a point with small expected gradient norm?

## Result

Write $T=L\Delta/\varepsilon^2$ and $V=\sigma^2/\varepsilon^2$. For **every randomized adaptive algorithm** and every integer budget from zero through $\lfloor\tau TV/2^{56}\rfloor$, there is a single admissible objective and oracle on which its output fails the expected-norm criterion. The instance can depend on the algorithm and budget and is fixed before either source of randomness is realized. Thus the minimum sufficient budget exceeds $\tau TV/2^{56}$, including when the floor is zero. The companion upper bound takes $O(\tau TV\log V)$ calls in the identical model. [Lower theorem](MarkovNonconvexStationarityLowerBound.lean) · [Setup](../Setups/MarkovSmoothNonconvex.lean) · [Upper bound](MarkovNonconvexStationarity.md)

The result holds for every $L,\varepsilon>0$, $\tau\ge1$, $T\ge2^{26}$, $\sigma\ge4096\varepsilon$, and integer $d\ge2^{40}(1+\tau TV)^4$, at any specified starting point. These sufficient dimension and accuracy conditions come from hiding a chain and controlling universal constants. They include arbitrarily large $T/V$ and $V/T$ and make no assertion of hardness in small dimension or at vanishing noise. Taking $L=\varepsilon=\tau=1$, $\Delta\ge2^{26}$ and $\sigma\ge4096$ and rounding the dimension up gives a nonempty regime.

## Problem and information

The objective is differentiable on Euclidean space, has $L$-Lipschitz gradient, and satisfies $f(x_0)-\inf f\le\Delta$. The chain has transition kernel $P$, invariant law $\pi$, and $\sup_z\|P^t(z,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\rm TV}\le2e^{-t/\tau}$. This represents ordinary uniform mixing time up to constants. For example, total variation at time $s$ at most $1/4$ gives pairwise distance at most $1/2$ and hence distance at most $2\exp[-(\log 2)t/s]$. The algorithm knows the parameter bounds and starting point. It sees one reply $g(x,Z_t)$, can choose its next point arbitrarily, and pays for each transition. Its output may be a point it never queried. The setup imposes joint measurability and stationary integrability on the vector oracle. Sample labels, function values, resetting, and querying a past sample at a new point are unavailable.

This models streaming data with temporal dependence. A concrete nonlinear family is $f(x)=\sum_j b_j(1-\cos(a_j^\top x))$ with positive $b_j$ and nonorthogonal directions $a_j$. Its gradient Lipschitz constant is at most $\sum_j b_j\|a_j\|^2$. Adding any square-integrable centered vector of the chain state to its gradient gives such an oracle. The worst-case construction also allows point-dependent noise, for which suprema of the error over states can greatly exceed its root mean square under stationarity.

## Why the mixing and variance factors multiply

The hidden smooth chain has order $T$ links. At a query the observable part of its gradient is deterministic. A bounded correction carrying the next link is either omitted or amplified by the inverse of its reveal probability. Its bound is independent of the number of previously exposed links. The Markov state is blank or one of two colors, each of stationary mass $q=2^{22}/V$. At each transition it refreshes from stationarity with probability $1/\tau$. Which color reveals the correction depends on the parity of the query's chain stage. This yields the exact stationary mean and controls the centered second moment uniformly over queries. [Oracle and moment calculation](../Proofs/MarkovParityOracle.lean) · [Refresh chain](../Proofs/MarkovParityChain.lean)

Holding a color for many steps permits many adaptive queries, including queries at earlier stages. A simulator given only exposed coordinates tracks the longest prefix that could have been learned. Each new link changes the required color, so a run in the same color reveals at most one additional link. The expected number of entries into a nonblank color by call $b$ is at most $1+2bq/\tau$. Inside the localized chain, finite averaging over injections of chain coordinates bounds the chance of guessing a hidden coordinate at any query or at the output. Outside the localization radius the oracle reply is independent of the embedding. Averaging over embeddings and both sources of randomness gives a fixed embedding on which expected stationarity fails. ## Closest bounds

Arjevani et al., Theorem 3, equation (23), establish the unrestricted fresh-sample $\Omega(TV)$ lower bound. Its probabilistic zero-chain and techniques for hiding directions underlie this construction. IID hardness applies at $\tau=1$. Slowing down a sample by repeating it allows an adaptive algorithm to evaluate its vector field at many different points during one holding interval. Counting only independent refreshes therefore requires a proof about the entire adaptive transcript. [Arjevani et al., Mathematical Programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02365)

Sun and Wei already use alternating parity in their Markov lower construction. Their ICML Theorem 3.1, equations (12), (13), and (21), and NeurIPS Theorem 3.1, equations (14)–(15), concern zero-respecting methods and use hitting-time parameters. Their chain-length calibration involves $\min\{\sigma^2,L\Delta\}/\varepsilon^2$. A rare state can have expected hitting time of order $\tau/q$ in a chain mixing in order $\tau$; replacing one parameter by the other in a lower bound changes the instance class. Here a query-dependent mask restricts noise to one whole unresolved link, so chain length remains of order $T$ even when $V\ll T$. The prefix and output argument also covers unrestricted random queries. These distinctions concern the class of vector oracles above. Stronger component-wise smoothness and sample-access models need separate analysis. [Sun and Wei, ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/sun25u.html) · [Sun and Wei, NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/1cdbce34681a3aaa0154b300706743d8-Paper-Conference.pdf)

Even's Theorem 4.1 studies a per-node span algorithm model and a hitting-time lower bound. It establishes a different obstruction. The present lower theorem identifies a joint mixing-time and stationary-variance penalty in a single-trajectory vector oracle at expected norm accuracy. The formalized upper and lower budgets differ by a logarithm in $V$ and by universal constants. [Even, ICML 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/even23a.html)
