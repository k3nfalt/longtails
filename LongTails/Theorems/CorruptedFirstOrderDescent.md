# A stationary point with an additive cost for corrupted evaluations

Let $T=L\Delta/\varepsilon^2$. A deterministic algorithm returns a point with gradient norm at most $\varepsilon$ in at most $\lceil16T\rceil+5k+4$ joint value-and-gradient calls when up to $k$ replies can be replaced arbitrarily. The paired lower bound rules out $k+\lfloor T/2^{25}\rfloor$ calls even for randomized algorithms with failure probability $1/4$, in the high-dimensional regime specified below. The new result is the additive upper bound for an adaptive temporal corruption budget. The hard direction uses the classical stationarity lower bound and a delay. [Upper theorem](CorruptedFirstOrderDescent.lean) · [Lower theorem and construction](CorruptedFirstOrderLowerBound.md)

## Problem and information

The objective is differentiable on $\mathbb R^d$, has $L$-Lipschitz gradient, and satisfies $f(x_0)-\inf f\le\Delta$. The public input includes $x_0,L,\Delta,\varepsilon,k,d$ and a permitted failure probability in $[0,1/4]$. Neither the objective infimum nor the identities of corrupted calls are supplied. A query at any point returns a scalar and a vector. An adversary can replace either or both on at most $k$ calls along any attainable history. It can choose the times and replies adaptively, concentrate all failures in one burst, and return numbers of any magnitude. One joint reply costs one call, including repeated evaluations.

On a clean call at $x$ the value may vary within $\eta=\varepsilon^2/(128L)$ of $f(x)$. The vector comes from a fixed map $g$ with $\|g(x)-\nabla f(x)\|\le\varepsilon/8$. The exact first-order oracle satisfies both conditions. For $d≥1$, rounding each exact gradient coordinate to the nearest multiple of $ε/(4√d)$ gives a fixed approximation within the error bound. Voting uses stability of the clean threshold decision at a fixed point. Fixed gradients suffice for this property. No unbiasedness, independence, convexity, dimension threshold or upper bound on the gradient norm is required for the upper bound. The success test bounds the outer probability of excessive gradient norm under the product law of the algorithm and oracle. The constructed algorithm succeeds on every seed and fault history. [Setup](../Setups/CorruptedFirstOrderNonconvex.lean) · [Algorithm and output](../Algorithms/CorruptedFirstOrderDescent.lean)

A motivating example is a repeatable full-objective computation with transient silent evaluation failures. Ma et al. used deterministic execution to investigate deviations in model training caused by hardware faults. A bound on the number of faulty evaluations is a parameter of the mathematical model. Errors that alter the optimizer's saved state fall outside that model. An explicit coupled nonlinear family is
$f(x)=\lambda\|x\|^2/2+\sum_i a_i(1-\cos\langle u_i,x\rangle)$,
with positive coefficients and nonorthogonal directions. The Hessian norm is at most $\lambda+\sum_i a_i\|u_i\|^2$, $f(0)=\inf f=0$, and suitable coefficients give negative curvature. For $λ>0$, the trigonometric contribution to the gradient is bounded, so a sufficiently distant starting point has gradient norm greater than $ε$. Set $Δ=f(x_0)-f(0)$. Both the dimension and the number of interacting directions can grow. [Ma et al., ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.996/)

## How recovery works

The first $2k+1$ queries estimate the initial value. Their median plus $2\eta$ gives an anchor $a\in[f(x_0)+\eta,f(x_0)+3\eta]$. The algorithm stores a stack of points. At depth $j$ the current point should satisfy the value threshold $a-jh$, with $h=\varepsilon^2/(16L)$. A failed check with no pending stationarity votes removes one point. On a passed check, a large vector yields the trial $x-g/L$. Even if the passed clean value check overestimates the available descent by $\eta$, the smooth upper model gives a decrease of at least $h+2\eta$ on a clean trial. The child is therefore below its own threshold by at least $\eta$. This local test uses the absolute depth and initial anchor. It can recover after an arbitrarily large false decrease has been reported.

For termination, a reply of norm at most $3\varepsilon/4$ starts or increments a vote at the same point. A large reply cancels one pending vote. The algorithm returns the point once $k+1$ votes accumulate. If the clean vector there exceeds the threshold, every positive vote at that point consumed a fault. Otherwise the true gradient norm is at most $7\varepsilon/8$.

To count calls, assign $+1$ to each stack position whose true value is below its depth threshold by at least $\eta$, and $-1$ to the others. Give pending votes a sign according to the clean gradient decision at the current point. Every clean nonterminal query increases the total by one, including when it removes an invalid position. Any corrupted query reduces it by at most one. At most $\lceil16T\rceil+1$ depths can have positive value scores, since $f\ge f(x_0)-\Delta$. There are at most $k$ pending votes. Counting initial queries and the final certification gives the public budget. The theorem accounts for the full fixed-budget execution, including any calls after the output becomes final.

## Prior bounds and limitations

Carmon, Duchi, Hinder and Sidford prove the randomized $\Omega(T)$ lower bound for clean smooth objectives in Theorem 2 with $p=1$. For exact clean replies, repeating every clean-method query $2k+1$ times simulates that method, at $O((k+1)T)$ cost. With uniformly inaccurate clean values and repeatable gradients, the median value and majority gradient permit the analogous per-step simulation of an inexact descent test. Generic fault-tolerant decision trees also exhibit multiplicative overhead in the clean depth. The additive guarantee improves on the generic replication bound by order $\min\{k+1,T\}$ for $T\ge1$. It recovers the clean complexity at $k=0$ and allows $k$ of order $T$ at the same asymptotic total cost. [Carmon et al., v3, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11606v3) · [Maharjan and Watson, FSTTCS 2022, §2](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2022.26)

Scheinberg and Xie analyze stochastic line search and trust regions with conditional accuracy probabilities and controls on value errors. Their Theorem 2 bounds a hitting time, and the progress estimate in Assumption 3 charges accepted value errors. One certain, unbounded replacement can violate both controls. Conversely, stochastic clean gradients can fluctuate across their decision boundary, whereas the voting proof here uses repeatability. Zhang and Cutkosky's ICML 2025 guarantees concern regret on a sequence of convex losses with corrupted gradients. The present contribution concerns a certified returned point of a fixed, possibly nonconvex function. The comparisons do not establish an additive corruption guarantee with gradient-only access or with arbitrary fluctuating clean gradient errors. [Scheinberg and Xie, v1, §§1.1, 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19411v1) · [Zhang and Cutkosky, ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhang25o.html)

The two budgets match up to universal constants for
$T\ge 2^{26}$ and $d\ge 2^{32}(1+\lfloor T/2^{25}\rfloor)^4$, for every $k\ge0$ and every allowed failure probability. Only the lower bound needs those restrictions. Its dimension requirement is independent of $k$. The constants and the dimension exponent are not optimized. Known $k$ supports initialization and stopping. The algorithm also uses $L$ and $\varepsilon$; $\Delta$ determines the execution budget.
