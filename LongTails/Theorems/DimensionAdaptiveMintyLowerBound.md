# A dimension-sharp lower bound for weak-Minty residuals

Let $F:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^d$ be $L$-Lipschitz, with a root $z$ within $R$ of $x_0$ satisfying $\langle F(x),x-z\rangle\ge-\rho\|F(x)\|^2$ for every $x$. Put $t=LR/\varepsilon$ and $H=d\log(1+t^2/d)$. For every $d\ge1$, $t>1$, $0\le\rho L\le1/20$, every randomized adaptive algorithm, and **every** integer query budget from zero through $\lfloor H/2^{22}\rfloor$, there is an admissible deterministic operator on which the algorithm fails. Success requires an integrable residual and $\mathbb E\|F(\hat x)\|\le\varepsilon$. Each call returns the full exact vector at an unrestricted point. The operator can depend on the algorithm and the budget and is fixed before its seed is drawn. The paired deterministic algorithm succeeds with $2\lceil1024H\rceil$ calls on exactly the same parameters. ## A Lipschitz field that controls what one reply reveals

Use coordinates relative to $x_0$. For positive budgets in the lower-bound range, take $k=\min\{d,\lfloor(t/256)^2\rfloor\}$, $s=8\varepsilon/L$, and $M=\lfloor t/(32\sqrt{k})\rfloor\ge8$. A hidden word $w\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}^k$ determines the first $k$ root coordinates $z_j=4s w_j$. The remaining root coordinates vanish. Set $\theta_j$ to $(x_j-z_j)/s$ clipped to $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$, and define
$$
F_j(x)=4\varepsilon\sin\theta_j\prod_{i<j}\cos\theta_i,\qquad 1\le j\le k,
$$
with zero remaining components. Each component has the sign of its displacement from the root, or is zero. Consequently the ordinary Minty inequality holds everywhere. The maximum root distance is bounded by $4sM\sqrt k\le R$. Appending the final cosine product to the unscaled vector gives a unit vector. Recursively splitting off its first coordinate bounds its squared chord length by twice the squared distance between the input coordinates in units of $s$. The resulting Lipschitz constant is at most $L$, uniformly in $k$. If even one angle saturates, the terminal product vanishes and the field has norm exactly $4\varepsilon$. [Sphere identities](../Proofs/MintyPrefixSphere.lean) · [Global field bounds](../Proofs/MintyPrefixField.lean)

Later coordinates depend on earlier query coordinates. For two active coordinates, at $0<\theta_1<\pi/2$ and in the interior of positive saturation of the second angle, the Jacobian is proportional to $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta_1&0\\-\sin\theta_1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. Its symmetric part has negative determinant. This gives coupled, nonmonotone instances in the recognized weak-Minty VI class. For a smooth scalar minimax payoff with the displayed coordinates assigned to opposite players, the off-diagonal derivatives must be opposite transposes. This Jacobian violates that identity. A matching lower bound on the saddle-gradient subclass would require a different construction or a reduction preserving the mixed partials.

## Counting adaptive transcripts

Until an angle saturates, each coordinate that falls in the open clipping interval matches at most one possible hidden letter because its width is $\pi s<4s$. The first saturated coordinate reveals its sign and multiplies every later component by zero. Thus the full real-valued reply can reveal several matched letters in a call. The count allows for that extra information. With $j$ shared initial letters and $N$ remaining calls, at most $4^N2^{k-j}$ words can end with every output coordinate matched. At the next coordinate each of the two saturated signs gives one common reply within its group and spends a call. The unique matched-letter group extends the shared prefix for free. Summing these branches, or inducting on calls and the remaining prefix length, gives the displayed count. A fixed final output matches at most one whole word when there are zero calls. [Counting lemma](../Proofs/MintyPrefixSearch.lean)

For the stated budget $2\cdot4^N2^k\le M^k$. At least half the words therefore have final residual $4\varepsilon$, pointwise in the algorithm seed. Integrating the finite sum gives a mean residual of at least $2\varepsilon$ over words whenever all the losses are integrable. One fixed word must fail. The proof allows adaptive policies without measurability of their internal states or queries. At zero budget, opposite translated linear fields already force worst-case expected residual at least $LR>\varepsilon$. Positive-budget packing and the zero-call construction together cover the entire nontrivial parameter range. For $LR\le\varepsilon$, returning $x_0$ suffices. ## Closest bounds and source coverage

- The earlier randomized Euclidean weak-Minty theorem proves a lower bound of order $\min\{t^2,d^{1/3}\}$ for this expected residual. The new lower bound reaches order $t^2$ already at $d\asymp t^2$ and gives order $d(1+\log(t/\sqrt d))$ below that transition. Standard concentration-based rotations of robust zero chains can also improve a polynomial dimension requirement. Such hiding does not account for the multiple precision bits per coordinate proved here. [Earlier theorem](EuclideanWeakMintyLowerBound.md) · [Carmon et al., Proposition 8](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v134/carmon21a/carmon21a-supp.pdf)
- Anagnostides et al. study weak-Minty separating cuts on a bounded feasible set. Their Propositions 6.8–6.10 address global optimization or feasibility of the Minty inequalities against all test points. A small local root residual is a different success criterion. Monotone operators also admit faster dimension-free residual bounds. The present nonmonotone hard family explains why those results can coexist. [Anagnostides et al., v4, §§4.3 and 6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03432v4) · [Yoon and Ryu, ICML 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/yoon21d.html)
- Lobel, Paes Leme and Vladu already obtained the information scale $d\log(1/(\varepsilon\sqrt d))$ by independent coordinate searches in a unit ball. Their Proposition 3.1 counts mistakes given an externally supplied direction and binary feedback. In the operator problem the algorithm chooses the entire query and receives a continuous vector, and the same fixed field must remain Lipschitz and weak-Minty at every possible query. Realizing and counting that information structure under these requirements is the new lower-bound step. [Multidimensional Binary Search, Proposition 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.00829)
- The primary literature search on 21 November 2026 UTC covered publicly available 2025 and 2026 ICML, ICLR and NeurIPS material and recent arXiv submissions and revisions, followed by older extragradient, contextual-search and randomized hiding results. Recent stochastic-oracle VI bounds and the November generalized-Lipschitz monotone VI preprint concern other oracle or operator classes. The official NeurIPS virtual conference page blocked automated access during one search; its public download inventory was available. The repository PR inventory through #239 included #182 (high-dimensional lower bound), #210 (fresh stochastic oracle) and #227 (second order). These differences leave the exact, first-order, all-dimension characterization as the specific novelty claim. [ICML 2026 stochastic VI paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/alacaoglu26a.html) · [NeurIPS 2026 inventory](https://neurips.cc/Downloads/2026) · [Descending Everest III, November 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20296)

All constants are universal and conservative. The upper bound uses exact centroids with unrestricted internal computation. The comparison is sharp for query complexity on the stated weak-Minty parameter range; the dependence as the allowance approaches a larger admissible boundary remains open here.
