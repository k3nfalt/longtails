# Expected stationarity for nonnegative smooth sample losses

Let $F(x)=\mathbb E_\xi \ell_\xi(x)$ on a separable real Hilbert space. Every sample loss is nonnegative and has an $L$-Lipschitz gradient. Its value is integrable at every point, its gradient is jointly measurable, and $F(x_0)\leq\Delta$. One call chooses two points before seeing a fresh IID sample and returns both gradients of that sample loss. The algorithm knows $L,\Delta,\varepsilon,x_0$. The class includes nonconvex losses with incompatible sample minima. [Setup](../Setups/NonnegativeSmoothSampleLoss.lean)

Write $R=L\Delta/\varepsilon^2$. The algorithm returns a measurable iterate $X$ with integrable gradient norm and $\mathbb E\|\nabla F(X)\|\leq\varepsilon$ using $\lceil8192(1+R)^{3/2}\rceil$ paired calls. Each call consumes one independent sample and returns two gradient vectors, including when the points coincide. The expectation includes the algorithm’s independent choice of output index. The bound holds for arbitrary sample spaces satisfying the setup. [Theorem](NonnegativeSampleStationarity.lean) · [Execution proof](../Proofs/NonnegativeSampleStorm.lean)

## Algorithm and upper bound

Set $S=4L\Delta+2\varepsilon^2$, $a=\varepsilon^2/(\varepsilon^2+400S)$, and $\eta=\varepsilon\sqrt a/(40L)$. Average $b_0=\lceil192 S/\varepsilon^2\rceil+1$ fresh gradients at $x_0$ to initialize $d_0$. Take $\tau$ uniformly in $\{0,\ldots,T\}$, with $T=\lceil80 L\Delta/(\varepsilon^2\sqrt a)\rceil$. For each update, set $y=x-\eta d/\|d\|$, taking $y=x$ for $d=0$. Query $(y,x)$ and update $d$ to $g(y,\xi)+(1-a)(d-g(x,\xi))$. Return $x_\tau$. Once the selected iterate is reached, the template continues consuming the remainder of the public budget with its output frozen. [Algorithm](../Algorithms/NormalizedStorm.lean) · [Tuning](../Proofs/NormalizedStormConvergence.lean)

The descent inequality applied to each sample at $x-L^{-1}\nabla\ell_\xi(x)$ gives $\|\nabla\ell_\xi(x)\|^2\leq2L\ell_\xi(x)$. This controls the second moment at the current objective value. Individual smoothness also bounds the same-sample gradient difference by $L\|x-y\|$. Integrating the sample Taylor remainder proves $\nabla F=\mathbb E\nabla\ell_\xi$. In particular $\ell_\xi(x)\leq 2\ell_\xi(x_0)+L\|x-x_0\|^2$ by smoothness, the self-bound, and Young’s inequality. For measurable sample losses, integrability at each point thus follows already from the finite initial risk. These facts give the existing expected-smoothness STORM theorem with $A=L,C=0$ and lower bound zero. The upper rate is a specialization of that result. Smooth bounded-below component losses are also the setting of Proposition 2 of Khaled and Richtárik. [Moment transfer](../Proofs/NonnegativeSampleMoments.lean) · [Existing STORM result](NormalizedStormExpectedSmoothness.lean) · [Khaled and Richtárik](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03329)

For example, consider $\ell_{a,b}(x)=\tfrac12(\langle a,x\rangle-b)^2+\lambda(1-\cos\langle v,x\rangle)$, with bounded $a,b,v$, $\lambda>0$, and nonparallel feature directions. Its gradient is Lipschitz with constant at most $\|a\|^2+\lambda\|v\|^2$. The cosine term permits negative curvature in directions orthogonal to $a$, and dense features couple the coordinates. Random design can give unbounded gradient variance as $\|x\|$ grows. The sample offsets have statistical meaning since the initialization parameter bounds the expected loss itself.

## What the pair establishes

The companion lower bound is the new research contribution. For sufficiently large $R$, a growing finite-dimensional family obeying the same statistical assumptions forces order $R^{3/2}$ paired calls for every randomized algorithm. The two theorems give the worst-case order of this risk model. The lower result uses finite uniform sampling and polynomially large dimensions. It leaves fixed low dimensions, fixed sample cardinality, and sharp constants open. In the coarse regime $\varepsilon^2\geq2L\Delta$, the same self-bound already certifies $x_0$ without a call.
