# Pipelined variance reduction on heterogeneous workers

How long does it take to find a stationary point when workers have different speeds? A method can attain optimal serial sample complexity and still spend most of its time waiting for gradient batches. On a cluster whose $i$-th fastest worker takes time proportional to $\sqrt i$, this result improves the elapsed-time guarantees of Rennala MVR and a direct distributed implementation of a serial multilevel estimator. Workers compute corrections for several future iterates concurrently. The theorem returns a point with $\mathbb E\|\nabla f(X)\|^2\leq\varepsilon^2$. ## Problem and access

The objective is differentiable on arbitrary finite-dimensional Euclidean space, with a global lower bound $a$ and $f(x_0)-a\leq\Delta$. Worker $i$ has a jointly measurable, unbiased gradient oracle $g_i(x,\xi)$ with centered second moment at most $\sigma^2$. For every $i,x,y$, the same draw satisfies $\mathbb E\|g_i(x,\xi)-g_i(y,\xi)\|^2\leq L^2\|x-y\|^2$. This implies $L$-smoothness of the mean objective. Each worker's samples are IID and worker streams are independent. Workers can have different noise laws but must share the same mean gradient. The sample space can be infinite. These conditions include the homogeneous stochastic objectives of Rennala MVR, Assumptions 2.2–2.4. Its $\bar L$ is our $L$. [Tovmasyan, Maranjyan and Richtárik, May 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08871v1)

For example, take $f(x)=m^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^m(1-\cos(a_j^\top x))$ for dense, nonorthogonal unit vectors and use the gradient of the sample loss $f(x)+\xi_i\sin(v^\top x)$, with $v$ a unit vector and $\xi_i$ centered of variance at most $\sigma^2$. The population Hessian varies with $x$ and couples coordinates. The MSS constant can be taken as $\sqrt{1+\sigma^2}$ by centering the cross term. This example admits continuous, unbounded noise.

The coordinator has no gradient access. A worker receives two points and evaluates both on its next sample, at cost $2h_i>0$ even for repeated points. Independent directed links connect each worker to the coordinator, with latency $\ell$ and time $b$ per bit. There is one pending message per directed link and one active computation per worker. The protocol uses at most one outstanding round trip per worker. Public inputs include $x_0,d,n,h,\ell,b,L,\Delta,\sigma,\varepsilon$. Local arithmetic and scheduling are free in this model. A shared bottleneck, time-varying rates or different local mean gradients would require a different analysis. [Network](../Setups/StarNetwork.lean) · [Event model](../TemplateDistributed.lean)

## Bound

Write $K=\max(1,\lceil 2^{19}L\Delta/\varepsilon^2\rceil)$, $S=\max(1,\lceil(512\sigma/\varepsilon)^2\rceil)$. Take $J$ as the smaller of $\lfloor\log_2 K\rfloor$ and the integer base-four logarithm of $\lfloor S/(1+\lfloor\log_4 S\rfloor)\rfloor$, with integer logarithm zero at zero. Each transmitted coordinate uses $w=14+4\lfloor\log_2(2+d+K+S)\rfloor$ bits. One complete transaction on worker $i$ costs $c_i=2h_i+2\ell+b(129+3dw)$, including the one-bit request flag, two point vectors, one reply vector and both 64-bit clock fields. Define

$$
 B(m)=4\min_i\max\left\{c_i,\,
 \frac{m}{\sum_{u:c_u\le c_i}1/c_u}\right\}.
$$

The proved deadline is

$$
 B(S)+K\max_{0\leq j\leq J} 2^{-j} B\!\left(2(J+1)4^j+\mathbf 1_{j=J}S\right).
$$

The initial snapshot is included. The result proves finite event execution for every seed at every earlier deadline and bounds the expected squared gradient of the coordinator's actual output at the stated deadline. For $S=1$ the hierarchy collapses to one level. The batch sizes and calendar use the public inputs. [Timetable](../Proofs/MultilevelTimetable.lean) · [Execution](../Proofs/MultilevelExecution.lean)

## Why the levels can run concurrently

For scale $r=2^j$, while updates $sr,\ldots,(s+1)r-1$ are in progress, workers process a batch involving the grid point for $x_{sr}$. Its parent is the coarser anchor that will be active when this batch becomes usable. Both points are already determined at release. At iteration $k$, scale $r$ uses anchor $\max(0,\lfloor k/r\rfloor-1)r$. Differences between successive anchors telescope to the true gradient at the quantized $x_{\max(0,k-1)}$. The displacement from $x_k$ is bounded because each normalized update moves at most $\delta/L$, for $\delta=\varepsilon/512$. This one-step lag at the finest scale lets even the largest correction use a full period to finish. [Causality](../Proofs/MultilevelInformation.lean) · [Path and telescope](../Proofs/MultilevelPath.lean)

A non-root batch at scale $r$ uses $(J+1)r^2$ same-sample differences of points at distance $O(r\delta/L)$. A coarsest batch uses $S$ snapshots. The sum of all finer work inside a scale-$r$ window is geometric. Reserving disjoint whole worker slots from fine to coarse gives each batch its full allocation inside its window, even at nonintegral hardware speeds. The maximum of the level service times per unit period therefore suffices. Replies can arrive in a different order from the logical tree. The proof centers each clipped, quantized reply in physical request order. For a fixed iteration its coefficient on the logical path is deterministic, so its centered noise is orthogonal to preceding contributions. Bounding clipping bias separately gives mean squared estimator error at most $168\delta^2$, including the stale-point and grid errors. [Slot allocation](../Proofs/MultilevelWorkerSchedule.lean) · [Conditional second moments](../Proofs/MultilevelPacketMoments.lean) · [Martingale proof](../Proofs/MultilevelMartingale.lean)

Normalized descent controls the sum of estimated norms. The coordinator samples two independent uniform iteration indices and returns the point with the smaller estimated norm. Conditional on the trajectory, the square of that smaller norm is at most the product of the two norms, whose expectation is the squared trajectory average. The estimator second-moment bound controls the error at the selected point by the sum at the two indices. This gives the squared-norm criterion using the stored replies. [Output proof](../Proofs/MultilevelAccuracy.lean) · [Two-index inequality](../Proofs/LeonAccuracy.lean)

## Comparison and limits

Write $K_0=L\Delta/\varepsilon^2$ and suppress universal constants. On the common-data subclass with $L=\Delta=\sigma=1$, $S\asymp K$, $n\asymp S$, $h_i=\sqrt i$ in increasing order, and fixed $d,\ell,b>0$, the deadline is **$O(K\log S)$**. Indeed $B(m)=O(\log S+\sqrt m)$ for the batch sizes in the formula, by using the fastest $\min(n,\lceil m\rceil)$ workers. Here $2^J\asymp\sqrt{S/(1+\log S)}$. With free links the bound is **$O(\sqrt S+K\sqrt{\log S})$**. For growing $d\asymp S$ and gradient evaluation times $h_i=d\sqrt i$, the same calculation gives $O(dK\log S)$ with fixed positive bit tariffs. The compute-only comparisons below scale by $d$ on those machines.

- Rennala MVR Theorem 5.2 gives $O(T(S)+(K_0+\sqrt S)T(\sqrt S))$ after translating its squared accuracy parameter to $\varepsilon^2$, where $T(m)=\min_k(m+k)/\sum_{i\le k}h_i^{-1}$. On the displayed square-root speed profile $T(m)\asymp\sqrt m$ even with free communication, giving order $K S^{1/4}$ for $h_i=\sqrt i$. The gain persists under retuning of its analysis. Equations (10)–(11) require inner batch size $m\gtrsim pS$ at refresh weight $p$ and a certified iteration count of order at least $K_0(1+(1-p)/\sqrt{pm})$. Paying $\sqrt m$ per iteration gives order at least $K_0 S^{1/4}$ in that certificate. The single-snapshot SPIDER batching calculation improves on this comparison. With an epoch of $q$ unit-scale updates, its standard path-variance bound takes order $q$ differences per update. Even granting overlap of refresh and updates gives a per-update service estimate of order $\max(\sqrt q,\sqrt S/q)$, minimized at order $S^{1/6}$. These comparisons concern guarantees from those analyses. [Rennala MVR, Theorems 5.1–5.2 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08871v1)
- Nested reference points and binary gradient-difference paths already appear in SNVRG and in private optimization. The repository's dyadic Hölder result already provides the squared-lag batch allocation at Hölder exponent one. Serializing those batches pays for each level. Even with free communication, a serial epoch of length $q$, binary strides $r$ and batches $m_r$ has the usual path variance condition $\sum_r r^2/m_r=O(1)$ at unit normalized stride. Hölder's inequality gives $\sum_r\sqrt{m_r}/r=\Omega((\log q)^{3/2})$ for its per-update service estimate. The amortized snapshot term is $\Theta(\sqrt S/q)$, so optimizing $q$ leaves order $(\log S)^{3/2}$. The same certificate allows radix $t_j$, stride $r_j$, digit $u$ uniform on $0,\ldots,t_j-1$, and digit-specific batches $m_{j,u}$. On nonzero digits the variance charge is $a=u^2r_j^2/m_{j,u}$ and the serial time charge is $z=\sqrt{m_{j,u}}/r_j$. Since $a^{1/3}z^{2/3}=u^{2/3}$, Hölder bounds the total time below by a constant times $(\sum_j \mathbb E[u^{2/3}])^{3/2}\gtrsim(\sum_j\log t_j)^{3/2}$. The tradeoff thus survives changing the tree radix or giving the serial scheme the same two-index selection. The new ingredient is the causal multiscale schedule and its maximum-over-levels time bound. [Zhou, Xu and Gu, Algorithm 1 and Theorem 12](https://jmlr.org/papers/v21/18-447.html) · [Arora et al., ICML 2023, Algorithm 1 and Appendix C](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/arora23a.html) · [Dyadic Hölder theorem and proof context](DyadicVarianceReductionHolder.md)
- Jiang, Yu and Li analyze delayed stochastic paired feedback in issued updates, including a guarantee in terms of weighted missing feedback. Their asynchronous full-snapshot result uses a finite sum. Neither theorem schedules these streaming corrections for different lags under a heterogeneous service profile. The coordinatewise finite-bit overhead here grows with $d$ and $\log(K+S+d)$. [Jiang, Yu and Li, October 2026 preprint, Sections 4–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28510v1) · [Finite-bit first-order comparison](FiniteBitLeon.md)

With a single worker and $K\asymp S$, the allocation can cost a factor of order $\sqrt{\log S}$ in work per update relative to a one-level variance-reduced method. The improvement is specific to elapsed time on suitable worker profiles. Theorem 6.7 of Rennala MVR gives a free-communication lower bound for its high-dimensional zero-respecting protocol. On the square-root speed profile its expression is of order $K_0+\sqrt S$ in the displayed high-noise regime. Optimality among unrestricted distributed algorithms, a matching finite-bandwidth lower bound, and the optimal possible logarithmic dependence remain unresolved by this theorem. [Rennala MVR, Protocol 2 and Theorem 6.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08871v1)
