# Expected stationarity from a single Markov stream

For an $L$-smooth, possibly nonconvex objective with initial gap at most $\Delta$, consider a vector oracle driven by one stationary Markov chain. At a fixed query its reply has stationary mean $\nabla f(x)$ and stationary mean squared error at most $\sigma^2$. The kernel satisfies the uniform total variation bound $2e^{-t/\tau}$. Replies can depend on the query in a merely measurable way. For example, a sum of cosines of nonorthogonal linear forms gives a smooth interacting objective in arbitrary finite dimension; adding a centered square-integrable vector of the chain state produces an admissible oracle. The algorithm knows the stated parameter bounds, including $\tau$, and sees only the current vector. [Setup](../Setups/MarkovSmoothNonconvex.lean)

## Algorithm and guarantee

Set $C=64\sigma^2/\varepsilon$, $\eta=\varepsilon/(64LC)$, $K=\lceil32768L\Delta\sigma^2/\varepsilon^4\rceil$ and $m=\lceil\tau\log(8192\sigma^2/\varepsilon^2)\rceil$. Start from $x_0$. At times $0,m,\ldots,Km$, query at the current iterate and subtract $\eta$ times the reply projected onto the radius-$C$ ball. The algorithm uses an independent uniform index in $\{0,\ldots,K\}$ to return one of the **pre-update iterates**. Each block consumes exactly $m$ calls, including discarded replies. The returned point $X$ is measurable, its gradient norm is integrable, and $\mathbb E\|\nabla f(X)\|\le\varepsilon$ after $(K+1)m$ calls. The expectation includes both the trajectory and the index. Writing $T=L\Delta/\varepsilon^2$ and $V=\sigma^2/\varepsilon^2$, the two public theorems use the identical regime $T\ge2^{26}$, $V\ge2^{24}$, $\tau\ge1$, $d\ge2^{40}(1+\tau TV)^4$, with $L,\varepsilon>0$. These dimension and noise restrictions permit a direct comparison with the hard construction. The upper proof uses only elementary bounds on the noise-to-accuracy ratio and has no dimension factor in its call count. The pair gives an $O(\tau TV\log V)$ upper and $\Omega(\tau TV)$ lower, including when $1\ll V\ll T$. A guarantee on expected squared gradient norm would imply expected norm accuracy by Jensen at squared tolerance $\varepsilon^2$. This theorem proves the latter criterion directly. ## How the proof handles adaptive queries

Fix a point, let $u=\nabla f(x)$, and let $v$ be the clipped reply. Smooth descent depends on $D=(L\eta/2)\|v\|^2-\langle v,u\rangle$. When $\|u\|\le C/2$, clipping bias is at most $2\sigma^2/C$. For larger gradients the projection is nonexpansive and the mean deviation from the projected true gradient is at most $\sigma$. Both cases give stationary drift at most $\varepsilon^2/16-(3\varepsilon/8)\|u\|$. Conditional on the last used state and its history, the next state is $m$ transitions away. Boundedness of $v$ controls the error in the conditional drift by $2\,\mathrm{TV}\big((L\eta/2)C^2+C\|u\|\big)$. The spacing absorbs the term proportional to $\|u\|$ without a global gradient bound. Telescoping the resulting descent and averaging the pre-update gradients gives the stated expected norm. ## Position relative to earlier bounds

The principal advance of the pair is the matching polynomial **lower** dependence for unrestricted algorithms under a mixing-time bound. Arjevani et al., Theorem 3, equation (23), prove fresh-sample hardness. Sun and Wei, ICML and NeurIPS 2025, Theorem 3.1 of each paper, analyze zero-respecting algorithms using hitting-time parameters. Neither a fresh-sample lower bound nor a lower bound in terms of rare-state hitting time establishes the joint $\tau TV$ obstruction in this oracle model. [Arjevani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02365) · [ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/sun25u.html) · [NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/1cdbce34681a3aaa0154b300706743d8-Paper-Conference.pdf)

Sun and Wei explicitly identify the finite-stationary-variance upper-bound question in the footnote following Theorem 3.1 of their NeurIPS paper. Their Assumption 2.3 is the stationary oracle moment model used here. Beznosikov et al. analyze Markov gradient methods with a uniform statewise error assumption. Their stationary batch estimate in the proof of Lemma 1, equation (5), uses stationary variance. It suggests a sharper comparison under the present oracle as well. Freeze the query for a batch, burn a constant multiple of $\tau$ transitions, and take order $\tau V$ consecutive samples. Under stationarity the batch mean then has root mean squared error $O(\varepsilon)$. Taking a bounded normalized step limits the change in mean direction under the burn-in total variation discrepancy. With constants small enough this gives positive descent proportional to gradient norm above the target scale, suggesting $O(T)$ batches and $O(\tau TV)$ calls. The formal upper theorem here uses spaced single replies and retains the logarithm. We claim neither a necessary logarithm nor an improved upper complexity over batch arguments. [Beznosikov et al., NeurIPS 2023, §2.3 and Lemma 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15938)
