# When a learned two-tap filter speeds up full-batch logistic GD

Section 1 of Tyurin’s ICML 2026 paper fits 5,000 CIFAR-10 training images from classes 0 and 1. Both models can express every linear classifier. With a constant step throughout each run, the fastest plotted two-tap run finds a strict separator in about 20,000 updates. The fastest plotted plain linear run takes about 330,000. The target is the first time **all signed training scores are positive**. One misclassified image matters, however small the mean logistic loss or the test error. [Figure 1, Section 1.1 and Appendix G](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.11587v2#S1.SS1)

The new bound predicts how much further constant-step GD must run once it has acquired a direction that keeps improving a difficult image. It also checks whether the same updates preserve enough margin on every other image. On two 5,000-image CIFAR problems the single formula gives 3,542 and 2,246 further full gradients. Starting plain GD at exactly the same image scores, every step size in its initial local stability range needs more updates. The general formal result is the upper bound for the learned model. The plain-GD comparisons below are separate finite-data numerical enclosures. ## The guarantee

Let the signed images be $x_i=y_i b_i\in\mathbb R^D$, for any positive sample count $n$, and let $S$ shift the entries of an image cyclically by one position. Write $z=(a,b,v)$ for the two scalar taps and the readout. The scores and loss are
$$
m_i(z)=a\langle x_i,v\rangle+b\langle Sx_i,v\rangle,\qquad
F(z)=n^{-1}\sum_i\log(1+\exp(-m_i(z))).
$$
The only oracle returns the joint negative gradient of this mean loss. The algorithm starts at the supplied point, uses the supplied constant $\eta>0$, and outputs its last iterate. Its inputs are the dimension, sample count, initial parameters and step size. Each update evaluates the full gradient once, equivalent to $n$ indexed sample-gradient contributions. The identity between the oracle and the gradient is proved in Lean. [Gradient and margin identities](../Proofs/SpectralTwoTapGeometry.lean)

Take an opening after $K$ updates and a reference approximation to that opening. The designated image has a negative reference score of magnitude at most $d$. At the reference opening, or at the midpoint of its first two reference states, form the Hessian $H$ and the gradient step $b_0=-\eta\nabla F$. Set $J=\nabla m_i$ for the designated image at that center. In an orthonormal eigenbasis of $H$, let $\mu_j=1-\eta\lambda_j$. The comparison can also cap these multipliers from below at $-1$, the point where a linear mode changes sign without decreasing in magnitude. All ensuing discrepancies are charged to the nonlinear correction. GD itself always takes the original step.

Let $M$ be this comparison map. The **signed work over two linearized updates** is
$$
w_j=\langle J,e_j\rangle\langle b_0,e_j\rangle(1+\mu_j),\qquad
W=\sum_j w_j=\langle J,(I+M)b_0\rangle .
$$
The assumptions below imply strict positivity of every training margin at the deterministic deadline
$$
\boxed{K+2\left\lceil\frac{2d}{W}\right\rceil}.
$$
This also bounds the first separation time. The theorem makes no persistence claim at later iterates. There is no randomization or confidence parameter.

**What must hold on the data and starting point?**

1. **Persistence of directional work.** Require $0<W\le4d$ and put $\rho=1-W/(4d)$. Choose a cap $R\ge\max(\rho,\max_j\mu_j^2)$ and an uncertainty allowance $\sigma\ge0$. At every $0\le t\le R$ require
   $$
   \sum_j w_j(\mu_j^2-t)_+ + \sigma(R-t)_+ \ \ge\ (4W/5)(\rho-t)_+ .
   $$
   Both sides are piecewise linear, so the breakpoints suffice. This test keeps the signs of the alignments of the gradient with the difficult image in each mode. The extremal eigenvalues or the usual positive spectral distribution discard those signs. The allowance covers bounded error when estimating a true spectrum with rounded matrices.
2. **Accuracy of an independent reference calculation.** For a sequence of reference states and local linearizations, bound the one-step defect throughout a ball about each preterminal state. On each block, propagate the entry ball and arbitrary disturbances of these sizes through the ordered linear maps. The resulting radius has to fit inside the proposed ball. This is a finite, instance-dependent condition on field evaluations and linear maps. It allows noncontractive intermediate updates. The reference is used only in the analysis. The specification permits any reference satisfying the inequalities, including a sufficiently accurate trajectory. The independence and computational cost of a particular construction therefore matter when interpreting a prediction.
3. **Size of the nonlinear correction.** Freeze $M,b_0$ and compute the affine displacement after $T=2\lceil2d/W\rceil$ updates. At each preterminal reference point compute the difference between the actual gradient field and this frozen affine field. Propagate those differences by powers of $M$, including the opening displacement about the chosen center. The bilinear score contributes an exact quadratic endpoint term. The setup charges the resulting adverse score change, with the field and score uncertainties from the reference balls. On the designated image the charge plus $\sigma\sum_{j<T/2}R^j$ must be at most $d/4$. On every other image the opening score plus the frozen linear work must be positive and the charge must use at most three quarters of that quantity. These hypotheses concern independently computable responses of the data. They imply a bound on a simultaneous fit even when other images temporarily become incorrect.

The spectral test and relative-error conditions are new sufficient assumptions that restrict the class. They are motivated by the question of which training examples benefit from feature learning under finite steps. They do not follow from linear separability. No restriction on $n/D$, commuting sample Hessians, alignment of all sample gradients, or monotone margin growth is used. The constants leave room for estimation error and have not been optimized.

**Why the test gives the rate.** At pair $j$ the affine work is $\sum_i w_i(\mu_i^2)^j$. For $j\ge2$, $x^j$ is the integral of $(x-t)_+$ against $j(j-1)t^{j-2}\,dt$. Thus the signed inequality supplies a floor on every pair, with the stated allowance. Summing through $N=\lceil2d/W\rceil$ pairs yields at least $(4W/5)\sum_{j<N}\rho^j>5d/4$ before the allowance. The opening debt and the adverse nonlinear work together cannot consume this amount. Applying the bilinear identity on the other rows gives simultaneous separation. Finite-dimensional spectral decomposition, the integral identity and propagation of numerical defects are established tools. The contribution is the signed persistence criterion and its conversion into a finite first-separation budget with measured nonlinear and all-row margins. ## What it predicts on images

For the first two cases below, the check starts from the stated cold initialization and counts the entire prefix. The observation used to choose the opening was two successive updates with all rows other than the largest raw hard-margin SVM dual row correct, mean loss at most 0.0182 and curvature along the constant-pixel readout direction at most 16. This selection rule is specific to these experiments. Its three scalar tests alone do not establish any future success. The spectral and nonlinear tests are verified separately.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CIFAR-10 airplane/car | 2800 | 20,914 | 0.643410 | 0.000726800 | 24,456 | 23,431 |
| CIFAR-10 car/ship | 2844 | 21,471 | 0.243477 | 0.000433953 | 23,717 | 22,691 |

The table rounds the displayed debt and work; the deadlines use their enclosures. Both learned runs use $\eta=1/8$. The worst charged adverse change relative to the other rows’ individual affine margins is below 2.18% and 3.89%, respectively. On the designated rows the verified charges, including the spectral uncertainty, are below 0.142 and 0.032, within the quarter-debt allowances of 0.161 and 0.061. Neither calculation uses a future true margin as the acceptance test. The single-Hessian forecast samples the nonlinear gradient at reference points derived from the opening. For airplane/car the source states in the error proof are 832-bit locally corrected states. The coarser single-Hessian forecast is used for field evaluation with its discrepancy from those states included. Ordered Jacobian products handle transient expansion. Tighter dataset-specific signed spectral approximations give 2,540 and 1,248 additional updates, compared with the exact 2,517 and 1,220. These refined values use more spectral information than the public formula.

**What accounts for the speedup?** The filter and readout have the same representable image scores as a plain linear model. Training both changes the direction in score space produced by a gradient call. At the acquired CIFAR openings, enough of that direction continues to lift the hard row. The two-step spectrum measures the part that survives alternating updates. From exactly the same scores, fresh plain-GD runs with any fixed positive step up to $2/\lambda_{\max}(H_{\rm raw})$ at the opening cannot first fit before updates 18,322 and 11,373, respectively. This cap describes initial local linear stability. It allows a different step for every run. The corresponding full-gradient upper bounds above are smaller by factors exceeding 5.17 and 5.06 during this phase. An unrestricted plain step of 2,000 can already separate the warm car/ship data in one update. The warm comparisons have no implication for all positive steps.

The same opening Hessians also distinguish the two parameterizations without comparing later hit counts. Normalize the signed work by $W$. For positive initially stable raw steps, the squared multipliers lie in $[0,1]$. The increasing convex test function $f_h(x)=[1+8(1-x)/h]^{-1}$ therefore gives a necessary consequence of exact signed persistence: the normalized raw response must be at least $(4/5)f_h(\rho)$. Applying the resolvent of the raw Hessian at the matched scores gives an upper bound on the response minus this requirement below $-0.475$ on airplane/car and $-0.621$ on car/ship, uniformly over the corresponding stable step intervals. On the learned side the small spectral uncertainty is accounted for in the quarter-debt charge. This calculation distinguishes the measured spectral mechanism. The independent time exclusion establishes how long the raw iterates actually fail. On other datasets plain GD can also pass the spectral test.

Two controls distinguish finite training dynamics from an improvement in the optimal normalized margin. Optimizing over all two-tap shapes can raise the squared normalized margin by less than 0.133% and 0.044% on these splits. In a second experiment each physical pixel is repeated twice with isometric normalization and the matching initialization. This preserves every raw GD score at every update. It changes the joint model’s access to adjacent-pixel differences and moves the exact first fit on airplane/car from 23,431 to 32,714. Thus a property of the labeled data **relative to the convolutional shift** matters even with the whole raw Gram matrix fixed. For the full run with plain and learned steps both fixed at 1/8, plain GD fails through update 393,215 on each of the two native-resolution splits.

An informative stronger baseline uses the unlabeled pixel arrays. Compute the one-lag regression coefficient $c=\sum_i\langle x_i,Sx_i\rangle/\sum_i\|x_i\|^2$, freeze the normalized filter $(1,-c)/\sqrt{1+c^2}$ and match the original raw scores at initialization. For airplane/car this fixed-feature method with step 100 reaches strict separation in 5,290 real updates, earlier than 23,431 for learning the taps at step 1/8. Computing the filter and matching the initial readout use access to the feature arrays. The advantage established here is relative to ordinary plain GD under the stated oracle and step comparisons. It is not a state-of-the-art first-separation count over data-dependent algorithms.

There is also evidence about how the favorable filter forms. Write $r_t=a_t-b_t$ and $g_t=n^{-1}\sum_i x_i/(1+\exp m_i(z_t))$. Substitution of the previous readout updates into the update of $r_t$ gives a feedback term proportional to $\sum_{i<j}r_i\langle (I-S)g_i,(I-S)g_j\rangle$. Thus agreement of successive voted pixel differences can amplify the tap contrast. A forecast using the scalar-filter limiting dynamics puts the first unit tap contrast on airplane/car at 500 updates (observed 558), on car/ship at 582 (observed 616) and on MNIST 7/8 beyond 32,767. Removing only this history term prevents full separation through 98,303 steps on both CIFAR splits in numerical ablations. These modified dynamics test the proposed cause. They are distinct from the proved budget for unmodified GD. The general bound still charges the full time before the opening.

**Transfer and limits.** At separately selected dyadic checkpoints the same spectral rule predicts at most 692 and 182 more steps on two 2,000-image Fashion-MNIST subsets (5/7 and 7/9), and 1,612 on an independently sampled CIFAR airplane/car subset. The independently checked first-fit times from those checkpoints are 342, 95 and 873. These are warm-start comparisons. On full MNIST 7/8 the whole-run ordering reverses: raw GD with step 100 first fits at 6,578, and the learned model at step 1/16 first fits at 38,398. The winning raw run alternates its last failing images. On 64 small perturbations of an intermediate CIFAR state, a single instantaneous spectrum often gave a poor prediction. A two-state midpoint, clipping only in the comparison, and retesting at fixed 32-update intervals produced positive numerical predictions on all 64. Five had to wait 608 additional updates to pass. These sensitivity tests are empirical.

$K$ is paid in full. For airplane/car a source constructed from the first 64 actual steps and subsequent independent field evaluations certifies the opening. For car/ship the prefix can also be verified by enclosing a high-precision cold evolution. A much cheaper evolving spatial subspace predicts qualifying openings at 21,250 and 22,027 on the two splits. There is no proved error bound for that cheap forecast. The general theorem does not derive a universal acquisition time from a margin, label balance, or dimension alone. It gives a data-dependent explanation of the acceleration once this geometry develops. Verifying a reference may cost more computational work than running both optimizers. Its cost is distinct from the gradient oracle budget of GD.

## Comparison with prior guarantees

* Tyurin’s Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 compare a quadratic perceptron with an ordinary perceptron on two specially constructed images, giving a dimension-dependent speedup there. Theorem 2.1 relates large-scale logistic dynamics to the former. Section 1.1 poses the many-image experiment. The new theorem handles finite logistic votes from all images at the actual constant step. [ICML 2026 paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/tyurin26a.html)
* PR 253, the finite-logistic theorem, treats the two-example construction with a prescribed small random initialization radius, counts two indexed queries per full update and provides tunable confidence. Here the initialization is deterministic and the signed-work inputs have to be verified. PR 262 treats many samples under simultaneous plus/minus feature separation with a large a priori budget. Its guarantee alone does not establish that joint training needs fewer steps than plain GD on the plotted inputs. Neither result implies the present directional calculation by substituting a margin or the sample count. [PR 253](https://github.com/k3nfalt/longtails/pull/253) · [PR 262](https://github.com/k3nfalt/longtails/pull/262)
* PR 254 already proves an asymptotic separation on the same six signed rows for both models. With fixed nuisance parameters and small contrast $\mu$ its joint upper bound grows as $\mu^{-1}$ up to logarithms, and the zero-initialized plain model needs order $\mu^{-2}$ updates for **every** positive constant step. Its many-row upper bound includes exact orthogonality between all sum-channel and difference-channel features. This is a stronger step-uniform lower bound on that particular family. The present result accommodates general interacting image rows and evaluates a different, directional condition directly on two large CIFAR problems. [PR 254](https://github.com/k3nfalt/longtails/pull/254)
* For raw linear logistic regression, a loss bound below $\log(2)/n$ suffices for strict full separation. Muşat and Boumal’s Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2 analyze the large-step transition and decay of the loss; Section 5 gives a dataset-dependent large-enough step for arbitrary accuracy in a fixed number of iterations. Wen, Wen and Teng study worst-case transition times in the large-step regime. These 2026 preprints address different parameterizations and guarantees. They do not supply this finite-step comparative margin budget. [Muşat and Boumal, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26748v1) · [Wen, Wen and Teng, v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01459)
* Jagadeesan, Razenshteyn and Gunasekar characterize the induced norm for a bounded two-layer linear convolutional network. Their static characterization is useful for the normalized-margin control. It does not describe the signed work of finite discrete updates. [COLT 2022, Section 3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v178/jagadeesan22a.html)

## Reproducing Figure 1

Use the training split of the public CIFAR-10 Parquet dataset, revision `0b2714987fa478483af9968de7c934580d0bb9a2`. With NumPy’s `default_rng(20261125)`, choose 2,500 indices without replacement from the ordered list for each of classes 0 and 1, in that order; concatenate the lists. The SHA-256 of the resulting little-endian int64 indices is `6016298cc0cc1916374372cf30da4964341cc524ff87dfc872a01b1ac94d6c7a`. Decode pixels in RGB channel-major order, map byte $p$ to $2p/255-1$, flatten and multiply the first class by $-1$. For car/ship use seed 20261126 and classes 1 and 8. Set the PyTorch CPU RNG to the same seed, draw $D$ float32 uniforms and scale into $[-1/\sqrt D,1/\sqrt D]$ for the readout, then draw two uniforms and scale into $[-1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2]$ for the taps. The exact CPU float32 initialization expressions were `torch.rand((D, 1)).mul_(2/math.sqrt(D)).sub_(1/math.sqrt(D))` and then `torch.rand((2, 1)).mul_(2/math.sqrt(2)).sub_(1/math.sqrt(2))`. For the native run the taps are $(0.20154595375061035,-0.13351017236709595)$. This is a seeded reproduction; the original Figure 1 does not identify its exact random draw. The following is the full simultaneous update kernel. Each column of `v` and `c` holds one run. Start the plain columns with taps $(1,0)$, set their Boolean mask `learn` to false, and broadcast `eta` along columns. Record the first strictly positive minimum before advancing. The shift operates on flattened coordinates.

```python
u = c[0] * v + c[1] * v.roll(-1, 0)
m = X @ u
g = X.T @ torch.sigmoid(-m)
sg = g.roll(1, 0)
dv = c[0] * g + c[1] * sg
dc = torch.stack([(g * v).sum(0), (sg * v).sum(0)]) * learn
v.add_(dv * (eta / len(X)))
c.add_(dc * (eta / len(X)))
```
