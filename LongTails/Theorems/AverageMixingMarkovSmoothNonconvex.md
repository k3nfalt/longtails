# Finding a stationary point from one Markov trajectory

How many successive gradient observations does it take to find a point with small gradient when a stationary data source produces rare, persistent events? Write $D=L\Delta/\varepsilon^2$ and $S=\sigma^2/\varepsilon^2$. A normalized batch method uses $O((1+D)(1+S)\tau\log(2+S))$ observations. Its output satisfies $\mathbb E\|\nabla f(X)\|\le\varepsilon$, including the randomness of the data and the choice of iterate. The companion lower bound forces the same parameter dependence up to universal constants for large $D,S$ and sufficiently high dimension. [Upper theorem](AverageMixingMarkovSmoothNonconvex.lean) · [Lower bound and its parameter regime](AverageMixingMarkovSmoothNonconvexLowerBound.md)

## Oracle and examples

The objective on Euclidean $d$-space has an $L$-Lipschitz gradient and initial gap at most $\Delta$ above its finite infimum. The algorithm queries one point at a time. Call $t$ returns $g(x,Z_t)$ from a hidden stationary Markov chain on real Borel space with kernel $P$ and invariant law $\pi$. The estimator is jointly measurable. At each fixed $x$, its stationary mean is $\nabla f(x)$ and its stationary centered second moment is at most $\sigma^2$. The dependence parameter bounds the stationary average of total variation distances,
\[
 \beta(t)=\int \|P^t(z,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\rm TV}\,d\pi(z)\le 2e^{-t/\tau},\qquad \tau\ge1.
\]
The algorithm knows $d,x_0,L,\Delta,\sigma,\varepsilon,\tau$. It receives only replies and has access to one uninterrupted trajectory. Upper-bound validity allows every finite dimension, $\sigma,\Delta\ge0$ and $L,\varepsilon>0$. The model permits a query to depend on earlier replies, so the fixed-point moment bound alone gives no conditional variance bound at that query. [Lean specification](../Setups/AverageMixingMarkovSmoothNonconvex.lean)

For example, take a finite average of losses $f_i(x)=a_i(1-\cos\langle v_i,x\rangle)$ with dense, nonorthogonal unit vectors $v_i$ and $0\le a_i\le M$. Sampling component gradients with a uniform stationary index gives $L\le M$ and centered second moment at most $M^2$. An index chain that keeps its previous index with probability $e^{-1/\tau}$ and otherwise draws a fresh uniform index satisfies the envelope. These objectives can have interacting coordinates in arbitrarily many dimensions. This example uses conservative sample gradients. The upper bound also covers unbiased vector estimators.

## Algorithm and proof

Set $\eta=\varepsilon/(128L)$, $N=1+\lfloor2048D\rfloor$, $B=1+\lfloor256S\rfloor$, and $h=1+\lfloor\tau\log(32+8192S)\rfloor$. During each epoch, keep the query fixed for $Bh$ calls and retain replies in slots $0,h,\ldots,(B-1)h$. Move a distance $\eta$ against the normalized sum, using the zero direction if the sum vanishes. Return the starting iterate of an independently and uniformly sampled epoch among the $N$ epochs. The template execution consumes exactly $NBh$ calls, including every intermediate transition and the final epoch. [Algorithm and output](../Algorithms/MarkovNormalizedBatch.lean) · [Execution proof](../Proofs/MarkovNormalizedExecution.lean)

At a fixed point $x$, an independent batch has expected alignment with the gradient at least $G-\varepsilon/4$, where $G=\|\nabla f(x)\|$. Thinning an entire adaptive run and charging the probability of any failed coupling would introduce dependence on the number of epochs inside the logarithm. The proof instead replaces the samples of one batch one at a time in the scalar alignment statistic. At a replacement the error is bounded by twice the expectation of $G_j u(Z_s)$, where $G_j$ is the gradient norm at epoch $j$ and $u(z)=\|P^h(z,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\rm TV}$. The two factors may be correlated. Each past epoch changes the gradient norm by at most $L\eta$. Decomposing $G_j$ into those changes bounds their covariances by a geometric sum over the distances from their last observations. Summing over the entire batch costs at most $16L\eta$ plus an absorbable fraction of $\mathbb E G_j$. This bound is independent of the initial gradient norm and of the number of epochs. Smooth descent and uniform output selection finish the argument. [Alignment proof](../Proofs/MarkovBatchAlignment.lean) · [Descent and output guarantee](../Proofs/MarkovNormalizedRate.lean)

## Comparison and scope

The uniform-mixing theorem also gives $O(\tau DS\log S)$ calls in the overlapping high-noise, high-dimensional regime. Its mixing envelope holds at every starting state. Here the envelope holds after averaging over the stationary law, and the upper theorem also covers arbitrary finite dimension and nonnegative noise. [Uniform-mixing upper bound](MarkovNonconvexStationarity.md)

Arjevani et al. establish the independent-oracle noise term of order $DS$ for smooth nonconvex stationarity. Independence lets a new query use its fixed-point moment bounds directly. Simply multiplying the independent complexity by a decorrelation time of order $\tau$ would miss the additional logarithm forced by the lower bound here. [Arjevani et al., Theorem 1 and the probabilistic zero-chain construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02365v2)

Beznosikov et al. analyze randomized batch sizes for smooth nonconvex objectives. In their bounded-noise case (A4 with $\delta=0$), Corollary 2 gives $\widetilde O(\tau_{\rm unif}D(1+S))$ oracle calls for an average expected squared gradient norm at most $\varepsilon^2$. Their A3 imposes uniform geometric mixing and A4 bounds the estimator error samplewise. Here a rare state can have a large error and a long excursion, subject to the stationary variance and average mixing constraints. The new upper theorem extends coverage to these chains with a deterministic call count. Its first-moment accuracy is weaker than their squared-norm guarantee at the corresponding scale. [Beznosikov et al., NeurIPS 2023, Section 2.3, Theorem 3 and Corollary 2](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/8c3e38ce55a0fa44bc325bc6fdb7f4e5-Paper-Conference.pdf)

Sun and Wei study stationary Markov stochastic nonconvex optimization in ICML and NeurIPS 2025. Their NeurIPS Theorem 3.1 states a hitting-time lower bound for zero-respecting algorithms. Its noise term involves $\min\{c_1\sigma^2,c_2L\Delta\}$. Their Theorem 4.4 gives a variance-reduced upper bound using mean-square smoothness and a dependence on minimum stationary mass. Here the objective alone needs a Lipschitz gradient, and the cost applies to a real Borel chain under stationary-average mixing. Hitting time, minimum stationary mass and the average-mixing envelope describe different information about the data source. [ICML proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/sun25u.html) · [NeurIPS proceedings, Sections 2–5](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/1cdbce34681a3aaa0154b300706743d8-Paper-Conference.pdf)

The companion result identifies the worst-case cost on the overlap of the two parameter regimes. The pair gives a partial picture outside that overlap. For $D\le1/2$, smooth descent gives $\|\nabla f(x_0)\|^2\le2L\Delta\le\varepsilon^2$, so zero calls suffice. At $\sigma=0$ the budget can overpay for spacing. Along any finite deterministic exact-gradient trajectory, stationarity makes the replies at all its query points exact almost surely. A lower bound for conservative sample-gradient oracles alone would require a separate construction. [Companion theorem](AverageMixingMarkovSmoothNonconvexLowerBound.lean)
