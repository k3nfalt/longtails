# Finite-bit decentralized optimization with heterogeneous gradients

A collection of workers can minimize a smooth nonconvex average even when their local gradients are arbitrarily large and cancel. Leon SGD addresses this setting with an exact vector AllReduce. The theorem here implements stochastic optimization on a complete point-to-point network with binary messages. It preserves Leon's heterogeneous computation term and achieves expected squared stationarity with an amortized word width independent of the target accuracy. Its communication guarantee concerns this specific network. ## Guarantee and model

Write $F=n^{-1}\sum_i f_i$ on $\mathbb R^d$. Each $f_i$ is differentiable, $\nabla F$ is $L$-Lipschitz, and $F(x_0)-\inf F\le\Delta$. The local oracle is jointly measurable and unbiased for $\nabla f_i(x)$ at every $x$, with centered second moment at most $\sigma^2$. Samples are IID within a worker and independent across workers. No bound on the range of gradients, local smoothness constant, or disagreement enters the theorem. For example, take $F(x)=\sum_r a_r(1-\cos(u_r^\top x))$ with $a_r>0$ and nonorthogonal directions. Add $\sin((v^\top x)^3)$ to one local objective and subtract it from another. The local gradients and Hessians can be unbounded while the coupled nonlinear average remains smooth. Independent centered noise of finite second moment gives admissible stochastic oracles.

Worker $i$ takes $h_i>0$ per sample and may have one batch outstanding. A batch of $m$ returns $m$ fresh local gradients at the query point after $mh_i$. Each ordered pair has its own FIFO link. Sending $B$ payload bits reserves the link for $\ell+b(B+64)$, including the template's clock suffix. Both directions and different links can be active concurrently. Local arithmetic has zero elapsed-time cost. The implementation pays the same tariff on self-delivery and includes the latency and headers of **both** exchange phases. Timing and scale parameters are public.

For $L,\varepsilon>0$, $\Delta,\sigma\ge0$, set $H=\sum_i h_i$ and $m_d=\lceil d/n\rceil$. At the explicit deadline in the Lean theorem the returned point $X$ is measurable and satisfies $\mathbb E\|\nabla F(X)\|^2\le\varepsilon^2$. The elapsed time is

$$
O\!\left(\left(1+\frac{L\Delta}{\varepsilon^2}\right)
\left[\max_i h_i+\frac{\sigma^2 H}{n^2\varepsilon^2}
+\ell+b\left(64+m_d\log(2+n(d+1))\right)\right]\right).
$$

This bound is for the actual event-driven execution. Packets can arrive before the associated receive instruction. Per-source queues preserve their phase order. The timing proof reserves each batch and both transmissions, and the event proof covers every earlier deadline, including zero and ties between events.

## Algorithm and proof

Set $\delta=\varepsilon/512$. Round the desired number $\max(1,\lceil2L\Delta/\delta^2\rceil)$ up to a power $K=4^J$. Traverse a complete four-ary tree in preorder. Each node computes a fresh estimate at the current common iterate. Only the leaves move the iterate, by normalized descent of length $\delta/L$ (zero for a zero estimate). At a level-$j$ node, worker $i$ takes $2^j\max(1,\lceil\sigma^2 H/(n^2\delta^2h_i)\rceil)$ samples. The aggregate of the unquantized local means thus has conditional mean squared error at most $\delta^2/2^j$, also at the adaptively chosen query point.

Each worker subtracts the common parent estimate from its local mean, rounds coordinates on a lattice of spacing $\delta/(d+1)$, and sends integer residues. A reduce-scatter followed by an allgather sums the residues before decoding. Each directed link carries one padded block of $m_d$ residues per phase. The integer width is $7+\lfloor\log_2(n(d+1))\rfloor+2j$. Every worker recovers the same displacement and rejects it if its norm exceeds $8\cdot4^j\delta$. This test bounds the damage of an incorrect decode. Individually unbounded local offsets cancel in the residue sum.

From a parent evaluation to any child at level $j$, at most $3\cdot4^j$ normalized moves have occurred, so the true gradient has traveled at most $3\cdot4^j\delta$. On a root-to-leaf path, the maximum of the unquantized errors has bounded second moment by summing $\delta^2/2^j$ over the path. Above the scale set by this maximum, lattice decoding recovers the rounded aggregate exactly. Below it, the reject rule and geometric decrease of the radii bound the accumulated displacement error. The resulting leaf estimate has mean squared error at most $7142\delta^2$, uniformly over leaves. A full tree costs less than $2K$ base batch units, since there are $K/4^j$ evaluations at level $j$. The same geometric sum pays for increased precision and all internal-node messages.

The workers keep the leaf points and estimated gradient norms. They share two independent uniform leaf indices, sampled independently of the oracles, and return the candidate with the smaller stored norm. Normalized descent controls the *average estimated norm*. For two draws with estimated norms $a,b$, $\min(a,b)^2\le ab$, so averaging over the two indices gives a squared average. The selected error squared is bounded by the sum of the two candidate errors squared. These two facts give $\mathbb E\|\nabla F(X)\|^2\le (8+28\cdot7142)\delta^2\le\varepsilon^2$, without another oracle call.

## What changes relative to prior work

Tyurin's Theorem 1.7 and Algorithm 4 give Leon SGD for heterogeneous objectives under global smoothness and bounded local oracle variance. Appendix F.1 treats AllReduce on a graph with coordinate capacities. After normalizing its squared-gradient tolerance to $\varepsilon^2$, its time per iteration contains $\max_i h_i$, $\sigma^2H/(n^2\varepsilon^2)$, and a dimension-over-minimum-cut term for transmitting vectors. An exact real coordinate has no uniformly bounded binary representation. The result here supplies a finite-bit alternative on a complete multiport network and charges the complete packet lengths. Tyurin covers broader topologies. Its Theorem 5.2 uses restrictions on algorithms and communicated coordinates that do not furnish a lower bound for this binary setup. The March 2026 arXiv version is the checked manuscript. OpenReview lists a later submission; its PDF could not be independently compared line by line. [Tyurin, Theorems 1.7 and 5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20735v1) · [OpenReview submission](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BiaD9zBnSY)

An explicit finite-bit baseline uses a single full-range modulus on every round. Normalized moves give $\|\nabla F(x_k)\|\le\sqrt{2L\Delta}+k\delta$. Covering that deterministic envelope at lattice resolution $\delta/(d+1)$ costs $O(\log(2+n(d+1)K))$ bits per coordinate. A radius test can control the contribution of sample outliers by their second moment. Batching alone reduces variance but leaves this range term. In the theorem, occasional coarse anchors replace the $\log K$ contribution to the amortized width. For example, keep $n\ge2,d\ge1,L,\Delta,h_i,\ell,b$ fixed and positive and set $\sigma=c\varepsilon$ with fixed $c>0$. Sample time per base round stays bounded while $K$ grows. The full-range encoder then spends a logarithmically growing factor more time on each packet. With fixed nonzero noise and vanishing $\varepsilon$, sample time can dominate both protocols. In the noiseless case ordinary one-step differential coding can already track a bounded gradient increment after initialization. No optimality or separation from all alternative binary protocols is established.

Modulo coding with side information and reduce-scatter/allgather are established techniques. Moniqua's Lemma 2 relies on encoder–decoder proximity; its nonconvex analysis imposes local regularity and stochastic gradient bounds. Davies et al.'s mean-estimation and variance-reduction tasks use a pairwise input-distance bound or samples of a common mean. Arbitrarily dissimilar local means do not meet those conditions. The additional mechanism here is to encode the *aggregate*, recover from errors across multiple time scales, and allocate enough samples to each scale to control the second moment of the returned point. The costs include retaining the leaf history, public tuning parameters, complete-network connectivity and full multiport concurrency.
