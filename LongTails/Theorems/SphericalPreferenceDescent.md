# Convex minimization from comparisons with unknown spherical tastes

How many pairwise choices suffice to minimize a convex objective when different respondents value the same attributes differently? On a Euclidean ball of radius $R$, the answer is $\Theta(dB^2R^2/\varepsilon^2)$ under a median bound $B$ on respondents' Lipschitz constants. The upper bound uses one bit per round and permits nonsmooth objectives in every finite dimension $d\ge2$. The lower bound allows arbitrary randomized, adaptive queries in the same ball. [Upper theorem](SphericalPreferenceDescent.lean) · [Lower theorem and construction](SphericalPreferenceLowerBound.md)

## Observation and accuracy

A respondent with taste vector $Z$ ranks a pair $x,y$ by comparing the two values of $f(\cdot)+\langle Z,\cdot\rangle$. Ties favor the first query point. Successive respondents are IID. The law of $Z$ is invariant under orthogonal transformations and can have atoms and infinite first moment. At least half of the draws produce a utility that is $B$-Lipschitz throughout the ball. The target $f(x)$ is a pointwise median of the sample utility by central symmetry. It is also its expectation whenever the taste vector is integrable. The algorithm knows $d,R,B,\varepsilon$ and observes only the comparisons. [Lean specification](../Setups/SphericalRandomUtility.lean)

Random-coefficient choice models motivate the additive linear taste. Spherical symmetry specifies a common Euclidean attribute metric. For instance, take $f(x)=\max_j\{a_j^\top x+b_j\}$ with nonorthogonal dense features and $\max_j\|a_j\|\le B/2$. A radial taste with uniform direction and probability at least one half of radius at most $B/2$ satisfies the envelope, irrespective of its upper tail. This includes simultaneous active constraints, nondifferentiable ridges and interacting coordinates. Natenzon derives elliptical random coefficients from choice axioms. Kornilov et al. use moments of sample Lipschitz constants for a two-value oracle. Here the median replaces that moment condition in a structured one-bit model. [Natenzon, §4](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Natenzon.Random-Choice-and-Differentiation.pdf) · [Kornilov et al., NeurIPS 2023, Assumption 2](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/ca24eb48806df3af49e5ac59d8a46f67-Abstract-Conference.html)

## Algorithm and proof

For $0<\varepsilon\le BR/16$, use $n=\lceil d(672BR/\varepsilon)^2\rceil$, $\eta=R/\sqrt n$, $r=\eta/4$ and $h=r/(4d\sqrt n)$. Starting at zero, draw $w$ uniformly in the radius-$r$ ball and an independent unit direction $u$. Set $y=(1-2r/R)x+w$ and compare $y+hu$ to $y-hu$. With $b=1$ if the first utility is at least the second and $b=-1$ otherwise, project $x-\eta b u$ back to the feasible ball. The average of the $n$ pre-update iterates has expected objective gap at most $672BR\sqrt{d/n}$. Each round uses a single fresh respondent and one comparison. [Algorithm and output](../Algorithms/SphericalPreferenceDescent.lean) · [Execution and expected error](../Proofs/SphericalPreferenceExecution.lean)

Three facts make the median condition sufficient.

1. The closed set of good tastes contains some antipodal pair. Otherwise this set and its negative would partition the support into disjoint closed pieces, separating the connected sphere through a supported good point. Averaging the two antipodal utilities gives $\operatorname{Lip}(f)\le B$. Every good draw then satisfies $\|Z\|\le2B$. [Envelope proof](../Proofs/SphericalPreferenceEnvelope.lean)
2. At a differentiability point with gradient $g$, averaging the signed direction over the sphere gives $\kappa_d (g+Z)/\|g+Z\|$, with zero assigned at the origin and $\kappa_d\ge1/(8\sqrt d)$. Averaging again over tastes gives a vector parallel to $g$. Monotonicity of normalization and its transverse variation on $\|Z\|\le2B$ give an inner product with $g$ of at least $\|g\|^2/(192 B\sqrt d)$. The argument uses bounded normalized vectors throughout. [Angular estimate](../Proofs/SphericalPreferenceDirection.lean) · [Radial estimate](../Proofs/SphericalPreferenceTaste.lean)
3. A finite secant can have the opposite sign from the derivative at its center. Convexity bounds its sign between the two endpoint derivative signs almost everywhere. Translating the random center by $hu$ changes the expected sign by at most $2dh/r$, uniformly in the taste and direction. This permits an explicit positive probing radius without a smoothness assumption. The inward shift keeps both queries feasible. [Secant bound](../Proofs/SphericalPreferenceSecant.lean) · [Integrated descent](../Proofs/SphericalPreferenceIntegration.lean)

## Comparison and limits

Kovalev, Stonyakin and Gasnikov prove impossibility for general stochastic order comparisons. Their Theorem 2 treats a known normalized probit with coordinate noise scale $\sigma$. Its convex upper error is $C(M/\sigma)\min\{MR,d\sigma R/\sqrt n\}$ for an $M$-Lipschitz objective. Appendix B.1 uses the inverse probit to recover directional gaps. The unknown radial law here can induce a discontinuous link, for example through an atom at zero. Repeating a query estimates a preference probability while leaving its conversion to a gap unknown. The new guarantee retains the optimal dependence on the median sample scale without calibration or any dependence on an inverse-link constant. Their Gaussian subclass already supplies the matching lower-order obstruction. For a class with *separate* objective Lipschitz bound $M$ and median noise radius $s$, setting $B=M+s$ gives the upper rate. The Gaussian lower term scales as $ds^2R^2/\varepsilon^2$ and matches that upper dependence when $s\ge M$. Optimality for the single-envelope class leaves room for improvements on low-noise subclasses. [Kovalev et al., preprint v1, Theorems 1–2 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19611v1)

Saha, Koren and Mansour analyze transfers of the unnormalized gap for smooth objectives. Chayti, El Bakkali and Saadi reconstruct gaps using a known inverse link with a convergent analytic expansion. Lobanov, Gasnikov and Krasnov analyze stochastic order comparisons asymptotically for smooth optimization. Their angular identity for a fixed direction of the gradient is compatible with this proof. The finite-radius comparison estimate and the median-only radial bound are the ingredients needed here for nonsmooth expected suboptimality. [Saha et al., ICML 2025, §3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/saha25a.html) · [Chayti et al., preprint v3, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05622v3) · [Lobanov et al., NeurIPS 2024, §5](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/26289c647c6828e862e271ca3c490486-Paper-Conference.pdf)

In dimension one, equiprobable tastes $Z=\pm s$ make all comparisons identical in law for the two objectives $f_\pm(x)=\pm cx$ when $0<c<s$. The same example along a single axis shows how central symmetry alone can destroy identifiability in higher dimensions. Arbitrary elliptical geometry, a strong-convexity rate, and tunable high-probability error remain outside the guarantee.
