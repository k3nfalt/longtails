\section*{Muon and Scheduled Momentum Comparisons}
We consider nonconvex minimization of a differentiable function $f:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\to\mathbb{R}$, where $m,n$ are positive integers. The gradient is Lipschitz from the spectral norm to the nuclear norm with constant $L>0$, and a given starting point $x^0$ has gap at most $\Delta\geq0$ above a global lower bound. The oracle returns unbiased stochastic gradients with independent, identically distributed samples and rowwise second moments controlled by $s\geq0$. For $\varepsilon>0$, batched Muon must return $\bar{x}$ with $\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|_*\leq\varepsilon$, where $\|\cdot\|_*$ is the nuclear norm and expectation includes sampling and algorithm randomness. The task also requires worst-case oracle-call comparisons for scheduled SGD and normalized SGD with momentum.

The optimization problem is
\[
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}} f(x).
\]
Equip the matrix space with the Frobenius inner product $\langle X,Y\rangle_F=\operatorname{tr}(X^{\mathsf T}Y)$ and norm $\|\cdot\|_F$; gradients are taken with respect to this inner product. Write $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ for the induced Euclidean operator norm and $\|\cdot\|_*$ for its dual nuclear norm. Set $r=\min\{m,n\}$. The parameters satisfy
\[
r\geq2,\qquad L>0,\qquad \Delta,s\geq0,\qquad
\varepsilon>0,\qquad 72\varepsilon^2\leq L\Delta.
\]
The algorithm receives $m,n,L,\Delta,s,\varepsilon$, and $x^0$; the objective and the stochastic oracle model are accessed only through oracle replies.

Our assumptions:
\begin{assumption}[Spectral smoothness and initial gap]
The function $f$ is differentiable everywhere, and
\[
\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|_*
\leq L\|x-y\|_{\mathrm{op}},
\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}.
\]
There exists $f_{\mathrm{low}}\in\mathbb{R}$ such that
\[
f(x)\geq f_{\mathrm{low}}\quad\text{for every }x,
\qquad f(x^0)-f_{\mathrm{low}}\leq\Delta.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Unbiased oracle with a rowwise variance profile]
Let $\nu$ be a probability measure on a measurable sample space $\Omega$. The oracle is a jointly measurable map
$g:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$.
For each $x$, $g(x,\cdot)$ is Bochner integrable and
\[
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\nu}[g(x,\xi)]=\nabla f(x).
\]
There exist an orthonormal basis $(b_i)_{i=1}^m$ of $\mathbb{R}^m$ and numbers $\sigma_i\geq0$ with $\sum_{i=1}^m\sigma_i\leq s$ such that, for every $x$ and $i$, the vector
$(g(x,\xi)-\nabla f(x))^{\mathsf T}b_i$ belongs to $L^2(\nu;\mathbb{R}^n)$ and
\[
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\nu}
\bigl[\|(g(x,\xi)-\nabla f(x))^{\mathsf T}b_i\|_2^2\bigr]
\leq\sigma_i^2.
\]
At call $k$, a query $x$ receives $g(x,\xi_k)$, where the measurable random variables $(\xi_k)_{k\geq0}$ are independent with common law $\nu$. Algorithm randomness is independent of this sample sequence. Queries may be made anywhere in the matrix space.
\end{assumption}

Our formulation is algorithm-specific: we analyze batched Muon with a constant step size and no momentum. Define $\operatorname{polar}(G)$ to be the minimum-Frobenius-norm element of
\[
\mathop{\operatorname{argmax}}_{\|U\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq1}
\langle G,U\rangle_F.
\]
The algorithm chooses $\eta>0$, an integer batch size $B\geq1$, and an integer $T\geq0$ from its inputs. Starting at the given $x^0$, it queries $B$ times at each $x^t$ and follows
\[
\begin{aligned}
G_t&=\frac1B\sum_{j=0}^{B-1}g(x^t,\xi_{Bt+j}),\\
x^{t+1}&=x^t-\eta\operatorname{polar}(G_t),
\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.
\end{aligned}
\]
After $N=B(T+1)$ calls, it returns $\bar{x}=x^J$, where $J$ is a measurable function of algorithm randomness, uniform on $\{0,\ldots,T\}$. The same output is retained after any additional calls. Thus the batch at $x^T$ is counted even though $x^{T+1}$ is not an output candidate. These query and output rules must hold for every realization of algorithm randomness and every possible reply sequence.

For any objective and oracle satisfying the assumptions, the required stationarity criterion is
\[
\mathbb{E}_{P}\bigl[\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|_*\bigr]\leq\varepsilon,
\]
with $\bar{x}$ measurable and $\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|_*$ integrable under $P$, the joint law of algorithm randomness and oracle samples.

The task additionally includes two worst-case comparisons at the same parameter values. The comparison methods are SGD with momentum and Frobenius-normalized SGD with momentum, with directions
\[
d_{\mathrm{SGD}}(M)=M,\qquad
 d_{\mathrm{NSGD}}(M)=
\begin{cases}
M/\|M\|_F,&M\ne0,\\
0,&M=0.
\end{cases}
\]
For either direction $d$, fix any $\beta\in[0,1)$, any real-valued schedule $(\eta_t)_{t\geq0}$, and integers $B\geq1$ and $T\geq0$. The comparison recurrence, initialized at $(y^0,M^0)=(x^0,0)$, is
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_t&=\frac1B\sum_{j=0}^{B-1}g(y^t,\xi_{Bt+j}),\\
M^{t+1}&=\beta M^t+(1-\beta)H_t,\\
y^{t+1}&=y^t-\eta_t d(M^{t+1}),
\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every batch consists of $B$ queries at $y^t$. After $N=B(T+1)$ calls the output is $\bar{y}=y^J$, with $J$ uniform on $\{0,\ldots,T\}$ and drawn measurably from algorithm randomness independently of the oracle samples; this output is retained after additional calls. The recurrence, queries, and output rule are required for every possible reply sequence and every realization of algorithm randomness.

For every such SGD algorithm, the required comparison asserts the existence of an objective and oracle satisfying the assumptions for which measurable, integrable expected stationarity of $\bar{y}$ at tolerance $\varepsilon$ implies
\[
(1-\beta)(r-1)L\Delta s\leq81\varepsilon^3N.
\]
For every such normalized SGD algorithm, a possibly different objective and oracle satisfying the assumptions must exist for which the same stationarity criterion implies at least one of
\[
\begin{aligned}
(1-\beta)(r-1)L\Delta s
&\leq288\varepsilon^3
\left(2+\log\left(1+\frac{s\sqrt r}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)N,\\
(1-\beta)^2(r-1)s^2
&\leq24\varepsilon^2N.
\end{aligned}
\]
These existential comparisons are part of the required task, together with the expected stationarity requirement for batched Muon.
