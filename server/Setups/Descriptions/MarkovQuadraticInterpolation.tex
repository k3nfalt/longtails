\section*{Quadratic Interpolation with Markov Oracles}
We consider minimizing a quadratic function $f:E\to\mathbb{R}$ on a separable real Hilbert space $E$. Its component Hessians $H_i$ are self-adjoint and satisfy $0\preceq H_i\preceq LI$ for $L>0$, where $I$ is the identity; all components share a minimizer $x^\star$ with $\|x^0-x^\star\|\leq R$, where $x^0\in E$ and $R\geq0$. A call at $x\in E$ returns $H_i(x-x^\star)$, with $i$ following a finite Markov chain from an arbitrary initial law. Its transition law dominates $\alpha$ times its invariant law, for $0<\alpha\leq1$. The measurable output $\bar{x}\in E$ must have integrable objective gap and satisfy $\mathbb{E}[f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)]\leq\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon>0$ and expectation includes all randomness. Only $(E,L,x^0,\alpha)$ are public inputs.

The optimization problem is
\[
\min_{x\in E} f(x),
\]
with the norm induced by the inner product on $E$. The objective and its oracle admit the following common representation.

\begin{assumption}[Interpolating quadratic components]
There exist a finite index set $\mathcal{I}=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$ with $m\geq1$, a probability distribution $\pi$ on $\mathcal{I}$, bounded linear operators $H_i:E\to E$, and a point $x^\star\in E$ such that, for every $i\in\mathcal{I}$ and $v,x\in E$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_i^* &= H_i,\\
0\leq\langle v,H_i v\rangle &\leq L\|v\|^2,\\
f(x)-f(x^\star)
&=\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\pi_i
  \langle x-x^\star,H_i(x-x^\star)\rangle,\\
\|x^0-x^\star\|&\leq R.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $H_i^*$ denotes the adjoint and $\pi_i=\pi(\{i\})$. In particular, $x^\star$ is a global minimizer, and $f$ is convex and differentiable with $L$-Lipschitz gradient.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Invariant law and refresh condition]
There exist an arbitrary initial probability distribution $\mu$ on $\mathcal{I}$ and a Markov transition matrix $\kappa$ satisfying, for every $i,j\in\mathcal{I}$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_{ij}&\geq0,
&\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}\kappa_{ij}&=1,\\
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\pi_i\kappa_{ij}&=\pi_j,
&\kappa_{ij}&\geq\alpha\pi_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
The oracle indices $(Z_k)_{k\geq0}$ have the time-homogeneous Markov law
\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_0&\sim\mu,\\
\mathbb{P}(Z_{k+1}=j\mid Z_0,\ldots,Z_k)
&=\kappa_{Z_kj}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $\pi$ is invariant, while the initial distribution need not equal $\pi$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Markov component-gradient access]
At call $k$, a query $x\in E$ returns only the vector
\[
g(x,Z_k)=H_{Z_k}(x-x^\star)\in E.
\]
Each reply costs one oracle call and uses the next index in the chain. Queries may be adaptive and may lie anywhere in $E$. The algorithm's internal randomness is independent of the entire oracle chain.
\end{assumption}

The map $(x,i)\mapsto g(x,i)$ is jointly measurable. For every fixed $x$, its finite stationary average is a Bochner integral satisfying
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\pi_i g(x,i)=\nabla f(x).
\]
This identity concerns averaging under $\pi$; it does not assert conditional unbiasedness at adaptive query points.

The algorithm is selected using the public inputs specified above. The quantities $R$ and $\varepsilon$, the objective, and its representation $(m,\pi,\mu,\kappa,(H_i)_{i\in\mathcal{I}},x^\star)$ are not supplied to it. An externally specified budget $K\in\mathbb{N}$ may depend on the full parameter tuple $(E,L,x^0,\alpha,R,\varepsilon)$. Every consumed chain index, including one whose reply is discarded, counts toward this budget.

After $K$ calls, the algorithm returns $\bar{x}\in E$. Let $\mathbb{P}$ denote the joint law of its independent internal randomness and the oracle chain. Success requires $\bar{x}$ to be measurable and its objective gap to be integrable, with
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int \bigl|f(\bar{x}(\omega))-f(x^\star)\bigr|
\,d\mathbb{P}(\omega)&<\infty,\\
\int \bigl(f(\bar{x}(\omega))-f(x^\star)\bigr)
\,d\mathbb{P}(\omega)&\leq\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\]
The minimizer in the quadratic representation may be used here because all global minimizers have the same objective value.
