\section*{Bandwidth-Limited Distributed Nonconvex Optimization}
We seek a stationary point of a possibly nonconvex objective $f$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$, where $d\geq 0$ is the dimension. The objective averages differentiable worker functions, has a Lipschitz gradient, and admits a lower bound with a prescribed gap from the initial value. Each worker returns a batch of unbiased local gradient estimates with bounded variance, using independent samples within and across workers. Workers have heterogeneous computation times and communicate over directed links with latency and per-bit costs. For tolerance $\varepsilon>0$, the goal is an output $\bar{x}$ satisfying $\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|^2\leq\varepsilon^2$, averaging over sampling and algorithm randomness.

With $n\geq 1$ workers and local functions $f_i:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$, the optimization problem is
\[
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^d} f(x),
\qquad f(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n f_i(x).
\]
All norms are Euclidean. The algorithm receives $d$, $n$, the measurable sample space $\Omega$, the starting point $x^0\in\mathbb{R}^d$, the smoothness constant $L>0$, the initial gap bound $\Delta\geq 0$, the noise bound $\sigma\geq 0$, the tolerance $\varepsilon$, the worker computation times $h_i>0$, the link latency $\ell\geq 0$, and the transmission time per bit $b\geq 0$.

Our assumptions:
\begin{assumption}[Aggregate smoothness and initial gap]
Each $f_i$ is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}^d$. There exists $a\in\mathbb{R}$ such that, for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^d$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\| &\leq L\|x-y\|,\\
a &\leq f(x),\\
f(x^0)-a &\leq \Delta.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Local stochastic gradients]
Worker $i$ has a sequence $(\xi_{i,k})_{k\geq 0}$ of independent measurable samples with common law $\mu_i$ on $\Omega$. The worker sequences are mutually independent, and the algorithm's randomness is independent of the entire sample array. Each map $g_i:\mathbb{R}^d\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^d$ is jointly measurable. For every $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$ and $\xi\sim\mu_i$, the vector $g_i(x,\xi)$ is Bochner integrable, its squared error is integrable, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu_i}[g_i(x,\xi)] &= \nabla f_i(x),\\
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu_i}\bigl[\|g_i(x,\xi)-\nabla f_i(x)\|^2\bigr] &\leq \sigma^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

An idle worker $i$ accepts a query consisting of a point $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$ and an integer batch size $m\geq 1$. After $m h_i$ units of time it returns the ordered list
\[
\bigl(g_i(x,\xi_{i,k}),\ldots,g_i(x,\xi_{i,k+m-1})\bigr),
\]
where $k$ is the number of samples already used by that worker. A worker ignores further computation requests while its batch is pending. Different workers may compute concurrently.

Every ordered pair of workers, including a worker paired with itself, has a directed link. Messages on each link are queued in submission order. A message submitted at time $t$ with a payload of $q\geq 0$ bits is ready for delivery at time
\[
\max\{t,c\}+\ell+b(q+64),
\]
where $c$ is the completion time of the preceding message on that link, or zero for the first message. The receiver obtains the sender's identity and the payload followed by the $64$ zero bits included in the transmission cost. Distinct directed links may transmit concurrently, and communication may overlap computation.

For an elapsed-time budget $T\geq 0$, almost surely every deadline $s\in[0,T]$ must be reached with only finitely many events through that deadline. At time $T$, the algorithm's designated root worker must produce a measurable output $\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^d$ with integrable squared gradient norm satisfying
\[
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|^2\bigr]\leq\varepsilon^2.
\]
