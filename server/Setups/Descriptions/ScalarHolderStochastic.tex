\section*{Scalar H\"older Stochastic Stationarity}
We consider a differentiable, possibly nonconvex function $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ with a globally H\"older continuous derivative. A given point $x^0\in\mathbb{R}$ has objective gap at most $\Delta>0$ above a global lower bound. At $x$, the oracle returns an unbiased scalar estimate of $f'(x)$ using a fresh independent sample; its error has uniformly bounded $p$th absolute moment for $1<p\leq2$, allowing heavy-tailed noise. For $\varepsilon>0$, the goal is a measurable output $\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}$ satisfying $P(|f'(\bar{x})|>\varepsilon)\leq1/3$, where $P$ is the joint law of the samples and the algorithm's independent randomness.

The underlying optimization problem is
\[
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}} f(x),
\]
with accuracy measured by stationarity. Fix a measurable sample space $\Omega$ and parameters
\[
H>0,\qquad 0<\nu\leq1,\qquad \Delta>0,\qquad
\sigma\geq0,\qquad 1<p\leq2,\qquad \varepsilon>0.
\]
The algorithm receives $\Omega$, $H$, $\nu$, $\Delta$, $\sigma$, $p$, $\varepsilon$, and $x^0$; the objective and sampling distribution are accessed through the oracle.

Our assumptions:
\begin{assumption}[H\"older continuity]
The function $f$ is differentiable everywhere, and
\[
|f'(x)-f'(y)|\leq H|x-y|^{\nu},
\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{R}.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Lower bound and initial gap]
There exists $\ell\in\mathbb{R}$ such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\ell&\leq f(x) &&\text{for every }x\in\mathbb{R},\\
f(x^0)-\ell&\leq\Delta.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Independent stochastic derivative oracle]
There exist a probability measure $\mu$ on $\Omega$ and a jointly measurable function $g:\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}$. For each $x\in\mathbb{R}$, both $g(x,\cdot)$ and $|g(x,\cdot)-f'(x)|^p$ are integrable under $\mu$, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu}[g(x,\xi)]&=f'(x),\\
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mu}[|g(x,\xi)-f'(x)|^p]&\leq\sigma^p.
\end{aligned}
\]
At call $k\geq0$, a query $x\in\mathbb{R}$ receives the scalar reply $g(x,\xi_k)$. The samples $(\xi_k)_{k\geq0}$ are measurable, mutually independent, and each has law $\mu$. The algorithm's randomness is independent of the entire sample sequence.
\end{assumption}

Queries may be made at any point of $\mathbb{R}$. After the allotted oracle calls, the output $\bar{x}$ must be measurable and satisfy
\[
P\bigl(|f'(\bar{x})|>\varepsilon\bigr)\leq\frac{1}{3}.
\]
