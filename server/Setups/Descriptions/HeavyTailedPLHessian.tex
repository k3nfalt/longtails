\section*{Heavy-Tailed Polyak--\L{}ojasiewicz Minimization}
We consider minimizing a possibly nonconvex function $f:E\to\mathbb{R}$ on a finite-dimensional real inner product space $E$. The objective has Lipschitz gradient and Hessian, satisfies a Polyak--\L{}ojasiewicz inequality, and admits a global minimizer $x^\star$. A given starting point $x^0\in E$ has bounded objective gap. The oracle returns an unbiased stochastic gradient at each query, using independent, identically distributed samples with bounded noise moments of order $p\in(1,2]$, allowing heavy tails. For $\varepsilon>0$, the goal is a measurable output $\bar{x}\in E$ with integrable objective gap satisfying $\mathbb{E}[f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)]\leq\varepsilon$, where expectation includes sampling and independent algorithm randomness.

The optimization problem is
\[
\min_{x\in E} f(x).
\]
Write $\|\cdot\|$ for the norm induced by the inner product and $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ for the induced operator norm. Fix parameters
\[
0<\mu\leq L,\qquad \rho,\sigma,\Delta\geq0,
\qquad 1<p\leq2,\qquad \varepsilon>0.
\]
The algorithm receives $E$, the measurable sample space $\Omega$, $L$, $\mu$, $\rho$, $\sigma$, $p$, $\Delta$, $\varepsilon$, and $x^0$.

Our assumptions:
\begin{assumption}[Gradient and Hessian regularity]
The function $f$ is Fr\'echet differentiable everywhere, and its gradient is Fr\'echet differentiable. The Hessian $\nabla^2 f(x)$ is a linear operator from $E$ to $E$. For all $x,y\in E$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|&\leq L\|x-y\|,\\
\|\nabla^2 f(x)-\nabla^2 f(y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}&\leq\rho\|x-y\|.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Polyak--\L{}ojasiewicz inequality and initialization]
There exists $x^\star\in E$ such that, for every $x\in E$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x^\star)&\leq f(x),\\
2\mu\bigl(f(x)-f(x^\star)\bigr)&\leq\|\nabla f(x)\|^2,
\end{aligned}
\]
and the given starting point satisfies
\[
f(x^0)-f(x^\star)\leq\Delta.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Unbiased oracle with heavy-tailed noise]
There is a probability measure $\nu$ on $\Omega$ and a jointly measurable map $g:E\times\Omega\to E$. For each $x\in E$, the map $g(x,\cdot)$ is Bochner integrable and $\|g(x,\cdot)-\nabla f(x)\|^p$ is integrable under $\nu$, with
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\nu}[g(x,\xi)]&=\nabla f(x),\\
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\nu}\bigl[\|g(x,\xi)-\nabla f(x)\|^p\bigr]&\leq\sigma^p.
\end{aligned}
\]
The samples $(\xi_k)_{k\geq0}$ are measurable, mutually independent, and each has law $\nu$ under their joint probability law on $\Omega^{\mathbb{N}}$. At the $k$th query $x\in E$, the oracle returns $g(x,\xi_k)$. Algorithm randomness is independent of this sample sequence.
\end{assumption}

Queries may be made at any point of $E$. After the allotted oracle calls, the output $\bar{x}$ must be measurable and have integrable objective gap, with
\[
\mathbb{E}_{P}\bigl[f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)\bigr]\leq\varepsilon,
\]
where $P$ is the joint law of the samples and the algorithm's randomness.
