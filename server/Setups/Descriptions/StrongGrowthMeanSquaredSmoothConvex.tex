\section*{Strong-Growth Mean-Square Smooth Convex Minimization}
We consider minimizing a differentiable convex function $f:E\to\mathbb{R}$ on a separable real Hilbert space $E$. A given point $x^0\in E$ lies within distance $R\geq0$ of a global minimizer $x^\star$. The oracle returns $(g(x,\xi),g(y,\xi))$ at a pair $(x,y)\in E^2$, using a shared sample $\xi$ from a probability law $p$ on a measurable space $\Omega$, independently across calls. The estimator $g:E\times\Omega\to E$ is unbiased, with variance bounded by $\Gamma^2\|\nabla f(x)\|^2$, where $\Gamma\geq0$, and is mean-square Lipschitz with constant $\bar{L}>0$. For $\varepsilon>0$, the goal is a measurable output $\bar{x}\in E$ with integrable objective gap satisfying $\mathbb{E}[f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)]\leq\varepsilon$, averaging over oracle and algorithm randomness.

The optimization problem is
\[
\min_{x\in E} f(x).
\]
The norm $\|\cdot\|$ is induced by the inner product on $E$, which is equipped with its Borel sigma-algebra. The algorithm receives $E$, $\Omega$, $\bar{L}$, $\Gamma$, $R$, $\varepsilon$, and $x^0$; the objective is accessed through the oracle.

Our assumptions:
\begin{assumption}[Convexity and initial distance]
The function $f$ is Fr\'echet differentiable everywhere on $E$, and for every $x,y\in E$ and $t\in[0,1]$,
\[
f((1-t)x+ty)\leq(1-t)f(x)+tf(y).
\]
There exists $x^\star\in E$ such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x^\star)&\leq f(x) &&\text{for every }x\in E,\\
\|x^0-x^\star\|&\leq R.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Unbiasedness and strong growth]
The map $g:E\times\Omega\to E$ is jointly measurable. For every $x\in E$, $g(x,\cdot)$ is Bochner integrable under $p$, the squared error $\|g(x,\cdot)-\nabla f(x)\|^2$ is integrable, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim p}[g(x,\xi)]&=\nabla f(x),\\
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim p}\bigl[\|g(x,\xi)-\nabla f(x)\|^2\bigr]
&\leq\Gamma^2\|\nabla f(x)\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Mean-square smoothness]
For every $x,y\in E$, the squared difference $\|g(x,\cdot)-g(y,\cdot)\|^2$ is integrable under $p$, and
\[
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim p}\bigl[\|g(x,\xi)-g(y,\xi)\|^2\bigr]
\leq\bar{L}^2\|x-y\|^2.
\]
\end{assumption}

More precisely, the oracle samples form a sequence $(\xi_k)_{k\geq0}$ of measurable, mutually independent random variables, each with law $p$. Algorithm randomness is independent of this sequence. At call $k$, the algorithm may query any pair $(x,y)\in E\times E$ and receives
\[
\bigl(g(x,\xi_k),g(y,\xi_k)\bigr).
\]
Each paired reply counts as one oracle call; both components use the same sample.

After the allotted oracle calls, the output $\bar{x}$ must be measurable, its objective gap $f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)$ must be integrable, and
\[
\mathbb{E}_{P}\bigl[f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)\bigr]\leq\varepsilon,
\]
where $P$ is the joint law of the algorithm randomness and the oracle sample sequence.
