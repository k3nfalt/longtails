\section*{Adam with Heavy-Tailed Gradients}
We consider stationarity for a differentiable, possibly nonconvex function $f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$, where $d\geq0$ is an integer. Its gradient is $L$-Lipschitz, with $L>0$, and a given starting point $x^0$ satisfies $f(x^0)-\inf_x f(x)\leq\Delta$, with $\Delta\geq0$. The oracle returns unbiased stochastic gradients with independent, identically distributed samples and coordinatewise noise moments of order $\alpha\in(1,2]$ whose sum is bounded by $\sigma^\alpha$, where $\sigma\geq0$. We analyze Adam with a randomly selected iterate $\bar{x}$; the goal is $\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|\leq\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon>0$ and expectation includes sampling and iterate selection.

The underlying optimization problem is
\[
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^d} f(x),
\]
and $\|\cdot\|$ denotes the Euclidean norm. The algorithm receives $d$, the measurable sample space $\Omega$, $L$, $\sigma$, $\alpha$, $\Delta$, $\varepsilon$, and $x^0\in\mathbb{R}^d$.

Our assumptions:
\begin{assumption}[Smoothness and initial gap]
The function $f$ is differentiable everywhere, and for every $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^d$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|&\leq L\|x-y\|,\\
f(x^0)-f(x)&\leq\Delta.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Heavy-tailed stochastic gradients]
Let $p$ be a probability measure on $\Omega$. The oracle is a jointly measurable map $g:\mathbb{R}^d\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^d$. For each $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$, the map $g(x,\cdot)$ is Bochner integrable, and the scalar random variable $\sum_{i=1}^d |g_i(x,\xi)-\nabla_i f(x)|^\alpha$ is integrable for $\xi\sim p$. They satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim p}[g(x,\xi)]&=\nabla f(x),\\
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim p}\left[
\sum_{i=1}^d |g_i(x,\xi)-\nabla_i f(x)|^\alpha
\right]&\leq\sigma^\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]
At call $k$, a query $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$ receives $g(x,\xi_k)$. The measurable sample variables $(\xi_k)_{k\geq0}$ are mutually independent with common law $p$ under their joint law $Q$, and are independent of the algorithm's randomness.
\end{assumption}

Our result is algorithm-specific: we analyze Adam with constant parameters and without bias correction. The algorithm chooses
\[
0\leq\beta<1,\qquad 0<\rho\leq1,\qquad
\eta>0,\qquad\tau>0,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq0},
\]
using the available inputs. Starting from the given $x^0$ and $m^0=v^0=0$, it queries at $x^k$ and performs the coordinatewise updates
\[
\begin{aligned}
g^k&=g(x^k,\xi_k),\\
m^{k+1}&=\beta m^k+(1-\beta)g^k,\\
v_i^{k+1}&=(1-\rho)v_i^k+\rho(g_i^k)^2,\\
x_i^{k+1}&=x_i^k-\eta\frac{m_i^{k+1}}{\sqrt{v_i^{k+1}}+\tau},
\qquad i=1,\ldots,d.
\end{aligned}
\]
Independently of the samples, it draws $J$ uniformly from $\{0,\ldots,n\}$. Its saved output is initialized to $x^0$ and replaced by the pre-update iterate $x^J$ when call $J$ is made. Thus, after $B\geq0$ oracle calls, its output is
\[
\bar{x}=
\begin{cases}
x^J,&J<B,\\
x^0,&J\geq B.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, with $B=n+1$ calls the output is the uniformly selected iterate $x^J$ from $x^0,\ldots,x^n$; the final call and update are included in this budget.

Let $P$ be the joint law of the samples and the independent index $J$. The required output $\bar{x}$ is measurable, $\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|$ is integrable under $P$, and the accuracy requirement is
\[
\mathbb{E}_{P}\bigl[\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|\bigr]\leq\varepsilon.
\]
