\section*{Hidden Diagonal Curvature Minimization}
We consider minimizing a differentiable function $f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$, where $d\geq1$, with a given starting point $x^0\in\mathbb{R}^d$. For hidden weights $w_1,\ldots,w_d>0$, its first-order remainder lies between $\mu\|y-x\|_w^2/2$ and $\|y-x\|_w^2/2$, where $\|v\|_w^2=\sum_{i=1}^d w_i v_i^2$ and $0<\mu\leq1$. A global minimizer $x^\star$ satisfies $f(x^0)-f(x^\star)\leq\Delta$, with $\Delta\geq0$. The oracle returns $(f(x),\nabla f(x))$ at $x$. For $\varepsilon>0$, the goal is $f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)\leq\varepsilon$ almost surely under $P$, the law of the algorithm's randomness.

The optimization problem is
\[
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^d} f(x).
\]
The inner product and gradient are Euclidean. The algorithm receives only $d$ and $x^0$; the weights $w_1,\ldots,w_d$, relative curvature parameter $\mu$, initial gap bound $\Delta$, and tolerance $\varepsilon$ may enter the oracle-call budget but are not algorithm inputs.

Our assumptions:
\begin{assumption}[Diagonal curvature bounds]
The function $f$ is differentiable everywhere. For every $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^d$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(y)&\leq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle
       +\frac{1}{2}\|y-x\|_w^2,\\
f(y)&\geq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle
       +\frac{\mu}{2}\|y-x\|_w^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Initial objective gap]
There exists $x^\star\in\mathbb{R}^d$ such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x^\star)&\leq f(x) &&\text{for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^d,\\
f(x^0)-f(x^\star)&\leq\Delta.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

Queries may be made at any point of $\mathbb{R}^d$, with each call returning the exact function value and Euclidean gradient together. After the allotted oracle calls, the output $\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^d$ must satisfy
\[
f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)\leq\varepsilon
\quad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
