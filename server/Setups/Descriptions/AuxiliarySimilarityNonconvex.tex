\section*{Nonconvex Optimization with Auxiliary Similarity}
We consider minimizing a possibly nonconvex function $f:E\to\mathbb{R}$ on a finite-dimensional real inner product space $E$, with a differentiable auxiliary function $h:E\to\mathbb{R}$. The objective is twice continuously differentiable with Lipschitz Hessian; both the auxiliary gradient and the gradient difference $\nabla f-\nabla h$ are Lipschitz. A given point $x^0\in E$ has bounded objective gap above the infimum. Two exact oracles return $(f(x),\nabla f(x))$ and $(h(x),\nabla h(x))$ at a query $x\in E$. For $\varepsilon>0$, the goal is an output $\bar{x}\in E$ satisfying $\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|\leq\varepsilon$ almost surely under $P$, the law of the algorithm's randomness.

The optimization problem is
\[
\inf_{x\in E} f(x).
\]
The norm $\|\cdot\|$ is induced by the inner product on $E$, and $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ denotes the induced operator norm. The parameters satisfy
\[
L\geq 0,\qquad \delta>0,\qquad \rho>0,\qquad
\Delta\geq 0,\qquad \varepsilon>0.
\]
The algorithm receives $E$, $x^0$, $L$, $\delta$, $\rho$, $\Delta$, and $\varepsilon$.

Our assumptions:
\begin{assumption}[Hessian regularity]
The objective $f$ is twice continuously Fr\'echet differentiable on $E$. Its Hessian, viewed as a linear operator from $E$ to $E$, satisfies
\[
\|\nabla^2 f(x)-\nabla^2 f(y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq \rho\|x-y\|,
\qquad x,y\in E.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Auxiliary smoothness and similarity]
The auxiliary function $h$ is Fr\'echet differentiable on $E$. For every $x,y\in E$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\nabla h(x)-\nabla h(y)\|
&\leq L\|x-y\|,\\
\|(\nabla f(x)-\nabla h(x))
-(\nabla f(y)-\nabla h(y))\|
&\leq \delta\|x-y\|.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Initial objective gap]
The given point $x^0$ satisfies
\[
f(x^0)-f(x)\leq\Delta,
\qquad x\in E.
\]
\end{assumption}

Both oracles accept queries at any point of $E$, and their calls are counted separately. After the allotted calls, the required accuracy is
\[
\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|\leq\varepsilon
\quad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
