\section*{BFGS for Linear Predictors}
We consider BFGS for minimizing $f(x)=h(Ax)$ on $\mathbb{R}^d$, where $A:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^m$ is linear and $h:\mathbb{R}^m\to\mathbb{R}$ is convex with Lipschitz gradient. A given starting point $x^0$ lies within distance $R\geq0$ of a global minimizer $x^\star$, and the objective gap controls squared prediction distance on the initial sublevel set. The oracle returns a step length and the exact gradient at the resulting point, satisfying Armijo--Wolfe conditions for descent directions. For $\varepsilon>0$, the goal is a last iterate $\bar{x}$ satisfying $f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)\leq\varepsilon$ almost surely under $P$, the law of the oracle's randomness.

The optimization problem is
\[
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^d} f(x).
\]
Here $d,m\in\mathbb{N}_0$, both spaces carry their Euclidean inner products and norms, and $\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ denotes the induced operator norm. The parameters satisfy
\[
0<\mu\leq L_h,\qquad L>0,\qquad
0<c_1<c_2<1,\qquad R\geq0,\qquad\varepsilon>0.
\]
The algorithm receives only $d,m,x^0,L$; the parameters $L_h,\mu,c_1,c_2,R,\varepsilon$ may enter the oracle-call budget but are not algorithm inputs.

Our assumptions:
\begin{assumption}[Convexity and smoothness]
The function $h$ is differentiable everywhere, and for every $z,w\in\mathbb{R}^m$ and $t\in[0,1]$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
h((1-t)z+tw)&\leq(1-t)h(z)+t h(w),\\
\|\nabla h(z)-\nabla h(w)\|&\leq L_h\|z-w\|.
\end{aligned}
\]
The linear map satisfies
\[
L_h\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\leq L.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Initial distance and prediction growth]
There exists $x^\star\in\mathbb{R}^d$ such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x^\star)&\leq f(x)\qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^d,\\
\|x^0-x^\star\|&\leq R.
\end{aligned}
\]
For every $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$ with $f(x)\leq f(x^0)$,
\[
\frac{\mu}{2}\|Ax-Ax^\star\|^2
\leq f(x)-f(x^\star).
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Armijo--Wolfe oracle]
A query is any pair $(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^d\times\mathbb{R}^d$. The oracle returns $(\alpha,g)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^d$ with
\[
g=\nabla f(x+\alpha v).
\]
Whenever $\langle\nabla f(x),v\rangle<0$, the reply also satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&>0,\\
f(x+\alpha v)&\leq f(x)+c_1\alpha\langle\nabla f(x),v\rangle,\\
\langle g,v\rangle&\geq c_2\langle\nabla f(x),v\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]
These conditions hold for every query, every prior query--reply history, and every realization of the oracle randomness. Replies may depend on the history and may be dependent across calls.
\end{assumption}

The task is algorithm-specific: we analyze BFGS with a scalar initial Hessian approximation. The algorithm chooses $c>0$ using only its available inputs and initializes $B_0=cI_d$ at the given point $x^0$. One initial query $(x^0,0)$ supplies $g_0=\nabla f(x^0)$; its step-length reply is discarded. For $k\geq0$, the method queries $(x^k,v_k)$, receives $(\alpha_k,g_{k+1})$, and updates
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_k&=-B_k^{-1}g_k,\\
s_k&=\alpha_k v_k,\qquad x^{k+1}=x^k+s_k,\\
y_k&=g_{k+1}-g_k,\\
B_{k+1}&=B_k
-\frac{(B_ks_k)(B_ks_k)^{\mathsf T}}{s_k^{\mathsf T}B_ks_k}
+\frac{y_ky_k^{\mathsf T}}{y_k^{\mathsf T}s_k}.
\end{aligned}
\]
To define the recurrence also in singular cases, a scalar reciprocal at zero is taken to be zero, and the inverse of a singular matrix is the zero matrix.

After the initial query and $K\in\mathbb{N}_0$ updates, the output is the last iterate $\bar{x}=x^K$, using $K+1$ oracle calls in total. With zero calls, the output is $x^0$. The required accuracy is
\[
f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)\leq\varepsilon
\quad P\text{-a.s.}
\]
