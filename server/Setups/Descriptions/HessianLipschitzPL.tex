\section*{Hessian-Lipschitz Minimization Under Gradient Dominance}
We minimize a twice differentiable function $f:E\to\mathbb{R}$ on a finite-dimensional real inner product space $E$. Its Hessian is $H$-Lipschitz for $H>0$, and $f$ satisfies the Polyak--{\L}ojasiewicz inequality with constant $\mu>0$. The given starting point $x^0\in E$ has objective gap at most $\Delta\geq 0$ relative to a global minimizer $x^\star$. At any $x\in E$, the oracle returns $(f(x),\nabla f(x),\nabla^2 f(x))$. For $\varepsilon>0$, the goal is a measurable output $\bar{x}\in E$ with integrable gap satisfying $\mathbb{E}[f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)]\leq\varepsilon$, where the expectation is over the algorithm's randomness.

The optimization problem is
\[
\min_{x\in E} f(x).
\]
The algorithm receives only $E$, $H$, and $x^0$. The parameters $\mu$, $\Delta$, and $\varepsilon$ may enter only the oracle-call budget. We use the inner product norm on $E$ and the induced operator norm $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ on linear maps from $E$ to $E$.

Our assumptions:
\begin{assumption}[Lipschitz Hessian]
The function $f$ and its gradient are Fr\'echet differentiable everywhere on $E$. The Hessian satisfies
\[
\|\nabla^2 f(x)-\nabla^2 f(y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq H\|x-y\|,
\qquad x,y\in E.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Gradient dominance and initial gap]
There exists a global minimizer $x^\star\in E$ such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\mu\bigl(f(x)-f(x^\star)\bigr)&\leq \|\nabla f(x)\|^2 &&\text{for every }x\in E,\\
f(x^0)-f(x^\star)&\leq \Delta.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

After the allotted oracle calls, the output must satisfy the expected objective-gap criterion
\[
\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)\bigr]\leq\varepsilon.
\]
