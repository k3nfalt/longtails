\section*{Adaptive Heavy-Tailed Simplex Optimization}
We consider minimizing $f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ over the simplex $\Delta_d=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^d:x_i\geq0,\ \sum_{i=1}^d x_i=1\}$, where $d\geq2$. The objective is convex on $\Delta_d$, differentiable at its points, and attains its minimum at $x^\star\in\Delta_d$. Its gradient is $L$-Lipschitz from the $\ell_1$ norm to the $\ell_\infty$ norm, with $L>0$. Each call returns an unbiased random gradient with independent sampling and $p$th noise moment at most $\sigma^p$ in $\ell_\infty$, where $1<p\leq2$ and $\sigma\geq0$. The measurable output $\bar{x}\in\Delta_d$ must satisfy $\mathbb{P}(f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)>\varepsilon)\leq\delta$, where $\varepsilon>0$, $0<\delta<1$, and $\mathbb{P}$ is the joint randomness law. Only $(d,L,\delta)$ are public.

The constrained optimization problem is
\[
\min_{x\in\Delta_d} f(x).
\]
The ambient space carries the standard Euclidean inner product, which defines $\nabla f$. The norms governing smoothness and oracle noise are
\[
\|v\|_1=\sum_{i=1}^d |v_i|,
\qquad
\|v\|_\infty=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}|v_i|.
\]
The admissible objectives and oracle laws satisfy the following conditions.

\begin{assumption}[Convexity and smoothness]
At every $x\in\Delta_d$, the function $f$ is differentiable as a function on $\mathbb{R}^d$, with gradient $\nabla f(x)$. For every $x,y\in\Delta_d$ and $t\in[0,1]$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
f((1-t)x+ty)&\leq(1-t)f(x)+tf(y),\\
\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|_\infty
&\leq L\|x-y\|_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Attainment]
There exists $x^\star\in\Delta_d$ such that
\[
f(x^\star)\leq f(x)
\qquad\text{for every }x\in\Delta_d.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Independent oracle sampling]
Let $\Omega$ be a measurable space and $\nu$ a probability measure on it. The oracle uses samples $(\xi_k)_{k\geq0}$ whose joint law on $\Omega^{\mathbb{N}}$ makes the coordinate maps measurable and mutually independent, with marginal law $\nu$ at every index. A call at $x\in\Delta_d$ returns $g(x,\xi_k)\in\mathbb{R}^d$, where $g:\Delta_d\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^d$ is jointly measurable for the simplex's Borel structure. Each reply costs one oracle call. Internal algorithm randomness is independent of the entire sample sequence.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Unbiasedness and heavy-tail moment]
For every $x\in\Delta_d$, the map $g(x,\cdot)$ is Bochner integrable under $\nu$, and the scalar map $\|g(x,\cdot)-\nabla f(x)\|_\infty^p$ is integrable under $\nu$. They satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_\Omega g(x,\omega)\,d\nu(\omega)
&=\nabla f(x),\\
\int_\Omega \|g(x,\omega)-\nabla f(x)\|_\infty^p\,d\nu(\omega)
&\leq\sigma^p.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

The objective is accessed through this oracle, with queries chosen adaptively within $\Delta_d$. The algorithm is selected using only $(d,L,\delta)$; the moment order $p$, noise bound $\sigma$, and accuracy level $\varepsilon$ are not supplied to it. An externally specified budget $K\in\mathbb{N}$ may depend on the full setup parameters and counts every oracle reply.

After $K$ calls, the algorithm must return a measurable feasible point $\bar{x}\in\Delta_d$. Under the joint law $\mathbb{P}$ of its internal randomness and the oracle samples, success requires a minimizer $x^\star$ satisfying
\[
\mathbb{P}\bigl(f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)>\varepsilon\bigr)
\leq\delta.
\]
