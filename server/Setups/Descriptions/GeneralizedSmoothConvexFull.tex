\section*{Generalized Smooth Convex Minimization}
We consider minimizing a convex function $f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ in dimension $d\in\mathbb{N}$. The function $f$ is $(L_0,L_1)$-smooth, meaning its differentiable gradient satisfies $\|\nabla^2 f(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq L_0+L_1\|\nabla f(x)\|$ everywhere, with $L_0>0$ and $L_1\geq0$. A given point $x^0\in\mathbb{R}^d$ lies within distance $R>0$ of a global minimizer $x^\star$. The oracle returns $\nabla f(x)$ at $x$. For a tolerance $\varepsilon>0$, the goal is an output $\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^d$ with integrable objective gap and $\mathbb{E}_{P}[f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)]\leq\varepsilon$, where $P$ is the law of the algorithm's randomness.

The optimization problem is
\[
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^d} f(x).
\]
We use the Euclidean norm and its induced operator norm. The parameters obey
\[
\begin{aligned}
&L_0>0,\qquad L_1\geq0,\qquad R>0,\\
&0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{L_0R^2}{16},\\
&d\geq\left\lceil
17592186044416\left(1+L_1R+\sqrt{\frac{L_0R^2}{\varepsilon}}\right)^5
\right\rceil.
\end{aligned}
\]
The algorithm receives $d$, $L_0$, $L_1$, $x^0$, $R$, and $\varepsilon$.

Our assumptions:
\begin{assumption}[Convexity]
For every $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^d$ and $t\in[0,1]$,
\[
f((1-t)x+ty)\leq(1-t)f(x)+tf(y).
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Generalized smoothness]
The function $f$ is differentiable everywhere, its gradient is Fr\'echet differentiable everywhere, and
\[
\|\nabla^2 f(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq L_0+L_1\|\nabla f(x)\|,
\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^d.
\]
Here $\nabla^2 f(x):\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^d$ is the derivative of the gradient.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Initial distance]
There exists $x^\star\in\mathbb{R}^d$ such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x^\star)&\leq f(x) &&\text{for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^d,\\
\|x^0-x^\star\|&\leq R.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

Queries may be made at any point of $\mathbb{R}^d$. After the allotted oracle calls, the output must have an integrable objective gap under $P$ and satisfy
\[
\mathbb{E}_{P}\bigl[f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)\bigr]\leq\varepsilon.
\]
