\section*{Strongly Convex Composite Finite Sums}
We minimize $F(x)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n f_i(x)+r(x)$ on a real Hilbert space $E$, where $n\geq1$, the functions $f_i:E\to\mathbb{R}$ are convex with $L$-Lipschitz gradients, and $r:E\to\mathbb{R}$ is $\mu$-strongly convex, with $L,\mu>0$. A given point $x^0\in E$ satisfies $F(x^0)-F(x^\star)\leq\Delta$ for a global minimizer $x^\star$ and $\Delta\geq0$. The oracle returns component gradients and exact proximal minimizers of $r$. For $\varepsilon>0$, the goal is a measurable output $\bar{x}\in E$ with integrable objective gap and $\mathbb{E}_{P}[F(\bar{x})-F(x^\star)]\leq\varepsilon$, where $P$ is the law of the algorithm's randomness.

The optimization problem is
\[
\min_{x\in E} F(x).
\]
The norm $\|\cdot\|$ is induced by the inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ on $E$, which is equipped with a measurable structure. The algorithm receives $E$, $n$, $L$, $\mu$, $\Delta$, $\varepsilon$, and $x^0$; the component functions and regularizer are accessed through the oracle.

Our assumptions:
\begin{assumption}[Convex smooth components]
Each $f_i$ is Fr\'echet differentiable on $E$. For every $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$, $x,y\in E$, and $t\in[0,1]$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_i((1-t)x+ty)&\leq(1-t)f_i(x)+t f_i(y),\\
\|\nabla f_i(x)-\nabla f_i(y)\|&\leq L\|x-y\|.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Strongly convex regularizer and proximal access]
For every $x,y\in E$ and $t\in[0,1]$,
\[
r((1-t)x+ty)
\leq (1-t)r(x)+t r(y)
-\frac{\mu}{2}t(1-t)\|x-y\|^2.
\]
There is a map $p:\mathbb{R}\times E\times E\to E$ such that, for every $a>0$ and $z,g\in E$,
\[
p(a,z,g)\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{v\in E}
\left\{\frac{\|v-z\|^2}{2a}+\langle g,v-z\rangle+r(v)\right\}.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Initial objective gap]
There exists $x^\star\in E$ such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(x^\star)&\leq F(x) &&\text{for every }x\in E,\\
F(x^0)-F(x^\star)&\leq\Delta.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

A component-gradient query specifies $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and $x\in E$; the oracle returns $\nabla f_i(x)$ at $x$. A proximal query specifies $a\in\mathbb{R}$ and $z,g\in E$; the oracle returns $p(a,z,g)$. Its minimization property is required only for $a>0$. Each query of either kind counts as one call toward a shared oracle-call budget.

After the allotted calls, the output $\bar{x}$ must be measurable, its objective gap must be integrable under $P$, and it must satisfy
\[
\mathbb{E}_{P}\bigl[F(\bar{x})-F(x^\star)\bigr]\leq\varepsilon.
\]
