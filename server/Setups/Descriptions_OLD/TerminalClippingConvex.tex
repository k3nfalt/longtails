\section*{Convex Optimization with Growing Oracle Moments}
We consider minimizing a measurable function $f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}$, convex on a closed convex set $C$, with the Euclidean norm $\|\cdot\|$. The initial point $x^0\in C$ satisfies $\|x^0-x^\star\|\leq R$ for a minimizer $x^\star\in C$ and $R\geq0$. Each oracle call returns either an exact projection onto $C$ or an independently sampled, possibly heavy-tailed vector whose mean is a subgradient on $C$ and whose second moment at $x\in C$ is at most $B^2\|x-x^0\|^2+G^2$, where $B,G>0$. Given $\varepsilon>0$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$, success requires a measurable feasible output $\bar{x}$ with $\mathbb{P}(f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)>\varepsilon)\leq\delta$, where $\mathbb{P}$ includes oracle and algorithm randomness.

The optimization problem is
\[
\min_{x\in C} f(x).
\]
The dimension $d\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$ and the tuple $(x^0,B,G,\delta,R,\varepsilon)$ are public inputs. Inner products are Euclidean. The objective and feasible set are accessed through the oracle described below.

\begin{assumption}[Convexity and attainment]
The set $C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$ is closed and convex, $x^0\in C$, and $f$ is Borel measurable. For every $x,y\in C$ and $t\in[0,1]$,
\[
f((1-t)x+ty)\leq(1-t)f(x)+tf(y).
\]
There exists $x^\star\in C$ satisfying
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x^\star)&\leq f(x) &&\text{for every }x\in C,\\
\|x^0-x^\star\|&\leq R.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Projection access]
A projection query at $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$ returns $\Pi_C(x)$ deterministically. The map $\Pi_C:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^d$ is Borel measurable and satisfies, for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$ and $u\in C$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pi_C(x)&\in C,\\
\langle x-\Pi_C(x),u-\Pi_C(x)\rangle&\leq0.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Independent stochastic access]
There is a measurable sample space $\Omega$, a probability measure $\nu$ on $\Omega$, and a jointly measurable map
\[
g:\mathbb{R}^d\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^d.
\]
Under a probability law $P$ on $\Omega^{\mathbb{N}}$, the coordinate samples $(\xi_k)_{k\geq0}$ are measurable, mutually independent, and each has law $\nu$. At call index $k$, a stochastic subgradient query at $x$ returns $g(x,\xi_k)$. Internal algorithm randomness is independent of the entire sample sequence. The sample space, sampling laws, and map $g$ are not public inputs.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Mean subgradients and moment growth]
For every $x\in C$, the map $g(x,\cdot)$ is Bochner integrable under $\nu$, and $\|g(x,\cdot)\|^2$ is integrable. Its mean
\[
m(x):=\int_{\Omega}g(x,\omega)\,\nu(d\omega)
\]
satisfies, for every $x,y\in C$,
\[
f(x)-f(y)\leq\langle m(x),x-y\rangle.
\]
The second moment obeys, for every $x\in C$,
\[
\int_{\Omega}\|g(x,\omega)\|^2\,\nu(d\omega)
\leq B^2\|x-x^0\|^2+G^2.
\]
\end{assumption}

Queries may be adaptive and may use either oracle operation at any point in $\mathbb{R}^d$; the subgradient and moment guarantees apply on $C$. Each operation costs one oracle call, including every projection query. The algorithm is selected using only the public inputs and is run for an externally specified budget $K\in\mathbb{N}$, counting both kinds of calls.

The returned point $\bar{x}$ must be measurable and belong to $C$ for every realization of the randomness. Under the joint law $\mathbb{P}$, success requires
\[
\mathbb{P}\bigl(f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)>\varepsilon\bigr)
\leq\delta.
\]
Here $x^\star$ may be the minimizer witnessing the initial distance bound, since all minimizers over $C$ have the same objective value.
