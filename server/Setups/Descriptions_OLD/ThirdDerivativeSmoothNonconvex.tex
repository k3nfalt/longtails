\section*{Third-Derivative Smooth Nonconvex Optimization}
We consider minimizing a possibly nonconvex function $f:E\to\mathbb{R}$ on a finite-dimensional real inner product space $E$. The function is three times continuously differentiable, with $L_3$-Lipschitz third derivative in operator norm, where $L_3>0$. At a given point $x^0\in E$, the global objective gap is at most $\Delta>0$, and the Hessian norm is at most $H_0\geq0$. Two exact oracles return, respectively, the function value and gradient, or the Hessian. Given $\varepsilon>0$, the goal is an output $\bar{x}\in E$ satisfying $\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|\leq\varepsilon$ almost surely, where $\nabla f$ denotes the gradient.

The optimization problem is
\[
\inf_{x\in E} f(x),
\]
where the norm on $E$ is induced by its inner product. All parameters $(E,x^0,L_3,\Delta,H_0,\varepsilon)$ are public inputs. Write $D$ for the Fr\'echet derivative and define
\[
H(x)=D(\nabla f)(x),\qquad T(x)=D\bigl(D(\nabla f)\bigr)(x).
\]
Thus $H(x)$ is the Hessian as a linear operator on $E$, and $T(x)$ represents the third derivative through the inner product. Norms of these derivatives are the corresponding operator norms. The objective and oracle access satisfy the following conditions.

\begin{assumption}[Third-order regularity]
The function $f$ belongs to $C^3(E;\mathbb{R})$, and its third derivative satisfies
\[
\|T(x)-T(y)\|\leq L_3\|x-y\|
\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in E.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Initial gap and curvature]
The initial point satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x^0)-f(x)&\leq\Delta &&\text{for every }x\in E,\\
\|H(x^0)\|&\leq H_0.
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular, $f(x^0)-\Delta$ is a global lower bound on the objective.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Exact first- and second-order access]
At every call index and query point $x\in E$, the two oracles return
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{oracle }0 &: \quad (f(x),\nabla f(x)),\\
\text{oracle }1 &: \quad H(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
These replies are exact for every realization of oracle randomness. A query to oracle $0$ costs one call to oracle $0$ and supplies both the value and gradient; a query to oracle $1$ costs one separately counted call to oracle $1$ and supplies the full Hessian operator. Any internal algorithm randomness is independent of oracle randomness.
\end{assumption}

The objective is accessed through these oracles, with adaptive queries permitted anywhere in $E$. The algorithm may use the entire public parameter tuple. Its call budgets $K_0,K_1\in\mathbb{N}$ may depend on that tuple and bound the respective oracle counts, with total cost at most $K_0+K_1$.

The required output is a point $\bar{x}\in E$. Writing $\mathbb{P}$ for the joint law of algorithm and oracle randomness, the accuracy criterion is
\[
\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|\leq\varepsilon
\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-almost surely}.
\]
