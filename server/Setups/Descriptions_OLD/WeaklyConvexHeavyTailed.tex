\section*{Weakly Convex Heavy-Tailed Optimization}
We consider minimizing $f:E\to\mathbb{R}$ over a nonempty closed convex set $D$ in a finite-dimensional real inner product space $E$. The objective admits a bounded subgradient selection with weak-convexity parameter $\rho>0$, and the given initial point $x^0\in D$ has bounded gap to a lower bound. Each oracle call returns either an unbiased stochastic subgradient with an $\alpha$-moment noise bound, where $1<\alpha\leq2$, or the exact projection onto $D$. For $\varepsilon>0$, success requires a measurable feasible output $\bar{x}$ with $\mathbb{E}[(2\rho)^2\|\bar{x}-p(\bar{x})\|^2]\leq\varepsilon^2$ for every map $p(x)\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{y\in D}\{f(y)+\rho\|y-x\|^2\}$; such a map must exist. Expectation includes all algorithm and oracle randomness.

The constrained optimization problem is
\[
\inf_{x\in D} f(x),
\]
where $\|\cdot\|$ is induced by the inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ on $E$. Equip $E$ with its Borel sigma-algebra, and let $\Omega$ be a measurable sample space. The public inputs are
\[
(E,\Omega,\rho,G,\sigma,\alpha,\Delta,\varepsilon,x^0),
\]
with $G>0$, $\sigma\geq0$, and $\Delta\geq0$. The objective and feasible set are accessed through the oracle described below. The admissible instances satisfy the following conditions.

\begin{assumption}[Feasibility and weak subgradients]
The set $D\subseteq E$ is closed and convex, and $x^0\in D$. There is a selection $v:E\to E$ such that, for every $x,y\in D$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x)+\langle v(x),y-x\rangle
    -\frac{\rho}{2}\|y-x\|^2 &\leq f(y),\\
\|v(x)\| &\leq G.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Lower bound and initial gap]
There exists $\ell\in\mathbb{R}$ such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\ell &\leq f(x) \qquad \text{for every }x\in D,\\
f(x^0)-\ell &\leq\Delta.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Exact projection access]
There is a measurable map $\Pi:E\to E$ satisfying, for every $x\in E$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pi(x)&\in D,\\
\langle x-\Pi(x),u-\Pi(x)\rangle&\leq0
    \qquad \text{for every }u\in D.
\end{aligned}
\]
A projection query at $x$ returns $\Pi(x)$ deterministically and costs one oracle call.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Independent heavy-tailed subgradient access]
There are a probability measure $\nu$ on $\Omega$ and a jointly measurable map $g:E\times\Omega\to E$. For every $x\in D$, the map $g(x,\cdot)$ is Bochner integrable and the scalar map $\|g(x,\cdot)-v(x)\|^\alpha$ is integrable with respect to $\nu$, with
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\Omega} g(x,z)\,\nu(dz)&=v(x),\\
\int_{\Omega}\|g(x,z)-v(x)\|^\alpha\,\nu(dz)
    &\leq\sigma^\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]
The oracle samples form a sequence $(\xi_k)_{k\geq0}$ of measurable, mutually independent random variables, each with law $\nu$, under a probability law on $\Omega^{\mathbb{N}}$. A subgradient query at $x\in E$ on call $k$ returns $g(x,\xi_k)$ and costs one oracle call. The stated integrability and moment conditions apply to query points in $D$. Any internal algorithm randomness is independent of the entire oracle sample sequence.
\end{assumption}

Queries may be chosen adaptively anywhere in $E$, with either query type consuming one call. The algorithm is selected using the public inputs, and an externally specified budget $K\in\mathbb{N}$ may depend on their full tuple. All projection and subgradient queries count toward this budget.

After at most $K$ calls, the algorithm returns a measurable random point $\bar{x}$ that belongs to $D$ for every realization. To specify its required accuracy, define the class of proximal selections
\[
\mathcal{P}_{\rho}
=\left\{p:E\to E:
\begin{aligned}
&p(x)\in D \quad \text{for every }x\in E,\\
&f(p(x))+\rho\|p(x)-x\|^2\\
&\qquad\leq f(y)+\rho\|y-x\|^2
\quad\text{for every }x\in E,\ y\in D
\end{aligned}
\right\}.
\]
Success requires $\mathcal{P}_{\rho}\neq\varnothing$. Writing $\mathbb{P}$ for the joint probability law of the algorithm and oracle randomness, it further requires, for every $p\in\mathcal{P}_{\rho}$, integrability of the scalar random variable $(2\rho)^2\|\bar{x}-p(\bar{x})\|^2$ and the accuracy bound
\[
\int (2\rho)^2
    \|\bar{x}(\omega)-p(\bar{x}(\omega))\|^2
    \,\mathbb{P}(d\omega)
\leq\varepsilon^2.
\]
