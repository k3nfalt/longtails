\section*{Convex Optimization with Heavy-Tailed Markov Gradients}
We consider minimizing a differentiable convex function $f:E\to\mathbb{R}$ on a separable real Hilbert space $E$, with Lipschitz continuous gradient and a global minimizer $x^\star$ within a prescribed distance of an initial point $x^0\in E$. Each oracle call returns a stochastic gradient driven by a stationary Markov chain with averaged polynomial total-variation mixing. The oracle is unbiased under the stationary law, with a uniformly bounded centered moment of prescribed order in $(1,2]$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, the goal is a measurable output $\bar{x}\in E$ with integrable objective gap satisfying $\mathbb{E}[f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)]\leq\varepsilon$, where expectation includes oracle and algorithm randomness.

The optimization problem is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:problem}
\min_{x\in E} f(x),
\end{equation}
where the norm on $E$ is induced by its inner product. The public inputs are $E$, a measurable sample space $\Omega$, the initial point $x^0$, and the parameters
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L&>0, & R&\geq 0, & \sigma&\geq 0,\\
1<\alpha&\leq 2, & \tau&>0, & \beta&>0, & \varepsilon&>0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The admissible objectives and oracle access satisfy the following conditions.

\begin{assumption}[Convexity and smoothness]
The function $f$ is differentiable everywhere on $E$, and, for every $x,y\in E$ and $s\in[0,1]$,
\begin{align}
f((1-s)x+sy)&\leq (1-s)f(x)+sf(y),\\
\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|&\leq L\|x-y\|.
\end{align}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Attainment and initial distance]
There exists $x^\star\in E$ such that
\begin{align}
f(x^\star)&\leq f(x) \qquad \text{for every }x\in E,\\
\|x^0-x^\star\|&\leq R.
\end{align}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Stationary Markov sampling and mixing]
Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the sigma-algebra on $\Omega$. There exist a Markov transition kernel $\kappa$ on $\Omega$ and a probability measure $\pi$ such that the oracle samples form a Markov chain $(Z_k)_{k\geq 0}$ with initial law $\pi$ and transition kernel $\kappa$. The measure $\pi$ is invariant:
\begin{equation}
\int_\Omega \kappa(z,A)\,\pi(dz)=\pi(A)
\qquad (A\in\mathcal{F}).
\end{equation}
For each integer $t\geq 0$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
d_t(z)&=\sup_{A\in\mathcal{F}}
\bigl|\kappa^t(z,A)-\pi(A)\bigr|,\\
\kappa^0(z,\cdot)&=\delta_z.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus total variation uses the supremum over measurable sets convention. Each $d_t$ is integrable under $\pi$, and
\begin{equation}
\int_\Omega d_t(z)\,\pi(dz)
\leq \left(1+\frac{t}{\tau}\right)^{-\beta}
\qquad (t\geq 0).
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Stochastic gradients and heavy-tailed noise]
There exists a jointly measurable map $g:E\times\Omega\to E$. At call $k$, a query $x\in E$ returns the vector $g(x,Z_k)$. For every fixed $x\in E$, the map $g(x,\cdot)$ is Bochner integrable under $\pi$, the function $\|g(x,\cdot)-\nabla f(x)\|^\alpha$ is integrable under $\pi$, and
\begin{align}
\int_\Omega g(x,z)\,\pi(dz)&=\nabla f(x),\\
\int_\Omega \|g(x,z)-\nabla f(x)\|^\alpha\,\pi(dz)
&\leq \sigma^\alpha.
\end{align}
These identities concern the stationary law at fixed query points; they do not assert conditional unbiasedness at adaptive queries.
\end{assumption}

The objective is accessed through this oracle, with queries chosen adaptively anywhere in $E$. Each returned vector costs one oracle call and uses the next chain sample; discarded replies also count toward the budget. Any internal algorithm randomness is independent of the entire oracle chain. The algorithm is selected using only the public inputs specified above; $f$, $g$, $\kappa$, and $\pi$ are not supplied as additional inputs. A call budget $K\in\mathbb{N}$ may depend on the full public parameter tuple.

After $K$ calls, the algorithm returns a measurable point $\bar{x}\in E$. Writing $\mathbb{P}$ for the joint law of algorithm and oracle randomness, success requires that $\omega\mapsto f(\bar{x}(\omega))-f(x^\star)$ be integrable under $\mathbb{P}$ and that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:accuracy}
\int \bigl(f(\bar{x}(\omega))-f(x^\star)\bigr)\,\mathbb{P}(d\omega)
\leq \varepsilon.
\end{equation}
All global minimizers have the same objective value, so the minimizer witnessing the initial distance bound may be used in \eqref{eq:accuracy}.
