\section*{PL Optimization with Fourth-Moment Noise}
We consider minimizing a possibly nonconvex function $f:E\to\mathbb{R}$ on a finite-dimensional real inner product space $E$. Its gradient and Hessian are globally Lipschitz, and its objective gap satisfies a Polyak--{\L}ojasiewicz inequality. A global minimizer $x^\star$ exists, and the gap at a given initial point $x^0\in E$ is bounded. Each oracle call returns an unbiased stochastic gradient with uniformly bounded second and fourth noise moments, using independent, identically distributed samples. The permitted method is constant-step stochastic gradient descent with a randomized stopping window. Its measurable output $\bar{x}\in E$ must satisfy $\mathbb{E}[f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)]\leq\varepsilon$, where $\varepsilon>0$ and expectation includes all randomness.

The optimization problem is
\[
\min_{x\in E} f(x),
\]
where $E$ carries its inner-product norm and Borel sigma-algebra. The public inputs are $E$, a measurable sample space $\Omega$, the initial point $x^0$, and the real parameters
\[
0<\mu\leq L,\qquad \rho\geq0,\qquad \sigma\geq0,\qquad
K\geq1,\qquad \Delta\geq0,\qquad \varepsilon>0.
\]
Here $K$ controls the fourth noise moment. The objective and oracle distribution are specified by the following conditions.

\begin{assumption}[Gradient and Hessian regularity]
The function $f$ is differentiable everywhere, and its gradient $\nabla f:E\to E$ is differentiable everywhere. Writing $H(x)=D(\nabla f)(x)$, for every $x,y\in E$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\| &\leq L\|x-y\|,\\
\|H(x)-H(y)\|_{\mathrm{op}} &\leq \rho\|x-y\|,
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ denotes the induced operator norm.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Attainment, PL inequality, and initialization]
There exists $x^\star\in E$ such that, for every $x\in E$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(x^\star) &\leq f(x),\\
2\mu\bigl(f(x)-f(x^\star)\bigr) &\leq \|\nabla f(x)\|^2,
\end{aligned}
\]
and the initial objective gap obeys
\[
f(x^0)-f(x^\star)\leq\Delta.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Sampling and stochastic-gradient access]
There are probability measures $P_0$ on $\Omega$ and $P$ on $\Omega^{\mathbb{N}}$, and a jointly measurable map $g:E\times\Omega\to E$. Under $P$, the coordinate samples $\xi_k$, $k\in\mathbb{N}$, are measurable, mutually independent, and have common law $P_0$:
\[
P\circ\xi_k^{-1}=P_0\qquad\text{for every }k\in\mathbb{N}.
\]
At call index $k$, the sole oracle, with ID $0$, returns $g(x,\xi_k)$ at a query $x\in E$. Each reply costs one oracle call. Queries may be adaptive and may lie anywhere in $E$. Internal algorithm randomness is independent of the entire sample sequence.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Unbiasedness and noise moments]
For each $x\in E$, the map $g(x,\cdot)$ is Bochner integrable under $P_0$, and
\[
\int_{\Omega} g(x,\omega)\,dP_0(\omega)=\nabla f(x).
\]
Define $e(x,\omega)=g(x,\omega)-\nabla f(x)$. For every $x\in E$, both $\|e(x,\cdot)\|^2$ and $\|e(x,\cdot)\|^4$ are integrable under $P_0$, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\Omega}\|e(x,\omega)\|^2\,dP_0(\omega)&\leq\sigma^2,\\
\int_{\Omega}\|e(x,\omega)\|^4\,dP_0(\omega)&\leq K\sigma^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{assumption}

The algorithm must belong to the following family. Using only the public inputs, it selects a step size $\eta>0$ and integers $T_a,T_b\geq0$, and draws $\tau$ uniformly from $\{0,\ldots,T_b\}$ independently of the oracle samples. For a call budget $N\in\mathbb{N}$, define the stochastic-gradient iterates by
\[
x^{k+1}=x^k-\eta g(x^k,\xi_k),
\qquad 0\leq k<\min\{N,T_a+\tau\}.
\]
The returned point is
\[
\bar{x}=x^{\min\{N,T_a+\tau\}}.
\]
After reaching index $T_a+\tau$, the method keeps its iterate fixed while making any remaining budgeted calls at that point and discarding their replies. Thus all $N$ calls are counted, including calls after the iterate stops changing. When $N\geq T_a+T_b$, the output is the iterate at a uniformly selected index in $\{T_a,\ldots,T_a+T_b\}$. The budget may depend on the full public parameter tuple.

Let $\mathbb{P}$ denote the joint law of the independent algorithm randomness and oracle samples. Success requires that $\bar{x}$ be measurable, that $f(\bar{x})-f(x^\star)$ be integrable under $\mathbb{P}$, and that
\[
\int \bigl(f(\bar{x}(\omega))-f(x^\star)\bigr)\,d\mathbb{P}(\omega)
\leq\varepsilon.
\]
The minimizer witnessing the initial-gap condition can be used here because every global minimizer has the same objective value.
