# Positive examples for contribution assessment

These are successful research directions selected by the user. The [comparison tables](tables/results_table.tex) provide the associated methods, baselines, review links, and qualifications. These examples illustrate improvements in complexity, assumptions, oracle access, adaptivity, or an existing method's scope; they do not all claim a uniformly better rate or the same degree of technical novelty. Recheck primary sources for new or changed claims rather than treating a review verdict or star rating as decisive.

- **GeneralizedSmoothAndConvex** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/GeneralizedSmoothAndConvex.lean)): accelerated convex optimization under `(L₀,L₁)`-smoothness improves prior accelerated complexities in explicit parameter regimes. Identify the terms and regimes that improve the total bound rather than claiming uniform dominance.
- **GeneralizedSmoothParameterFree** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/GeneralizedSmoothParameterFree.lean)): accelerated, gradient-only updates require neither smoothness parameters nor initial-distance and objective-gap bounds. Distinguish parameter-free updates from a parameter-dependent stopping budget.
- **CompositeGeneralizedSmooth** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/CompositeGeneralizedSmooth.lean)): accelerated complexity for constrained composite objectives `f + ψ`, with generalized-smooth convex `f` and exact proximal evaluations of convex `ψ`. Extending an accelerated guarantee to this composite setting is the contribution; a faster rate than AC-FGM is not established by merely substituting local smoothness constants.
- **StochasticGeneralizedSmoothAndConvex** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/StochasticGeneralizedSmoothAndConvex.lean)): accelerated high-probability guarantees under unbiased light-tailed noise improve the dependence on `L₁R` in the specified regimes. Keep the stochastic term when assessing total complexity.
- **FiniteVarianceGeneralizedSmoothAndConvex** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/FiniteVarianceGeneralizedSmoothAndConvex.lean)): under unbiased bounded-variance noise, the accelerated high-probability bound is uniformly no worse than the compared RCSAG bound on the common setup up to logarithms, replacing `L₁R` by `(L₁R)^(2/3)`. The adaptive construction also reduces confidence-related logarithmic overhead in specified regimes. Dominating unchanged terms can mask a strict total gain.
- **RelativeErrorSmoothConvex** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/RelativeErrorSmoothConvex.lean)): for `T = LR²/ε > 1` and `0 ≤ α ≤ 1/3`, relative-error acceleration improves the regularized accelerated bound `O((√T + αT)(1 + log T))` to `O(√T + α²T)`. The last-iterate guarantee extends to every `α < 1`, retaining the factors involving `1/(1−α)` outside the restricted comparison.
- **InexactGradientStronglyConvex** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/InexactGradientStronglyConvex.lean)): inexact gradients with error at most `α‖∇f‖ + δ` permit improved relative-error dependence and a lower guaranteed accuracy threshold from absolute error. At `α = 0`, the compared threshold improves from order `√κ δ²/μ` to `δ²/μ`. This is an improvement in certified guarantees, not a lower bound on what the prior method can achieve; errors need not be unbiased or independent.
- **ContinuizedSimilarQuasar** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/ContinuizedSimilarQuasar.lean)): the continuized and movement-based constructions improve component-gradient complexity for smooth strongly quasar-convex finite sums in high-similarity regimes, with deterministic oracle budgets and expected last-iterate objective accuracy. Count full-gradient refreshes; neither construction uniformly dominates the other.
- **GapSmoothStrongGrowth** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/GapSmoothStrongGrowth.lean)): high-probability acceleration under gap-dependent smoothness and second-moment strong growth improves the stochastic accuracy term from `ε⁻¹` to `ε⁻¹ᐟ²`, up to logarithms, relative to the RCSAG comparison. The total gain matters when that term dominates; the globally smooth special case already has accelerated methods.
- **GeneralizedLipschitzMonotone** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/GeneralizedLipschitzMonotone.lean)): for exact monotone root finding under generalized Lipschitz continuity, the leading residual-complexity dependence improves from `ε⁻²` in the compared extragradient bounds to `ε⁻¹`, with other parameters fixed. Compare the same residual criterion `‖F(x)‖ ≤ ε`.
- **StochasticGeneralizedMonotone** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/StochasticGeneralizedMonotone.lean)): finite-variance unbiased stochastic oracles admit high-probability residual accuracy under generalized Lipschitz continuity without quasi-sharpness. Up to logarithms the bound is `O(1 + L₀R/ε + (L₁R)² + σ²/ε²)`. It extends the globally Lipschitz scope while matching its polynomial accuracy dependence, rather than improving every term at `L₁ = 0`.
- **HolderMonotone** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/HolderMonotone.lean)): mixed Lipschitz-plus-Hölder monotone equations admit a stopped-residual bound of order `ε^(−1/α)` up to logarithms, with no `L,H,α,R` in the updates. This improves the compared parameter-free pure Hölder exponent `2/α`; the Lipschitz special case loses a logarithm to known-parameter anchored extragradient. The review's proposed standard reduction concerns technical novelty, not whether the displayed complexity improves.
- **StochasticHessianNcsc** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/StochasticHessianNcsc.lean)): gradient-only nonconvex–strongly-concave minimax optimization with a Lipschitz Hessian and finite-variance noise achieves expected stationarity with leading accuracy dependence `ε^(−7/2)` instead of the compared `ε⁻⁴`, up to logarithms, for fixed positive remaining parameters and sufficiently small `ε`. Credit this regime-specific gain while retaining the extra Hessian regularity and worse condition-number dependence.
- **TerminalClippingConvex** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/TerminalClippingConvex.lean)): high-probability last-iterate accuracy under distance-growing second moments needs neither smoothness nor uniformly bounded subgradients. Relative to the expected projected-Halpern bound converted by Markov's inequality, confidence dependence becomes logarithmic instead of polynomial, and the accuracy logarithm disappears at fixed confidence. The comparison is with that conversion, not with all possible high-probability analyses.
- **ZerothOrderSmoothAndConvex** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/ZerothOrderSmoothAndConvex.lean)): the accelerated two-point method handles independent, unbiased, finite-variance value noise. Against the retuned Bach–Perchet SGD analysis, for `T = LR²/ε ≥ 1` and `σ ≤ ε/(d√T)`, complexity improves from `O(dT)` to `O(d√T)`. Merely requiring `σ < ε/d` does not ensure an asymptotic gain; for `σ ≥ ε/d` the compared bounds have the same order. This example concerns the two-point theorem, not the coordinate-based sibling, whose rate matches a standard reduction.
- **ZerothOrderMatrixGeneralizedSmooth** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/ZerothOrderMatrixGeneralizedSmooth.lean)): exact function values suffice for accelerated high-probability optimization under matrix generalized smoothness, without knowing the matrix or paying for its inverse smallest eigenvalue. A supplied trace bound `tr H ≤ r` gives leading accuracy cost `√(d(r+1)L₀R²/ε)` up to logarithms. Compared with RS-NAG, the contribution extends to `L₁ > 0` and replaces directional derivatives with values; at `L₁ = 0` it matches the leading rate with extra terms.
- **DouglasRachfordSmoothConvex** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/DouglasRachfordSmoothConvex.lean)): fast Douglas–Rachford attains accelerated last-iterate objective accuracy beyond quadratic smooth terms, using two fixed-parameter proximal oracles. Its `O(1 + R/√(λγε))` complexity requires `4γL ≤ λ`. This is a meaningful extension of the method's guarantee with a smaller step-size range; a uniform problem-class improvement is not established. The review's same-oracle reduction has an unchecked step, so do not use it as a proved dismissal.
- **AdaptiveHeavyTailSimplex** ([setup](../LongTails/Setups/AdaptiveHeavyTailSimplex.lean)): accelerated high-probability optimization on the simplex allows heavy-tailed noise and arbitrary constrained minimizers, with updates independent of the noise moment order and scale. The stopping budget still depends on them. Credit adaptivity and scope without claiming an improved accuracy exponent.

A successful attempt can improve a known method's guarantee or establish a useful extension without inventing a new algorithm. Explain the concrete benefit and its limits. A gain confined to a meaningful regime, an unchanged accuracy exponent, or familiar proof ingredients alone does not negate a contribution; conversely, a new analysis alone does not establish a complexity improvement. Demonstrate any claimed standard reduction under the same assumptions, oracle access, and output criterion.
