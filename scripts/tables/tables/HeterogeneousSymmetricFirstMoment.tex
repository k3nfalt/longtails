\parbox{\linewidth}{\footnotesize
On $\mathbb R^{b+h}$, $b+h>0$, $f$ is differentiable with $L$-Lipschitz gradient and $f(x_0)-\inf f\le\Delta$. Samples are IID; the jointly measurable, integrable gradient oracle is unbiased. Writing $\zeta=g-\nabla f$, body coordinates have symmetric noise with $\mathbb E\|\zeta_b(x)\|_1\le\sigma_b$; the head satisfies $\mathbb E\|\zeta_h(x)\|^2\le\sigma_h^2$. Almost surely, $|\zeta_i(x)-\zeta_i(y)|\le K\|x-y\|$ for every body coordinate and all $x,y$, with measurable $K\ge0$ and $\mathbb E K\le M$. Here $L,\Delta,\varepsilon,\delta>0$ and $M,\sigma_b,\sigma_h\ge0$. The setup restricts the algorithm to bias-corrected Adam on the body ($\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$, stabilizer $\delta$) and coordinatewise AdaGrad on the head. Target: $\int\|\nabla f(x_J)\|\,d\mathbb P\le\varepsilon$, with $J$ uniform and independent of the sample sequence; $\mathbb P$ is their joint law.}\par\smallskip
