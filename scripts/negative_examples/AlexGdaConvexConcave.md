# Alex-GDA: novelty assessment

The [theorem](../../LongTails/Theorems/AlexGdaConvexConcave.lean), under its [setup](../../LongTails/Setups/BlockSmoothConvexConcave.lean), guarantees that [constant-step Alex-GDA](../../LongTails/Algorithms/AlexGda.lean) returns a best-seen point with \(\|\nabla_x f\|^2+\|\nabla_y f\|^2\le\varepsilon^2\), for every admissible objective and exact block oracle, in arbitrary real Hilbert spaces (indeed, for every seed).

Here \(f\) is globally differentiable convex–concave; \(L_x,L_y\ge0\) bound within-block gradient Lipschitzness, \(L_{xy}\ge0\) bounds both cross-block variations, and a saddle lies within Euclidean direct-sum distance \(R\) of initialization. Require \(L_x+L_{xy},L_y+L_{xy}>0\), \(1<\gamma\le2\), \(\delta\ge1\), \(R\ge0\), \(\varepsilon>0\).

The exact budget is
\[
2\left\lceil\frac{180\gamma^4\delta^4(\max\{L_x,L_y\}+L_{xy})^2R^2}{(\gamma-1)^3\varepsilon^2}\right\rceil,
\]
hence \(O(1+\gamma^4\delta^4(\max\{L_x,L_y\}+L_{xy})^2R^2/((\gamma-1)^3\varepsilon^2))\) partial-gradient calls. Steps are \(\alpha=(\gamma-1)/(4\gamma^2\delta^2(L_x+L_{xy}))\), \(\beta=(\gamma-1)/(4\gamma^2\delta^2(L_y+L_{xy}))\). Updates know the Lipschitz bounds and extrapolation parameters; stopping also needs \(R,\varepsilon\). Two calls compute \(g_k=\nabla_xf(x_k,\tilde y_k)\), \(h_k=\nabla_yf(x_k-\gamma\alpha g_k,y_k)\); the output selects k<K minimizing the recorded score \(\|g_k\|^2+\|h_k\|^2+\|h_{k-1}\|^2\), with zero initial memory. There are no function evaluations or selection calls. \(R=0\) costs zero; \(\delta=1\) is covered, \(\gamma=1\) excluded, and the bound deteriorates as \(\gamma\downarrow1\) or \(\delta\uparrow\infty\). \(L_{xy}=0\) reduces to independent gradient methods.

All table bounds use deterministic gradient-norm accuracy and partial-gradient calls on the finite-dimensional overlap. The saddle operator is monotone and Lipschitz with constant at most \(\max\{L_x,L_y\}+L_{xy}\), by the block inequalities.

Reduction check: for R>0 on the twice-differentiable finite-dimensional overlap, regularizing with \(\mu=\varepsilon/(4R)\) and applying [Lee–Cho–Yun, ICML 2024, Thm 5.1/Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10475v2) at \(\gamma=\delta=2\), equal steps \(1/[8(\max\{L_x,L_y\}+L_{xy}+\mu)]\), gives \(O((1+(\max\{L_x,L_y\}+L_{xy})R/\varepsilon)\log(2+(\max\{L_x,L_y\}+L_{xy})R/\varepsilon))\) calls: the regularized saddle is within \(R\), and distance error \(\varepsilon/[4(\max\{L_x,L_y\}+L_{xy}+\mu)]\) suffices. This changes the trajectory and needs \(R,\varepsilon\) in updates; setting strong-convexity moduli to zero supplies no residual rate for unmodified Alex-GDA.

Proof ancestry: Lyapunov telescoping and convex support inequalities; the [selected proof](../../LongTails/Proofs/AlexGdaStep.lean) adds a function-value term to its distance/cross/memory energy and proves gradient dissipation, unlike Lee's contraction certificate. [Clason–Mazurenko–Valkonen, AMO 2021, Thm 6.1, (15), §5](https://arxiv.org/html/1901.02746v4) requires extra three-point/derivative conditions and neighborhood control; it does not automatically instantiate from these hypotheses. [Yadav et al., ICLR 2018, Thm 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.07364v3) assumes bounded gradient second moments and bounded iterate distances; its averaged saddle-relative gap can vanish identically on bilinear games, so it supplies no comparable stationarity complexity.

Repository overlap: [AnchoredGeneralizedMonotone](../../LongTails/Theorems/AnchoredGeneralizedMonotone.lean), with \(L_0=\max\{L_x,L_y\}+L_{xy}\), \(L_1=0\), already gives \(O(1+(\max\{L_x,L_y\}+L_{xy})R/\varepsilon+\log(1+8(\max\{L_x,L_y\}+L_{xy})R/\varepsilon))\) block calls; [SmoothConvexConcave](../../LongTails/Theorems/SmoothConvexConcave.lean) instead concerns compact domains and averaged gap.

**Verdict: INCONCLUSIVE** — confidence: medium.

**New advantages**

- Against Lee–Cho–Yun Thm 5.1: strongly convex–concave, twice-differentiable Euclidean objectives → merely convex–concave Hilbert objectives with Lipschitz partial gradients; positive steps also cover \(\delta=1\), a nested objective-class extension, but \(\gamma\le2\) narrows extrapolation and the linear rate is lost.
- Against Yadav Thm 1 on its deterministic smooth overlap: assumed bounded gradient/iterate moments and diminishing steps → arbitrary initialization controlled only by \(R\), constant steps, and an explicit deterministic stationarity budget. Assumption sets are incomparable: full block smoothness is added, stochastic scope lost; gap and stationarity differ.
- Against regularized Alex-GDA: modified objective and accuracy-dependent updates → unmodified objective with \(R,\varepsilon\) needed only for the budget, at the cost of quadratic rather than near-linear accuracy dependence.

These verified baseline extensions could be substantive despite inferior problem-class complexity. Mathematical priority and originality of the dissipation mechanism remain unresolved because relevant competing full texts were unavailable; no “first” or optimality claim is established.
