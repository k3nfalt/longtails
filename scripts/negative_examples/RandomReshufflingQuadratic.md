# Random reshuffling for indefinite quadratics: novelty assessment

The [theorem](../../LongTails/Theorems/RandomReshufflingQuadratic.lean), through its [setup](../../LongTails/Setups/QuadraticFiniteSum.lean) and [algorithm](../../LongTails/Algorithms/QuadraticReshuffling.lean), proves $\mathbb E[F(y_K)-F(x_*)]\le\varepsilon$ for the final endpoint of independent uniform reshuffled passes, using exactly $nK$ exact component-gradient calls.

Here $F(x)=n^{-1}\sum_i(\langle x,A_ix\rangle/2+\langle b_i,x\rangle)$ on any real Hilbert space, $A_i$ are bounded self-adjoint operators, $\|A_i\|\le L$, and $n^{-1}\sum_iA_i\succeq\mu I$. For every admissible instance/oracle, a stationary $x_*$ must exist with $\|x_0-x_*\|\le R$ and $\max_i\|A_ix_*+b_i\|\le G$. Parameters satisfy $n\ge1$, $0<\mu\le L$, $R,G\ge0$, $\varepsilon>0$. Components may be indefinite and noncommuting; no interpolation or trajectory bound is assumed. Expectation concerns the final endpoint, not a confidence certificate; oracle randomness is immaterial.

The proved budget, including epoch rounding, is

$$O\!\left(n+\left[\frac{nL}{\mu}+\frac{L^{3/2}G}{\mu^2\sqrt\varepsilon}+\left(\frac{n^2L^3G^2}{\mu^4\varepsilon}\right)^{1/3}\right]\log\!\left(1+\frac{LR^2}{\varepsilon}\right)\right).$$

Updates use $\eta=[32nL+64L^{3/2}G/(\mu\sqrt\varepsilon)+4(n^2L^3G^2/(\mu\varepsilon))^{1/3}]^{-1}$; stopping uses $K=\lceil[64L/\mu+128L^{3/2}G/(n\mu^2\sqrt\varepsilon)+8(L^3G^2/(n\mu^4\varepsilon))^{1/3}]\log(1+LR^2/\varepsilon)\rceil$. Thus $n,L,\mu,G,\varepsilon$ enter updates and $R$ additionally enters stopping; neither $x_*$ nor $A_i,b_i$ is supplied. There are no initialization queries, searches, or restarts. Exactly zero calls occur when $R=0$; $G=0$ removes both heterogeneity terms. For $n=1$ the actual optimum gradient is zero, and for $\mu=L$ nontrivial-space admissibility forces $A_i=LI$: neither boundary supports an indefinite-component novelty claim.

All table bounds count component-gradient calls and certify the same expected objective gap. To avoid trivial initial accuracy, comparisons use $n\ge2$, $0<\varepsilon<LR^2/2$; $O$ hides only universal constants.

The prior bounds follow by translating $x_*$ to zero, converting squared distance with $L/2$, capping steps by stability and both noise tolerances, and geometrically suppressing initialization; averaging costs no queries.

Standard rescaling leaves $L/(n\mu)$ unchanged; convexifying components changes the reshuffling updates, while batching/replication changes the algorithm and does not remove the old transient at equal call cost. The extra mechanism is [collision-energy absorption](../../LongTails/Proofs/ReshufflingHomogeneousEnergy.lean): bound higher repeated-label Gram-product terms by $\sum_i\|A_iv\|^2$ and absorb them into its negative second-order term, allowing $\eta nL\le1/32$. Ahn supplies the expansion; [Peng, preprint, §4.2, Lemma 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.22620), supplies related endpoint-energy reasoning for a **mean** product at an $n^{-2}$ radius, not this Gram-product guarantee. They are proof ancestry, not additional complexity baselines.

**Verdict: SUBSTANTIVE NOVELTY SUPPORTED — confidence: medium.**

**New advantages**

- Against Ahn: the table's polynomial transient improvement for ordinary reshuffling, with identical assumptions/oracle, in its stated low-heterogeneity regime.
- Against Ahn: cubic heterogeneity cost loses $(\log(en))^{1/3}$.
- Against the examined product analyses: collision-energy control gives a conditioning-independent mean-square stability radius for indefinite components, rather than assuming convexity or analyzing only a mean product.

The substantive contribution is the uniform analysis of the actual ordinary-reshuffling implementation, not the existential public statement or Lean formalization. It establishes neither best unrestricted complexity nor optimality; stronger noise dependence and exhaustive priority remain unresolved.
