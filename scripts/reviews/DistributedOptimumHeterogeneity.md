# Novelty assessment: DistributedOptimumHeterogeneity

[`result`](../../LongTails/Theorems/DistributedOptimumHeterogeneity.lean) proves the following. Take $n$ workers with convex, $L_i$-smooth $f_i$ on $\mathbb R^d$, where the average $F$ has quadratic growth $\mu$ relative to $S=\arg\min F$. Assume there is some $z\in S$ with $\|x_0-z\|\le R$ and $\frac1n\sum_i\|\nabla f_i(z)\|^2\le\zeta^2$. Worker $i$ returns an unbiased gradient with variance at most $\sigma^2$ in exactly $h_i$ seconds, and the workers sit on a star with latency $\ell$ and per-bit time $b$. Under these conditions, a design-weighted batched SGD returns a fixed-index iterate with $\mathbb E\,\mathrm{dist}(x_{\rm out},S)^2\le\varepsilon^2$ by the deadline below ([setup](../../LongTails/Setups/DistributedOptimumHeterogeneity.lean), [algorithm](../../LongTails/Algorithms/OptimumHeterogeneitySgd.lean), [design](../../LongTails/Algorithms/OptimumHeterogeneityDesign.lean)).

**Elapsed time.** The weights $w$ come from a finite convex program, so there is no closed form:
$$w=\arg\min\Big\{\textstyle\sum_ih_iv_i^2:\ v\ge0,\ \min_{0\le a_i\le L_i,\ \sum a_i\ge n\mu}v^\top a\ge1,\ 4096\,n\zeta^2\sum_i(v_i-\bar v)^2\le\varepsilon^2\Big\}.$$
Up to universal constants, the Lean budget is
$$T=O\Big(\tfrac{\sigma^2\sum_ih_iw_i^2}{\varepsilon^2}+\big(\textstyle\sum_jh_j^{-1}\big)\max_i(h_iw_iL_i)\log\!\big(2+\tfrac{R^2}{\varepsilon^2}\big)\Big[\max_ih_i+\ell+b\Big(1+d\log\big(2+d+(\textstyle\sum_jh_j^{-1})\max_i(h_iw_iL_i)+\tfrac{(R+\max_i(h_iw_i)(\sigma+\sqrt n\zeta)/\max_i(h_iw_iL_i))^2}{\varepsilon^2}\big)\Big)\Big]\Big).$$
Uniform weights $v_i=1/(n\mu)$ are always feasible. Hence $\sum_ih_iw_i^2\le\sum_ih_i/(n\mu)^2$.

Prior bounds charge nothing for communication, so the table compares at $\ell=b=0$. Convexity plus quadratic growth implies PL with constant $\mu/4$. Also $L_F\le\bar L=\frac1n\sum_iL_i$, where $L_F$ is the smoothness constant of $F$.

**Lower-bound consistency.** Tyurin's Thm 2.3 gives $\Omega\big(\sigma^2\sum_ih_i/(n^2\mu^2\varepsilon^2)\big)$ on quadratics with curvature $\mu$ and offsets $\beta\approx\zeta$. It requires $\varepsilon^2\le c\beta^2/(\mu n)^2$, that is $\zeta\gtrsim n\mu\varepsilon$. There, this result's design is forced to be nearly uniform, so the two results do not conflict. Gains appear only when roughly $\zeta\lesssim\mu\varepsilon$, which means offsets at the optimum are below the target accuracy. Between these two thresholds lies a factor-$n$ window that neither result resolves.

**Reduction check and ancestry.** The new ingredient over the $\zeta=0$ result is short. Smooth convex components have constant gradients on $\arg\min F$ (cocoercivity), so the weighted surrogate's bias at any solution is at most $\sqrt n\zeta\|Pw\|$, and contraction absorbs it. When the weighted surrogate is strongly convex, this is the standard minimizer-perturbation reduction. For example, with $n=2$, $h=(1,s)$, $L_i=\mu$ and $\zeta\le\mu\varepsilon/64$, the theorem beats Malenia by a factor of about $s$. However, plain SGD on the fast worker alone achieves the same statistical time. The capped curvature certificate is inherited from the repository's $\zeta=0$ result; batching follows Malenia/Rennala and Tyurin's weighted Algorithm 1. The note's Gaussian benchmark and its sharpness claim are informal and not formalized.

## Conclusion

**The rate improves over Malenia SGD only by logarithms in the exhibited heterogeneous regime; the substantive change is extending the earlier capped weighting from common minimizers to small offsets at the optimum.**

The strongest baseline is Malenia SGD (Tyurin 2026, Thm F.2). It covers this entire class without knowing $\zeta$:
$$\underbrace{\big[\max_ih_i\,\bar L/\mu+\sigma^2\textstyle\sum_ih_i/(n\mu\varepsilon)^2\big]\log(R^2/\varepsilon^2)}_{\text{Malenia}}\quad\text{vs}\quad\underbrace{\sigma^2\textstyle\sum_ih_iw_i^2/\varepsilon^2+(\sum_jh_j^{-1})\max_i(h_iw_iL_i)\max_ih_i\log(2+R^2/\varepsilon^2)}_{\text{this result}}.$$
The noise term is never worse. It is strictly better only when curvature caps bind and roughly $\zeta\lesssim\mu\varepsilon$. In the note's example the total gain is $\Theta(\log^3N)$, one log of which comes from routine decreasing steps.

The gain is modest for concrete reasons. Beyond the repository's $\zeta=0$ theorem, the only new step is a standard bias bound. Polynomial gains on strongly convex instances are already available by running a fast worker alone. The startup term can be worse by up to $\max h/\min h$. The result also needs $\zeta$, every $L_i$, and exact $h_i$.

My search found no prior capped-weighting time bound with offsets at the optimum, but that does not prove priority. What can be claimed: an elapsed-time bound, including communication, for weighted SGD under heterogeneity at the optimum. Its noise term never exceeds Malenia's and shrinks when caps bind and offsets at the optimum fall below the target accuracy.

**Verdict: LIMITED OR INCREMENTAL NOVELTY**
**Confidence: medium**
