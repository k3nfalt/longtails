# Novelty assessment: DistributedWeightedInterpolation

[`result`](../../LongTails/Theorems/DistributedWeightedInterpolation.lean) proves the following. Take $n$ workers with convex $L_i$-smooth $f_i$ and a nonempty common minimizer set $S=\bigcap_i\arg\min f_i$, where the average $f$ has quadratic growth $\mu$ relative to $S$ and $\operatorname{dist}(x_0,S)\le R$. Each call at worker $i$ takes exactly $h_i$ seconds and returns an unbiased gradient with variance at most $\sigma^2$, with IID samples that are independent across workers. In a coordinator star with per-message latency $\ell$ and per-bit time $b$, the iterate returned by a speed-weighted batched SGD at a fixed index satisfies $\mathbb E\,\operatorname{dist}(x_{\rm out},S)^2\le\varepsilon$ ([setup](../../LongTails/Setups/DistributedWeakInterpolation.lean), [algorithm](../../LongTails/Algorithms/WeightedInterpolationSgd.lean), [profile](../../LongTails/Algorithms/WeakInterpolationDesign.lean)).

**Elapsed time.** Let $u_i=\min(L_i,c\,h_i)$, where $c\ge0$ solves $\sum_iu_i=n\mu$. This is a water-filling threshold over the breakpoints $L_i/h_i$, so the budget has no simpler closed form. Write $I=\sum_iu_i^2/h_i$, $H=\max_ih_i$ and $\kappa=(\sum_jh_j^{-1})\max_i(u_iL_i)/I$. Up to universal constants, the Lean budget is
$$
T=O\!\Big(\tfrac{\sigma^2}{I\varepsilon}+\kappa\log(2+\tfrac{R^2}{\varepsilon})\Big[H+\ell+b\Big(1+d\log\big(2+d+\kappa+\tfrac{R^2+\sigma^2\max_iu_i^2/\max_i(u_iL_i)^2}{\varepsilon}\big)\Big)\Big]\Big).
$$
The prior bounds below charge nothing for communication, so they are compared at $\ell=b=0$. Here $\bar L=\frac1n\sum_iL_i\ge L_f$, where $L_f$ is the smoothness constant of the average $f$. Note that $I\ge(n\mu)^2/\sum_ih_i$ always holds, because the uncapped allocation $u_i\propto h_i$ is optimal without caps.

**Reduction check.** The statistical gain over Malenia is not a substitution into F.2. It comes from a capped water-filling weighting together with a single inequality, $\sum w_i(a_i-u_i)\ge0$, which certifies both curvature and variance for the biased surrogate $\sum_iw_if_i$. The comparison with worker selection shows that most of the large-ratio gains are already available by dropping slow workers. The proof's ancestry is Malenia/Rennala-style batching and Tyurin's weighted Algorithm 1 (which leaves $w_i^k$ free), with standard SGD under quadratic growth and decreasing steps. Tyurin's Thm 3.2 lower bound (all $L_i=L_{\max}$, $\mu<L_{\max}/(2n)$) lies entirely in the uncapped regime, so it does not contradict the result. The note's own two-point lower-bound sketch is informal and is not formalized.

## Conclusion

**Yes, in a narrow regime: the statistical term beats the best published bound, but the initialization term is worse.** The strongest baseline is Malenia SGD (Tyurin 2026, Thm F.2), which applies to exactly this class with free communication:
$$
\underbrace{\big[H\bar L/\mu+\sigma^2\textstyle\sum_ih_i/(n^2\mu^2\varepsilon)\big]\log(R^2/\varepsilon)}_{\text{Malenia}}\quad\text{vs}\quad\underbrace{\sigma^2/(I\varepsilon)+\kappa H\log(2+R^2/\varepsilon)}_{\text{this result}}.
$$

Since $I\ge(n\mu)^2/\sum_ih_i$, the noise term is never worse. It is strictly better only when smoothness caps bind ($n\mu h_i>L_i\sum_jh_j$ for some $i$), which requires $\mu$ above about $L/n$. That is consistent with Tyurin's impossibility result for $\mu<L_{\max}/(2n)$. The price is a worse startup term: up to a factor $H/\min_jh_j$ in the uncapped case.

Much of the large gap over Malenia comes from slow workers carrying little curvature, and simply dropping those workers in Malenia already recovers that. Against that reduction, the verified gain from optimal weighting is $\Theta(\log N)$ in the note's example; a polynomial gap remains unshown. My search found no prior capped-weighting analysis under weak interpolation, but that is not proof of priority.

What can be claimed: a new weighted-SGD time bound under weak interpolation, whose leading noise term improves on Malenia and on worker selection when curvature caps bind. Over selection, the established gain is only logarithmic, and startup costs more.

**Verdict: LIMITED OR INCREMENTAL NOVELTY**
**Confidence: medium**
