# Novelty review — Projection complexity of stochastic convex optimization over many convex sets

**Selected theorem.** [`PenaltyKatyushaManyConstraint.result`](../../LongTails/Theorems/PenaltyKatyushaManyConstraint.lean) proves a single two‑oracle **upper bound (budget)** for the [setup](../../LongTails/Setups/ManyConstraintStochasticConvex.lean): minimizing a stochastic convex, componentwise‑`L`‑smooth objective `f = 𝔼 f_ξ` over the intersection `C = ⋂_{j≤m} C_j`, with a stochastic‑gradient oracle and an exact single‑set projection oracle `Π_{C_j}` counted separately, so that the [algorithm](../../LongTails/Algorithms/ManyConstraintMethod.lean) (penalty + strongly‑convex Katyusha over the `m` penalty components + minibatch SGD + Fejér clean‑up) outputs a point with `𝔼(f−f*)₊ ≤ ε` and `𝔼 dist(·,C)² ≤ δ²`. The matching lower bound in the companion note is a **paper proof only; it is not in the formal deliverable.**

**Proved budget, in the original parameters.** Writing `κ` = average‑form linear‑regularity constant, `σ²` = second moment of the stochastic gradient at `x*`, `L` = componentwise smoothness, `R ≥ ‖x₀−x*‖`, and `ℓ = log(κ(1 + σR/ε + LR²/ε))`:

- Projections `N_p = O( (m + √(mκ)·(σR/ε + R√(L/ε)))·ℓ + κ·log(R/δ) )`
- Gradients `N_g = O( (m + √(mκ)·(σR/ε + R√(L/ε)) + σ²R²/ε² + L R²/ε)·ℓ )`

(These are the two budget entries of the Lean `budget` with universal constants suppressed; `ε` = objective accuracy, `δ` = feasibility accuracy.)

**Standard‑reduction check / mechanism.** The upper bound follows by a **composition of known pieces**: quadratic penalty for the intersection (as in **Kundu–Bach–Bhattacharyya**, [arXiv:1710.06465](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06465), AISTATS 2018, for the deterministic smooth case), strongly‑convex **Katyusha** acceleration of the `m` penalty components ([Allen‑Zhu, arXiv:1603.05953](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05953)), minibatch SGD, and a Fejér clean‑up under linear regularity; the one genuinely non‑routine step proved here is absorbing the **state‑dependent** gradient noise into the Katyusha energy with a constant minibatch. The authors themselves report a strictly better composition ("B8″": penalty + gradient sliding, [Ivanova et al. arXiv:2002.02706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02706)) giving the **same `N_p` and better `N_g`**, so this method's gradient count is not optimal. The lower bound (reduction from **Woodworth–Srebro**, [arXiv:1605.08003](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08003)) is a proof ancestor of the *characterization* claim but is **not formalized**.

## Conclusion

**No optimization advantage over the strongest achievable method is established; the deliverable is a routine, if careful, composition that gives a regime‑specific projection‑complexity improvement over the written‑down baselines.** The strongest directly relevant baselines are the random single‑set‑projection SGD analyses (Wang et al.; Patrascu–Necoara) and the stochastic incremental‑proximal anchor (Vaidyan–Friedlander–Alacaoglu).

What is genuinely better: for **smooth** stochastic `f` with only a second moment at `x*`, the formalized upper bound accelerates the projection count from `O(κ(σ+LR)²R²/ε²)` (non‑accelerated, bounded‑subgradient) to `N_p = O((m+√(mκ)(σR/ε+R√(L/ε)))ℓ + κlog(R/δ))`, i.e. `ε⁻²→ε⁻¹`, and it outputs a stronger last‑iterate‑type `(f−f*)₊ + 𝔼 dist²` criterion. This gain survives shared terms when `m ≲ κ(σR/ε)²` and `L ≲ σ²/ε`; it loses when `m` is large (the `√(mκ)` and `m` terms dominate) and the gradient count is admittedly suboptimal.

Where it falls short of "substantive": every ingredient is standard (penalty à la Kundu + Katyusha + minibatch + clean‑up), the authors acknowledge a strictly better composition (B8″) exists, and the one feature that would make this a landmark — a *matching lower bound* turning the upper bound into a tight two‑oracle characterization — is a paper proof **outside the Lean deliverable**. The improvement over Wang/PN is also partly an artifact of assuming smoothness, so it is not an unconditional rate gain.

What can be claimed: a machine‑checked, accelerated two‑oracle upper bound (`N_p = Õ(m+√(mκ)Γ+κlog(R/δ))`) for stochastic smooth convex optimization over an intersection of `m` sets with a single‑set projection oracle, improving the projection‑count `ε`‑dependence and output criterion over the written‑down prior methods in an explicit regime, but by a standard composition with a suboptimal gradient budget and without the characterizing lower bound in the formal result.

**Verdict: LIMITED OR INCREMENTAL NOVELTY. Confidence: medium.**
