# Novelty assessment: DistributedRelativeGrowth

[`result`](../../LongTails/Theorems/DistributedRelativeGrowth.lean) proves that a speed-weighted, quantized, two-phase batched SGD on a coordinator star returns a fixed-index iterate $X$ with $\mathbb E\,\mathrm{dist}(X,S)^2\le\varepsilon$ within the stated elapsed time. The setting is $n$ workers with convex $L_i$-smooth $f_i$, where the mean $F$ has $\mu$-quadratic growth around $S=\arg\min F$, a minimizer lies within $R$ of $x_0$, and the gradients satisfy the RMS relative growth $\frac1n\sum_i\|\nabla f_i\|^2\le\rho\|\nabla F\|^2$ everywhere. Each worker has unbiased gradients with variance $\sigma^2$ and exact cost $h_i$ ([setup](../../LongTails/Setups/DistributedRelativeGrowth.lean), [algorithm](../../LongTails/Algorithms/RelativeGrowthBatches.lean)).

**Elapsed time.** Here $\ell$ is the per-message latency, $b$ the per-bit time, $d$ the dimension, and $H=\max_ih_i$. Let $u$ minimize $\sum_iv_i^2/h_i$ subject to $\sum_iv_i=n$ and $\sum_iv_i^2\le n\rho$. This is a one-dimensional implicit threshold, and no closed form exists for general $h$. Put $J=\sum_iu_i^2/h_i$ and $\kappa=2(\sum_jh_j^{-1})\max_iu_i\max(\mu u_i,L_i)/(\mu J)$. Up to universal constants, the Lean budget is
$$
T=O\!\Big(\tfrac{\sigma^2}{\mu^2J\varepsilon}+\kappa\log(2+\tfrac{R^2}{\varepsilon})\Big[H+\ell+b\,d\log\!\Big(4+d+\kappa+\tfrac{R^2+\sigma^2\max_iu_i^2/\max_i(u_i\max(\mu u_i,L_i))^2}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big]\Big).
$$
The two quantities that matter are $J\ge n^2/\sum_ih_i$ (equality when $u\propto h$ is feasible) and $J>n^2/\sum_ih_i$ exactly when $n\sum_ih_i^2/(\sum_ih_i)^2>\rho$. The prior bounds charge nothing for communication, so they are compared at $\ell=b=0$.

**Reduction check.** Plugging Malenia or Rennala into this class does not give $J$. The key step is $\sum_i(u_i/h_i)v_i\ge J$ over all feasible alignment profiles $v$, followed by slope comparison ([Pérez-Aros et al., Thm 3.1](https://repositorio.uchile.cl/bitstream/handle/2250/177278/Determination-of-convex-functions.pdf?sequence=1)). Together these transfer the growth of $F$ to the biased surrogate $\sum_i(u_i/h_i)f_i$. A cruder version of this step, Malenia on a fast subset, already gives polynomial gains over arithmetic Malenia. The note claims weighting beats the best uniform prefix by only $\Theta(\log m)$ in one example; I did not fully verify that claim. The surrounding framework is proof ancestry (Malenia batching, the repository's quantized two-phase protocol, SGD under quadratic growth). I checked the note's prose two-point lower bound ($f_i=\mu u_i(x_1\mp2\sqrt\varepsilon)^2/2$, KL $\le8T\varepsilon\mu^2J/\sigma^2$). It is correct and shows that $\sigma^2/(\mu^2J\varepsilon)$ is sharp whenever $L_i\ge\mu u_i$, but it is not formalized.

## Conclusion

**Yes: under RMS relative growth, the leading statistical time is provably better than the best published bound.** The strongest baseline is Malenia SGD (Tyurin 2026, Thm F.2), which applies to this class with free communication:

| | statistical term | transient |
|---|---|---|
| Malenia | $\sigma^2\sum_ih_i/(n^2\mu^2\varepsilon)\cdot\log(R^2/\varepsilon)$ | $HL_F/\mu\cdot\log(R^2/\varepsilon)$ |
| this result | $\sigma^2/(\mu^2J\varepsilon)$ | $\kappa H\log(2+R^2/\varepsilon)$ |

Since $J\ge n^2/\sum_ih_i$, the noise term is never worse. It is strictly better when speeds are uneven relative to $\rho$, i.e. $n\sum h_i^2/(\sum h_i)^2>\rho$. With workers taking $1,1,H$ seconds and $\rho=2$, the whole time improves from $O(H^3\log H)$ to $O(H^2)$ at $\varepsilon=H^{-2}$. This answers, for the zero-additive RMS class, the arithmetic-mean barrier that Tyurin's Theorem 2.3 shows when an additive heterogeneity term is present. The prose two-point argument indicates that the new coefficient is sharp.

The price is a startup term that can be worse than Malenia's under unequal speeds. The method also needs public $\rho$, $L_i$ and exact $h_i$. The result does not dominate the repository's capped weak-interpolation bound or, on its narrower class, Rennala. My search found no prior work with this weighting, but that does not establish priority.

What can be claimed: a new, hardware-dependent and apparently sharp statistical time for distributed SGD under mean relative gradient growth, polynomially better than arithmetic-mean Malenia when speeds are heterogeneous.

**Verdict: SUBSTANTIVE NOVELTY SUPPORTED**
**Confidence: medium**
