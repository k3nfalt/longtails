# Novelty assessment: BacktrackingCompositeGeneralized

The [theorem](../../LongTails/Theorems/BacktrackingCompositeGeneralized.lean) proves the following ([setup](../../LongTails/Setups/CompositeGeneralizedParameterFree.lean), [algorithm](../../LongTails/Algorithms/BacktrackingComposite.lean)). Minimize `F = f + ψ` over a convex `Q` in a real Hilbert space, where `f` is convex with continuous gradient and satisfies the symmetric first-order (L₀,L₁) condition, and `ψ` is convex on `Q`. The oracles are exact `∇f` and the exact prox. A similar-triangles accelerated method with step doubling and halving receives only `(x₀, h₀)`. After the stated number of calls its deterministic last iterate `x̂ ∈ Q` satisfies `F(x̂) − F* ≤ ε`. The call count includes all three calls of every rejected trial.

**Complexity.** Here `L₀, L₁` are the smoothness constants, `G ≥ ‖∇f(x*)‖`, `R ≥ ‖x₀ − x*‖`, `Δ ≥ F(x₀) − F*` and `h₀ > 0` is the arbitrary initial trial step. Let `L* = L₀ + L₁G`. The Lean budget is `3⌈4 + 61√(L*R²/ε) + (35+1067L₁R)·log(…) + 4log(…) + 2log(…)⌉`, which gives

`O( √((L₀+L₁G)R²/ε) + (1+L₁R)·log(2 + L₁R + 1/((L₀+L₁G)h₀) + L₁²Δ/(L₀+L₁G)) + log(2 + (L₀+L₁G)h₀ + L₁²Δh₀) )`

This holds for all `h₀, L₀, R, Δ, ε > 0` and `L₁, G ≥ 0`. The updates use none of these parameters. The stopping budget uses all of them.

**Reduction check.** Running Nesterov's backtracking composite method ([Math. Program. 140, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-012-0629-5)) with a smoothness constant on a ball around `x*` puts a factor of order `e^{L₁R}` into the leading term. Li et al. note that their method "needs a doubling or backtracking wrapper" when `R` and `τ` are unknown. With only gradient and prox oracles, a guess-and-double wrapper cannot check accuracy. The new step, which I checked against the companion note and Lean declaration names (`accepts_of_small`, `accepts_first`), shows that the gradient test accepts every step below a cap of order `min(A/(L*A + L₁²R²), A/(1+L₁R)²)` that depends on the current state. Its anchor is the main iterate `x`, together with the bound `‖p − x*‖ ≤ 21R/2` on the prox point. **Proof ancestry:** the composite gradient mapping and backtracking (Nesterov 2013), the centering identity with shifted cocoercivity (Li et al., Lemmas 4.1–4.2; also the repository's [`CompositeGeneralizedAccelerated`](../../LongTails/Theorems/CompositeGeneralizedAccelerated.md)), and the repository's unconstrained backtracking argument ([`BacktrackingGeneralizedAccelerated`](../../LongTails/Theorems/BacktrackingGeneralizedAccelerated.md)). Within the repository, this theorem is the merger of those two results.

## Conclusion

**The rate is unchanged; the gain is that the composite method no longer needs any problem parameter.** The strongest baseline is Li et al.'s accelerated proximal method (arXiv:2512.09443v3, Sept 2026). It covers the same composite (L₀,L₁) class with the same leading term, but its step rule needs `L₀, L₁, G, R` and `Δ`.

| | updates need | oracle calls |
|---|---|---|
| Li et al. | `L₀, L₁, G, R, Δ` | `√(L*R²/ε) + (1+L₁R)log(2+Δ/ε) + log(2+Δ/(L*R²))` |
| this result | `h₀ > 0` only | `√(L*R²/ε) + (1+L₁R)log(2+L₁R+1/(L*h₀)+L₁²Δ/L*) + log(2+L*h₀+L₁²Δh₀)` |

Both bounds have the same leading term. The logarithms trade an `ε`-dependence for an `h₀`-mismatch cost, and the selected method spends roughly three times as many calls per step. The stopping budget still needs every parameter. On the unconstrained special case it also improves GRAAL's `(L₁R)³` start-up to about `L₁R·(L₁R + log)`.

The comparison shows parameter-free updates and no new complexity order. My search found no earlier parameter-free accelerated composite (L₀,L₁) method without exponential factors, but that supports priority without proving it. Function-value methods such as AC-FGM remain unquantified on this class.

What can be claimed is the first checked parameter-free, gradient-and-prox accelerated method for composite (L₀,L₁) problems that matches the known-parameter complexity up to logarithms. The prior bound and lower-bound construction belong to Li et al.

**Verdict: SUBSTANTIVE NOVELTY SUPPORTED**
**Confidence: medium**
