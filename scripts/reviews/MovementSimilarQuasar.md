# MovementSimilarQuasar: novelty assessment

The [theorem](../../LongTails/Theorems/MovementSimilarQuasar.lean) proves that the [SVRG-type continuized accelerated method](../../LongTails/Algorithms/MovementQuasar.lean) returns a final iterate $X$ with $\mathbb E[F(X)]-F(x_\star)\le\varepsilon$ for every instance of the [setup](../../LongTails/Setups/ContinuizedSimilarQuasar.lean). There, $F=\frac1n\sum_i f_i$ on a real Hilbert space with differentiable $f_i$. The gradient $\nabla F$ is $L$-Lipschitz, and $F$ is $(\gamma,\mu)$-strongly quasar-convex about a global minimizer $x_\star$. The initial gap satisfies $F(x_0)-F_\star\le\Delta$. The components satisfy the centered similarity bound $\frac1n\sum_i\|\nabla(f_i-F)(x)-\nabla(f_i-F)(y)\|^2\le\delta^2\|x-y\|^2$. The components need not be convex or individually smooth, and the problem need not interpolate. The assumed quadratic growth follows from strong quasar-convexity ([Hinder–Sidford–Sohoni, COLT 2020, Cor. 1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v125/hinder20a/hinder20a.pdf)).

**Complexity.** Each epoch makes $n$ snapshot calls and then $n$ cached-SVRG jumps. Each jump follows an exponential-time flow. The exact budget is $2n+\lceil2560(n+\sqrt{L/\mu}/\gamma+(n\delta/\mu)^{2/3}\gamma^{-4/3})\log(1+2\Delta/(\gamma\varepsilon))\rceil$ component-gradient calls:
$$N=O\Big(n+\Big[n+\frac{\sqrt{L/\mu}}{\gamma}+\frac{(n\delta/\mu)^{2/3}}{\gamma^{4/3}}\Big]\log\Big(1+\frac{\Delta}{\gamma\varepsilon}\Big)\Big),\qquad 0<\mu\le L,\ 0<\gamma\le1,\ \delta,\Delta\ge0,\ \varepsilon>0.$$
The updates need $L,\mu,\gamma,\delta,n$, and the stopping rule needs $\Delta,\varepsilon$. No function values are used. Write $\kappa=L/\mu$. Strong quasar-convexity implies the PL inequality with constant $\gamma^2\mu$. The mean-square smoothness is at most $\sqrt{L^2+\delta^2}$.

**Reduction check and proof ancestry.** The PL reduction cannot yield $\sqrt\kappa/\gamma$, because PL alone forbids acceleration. Convex similarity methods (AccSVRS, Katyusha X) need convex $F$ and convex proximal subproblems; regularization need not preserve quasar-convexity. [Fu–Xu–Wilson, ICML 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04736) (QASVRG) assumes interpolation (§2, Assumption 2.7), so it does not apply. [Hermant, arXiv 2512.16533v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16533) assumes strong growth (Assumption 4), so it does not apply either. [Wang–Wibisono, ICLR 2023, Thm. 2/Cor. 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07851) and Even et al. (NeurIPS 2021) supply the continuized energy. That energy bounds the gap at Poisson times, or with constant probability, rather than at a fixed count. The new step is in the [epoch proof](../../LongTails/Proofs/QuasarMovementEpoch.lean). It adds a backward-weighted snapshot-distance term $W_k\|x-w\|^2$, in the spirit of Reddi et al. and Katyusha X. It also absorbs the flow movement and gradient dissipation under $n^2\delta^2\theta^3=O(\gamma\mu^2)$. This produces the $(n\delta/\mu)^{2/3}$ term rather than the earlier $\delta^2/\mu^2$ term.

**Conclusion**

**Yes: on strongly quasar-convex finite sums it improves the best checked bounds, and it is never worse than the PL-based baseline.** The strongest applicable baselines are PAGE and Hinder et al.'s full-batch accelerated method. PAGE becomes applicable because strong quasar-convexity implies PL.

| | component-gradient calls |
|---|---|
| PAGE | $(n+\sqrt n\sqrt{L^2+\delta^2}/(\gamma^2\mu))\log(\Delta/\varepsilon)$ |
| Hinder et al., full batch | $n\sqrt\kappa\,\gamma^{-1}\log(\kappa/\gamma)\log(\Delta/(\gamma\varepsilon))$ |
| this result | $(n+\sqrt\kappa/\gamma+(n\delta/\mu)^{2/3}\gamma^{-4/3})\log(1+\Delta/(\gamma\varepsilon))$ |

The gain over PAGE holds for all parameters, up to a $\log(1/\gamma)$ factor. It is polynomial; for example, $\kappa^{3/2}\to\kappa$ when $n=\kappa$ and $\delta$ is small. The full-batch method is better only when $\delta\gg\sqrt{n\gamma}\,\mu^{1/4}L^{3/4}$. On the convex overlap, AccSVRS has a better similarity term $n^{3/4}\sqrt{\delta/\mu}$ and wins when $\delta\approx L$.

The comparison establishes these gains. Literature priority is a separate question: I found no earlier accelerated variance-reduced guarantee for non-interpolating strongly quasar-convex finite sums. Within the repository, the earlier ContinuizedSimilarQuasar result already opened that setting. What this result adds is the improvement of $\delta^2$ to $(n\delta)^{2/3}$, which leaves it a polynomial improvement over the checked literature for large $\delta$.

The claimable contribution is a gradient-only accelerated variance-reduced bound for strongly quasar-convex finite sums under mean-square similarity. It dominates the PL baseline and beats full-batch acceleration unless similarity is very large, but it is not optimal in $\delta$ on the convex overlap.

**Verdict: SUBSTANTIVE NOVELTY SUPPORTED**
