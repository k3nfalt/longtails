# SymmetricThirdOrder: stochastic second-order convex optimization under a Lipschitz third derivative

[`result`](../../LongTails/Theorems/SymmetricThirdOrder.lean) assumes $f:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R$ is convex and $C^3$ with an $L_3$-Lipschitz third derivative on all of $\mathbb R^d$. A minimizer $x_\star$ satisfies $\|x_0-x_\star\|\le R$ and $f(x_0)-f_\star\le\Delta$. Each oracle call returns one joint sample $(g,H)$, where $g$ is unbiased with variance at most $\sigma_g^2$ and $H$ is any operator with $\mathbb E\|H-\nabla^2 f\|^2\le\sigma_h^2$ (it need not be symmetric, PSD or unbiased). Samples are IID and drawn fresh at every query. Under these assumptions, the output of the [constructed algorithm](../../LongTails/Algorithms/SymmetricThirdOrder.lean) lies in $\bar B(x_0,R)$ and satisfies $P\{f(\hat x)-f_\star\le\varepsilon\}\ge 2/3$ ([setup](../../LongTails/Setups/StochasticThirdDerivativeConvex.lean)). The algorithm knows $L_3,R,\Delta,\varepsilon,\sigma_g,\sigma_h$.

**Proved budget**, read off the Lean `budget` with only the $2^{100}$ constant suppressed. It is valid for all $L_3,R,\Delta,\varepsilon>0$ and $\sigma_g,\sigma_h\ge0$:
$$
N=O\!\Big(\big[1+\sqrt{\sigma_hR^2/\varepsilon}+(L_3R^4/\varepsilon)^{1/5}+\sigma_g^2R^2/\varepsilon^2\big]\cdot\log^4\!\big(2+\Delta/\varepsilon+\sigma_hR^2/\varepsilon+L_3R^4/\varepsilon\big)\Big)
$$
joint gradient–Hessian calls.

**Reduction check.** Neither direct composition gives this result. AINE needs exact gradients, and naive batching makes the cost multiplicative. The tensor version in Agafonov et al. (ICLR 2024, Thm 5.6) needs a third-derivative oracle. A finite-difference approximation of third derivatives from noisy Hessians or gradients amplifies the noise. The new mechanism is a residual certificate for AINE-style outer acceleration built from noisy gradients and Hessians, using robust pilots, constrained Newton cleanup, and a fresh unbiased correction batch, at additive statistical cost. Proof ancestry: Nesterov's symmetric (odd-part) surrogate ([JOTA 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10957-021-01930-y)), Kamzolov's $\tilde O(\varepsilon^{-1/5})$ hyperfast second-order method ([MOTOR 2020](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-58657-7_15)) and AINE supply the deterministic rate. They give no stochastic certificate. The terms $\sqrt{\sigma_hR^2/\varepsilon}$, $(L_3R^4/\varepsilon)^{1/5}$ and $\sigma_g^2R^2/\varepsilon^2$ match the restricted-class lower bounds in Chen et al. Prop 5.1 and Agafonov et al. (2024) Thm 4.2 up to the $\log^4$ factor. These lower bounds cover deterministic or linear-span algorithms only, so optimality is not established.

## Conclusion

**Yes, this appears to be a new end-to-end guarantee: the $\varepsilon^{-1/5}$ deterministic term of third-order-smooth second-order methods, combined with additive stochastic noise terms.**

The strongest proved stochastic baseline is accelerated stochastic cubic Newton (Agafonov et al. 2026). On instances that also have a Lipschitz Hessian, and up to logarithms:

| | constant-confidence calls |
|---|---|
| Agafonov et al. Thm 5.1 | $\sigma_g^2R^2/\varepsilon^2+\sqrt{\sigma_hR^2/\varepsilon}+(L_2R^3/\varepsilon)^{2/7}$ |
| This result | $[\sigma_g^2R^2/\varepsilon^2+\sqrt{\sigma_hR^2/\varepsilon}+(L_3R^4/\varepsilon)^{1/5}]\log^4$ |

The gain is polynomial when the deterministic term dominates and $L_3^7\varepsilon^3\ll L_2^{10}R^2$. For example, a quartic objective with an $O(1)$ third-derivative bound gains a polynomial factor in $R^4/\varepsilon$. When the noise terms dominate, the result is $\log^4$ worse. The two classes are incomparable: the selected result requires a global $L_3$ bound and a global oracle.

Without noise, Chen et al.'s AINE already achieves the same rate, so the deterministic exponent is not new. What is new is handling unbiased stochastic gradients and second-moment Hessian errors without the multiplicative batching cost that a naive reduction would incur. My comparison with the unverified batching reduction is heuristic.

Priority is supported only by searching a fast-moving four-month-old literature. The contribution that can be claimed is the first stochastic second-order guarantee I could find with additive noise terms and the $(L_3R^4/\varepsilon)^{1/5}$ term, not a new optimal rate.
