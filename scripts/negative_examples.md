# Negative examples for contribution assessment

These are user-reviewed examples of **NO IMPROVEMENT** for research selection and contribution assessment. They correct the positive verdicts in the existing reviews; do not reuse those verdicts as evidence of significance. Recheck primary sources for any new or changed claim, comparing the same assumptions, oracle costs, and accuracy/output criterion.

- **BoundedScalarConvex** ([review](negative_examples/BoundedScalarConvex.md)): Liu–Zhou's linear-decay guarantee already gives the same `O(1 + H²R²/ε²)` last-iterate complexity under broader assumptions. Comparing only with their weaker constant-step bound creates a misleading logarithmic improvement. Constant rather than decreasing steps does not by itself improve complexity, scope, or output.
- **InterpolatedFiniteSum** ([review](negative_examples/CyclicGradientInterpolation.md)): the cyclic bound is `O(m + m⁴LR²/ε)`, while accelerated optimization of the average uses `O(m + m√(LR²/ε))` component-gradient calls. A full step `1/L` instead of Cai–Diakonikolas's smaller step is not an advantage by itself. Evaluate the entire complexity and its parameter tradeoffs; a gain over one cyclic baseline does not establish an improvement over the strongest applicable method.
- **RegretMatchingPotential** ([review](negative_examples/RegretMatchingPotential.md)): the simultaneous RM+ guarantee costs `O(1 + B²(m+n)⁴/ε²)`, while the cited discounted alternating RM+ guarantee costs `O(1 + B² max(m,n)/ε²)` in the same paired-vector oracle units. Compare with that method, not only the weaker lazy simultaneous baseline. Removing alternating or discounted updates alone does not establish an improved equilibrium-computation guarantee.
- **BlockSmoothConvexConcave** ([review](negative_examples/AlexGdaConvexConcave.md)): a convergence analysis of unmodified Alex-GDA under merely convex–concave assumptions may be new; its priority remains unresolved. However, on the finite-dimensional overlap and for fixed extrapolation parameters, its `O(1 + L²R²/ε²)` selected-iterate guarantee is matched by optimistic gradient with last-iterate accuracy and improved by anchored methods with `O(1 + LR/ε)` calls, where `L = max(Lx, Ly) + Lxy`. Treat this as **NO IMPROVEMENT** unless an independent advantage is established. This is a caution about significance, not a claim that the proof is already known: a new method-specific analysis alone does not establish a better optimization guarantee.

- **QuadraticFiniteSum** ([review](negative_examples/RandomReshufflingQuadratic.md)): with `κ = L/μ` and `ℓ = log(1 + LR²/ε)`, the reshuffling bound is `O(n + [nκ + L^(3/2)G/(μ²√ε) + (n²L³G²/(μ⁴ε))^(1/3)]ℓ)`. Accelerated full-gradient descent gives `O(n + n√κ ℓ)` component-gradient calls on the same class, with deterministic objective accuracy and no dependence on `G` ([Theorem 3.18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.4980)). Indefinite components do not prevent acceleration of their strongly convex, smooth average; each full gradient costs exactly `n` component calls. The improved initialization term relative to Ahn–Yun–Sra's reshuffling analysis can matter when `κ ≫ n`, but it remains worse than acceleration even when `G = 0`. Treat this as **NO IMPROVEMENT** for the problem class, while acknowledging that the method-specific analysis may be new.

Additional motivation caution: **HessianLipschitzTemporalDiscrepancy** and **QuasarSignedBias** assume query-independent gradient errors with uniformly bounded cumulative sums. This cancellation helps the analysis, but no concrete application establishing the exact oracle model has been demonstrated. Treat the assumption as artificial until independently motivated; an analogy to error feedback alone is insufficient. This concerns relevance, not theorem correctness.

Do not exclude stronger applicable methods merely because the setup fixes an algorithm. A different parameter choice, schedule, or method-specific proof may be mathematically new without providing an improvement. Credit tuning changes only through improved total complexity in an explicit regime. To overturn one of these negative assessments, identify an independently supported advantage over the strongest applicable baseline, not just a different recurrence or proof.
